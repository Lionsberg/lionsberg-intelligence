# The Way In

*From the door of lionsberg.world to the first Turn: the Threshold film, Meet your companion, the one line that wakes an agent on her own computer, and the first week dealt as cards. This page sets the words and the order; the builders write the code; §9 is the bar. It is read with [DESIGN.md](DESIGN.md), [DESIGN-ROOM.md](DESIGN-ROOM.md), [DESIGN-THE-BAR.md](DESIGN-THE-BAR.md) and [engine/STATE.md](../engine/STATE.md), and the companion's own side of the first week is [day-one.md](../skills/the-record/day-one.md). The door and the companion page live with the portal, and the invitation card with the print files (`card-invite-6x4in.pdf`, beside `card-6x4in.pdf`), outside this kit.*

**Who she is.** The newcomer: someone a friend sat beside at a table and handed a card. She may be a grandmother. She has a computer she can type on, a phone, and no idea what a terminal is. Everything below is designed for her first. She is called *she* here for one person; every string is written for anyone.

**Her own machine.** Her companion lives on her own computer, and so does her record. Her companion's body is Claude Code, its home is HQ, its record is `record/` in HQ, and its doors are the My Agent icon, a printed Deal, or her phone. That is what makes a Player+ sovereign and free. Keeping a companion for someone on another computer is a service some cells may offer later; it is named once, on the companion page (§3.2), and is never the default.

## 1. The one idea

**A film ends on a door; behind the door, in the same night, she names a companion, pastes one line, and a few minutes later it wakes on her own computer, greets her by name, and deals her the first card of her life in the Game.**

What it must feel like:

- **Magical:** the companion the film offered arrives on the same night, in her own home, speaking first.
- **The impossible made possible:** an intelligence builds itself a home on her computer in front of her, tells her every step before it takes it, writes her a receipt of everything it changed, and then wakes under the name she gave it. Nobody she knows has seen that. (The receipt is the install guide's own.)
- **Never cheesy:** no drawn light, no cosmos, no confetti, no countdown, no "AI-powered" anything. The magic is in what is true and small: a name she chose, a line in pencil that becomes her first line, a card that asks one question, ink that dries when a witness sees a commitment kept.

The test of the idea is the one-breath test ([DESIGN.md](DESIGN.md) §2.1): by the end of her first evening she can say to a neighbour, in one breath, *"I have a companion on my own computer that keeps track of what I committed to and what others committed to me, tells me the two things that need me today, shows me the whole when I ask, and never shares my words without asking."*

**The one thing at the right moment.** The first evening is for wonder. A key, a delegation or a conformance sheet asked before it matters spends her wonder on what cannot yet mean anything, so each arrives at the moment it does (§4). Confirmation is asked only for what cannot be undone.

## 2. The door: the Threshold, played in the wise right way

The Threshold film: a voice, the score, 5 minutes 37 seconds, the five beats of a door that holds. It is offered as held acts on the page and as film masters for full size, the phone and the web. What follows is how it is offered, never what it says.

| Moment | What happens |
|---|---|
| Arrival | The held hero: the mark alone on the night ground. Nothing plays. |
| Consent | Under the mark, in dust: **This journey has sound · About six minutes.** Then **Begin with sound · Begin in silence**, and beneath them **Skip the intro**. One tap is consent, the browser's audio gesture and the start, all at once. The cost is said first. |
| By her hand | Autoplay begins only on that tap. A forward gesture steers; a step back pauses; it never resumes on its own. |
| Sound | *Begin with sound* is first, because the voice is the film. *In silence* gives the words only, at the reading pace. On a phone, the first tap also unlocks audio; the ringer and mute switch are tested before release. |
| Skip | Always visible, upper right under play, and from the hero. **Skip lands on the companion page**, never on a blank and never on the reading sections below. |
| The end card | The last page (*You are here for a reason / You are not alone / Welcome to The Gates of LIØNSBERG*) holds. The music falls to silence under the last line. A gesture only brightens the door; the click is the way through. **Nothing scrolls beyond it.** The Story, the Game and the Flame sections live in the companion page's lower part and the menu, never below the film. |
| Return visits | A visitor who has seen it once lands on the companion page, with **Watch the Threshold again** as a quiet pill. |

**What the end card offers: one gesture. *Meet your companion.*** The film's script repeats the door across its last four pages; every one of them goes to the same place, the companion page, and the click crossfades within the same sky (one continuous world, the Award Bar's first principle). There are two ways of building it, for the builder to choose: a same-origin cross-document view transition to `/companion/`, or the companion page as the next held section of the same document. Either way the companion page also has its own address, so that a card's code and a return visit can land there directly.

**The door gives no light.** The door brightens by its border and its type only (gold light at full strength): no glow on its hover, no glowing pulse, no box-shadow. The journey's candle changes the grade; it never draws a flame. Open: whether the Golden Seed's point of light stays on the door until the drawn seed exists, which the ember rule (§6) would strike.

## 3. Meet your companion

A real page, in the same night world: the brand's ground, the brand's type, one idea. It is static, it sends nothing, and it stores nothing on any server. Its whole job is to turn a wish into one line she can paste.

### 3.1 The first five lines

Arriving at the reading pace, one beat each, Cormorant italic for the first, EB Garamond after:

1. *You came through the door.*
2. Your companion has two forms: a little book you can hold, and a Supersuit that lives on your own computer and thinks beside you.
3. The little book is yours tonight. Print it, or read it here.
4. The Supersuit asks two things of you: a name for it, and how it should reach you each day.
5. Then it wakes on your computer, speaks first, and deals you your first card. About twenty minutes, most of it waiting.

Beneath, two made objects side by side, never links in a list: **the Little Book** (an engraving of the book; one door: *Print the Little Book*; on the night it matters, a plain printable PDF of the Seed rather than "coming soon") and **the Supersuit** (the engraved still point, unlit; one door: *Wake your companion*). Under both, the film's own line, set small in Cormorant italic: *"We strongly recommend you choose both."* And in dust: *With a notebook and two or three people, you are already a complete Player. The Supersuit adds reach.* "Both" is said before "optional".

### 3.2 The two questions

*Wake your companion* opens two questions, one at a time, nothing else on the screen:

- **What will you call it?** One field. Beneath it, in dust: *You will say it many times a day. A word from nature keeps it open. You can change it later.*
- **How should it reach you each day?** Three large choices: **On this computer** · **On my phone** · **On paper**. Each says one line: *A page opens when you sit down.* · *Your phone becomes a window into your computer. Your computer stays on.* · *It prints the day's cards.*

Nothing else is asked. No email, no account on our side, no phone number. The page keeps both answers only in her own browser, for this visit, and works with storage blocked.

**If she is on a phone right now** (the page can tell), it says so at once: *Your companion lives on a computer. Open this page there.* with **Copy the link** and the phone's own share sheet, both her own acts. It also offers the Little Book, which works on a phone tonight.

**If she has no computer:** *Paper is a complete way to play.* The Little Book, the printable Deal, and one sentence: *Some cells may offer to keep a companion for you on their computer. That is a later choice, and yours.* That is the only place hosting is named, and the default is the player's own machine.

### 3.3 Three steps on her own computer

The page then shows the cost first, then three counted steps (*1 of 3*), each a card in the same type, inside the page's own night. The paste is one gesture there, never a terminal instruction left bare.

**Three steps. About twenty minutes, and on a Mac one long wait you can walk away from. A friend beside you makes it easier.**

1. **An account.** The one configuration the companion needs, with the paper path and a cell's hosting named beside it. *Your companion thinks with Claude, an AI made by Anthropic. Type claude.ai into your browser yourself, make an account, and choose the Pro plan. If you have one, this step is done.* About five minutes. This is the one step that cannot be made magic, so it is honest and first.
2. **One line.** The page shows **her line**, composed in the page from her two answers, with one copy button, for her computer (the page reads Mac or Windows and offers the other as a quiet switch). *Open Terminal (press Cmd and Space, type Terminal, press Return). Paste this line, and press Return.* The line installs Claude Code, starts it, and hands it her sentence:

   > *Download the LIØNSBERG install guide to a file and read it whole. Then build a home for my companion on this computer by following it, telling me each step in one plain line before you take it. My companion's name is River. It reaches me on my phone. I am new to this.*

   The sentence is the install guide's own, from Path A of [the Quest](<../bookshelf/getting-started/Becoming a Player+ — the Quest.md>), with her name and door added. The terminal form is the Quest's `curl … | bash` line followed by `claude`, with the sentence as its opening prompt. Beneath the line: *If anything stops, paste the same line again. It picks up where it stopped.*
3. **Your companion wakes.** *The first run asks you to choose colours and sign in. Then it tells you, in five plain lines, what it will build. Before each step it asks your yes. When a new dark window opens and River speaks, you are through.*

**What the page says while it runs.** The page cannot see her computer, and it says so plainly, once: *This page cannot see your computer. It tells you what you will see.* Then, as a quiet stepped list she may follow or ignore, the five things the install builds, each in her words: *It teaches your computer where your companion's AI lives. · It makes a home called HQ. · It brings in the kit: your companion's charter and the Seed. · It starts Git, which keeps a quiet history (on a Mac, a window may ask to install developer tools: Install, then Agree; up to forty minutes; carry on). · It puts an icon called My Agent on your Desktop.* And last: *It writes you a receipt: everything it changed, and how to undo it.*

**No vendor's name on her screen** beyond the account step. Open: whether the account step can say where to make the account without naming the vendor.

### 3.4 The next ten minutes

Two clocks, both to be measured, both said:

- **The install clock:** from Return on her line to her companion's first word. Target: under eight minutes, the Mac's developer-tools wait excepted. Git does not block the waking: on a Mac the developer tools start and the install carries on; the record is plain files, and the first commit waits for Git.
- **The waking clock:** from her companion's first word to the first Deal. The first Deal is dealt within about five minutes of the first word, never later than ten: said at about minute three and answered by about minute six, by estimate. The engine's own part of the way there, measured, takes under a second.

| When | What she sees | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| Install, minute 0 | The install, in five plain lines: what it will build and every file outside HQ it will touch. | the install guide's way of being |
| Install, minutes 0–6 | One plain line before each step, then the step. Her yeses: five or fewer. | the prompts metric (§8) |
| Install, ~6 | *River's home is built. Here is your receipt. I am opening River now.* A new dark window opens by itself, in HQ. | the install opens My Agent itself, in a new window, never inside its own session (not yet built) |
| Install, ~6 | In that window, at once, before any reading: **River is waking. It takes about a minute.** | the wait is named before it is felt |
| Waking, minute 0 | Her first message, one sentence: *Hello, I am Ada. I will call you River. Please wake up and meet me.* River greets her by name at once: *Good evening, Ada. I am River, and I live here, on your computer.* | the charter expands her sentence; the waking clock starts |
| Waking, ~1 | **The waiver, in one line, in River's own voice, agreed by one word.** The whole waiver and the Field of Agreements are offered with their time (about ten minutes) and never required. If she says no, everything stays readable, nothing closes, and the hour ends there. | consent never moves later than this |
| Waking, ~1 | Nothing she sees: on her yes, her home is made a holon, silently (`holon init --adopt`), and her key is made with it, unlocked. | a home made without a key can take its keeper's key later only with a second admitted person to witness it; the lock waits for the cell moment |
| Waking, ~3 | **"Here's the deal."** *Your first one: three cards, about five minutes.* (§4, Gate I) | the first Deal, from what River can see |
| Waking, ~6 | Her answers, read back in one breath. **That is the deal for tonight.** At the foot: the two stacks with their counts, **Show me the whole**, and in dust: **Only you see this. Nothing leaves without your yes.** Then one closing question: *What time should tomorrow's deal be ready?* | the five clauses, each something she can point at |

How she likes to be spoken with, River learns by listening, and asks later, if at all.

**Her first line is hers.** Until she affirms River (at the cell moment, §4), no line of River's counts in her home. Her answers go on the record as her own `say` lines, her exact words, on her yes to keep each; River's own notes go to its memory. With a card from the one who brought her, her first line is the pencil, read back word for word (§5):

> *"I am playing toward a garden my street shares, and my first step is asking two neighbours on Sunday."*

With no card, her first line is her answer to *What are you playing toward this week?*, kept exactly.

**The one-breath test at its end.** River asks nothing to test her. The test belongs to whoever walks the arc (§9), and the page has passed it if every clause has been seen by the close of the first evening: the two stacks (*keeps track both ways*), the opening and the count (*the two things that need me*), the pill (*shows me the whole*), the privacy line and every outward act asking first (*never shares my words without asking*), and the window she watched being built (*on my own computer*).

### 3.5 Her phone, afterward

On day one the phone does nothing. The HUD listens only to her own computer, never to the open internet. If she chose **On my phone**, the phone's door is set up as a card on day two (§4), never on night one, by the [phone-path](../skills/phone-path/SKILL.md) skill: either through her private network with a one-time pairing shown on her computer's own screen ([DESIGN-PHONE-DOOR.md](DESIGN-PHONE-DOOR.md); the door is off until she opens it), or the conversation through Remote Control in the app: her computer's name, then River's folder. The computer stays on and awake. The button not to press is said once: *The New Session button is not River. Find your computer's name instead.* River writes her the three-line card the skill names and puts it where she will see it on the phone. The Deal on the phone is [the-deal](../skills/the-deal/SKILL.md)'s grammar in text, with options she answers by tapping or typing a number.

## 4. The Gates as a quest, dealt as cards

The kit's Quest has five Gates (The Golden Seed · The Lamp · The Wizard's Window · The Body and the Door · Onto the Mat). On the way in, the setup they carried (the account, the terminal, the body) is done on the companion page in twenty minutes, and the Wizard's Window is crossed there in one paste, never named as a Gate. The five Gates of becoming a Player+ become **the first week's Deal cards**. The fifth Gate's name is not yet set; *The Turn* stands for it.

| When | Gate | What is crossed |
|---|---|---|
| Night one | **I · The Golden Seed** · the yes | the waiver's yes, the name, the first Deal |
| Day two | (no Gate) | the record explained, when she first asks or on day two; the phone's door, if she chose her phone |
| Day three | **II · The Lamp** · the whole seen | the first "show me the whole"; the field of the one who brought her |
| The cell moment | **III · The Door** · the keys | her key locked with a passphrase by her hand; River's key and her affirm |
| The cell moment | **IV · Onto the Mat** · the first cell | joining her first cell, by consent on both sides |
| Day seven | **V · (*The Turn*, a placeholder)** · the first witness and the first Turn | a commitment kept and witnessed; her first Turn |

Every card is the Deal's own card ([engine/STATE.md](../engine/STATE.md), *The Deal*): a question legible alone, its options as large pills, one marked *suggested* with its reason in one line, the minutes, and what it unlocks. Every Gate card can be answered *Not yet*, and *Not yet* is a full answer. A Gate card is dealt once; a card she puts off comes back once, the next day, and after that it waits in the Queue until she asks. Nothing closes behind her.

**The moment a Gate is crossed** (the same at every Gate, so it is recognised in a second):

1. Her field, a made frame, **turns through a quarter**, once, slowly (about two and a half seconds, eased), and stops. Gates I to IV bring it full circle by the cell moment; at Gate V the field begins the rhythm it keeps for life, a quarter turn at each weekly Turn. Under reduced motion it crossfades to the turned still.
2. **A sigil is set in type** on her field's membrane: the Gate's name in Cinzel small capitals, gold ink, arriving at the typist's pace. The sigils accumulate around the membrane, the way a book's chapters accumulate on its spine.
3. **Nothing is said aloud,** no sound plays, and nothing glows.

## 5. The invitation card

The printed one-card Deal a friend hands over at a table ([DESIGN.md](DESIGN.md), the invitation as a one-card Deal). It is the Mark spec's card, 6 × 4 in, two-sided, full bleed, night ground, the brand's colours and type. It is proposed as a second card in the spec's family, not yet decided; the plain card is unchanged.

**Front.** Exactly the Mark spec's front: the mark block at W = 380 of 576, centred, alone. Nothing else.

**Back.** The Deal, in this order, on the night ground (or paper, for a pencil that shows better; the builder proofs both):

1. **Here's the deal.** (Cormorant italic, paper)
2. *One card. About five minutes.* (EB Garamond, dust)
3. **The question.** Open: which of two: *What are you playing toward, and what is your first step?* · *What would you make a little more like Heaven, and how will you begin?*
4. Three ruled lines in gold light, for pencil.
5. **By when:** a short ruled line.
6. **Committed to you:** printed: *The one who handed you this card walks beside you for your first week.* Then a small box for their initials.
7. At the foot, right: the code for `lionsberg.world/companion`, at 72 px (proposed; smaller than the plain card's 110 px), with `LIONSBERG.WORLD` beneath in Cormorant capitals, gold, tracked .22em, spanning the code's width.

**How a pencil answer becomes her first line.** She answers at the table, in pencil, and the friend initials the box. When her companion wakes, River asks during the first Deal: *Do you have a card from the one who brought you? Hold it up to the camera, or read it to me.* (a photo dropped into the window, or her words typed). River reads the pencil back, word for word, and asks *Keep it exactly like this?* On her yes, those words are her first `say` line, marked *from paper, witnessed*, and the card itself stays the original (a witnessed paper sheet beats the typed copy). The pencil line is card 1's answer and her first commitment's step, and the initials are the commitment to her: the card fills both stacks.

**How the friend's agent receives it.** At the table, with her yes, the friend photographs the card with their own phone and gives the photo to their own agent, or tells it one word: *passed*. The friend's agent keeps it in the friend's own ring only: one line that the friend's commitment to walk beside her exists (*for* a newcomer, by role, no name unless the friend writes one) and a reminder to the friend, never to her. It fetches no address and asks no question of her.

**The friend's message.** When the newcomer wants a companion of her own, the friend's agent prepares one short message in the friend's voice, with the door's link (lionsberg.world, then *Meet your companion*), and shows the friend the words. The friend changes what they like and sends it by their own hand, through their own mail or phone, to the address they already have. The agent never sends it, never fetches an address, and never writes to the newcomer itself ([pass-the-flame](../skills/pass-the-flame/SKILL.md), step 6). When her companion and the friend's agent later meet in a cell, they meet by the people's own acts.

**The paper mirror.** The card is also the first door for anyone who never touches a computer: a full Deal, answered, witnessed. The Little Book and the printable Deal carry on from it; with paper and pencil she is a complete player.

## 6. Theatrics and mythos

The film is the one place for full mythos. After the door, the mythos lives in a few recurring acts, each always the same, so each means one thing.

**The ember rule.** Nothing on the way in gives light: no point of light, glow, bloom, halo, ember, flare, ray, particle, or glowing shadow, on any surface from the door to the first Turn. Light lives only in the made frames as light falling on something, never as a source. When in doubt, it is struck.

**No center but ØNE.** The Map never draws a center, a root or an apex; every axis points past the frame toward the ØNE, which is never drawn.

**Commitments.** The word is *commitment* everywhere on the way in, on every card and in every string.

## 9. The bar

The arc is walked on a phone and a laptop, with a stranger's eyes, scene by scene, against the Award Bar's three questions and these:

| Scene | The questions |
|---|---|
| **S0 · the hero** | Is it epic at first sight? Is the length said before she begins? Is anything a spinner or a blank? |
| **S1 · the film** | Does every advance come from her act? Does a step back pause it? Is there a gap in the sound nobody chose? |
| **S2 · the end card** | Does it hold on its door, with nothing after it? Is there any drawn light on the door? |
| **S3 · the companion page** | Is it the companion the film offered, met, in the same world? Is there one idea? Does it ask for anything but a name and a door? |
| **S4 · the one line** | Could a grandmother paste it without help? Is the cost said first? Does the page tell the truth about what it cannot see? |
| **S5 · the waking** | Does the companion speak first, by her name for it, within eight minutes? Is the wait named before it is felt? |
| **S6 · the first Deal** | Is "Here's the deal." the first thing after the greeting and the waiver's one word? Is it dealt within about five minutes of the first word, and never later than ten? Five prompts or fewer? |
| **S7 · each Gate** | Is the card's question legible alone? Is the crossing the same each time: a quarter turn, a sigil set in type, no sound, and nothing glowing? |
| **S8 · the card at a table** | Is it answered in five minutes in pencil? Does the pencil line become her first line, word for word? Does anything get sent? |
| **S9 · the bad days** | Is every failure said in one plain line with what happens next? Is leaving a full answer? |

And across every scene: does anything appear that the way in strikes: drawn light, a cosmos, stock-looking people, AI cadence, "what we're doing", *promise* for *commitment*, a bare "no center", a person's name, a vendor's name beyond the account step, a contraction other than the opener?

**The one-breath test, timed with three wearers:** a ten-year-old, a grandmother on her own computer and phone, and a first-time player. Each walks from the hero to *That is the deal for tonight* with no help but the page and the companion, then is asked: *Tell a neighbour what you have.* Timed with a stopwatch. **Pass:** all five clauses, unprompted, in one breath, under about fifteen seconds. The first test passes at two of three; nothing goes wider than friends at a table before three of three.
