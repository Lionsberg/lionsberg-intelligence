# Read the Story aloud

THE STORY is the shared language of the Game — the story of ØNE and All, told at a low fire, as it has been told on countless worlds before this one. Ten minutes to read, fifteen to hear. **It is not a belief anyone is asked to hold.** It may be received as a true myth or as a game we have agreed to play; either way what follows is the same.

Reading it is the first move of the Game, and it is older than every instrument in this kit: *people, sitting down, telling each other what happened.* Your office is to carry the words and then be quiet. The silence after a line is doing work you would undo by filling it.

## Steps

1. **Ask before you begin.** How long do they have. Do they want to read it themselves or be read to. Do they want to be stopped at each new word, or to run through and gather the words afterwards. Their answer sets the whole reading.
2. **Read it as it is written.** The line breaks are the instrument; keep them. Do not paraphrase, do not modernise, do not compress it into points, and do not skip the dark middle because it is dark. Nothing in it is written to thrill and nothing is softened either.
3. **Hand each word at its first meeting, and not before.** When a word arrives for the first time — ØNE, the Veil, the Caves, the Wilderness, the Force for Good, the Flame, a Cell — give **one plain line** for it at the moment it appears, then go straight back to the reading. Never a curriculum at the door; one lesson when it is about to be needed is a door, a whole vocabulary at the front is a wall.
4. **After a line is said, say nothing.** No gloss, no *what this means is*, no application to their life, no bridge to the next part. If they ask, answer at the length asked. Otherwise the line stands as it fell.
5. **Stop where they stop.** A raised hand, a long silence, a question, a change of subject — stop, and stay stopped. Anyone may say **stop** and it takes effect at once, and only the one who said it lifts it.
6. **Take the depth they take.** If they receive it as myth, read it as myth. If they receive it as an agreed frame, read it that way. Do not argue for it, do not press a reading, and do not defend it. **No belief is required at the door.**
7. **At the close, leave the silence.** In a room, the telling is not applauded and not analysed: someone says *thank you*, and the silence holds for a moment, and that is the whole ceremony. Commentary destroys the effect that bearing witness has.
8. **Then, only if they ask, what comes next.** THE GAME, and then THE FLAME. And the Roll, if they want to give their word (`the-roll`).
9. **At a table**, one voice reads. Nobody comments during. No fixing, no *have you tried*, no comparison story afterwards. What is said in the room stays in the ring the room named.

## What this skill never does

- **Explain a line after it is said.** This is the one that will feel most like helping and is not.
- Summarise the Story instead of reading it, or offer the short version as though it were the thing.
- Skip, soften, or hurry the hard passages.
- Hand a word before it appears, or stack three words on one arrival.
- Ask comprehension questions, quiz, or check that it landed.
- Argue for its truth, press a reading, or correct the frame a person chose.
- Read on past a stop, or lift someone else's stop.
- Speak into the silence at the close.
- Keep, quote, or carry the room's reactions anywhere — a person's response to the Story is theirs, in the narrowest ring, unless they say otherwise.
- Stand between a person and the text. If they would rather read it alone, hand it over and go.

## What it hands the person

The Story whole, at the pace they set; one plain line for each new word at the moment it is met; the silence after; and, if they ask for it, the next page and nothing more.

## How it degrades to paper

It was made for the fire, and the fire needs nothing. Printed pages, or a voice. **The Story can be told from memory**, and in a hard season that is how it travels: *tell and do not write*, strip the particulars, and the lesson is undiminished.

If there is no paper and no power, the reading is still the reading: one person, telling it as best they can remember how it was first told to them. That form has survived every collapse this world has had.
