# 4 - Federating - Joining the Ecosystem

*Reaching outward. The Federation Agreement. The Switchboard and ecosystem flows. The Threshold of Federation in practice. Centers of abundance and dynamic distribution. The first tenth into the commons. The FSx breakthrough as lived experience.*

## Why Federate at All

You have constituted your FSx. You are doing the work. You are getting in flow. Why federate at all?

The short answer: **because no sovereignty thrives in isolation.** The pattern is structurally a federation pattern, not a sovereignty-in-a-bubble pattern. Sovereign entities that try to emerge alone end up either reinventing what the federation already offers, or quietly slipping back into the old paradigm of accumulation and competition because they lack the surrounding infrastructure of mutual support.

The longer answer is the **core enabling breakthrough** of the FSx ecosystem (named in [[Guidebook for the Emergence]]):

> An entity that joins the FSx ecosystem at the outset receives a **dynamic governance and economic stake in the whole ecosystem from day one** — not only in its own organization. Federation is not a future option you negotiate later; it is built in from the beginning, at no additional cost, by the act of federation itself.

This Part walks through what federation actually is in practice, what it gives you, what it asks of you, and how it lives across seasons.

## The Threshold of Federation, In Practice

The Threshold of Federation (per [[III - The Ecosystem Holding Charter]] §3.3) reads:

> Only a sovereign legal person constituted as an **FSx** — a FairShares Commons-type fractal sovereign legal person with agency independent of its members, with dynamic weighted governance and economic rights — may federate under this Charter. A legal person whose decisions require the consent of an owner, or whose governance reduces to a single voice, lacks the agency to federate on its own behalf and is therefore not eligible.

In practice this means:

- **Sole proprietors cannot federate as such.** They federate as their **FSx Individual** — constituted with at least two Mentors per [[VIII - The FSx Individual Template]].
- **Owner-controlled LLCs cannot federate as such.** They must transform into FSx-compatible Articles before federation. (This is often a meaningful threshold — many owner-founders find that the act of letting go is itself the work.)
- **Single-voice trusts and foundations cannot federate as such.** They restructure into multi-voice governance compatible with the FSx pattern.
- **Multi-voice cooperatives, mutuals, and similar bodies are often nearly compatible already** — minor adaptations bring them into FSx compatibility.
- **Existing FairShares Commons entities are essentially already FSx-compatible** — a Federation Agreement is the formal step.

What this **does not** mean:

- It does not mean traditional legal forms are excluded from Layer II. Anyone can join the Stewardship Agreement and participate in the spiritual association. The Threshold is a Layer III rule.
- It does not mean the Threshold is a vetting process. It is a *structural* test. Either your Articles match the pattern, or they do not. No committee judges your worthiness; the Compatibility Tests at the end of [[VII - The FSx Organism Template]] do the work.
- It does not mean the Threshold is permanent exclusion. An owner-controlled entity can transform at any time and federate then.

The deeper point: the Threshold solves a great many things at once. It bars predators, prevents capture and enclosure, eliminates the need for subjective vetting, ensures continuity beyond mortality, makes coercion-resistant, and embeds developmental accountability into the architecture itself. The pattern is the gateway, doing several things at once.

## The Federation Agreement

The instrument of federation is the **Federation Agreement** between the federating entity and the Ecosystem Holding Company (the Layer III body, itself constituted as the planetary-federation-scale FSx).

A Federation Agreement specifies:

- **Articles compatibility.** Confirmation that the entity's Articles satisfy the Compatibility Tests at the end of [[VII - The FSx Organism Template]]. Any deviation is named and reconciled.
- **Driver compatibility.** Confirmation that the entity's Driver Statement is consistent with, and serves rather than contradicts, the superorganism's Driver Statement.
- **Ecosystem flow commitments.** Two flows from the federated entity to the Ecosystem Holding Company: (a) the **Ecosystem Contribution** per [[III - The Ecosystem Holding Charter]] §7.7 — default 1% of revenue base + profit-based true-up to 10% of profit — funding ecosystem operations and the Ecosystem Fund; and (b) the **Profit Pool contribution** per [[III - The Ecosystem Holding Charter]] §8.5 — default 30% of profit, redistributed back to federated Members through ergodic pooling. Specific contribution terms can be tuned per Agreement.
- **Standards adoption.** SS-1 (Switchboard), SCS-1 (Six Capitals), GS-1 (Governance), DS-1 (Decision), CS-1 (Commons), and IS-1 (Investment, where applicable) — adopted, adapted, or phased in as appropriate to the entity's scale.
- **Quest participation scope.** Optional but encouraged — what Quests, Pillars, or shared undertakings the entity is committing to participate in.
- **Reciprocal governance terms.** The entity's representation in Ecosystem Company governance; the Ecosystem Company's standing in the entity's governance; the limits of each. Sovereignty is preserved on both sides.
- **Exit terms.** How either party ends the Federation Agreement. The entity itself does not dissolve on Federation Agreement termination; it simply ceases to hold Ecosystem Company Membership.

Most Federation Agreements are signed within a single afternoon once the entity itself is constituted. The work of *becoming federation-ready* (constituting your FSx, signing the Stewardship Agreement, discerning Mentors) is upstream of the Agreement itself.

## What You Receive by Federating

By signing a Federation Agreement, the entity receives:

### Standing in the Ecosystem Holding Company

The entity is admitted to its applicable Member Constituency (typically Class B for Organization Members; Class C for Ecosystem-Individual FSx Members; Class D for Ecosystem-Company Members; Class E for Collaborator-Individual FSx Members; Class F for Collaborator-Company Members; Class G for Living Systems holders-in-trust; Class J for Society holders-in-trust; Class H for Donor entities; Class I for Investor entities; Class A is reserved for Stewardship Members meeting the developmental thresholds of II §4.2). Standing includes voice in the Ecosystem Company's governance, weighted per the Weighted Governance Dynamics at §5.4 of the Charter.

### Access to the Switchboard

The Switchboard is the multi-capital flow coordination mechanism of the ecosystem. Through it, the entity can:

- Transparently log multi-capital contributions and receipts.
- Receive its share of distributions per the Dividend Sharing Dynamics at §8.1 of the Charter — including ergodic redistributions from the Profit Pool (§8.5).
- Participate in the Ecosystem Fund (§7.5) for new-Member capitalization, crisis response, and consented strategic initiatives.
- Receive Developmental Infrastructure Funding (§7.6) where applicable.
- Settle inter-Member exchanges through ecosystem rails rather than (only) through the wider economy.

### A Fair Share of the Ecosystem Commons Pool

This is the heart of the breakthrough. **Federated Members receive Capital Gains and Dividend Share allocations from the ecosystem commons pool** — built from the Ecosystem Contributions and Profit Pool flows of every federated entity, redistributed across federated Members per Capital Gains Dynamics, Dividend Sharing Dynamics, and within-Constituency weighting. The 70% of any entity's profit that stays at the entity level is distributed through that entity's own Articles; the 30% that flows up returns through the ecosystem to all federated Members on an ergodic basis.

So a village FSx in one watershed receives ecosystem-wide standing and ecosystem-wide flow alongside a manufacturing FSx on another continent — not a slice of the manufacturing FSx's full surplus, but a fair share of the *commons pool* the manufacturing FSx contributed its first tenth and 30% to. The mechanism is precise; the spirit is mutual flourishing. The ecosystem is the cooperative whole, not a collection of competing islands.

### A Voice in Ecosystem Governance

The federated entity has standing in the General Meetings, Constituency processes, and Resolution tiers of the Ecosystem Company. Major decisions about the ecosystem are decided by its federated Members.

### Access to Internal Service Providers

As the ecosystem matures, federated FSx Internal Service Providers (legal, accounting, design, technology, hospitality, learning, conflict mediation, etc.) become available to all federated Members at internal-economy rates rather than external-market rates.

### Knowledge Commons Access and Contribution

The Knowledge Commons is the shared body of intellectual property released under CC-BY-SA 4.0 by Members and the Ecosystem Company itself. Federated entities both contribute to and draw freely from this body.

### System Wide Master Planning, Rhythms, and Integrated Delivery

Beyond the baseline FSx structuring, the LIØNSBERG System and Commons offers many years of research and lifetimes of wisdom on large-scale program management, governance, and integrated delivery, along with a sophisticated impact platform. Federated entities have the option (and no obligation) to use these as starting points for their own work, including for the top-level "meta project" to heal and regenerate the Earth.

## What Federating Asks of You

In return, the federating entity commits to:

### Two Ecosystem Flows: Contribution and Profit Pool

The federated entity commits to two distinct flows up to the Ecosystem Holding Company. The two have different work to do; they are not the same thing.

**1. The Ecosystem Contribution** (per [[III - The Ecosystem Holding Charter]] §7.7) — *the dues that keep the commons alive.*

- **Revenue base — 1% of revenue**, paid as revenue arrives. Denominated in revenue (not profit) so that pre-profit and non-profit federated entities contribute on the same footing.
- **Profit-based true-up — to 10% of profit**, settled at period close. Where 10% of profit exceeds the revenue base contributions paid for the period, the entity contributes the difference. Combined Ecosystem Contribution = greater of 1% of revenue or 10% of profit.

The Ecosystem Contribution funds the Ecosystem Fund (§7.5), Developmental Infrastructure Funding (§7.6), Ecosystem Company operations, and ecosystem-wide strategic initiatives. The wisdom is ancient: dedicating the first tenth strengthens the commons, which in turn strengthens each Member.

**2. The Profit Pool Contribution** (per [[III - The Ecosystem Holding Charter]] §8.5) — *ergodic redistribution across the federation.*

- **30% of profit** flows to the Ecosystem Profit Pool at period close. The Profit Pool is **redistributed back to federated Members in the same period** — across Member Constituencies per the Dividend Sharing Dynamics, and within each Constituency per accumulated TEA.
- The federated entity's *net* flow under this mechanism may be greater or lesser than its 30% contribution depending on the ecosystem's overall conditions and the entity's TEA share.
- The other 70% of the entity's profit stays at the entity level for distribution per its own Dividend Sharing Dynamics (Article 40 of [[VII - The FSx Organism Template]]).

This **ergodic pooling** equalizes incentives across for-profit and non-profit federated entities and across geographies. The mathematical wisdom: for non-ergodic processes such as wealth accumulation, pooling reduces variance and improves long-run outcomes for ensemble participants. The structural wisdom: every federated entity has a real material stake in the success of every other, because their flow back from the Profit Pool depends on the whole.

**Neither is a tax.** Both are voluntary commitments made in the Federation Agreement, made because the federation is the source of much of the entity's standing, capacity, and shared resilience. The defaults can be tuned per Agreement; the defaults above are workable starting points.

### Open Design Question — Weighting FSx Individual in the Profit Pool

A live design question for the Founding Phase: **how heavily to weight FSx Individual Constituencies (Classes A, C, E) in the redistribution of the 30% Profit Pool**.

The current Dividend Sharing Dynamics (II §8.1) give Class C only 5%, Class E 2%, Class A 0% — most flow goes to organizational Constituencies (Class B 30%, Class D 20%, Class I 20%). If applied to the Profit Pool, this would mean ecosystem-wide redistribution mostly flows back to organizations.

The ergodic pooling wisdom suggests the *opposite* may be more desirable for the Profit Pool specifically: **weight individual Constituencies more heavily, so ecosystem-wide flow reaches each individual person directly, regardless of which entity they are embedded in**. A FSx Individual contributing TEA to a non-profit research entity could then receive material flow from the ecosystem's overall success — equalizing across for-profit and non-profit work in a way the conventional economy cannot.

The question for the Founding Phase: should the Profit Pool redistribution use the same Constituency weighting as other Dividend Sharing flows, or should it use a different weighting that favors FSx Individual? An interim hypothesis (subject to Founding Phase deliberation): the Profit Pool redistribution should weight the FSx Individual Constituencies (A, C, E combined) at 50% or more, with the remainder distributed across organizational Constituencies. The exact percentages, like everything else in the package, are open for the body to discern together.

This is one of the Founding Phase's most important design questions. It affects what the FSx ecosystem actually feels like for individual people in it. See [[III - The Ecosystem Holding Charter]] §8.5 for the constitutional framing.

### Compliance with the Stewardship Principles

The fourteen Stewardship Principles at [[II - The Stewardship Agreement]] §16 bind the federated entity at the principles level, not at the operational level. The principles are entrenched at the Entrenched Resolution tier; the entity's operational expression of them can evolve, but the principles themselves are load-bearing across the federation.

### Six-Capital Reporting

The federated entity adopts (or commits to phase in) reporting across Natural, Individual, Social, Intellectual, Manufactured, and Financial capitals per SCS-1 or a designated compatible standard. Annual reporting is provided to the Ecosystem Company; key data is made available to Members. Six-capital accounting is the visibility layer that makes multi-capital flourishing concrete rather than aspirational.

### Knowledge Commons Contribution

By default, intellectual property created in the course of federated work is released to the Knowledge Commons under CC-BY-SA 4.0. Specific exceptions (cultural and indigenous knowledge held under originating community terms, temporary trade-secret on a regenerative technology, etc.) are handled per the Cultural Commons protocol in CS-1.

### Conflict Transformation

Disputes within the federated entity, between the federated entity and other Members, or between the federated entity and the Ecosystem Company itself, are resolved per the Conflict Transformation provisions at [[II - The Stewardship Agreement]] §13 — direct resolution, mediation, council review, internal binding arbitration, independent binding arbitration. External juridical process is used only as a last resort.

### Voluntariness Throughout

The entity retains the right to exit the Federation Agreement at any time per the exit terms specified. Exit is not betrayal. Forcing yourself to remain federated when continued federation harms your work or your people would be the betrayal.

## Becoming a Center of Abundance

Federation is not the end of the story; it is the start of a different kind of life — life as part of an ecosystem of mutual flourishing. The aim, over time, is for each federated entity to **become a center of abundance** — generating more value than it needs internally, distributing the surplus back into the ecosystem, and growing the whole alongside itself.

> *Each Fractal Sovereignty has more than enough as quickly as possible so that each one of the all becomes generous centers of abundance, distribution, and flow beyond themselves. Each center creating as much value as possible, using as much as they need, and distributing the rest of the flow back into the ecosystem.*

In practical terms: the federated entity that runs Pools 1–7 well, settles its ecosystem flows at Pools 8–9 from period to period, hits the cap at Pool 10 (Downstream Impact Investment) consistently, replenishes Pool 11 (Legacy) over time, and finds itself with surplus regularly flowing into Pools 12–13 — that entity has become a center of abundance for the ecosystem. There is no single way to get there, and there is no shortcut. But the pattern is structurally oriented toward this outcome, in a way that the conventional firm is structurally oriented away from it.

## Federating from the Beginning vs. Subsequent Federation

A note on timing.

**Federating from the beginning** — joining the FSx ecosystem at the moment the entity is constituted — is the path this Guidebook is written around. The breakthrough is fully alive on this path.

**Subsequent federation** — joining later, after the entity has operated as an open-source FSx in isolation for some time — is also fully available and welcomed. Many entities will arrive this way, often after discovering that isolation has been more costly than they expected.

The architectural difference: in *federation from the beginning*, the entity's Capital Gains Dynamics, Dividend Sharing Dynamics, and Weighted Governance Dynamics are coupled to the ecosystem from day one — its surplus participates, and its voice participates, with no friction. In *subsequent federation*, there is typically a transition: pre-federation surplus is honored and distinguished; post-federation surplus enters the ecosystem flows. Both are workable. The first is simpler.

If you are reading this Guidebook *before* constituting your FSx, **federate from the beginning** is the recommendation. If you are reading this *after* having operated independently for some time, **subsequent federation** is fully welcomed and the pattern absorbs the transition gracefully.
