# II - The Ecosystem Holding Charter

> *The Articles of the Ecosystem Holding Company that the (Currently Nameless) Superorganism brings online to coordinate ecosystemic value flows across its federated participants. The economic body sits one level below the spiritual association of [[II - The Stewardship Agreement]] and operates under its Driver and entrenched Stewardship Principles.*

---

## 1. Form and Naming

1.1 The **Ecosystem Holding Company** (hereinafter the *Ecosystem Company*) is the top-level economic federation entity of the (Currently Nameless) Superorganism. It is itself constituted as an **FSx** — a FairShares Commons-type fractal sovereign legal person with agency independent of its members, with dynamic weighted governance and economic rights — operating at the planetary-federation scale of the FSx pattern (see [[VII - The FSx Organism Template]]).

1.2 The Ecosystem Company's primary jurisdiction of incorporation is to be specified in final filing during the Founding Phase. Parallel-compatible forms in other jurisdictions will be established by Ordinary Resolution to support participants in those jurisdictions.

1.3 The Ecosystem Company has full legal personhood and agency. It can hold property, enter contracts, and exercise the powers of a natural person consistent with this Charter and applicable law.

1.4 **No party owns the Ecosystem Company.** Federated participants hold standing as Members in the constituencies specified in §4. The ecosystem is specifically designed to replace the illusion of ownership and control with the dynamic paradigm of stewardship, influence, rights, and responsibilities. 

1.5 The Ecosystem Company operates **under and in service of** the Stewardship Agreement at [[II - The Stewardship Agreement]]. Where this Charter and the Stewardship Agreement appear to conflict, the Central Animating Spirit, Driver Statement, and Stewardship Principles of the Stewardship Agreement govern, and the Stewards shall reconcile the operational detail.

1.6 **Layered architecture.** The Ecosystem Company is one of two top-level federation entities. The Ecosystem Company (Layer III) coordinates governance, value flows, the Switchboard, and the federation spine. The **Ecosystem Resourcing Pool** at [[IV - The Ecosystem Resourcing Pool Charter]] (Layer IV) coordinates capital — investor capital accounts, fund-of-funds, cross-fund ergodic pooling, and capital deployment to federated operating entities. The two are **sibling FSx entities**, distinct in function and risk-isolated by structure, coupled in governance through reciprocal Constituency standing. The federated operating entities themselves sit at Layer VII ([[VII - The FSx Organism Template]]); the irreducible scale of the FSx Individual sits at Layer VIII ([[VIII - The FSx Individual Template]]).

## 2. Purpose and Driver

2.1 The Ecosystem Company's purpose is to bring online a transparent, just, regenerative, multi-capital economic ecosystem in service of the **Driver Statement** of the superorganism (entrenched at §2.3 of [[II - The Stewardship Agreement]] and reproduced in [[Appendix A - The Driver Statement]]):

> *The superorganism exists to unite and cohere the consciousness, capacities, and activities of sovereigns into a higher-order functional unity in service of Life and Source — capable of regenerating Earth, harmonizing her inhabitants into a world that works for All, and supporting the flourishing of all generations of life across all realms and worlds we are part of.*

2.2 The Driver is **entrenched**. It cannot be amended through any process available in this Charter. To change the Driver, the superorganism would have to dissolve and reconstitute under a different Stewardship Agreement.

2.3 The Ecosystem Company does this by federating value flows among its Members across all capitals — natural, individual, social, intellectual, manufactured, and financial — through the **Switchboard** (§7), accounted for under the **Six Capitals Standard** (§6), and distributed through the **Dividend Sharing Dynamics** (§8) and **Capital Gains Dynamics** (§4.5).

## 3. Relationship to the Stewardship Agreement

3.1 Membership in the superorganism's spiritual association under [[II - The Stewardship Agreement]] is **separate from and prior to** membership in the Ecosystem Company. Members of the Stewardship Agreement may, but are not required to, federate economically under this Charter.

3.2 Federation under this Charter requires (a) standing under the Stewardship Agreement; (b) constitution as an **FSx** per §3.3 below; and (c) signature of a **Federation Agreement** with the Ecosystem Company specifying the Member's contribution commitments, scope of participation, Standards adoption, and reciprocal governance terms.

3.3 **Threshold of Federation.** Only a sovereign legal person constituted as an **FSx** — a FairShares Commons-type fractal sovereign legal person with agency independent of its members, with dynamic weighted governance and economic rights — may federate under this Charter. A legal person whose decisions require the consent of an owner, or whose governance reduces to a single voice, lacks the agency to federate on its own behalf and is therefore not eligible. An individual wishing to federate economically must first constitute (or already operate as) an FSx Individual under [[VIII - The FSx Individual Template]]. Compatibility with the FSx pattern is set out in [[VII - The FSx Organism Template]] and tested by the Compatibility Tests therein. The pattern is the gateway: the spiritual association at Layer II is open to all who consent; the economic federation at Layer III is sovereign-to-sovereign throughout. This Threshold structurally protects the federation from capture, predation, enclosure, and the smuggling of extractive forms across the line.

3.4 The **one-voice principle** of the Stewardship Agreement (§7 thereof) governs the spiritual association. The **Weighted Governance Dynamics** of this Charter (§5.4 below) govern only the economic body. These two governance modes operate in different domains and do not contradict one another.

3.5 **Relationship to the Ecosystem Resourcing Pool (Layer IV).** The Resourcing Pool at [[IV - The Ecosystem Resourcing Pool Charter]] is the federation's **capital coordination entity** — the fund-of-funds and internal bank. It is a sibling FSx to this Ecosystem Holding Company at the federation level. **Investor capital accounts and capital deployment to federated operating entities are operationalized through the Resourcing Pool, not directly through this Charter.** Class I Investor Membership at this Layer III represents the investor's **governance standing** in the Holding Company; the **capital itself** is held in sub-fund FSx instances within the Resourcing Pool, with capped returns per IS-1, ergodic pooling across sub-funds, and Asset Lock applied at every level. The Resourcing Pool holds Class I standing in this Holding Company per §4.1; the Holding Company holds Class I standing in the Resourcing Pool per its §4.1; the two bodies are coupled in governance without merging in operations. The **Threshold of Federation** at §3.3 applies to both bodies.

## 4. Member Constituencies

4.1 The Ecosystem Company has the following constituencies of Membership. Per the **Threshold of Federation** at §3.3, every Member entity in any Constituency below — including Constituencies that bear the word "Individual" — is constituted as a sovereign legal person of the FSx pattern. References to "individuals" in Constituencies C, E, and the Stewardship eligibility criteria denote **FSx Individual** Members (per [[VIII - The FSx Individual Template]]), not flesh-and-blood persons in their unconstituted capacity:

- **Class A — Stewardship Members**: FSx Individual serving as Stewards of the Driver and the entrenched Stewardship Principles. Non-financial: Stewards receive no Dividend Share and no Capital Gains, but are compensated for time and costs of service. Selected through the process at §4.2.
- **Class B — Organization Members**: incorporated entities operating on FSx-compatible Articles that have signed a Federation Agreement.
- **Class C — Ecosystem-Individual FSx Members**: FSx Individual whose work substantially supports cross-cutting commons or developmental infrastructure of the superorganism.
- **Class D — Ecosystem-Company Members**: entities whose primary role is to support commons, practice, or infrastructure rather than primarily to operate their own market-facing production.
- **Class E — Collaborator-Individual FSx Members**: FSx Individual participating in specific Ecosystem Company projects without full federation.
- **Class F — Collaborator-Company Members**: companies collaborating on specific projects without full federation.
- **Class G — Living Systems Members**: holders-in-trust for specific natural domains and the more-than-human kin (a watershed, a forest, a species, a biome), with standing to represent those domains in Ecosystem Company decisions.
- **Class H — Donor Members**: contributors of capital without expectation of financial return.
- **Class I — Investor Members** (subdivided I-A and I-B): contributors of capital with capped return per the Investment Standard (IS-1). **Investor capital accounts and capital deployment are operationalized through the Ecosystem Resourcing Pool at Layer IV** (see §3.5 above and [[IV - The Ecosystem Resourcing Pool Charter]]); Class I Membership at this Layer III represents the investor's governance standing in the Ecosystem Holding Company itself.
- **Class J — Society Members**: holders-in-trust for specific cultural, social, and community domains (a language, a wisdom lineage, a community-of-place, a traditional governance body), with standing to represent those domains in Ecosystem Company decisions.
- **Class K — Superorganism Member**: the **(Currently Nameless) Superorganism** itself — in its spiritual aspect at [[II - The Stewardship Agreement]] — represented by the Stewardship Council or such other representative as the Federation Agreement specifies. Carries the **Superorganism Dynamic Stake** in this Ecosystem Holding Company: by default a **10% Dynamic Governance Stake** and a **10% Dynamic Value Stake**. This Constituency ensures the body of the whole holds voice and share in every layer of the federation.

4.2 **Stewardship Member candidacy** requires:

- Demonstrated capacity at Laske Dialectic Thought Form (DTF) assessment of 30 or greater, or equivalent assessment recognized by existing Stewards.
- Demonstrated capacity at Kegan Stage 4(5) or greater per Subject-Object Interview, or equivalent.
- At least ten years of sustained developmental practice in a tradition recognized by existing Stewards (the Way, sustained contemplative practice in a recognized lineage, equivalent indigenous transmission, etc.).
- Demonstrated service beyond narrow self-interest, including at least five years of service in a role in which duty to a larger whole has been tested.
- Absence of material conflict of interest that would impair Stewardship judgment.

Founding-Phase Stewards are nominated by the Founding Members. Post-Founding, Stewards are nominated by existing Stewards in consultation with Constituency Representatives, confirmed by Ordinary Resolution. Steward terms are seven years, staggered, renewable once (maximum fourteen consecutive years).

4.3 General Membership in any other constituency does not require a developmental threshold. All who consent to the Stewardship Agreement, sign the relevant Federation Agreement, and meet Constituency-specific requirements may become Members.

4.4 An Individual Member of the Stewardship Agreement may federate into one or more Constituencies here (typically C, E, G, H, J, or I) as appropriate to their relationship with the Ecosystem Company and its federated constituents. 

4.5 **Capital Gains Dynamics** allocate capital appreciation across Member Constituencies:

- **Superorganism Members (Class K): 10%** *(Superorganism Dynamic Value Stake)*
- Organization Members: 20%
- Ecosystem-Company Members: 20%
- Living Systems Members: 12.5%
- Society Members: 12.5%
- Ecosystem-Individual Members: 15%

## 5. Governance

5.1 **General Meetings.** The Annual General Meeting shall be held within twelve months of each financial year-end. It shall receive the Annual Report (with six-capital reporting per SCS-1), audited financial statements, the Switchboard Annual Settlement Report, the Stewardship Report, and Board election results. Extraordinary General Meetings may be convened by the Board or by Constituency Resolution of any Constituency.

5.2 **Quorum.** Ordinary Resolution: Members holding at least 50% of aggregate Weighted Governance Dynamics, with at least one Member of each Active Constituency present or represented. Special Resolution: 75% of aggregate Weighted Governance Dynamics, with every Active Constituency represented. *Active Constituency* means a Constituency with at least one Member in good standing at the date of notice.

5.3 **The Executive Board.** The Executive Board consists of at least five Directors in the Founding Phase, adjustable to eleven or thirteen by Ordinary Resolution as the Ecosystem Company scales. Directors are drawn from the Active Member Constituencies per the Governance Standard (GS-1) to ensure cross-constituency representation. Director terms are three years, staggered, with maximum two consecutive terms. The Board holds operational oversight, committee appointments, standard-setting by Ordinary Resolution, Federation Agreement review and signing, and matters delegated by General Meeting. The Board cannot exercise authority contrary to the Stewardship Principles, the Driver, the entrenched provisions, or the best interests of the federal constituents.

5.4 **Weighted Governance Dynamics** apply when fallback weighted-majority voting is triggered (after IDM at §5.5 has been exhausted on Ordinary Resolutions, or as part of Special Resolution thresholds).

**Founding Phase Weighted Governance Dynamics** (Year 1 onward, through Year-10 recalibration):

- Class J (Society): 4
- Class H (Donor): 8
- Class I (Investor): 7
- Class C (Ecosystem-Individual): 1

Both Constituencies represent the broader purpose of the body; Stewardship enacts the Driver within the Company, Superorganism represents the body of the whole. Stewardship at 50 remains the heaviest single Constituency. Classes E and F (Collaborator) carry 0 in the Founding Phase, gaining standing only at Year-10 Recalibration.*

**Year-10 Recalibration Weighted Governance Dynamics** (from the tenth Annual General Meeting onward, unless further recalibrated):

- **Class K (Superorganism): 10** *(Superorganism Dynamic Governance Stake)*
- Class A (Stewardship): 30
- Class G (Living Systems): 8

Weighted Governance Dynamics may be recalibrated by Special Resolution.

5.5 **Integrative Decision-Making (IDM)** is the default decision method for the Ecosystem Company, with the three-level response structure (agreement, concern, objection) and the rounds specified in the Decision Standard (DS-1). IDM applies at General Meetings, Board meetings, Constituency gatherings, and Committee sessions. Where IDM is exhausted without resolution, the fallback Weighted Governance Dynamics of §5.4 apply.

5.6 **Resolutions.** The Charter recognizes five Resolution tiers, mirroring the tiered structure of [[II - The Stewardship Agreement]] §11.4 with weighted-governance mechanics in place of one-voice mechanics.

- **Ordinary Resolution**: quorum per §5.2; constituency presence of each Active Constituency; integrative outcome (no unresolved objection); 60% Weighted Governance Dynamics majority on fallback.
- **Special Resolution**: quorum per §5.2; every Active Constituency represented; **unanimous consent of every sitting Steward**; bare majority within each Active Constituency counted as a bloc; 75% Weighted Governance Dynamics majority on fallback.
- **Entrenched Resolution**: quorum per §5.2; every Active Constituency represented; **unanimous consent of every sitting Steward**; bare majority within each Active Constituency counted as a bloc; **85% Weighted Governance Dynamics majority** on fallback; and a **one-year reflection period** between formal proposal and ratifying vote, during which the proposal is circulated to all Members, the Stewardship Council, and external counsel as the Stewards deem appropriate. Required for amendment of provisions entrenched at this tier (§10). The reflection period cannot be shortened by any other Resolution.
- **Constituency Resolution**: the affected Constituency meets separately, applies IDM within the Constituency, and resolves on Constituency-internal matters. Matters affecting the whole are escalated to the Board as formal proposals.
- **Emergency Resolution**: the Board may act when time is genuinely critical, with unanimous Steward consent required for Emergency Resolutions touching entrenched provisions. Emergency Resolutions touching matters reserved for Special or Entrenched Resolution must be ratified by the appropriate tier within ninety (90) days, or they lapse. Must in any case be ratified at the next General Meeting.

5.7 **Termination of Membership.** A Member may resign per their Federation Agreement (Organizations and similar) or per the Individual Membership terms (individuals). A Member may be removed for cause by (a) Ordinary Resolution of the Board for administrative or contribution default after notice and opportunity to cure; (b) Constituency Resolution of the Member's Constituency; or (c) Special Resolution for violation of the Stewardship Principles or activity structurally contradicting the Driver. **Removal does not claw back honest past contributions.**

## 6. Six Capitals Accounting

6.1 The Ecosystem Company adopts **six-capital accounting**, tracking Natural, Individual, Social, Intellectual, Manufactured, and Financial capital at the Ecosystem Company scale and across federated Members.

6.2 The **Six Capitals Standard (SCS-1)**, published in the Knowledge Commons, specifies methodology, reporting formats, and audit requirements. Federated Members commit, in their Federation Agreements, to six-capital reporting per SCS-1 or a designated compatible standard.

6.3 The Ecosystem Company's financial statements shall be prepared annually in accordance with generally accepted accounting principles in the primary jurisdiction, with additional reporting per SCS-1, and audited financial statements presented at each Annual General Meeting.

## 7. The Switchboard

7.1 The Ecosystem Company operates a **Switchboard** for multi-capital flow coordination across federated Members.

7.2 The Switchboard's functions are:

- To receive contributions from Members per Federation Agreement terms.
- To operate protocols for distribution of value flows according to Dividend Sharing Dynamics and supporting protocols.
- To maintain a transparent, auditable Ledger of all flows.
- To administer multi-capital coordination across the six capitals.
- To support Ecosystem Fund operations (§7.5).
- To support Developmental Infrastructure Funding (§7.6).

7.3 The **Switchboard Committee**, appointed by the Board, administers the Switchboard under protocols set by Ordinary Resolution.

7.4 The **Switchboard Standard (SS-1)**, published in the Knowledge Commons, specifies the operational methodology.

7.5 **Ecosystem Fund**: ten percent (10%) of distributable surplus is allocated to a strategic capital pool used for new-Member capitalization, Founding-Phase work, crisis response, and strategic initiatives consented by the Ecosystem Company's decision processes. By Special Resolution of both the Ecosystem Company and the Ecosystem Resourcing Pool (Layer IV), the Ecosystem Fund may be migrated to operate as a **Strategic Ecosystem sub-fund** within the Resourcing Pool's fund-of-funds architecture (per [[IV - The Ecosystem Resourcing Pool Charter]] §7.2), to consolidate capital coordination at Layer IV while preserving the Ecosystem Fund's strategic mandate.

7.6 **Developmental Infrastructure Funding**: a specified allocation from Switchboard contributions, defined in SS-1, funds developmental practice infrastructure.

7.6.1 **Coordination with the Ecosystem Resourcing Pool.** The Switchboard and the Resourcing Pool maintain reciprocal data and settlement integration. The Switchboard handles multi-capital coordination across the federation generally; the Resourcing Pool handles capital-specific deployment, investor accounts, and fund-of-funds operations. Where a flow concerns capital deployment to or from a federated operating entity at Layer VII, the Switchboard routes the operational signal and the Resourcing Pool executes the capital transfer, consistent with both bodies' Standards (SS-1 and RPS-1).

7.7 **Default Ecosystem Contribution rate.** Federation Agreements default to a two-tier **Ecosystem Contribution** flowing from each federated Member entity to the Ecosystem Holding Company:

(a) **Revenue base — one percent (1%) of revenue.** Paid as revenue arrives (off-the-top), denominated in revenue rather than profit so that pre-profit, early-stage, and non-profit federated entities contribute on the same footing. This base ensures every federated entity participates in strengthening the commons regardless of profit.

(b) **Profit-based true-up — to ten percent (10%) of profit.** At each accounting period close, where ten percent (10%) of profit for the period exceeds the cumulative revenue-base contributions paid for the period, the Member contributes the difference. The combined Ecosystem Contribution therefore equals the greater of 1% of revenue or 10% of profit.

The Ecosystem Contribution funds the Ecosystem Fund (§7.5), Developmental Infrastructure Funding (§7.6), the operations of the Ecosystem Holding Company itself, and (after these are met) is distributable to Member Constituencies per the Dividend Sharing Dynamics of §8. Specific contribution terms are set per Federation Agreement; the defaults above apply where the Agreement does not specify otherwise. The Ecosystem Contribution is distinct from the **Profit Pool** mechanism at §8.5.

## 8. Distribution of Surplus

8.1 **Dividend Sharing Dynamics** allocate distributable surplus across Member Constituencies:

- **Class K (Superorganism): 10%** *(Superorganism Dynamic Value Stake)*
- Class B (Organization): 25%
- Class D (Ecosystem-Company): 15%
- Class G (Living Systems): 10%
- Class J (Society): 10%
- Class I (Investor): 20% up to 7% maximum return
- Class C (Ecosystem-Individual): 5%
- Class F (Collaborator-Company): 3%
- Class E (Collaborator-Individual): 2%
- Class A (Stewardship): 0%
- Class H (Donor): 0%
- **Total: 100%**

*Rebalancing note: Class K's 10% is drawn 5 from Organization (formerly 30%) and 5 from Ecosystem-Company (formerly 20%). Investor stays at 20% (capped per IS-1); Living Systems, Society, and Ecosystem-Individual are preserved untouched.*

8.2 **Within-Constituency distribution** follows protocols combining contribution weight, need weight, and strategic weight as specified in SS-1.

8.3 **Bounded ratios**: no Member may receive a distribution exceeding **seven (7) times** the median distribution within their Constituency in a given year, unless adjusted by Special Resolution.

8.4 Dividend Sharing Dynamics may be adjusted only by Special Resolution.

8.5 **Profit Pool Mechanism (Ergodic Redistribution).** In addition to the Ecosystem Contribution at §7.7, federated Member entities at Layer VII (operating entities, per [[VII - The FSx Organism Template]]) contribute **thirty percent (30%) of profit** in each accounting period to an **Ecosystem Profit Pool** held at the Ecosystem Holding Company. The Profit Pool is, by structure, **redistributed back to federated Members in the same period** — across Member Constituencies per the Dividend Sharing Dynamics of §8.1, and within each Constituency per accumulated **TEA (Time, Energy, and Attention)** and the within-Constituency protocols at §8.2 / SS-1. Each federated entity therefore has 70% of its profit retained for distribution through its own internal Dividend Sharing Dynamics (per [[VII - The FSx Organism Template]] Article 40), and 30% of its profit redistributed across the federated commons.

This **ergodic pooling** structurally couples the success of each Member to the success of the whole, equalizing incentives across for-profit and non-profit federated entities and across geographies of differing economic conditions. The wisdom is mathematical: for non-ergodic processes such as wealth accumulation, pooling reduces variance and improves long-run outcomes for ensemble participants. Each federated entity's net flow under this mechanism may be greater or lesser than its 30% contribution depending on the ecosystem's overall conditions; over time, the mechanism tends toward equalization without erasing the rewards of distinctive contribution.

The Profit Pool fraction (default 30%) and its redistribution weighting (default per §8.1 Constituency stakes × within-Constituency TEA) may be adjusted by Special Resolution. The intra-Constituency weighting toward FSx Individual Constituencies (Classes A, C, E) within Profit Pool redistribution is an open question for the Founding Phase deliberation; ergodic pooling wisdom suggests weighting individuals more strongly than the baseline Dividend Sharing Dynamics, so that ecosystem-wide flow reaches each individual person regardless of which entity they are embedded in.

8.6 **Bounded ratios and within-Constituency distribution apply.** §8.2 (within-Constituency protocols), §8.3 (bounded ratios), and §8.4 (adjustment by Special Resolution) apply to Profit Pool redistribution as they apply to other Dividend Sharing Dynamics flows.

## 9. Intellectual Property and the Knowledge Commons

9.1 Intellectual property created in the course of Ecosystem Company work is released to the **Knowledge Commons** under **CC-BY-SA 4.0** or similar license.

9.2 Federated Members commit, in their Federation Agreements, to specified scopes of Knowledge Commons contribution.

9.3 Contributors receive **Knowledge Commons Credits** in proportion to peer-assessed utility of their contributions, per the Commons Standard (CS-1). Credits may be exchanged through the Switchboard for financial distribution at a defined conversion rate, applied as weighting in Weighted Governance Dynamics calculations within Class C, or used to fund Commons-adjacent work.

9.4 Contributions from participants, particularly when relating to sensitive cultural or technological knowledge, may be held under terms specified by the originating community or group rather than under default CC-BY-SA 4.0 license, per the Cultural Commons protocol in CS-1.

9.5 The Knowledge Commons is **inalienable**. It cannot be enclosed or sold. It persists independently of the Ecosystem Company's existence (see §11).

## 10. Entrenched Provisions

10.1 Provisions of this Charter are protected by **tiered entrenchment**, mirroring [[II - The Stewardship Agreement]] §11.4 and §11.7 with weighted-governance mechanics in place of one-voice mechanics:

(a) **Absolutely entrenched** — changeable only through dissolution and reconstitution. No Resolution available under this Charter may touch:
- The spirit of the **Driver Statement** (Service to Life and Source) at §2.1.
- The **no-extraction principle** of the Asset Lock at §10.2: that no capital contributor, Member, or officer may receive distribution exceeding return of invested principal or honest service compensation.
- The **right of voluntary entry and exit** — the principle that no Member is bound to remain.

(b) **Entrenched at the Entrenched Resolution tier** (§5.6) — changeable only by Entrenched Resolution (85% Weighted Governance Dynamics majority on fallback, bare majority within each Active Constituency, unanimous Steward consent, one-year reflection period):
- The **wording of the Driver Statement** (§2.1) — refinements of language consistent with the absolutely entrenched spirit.
- Each **Stewardship Principle** of [[II - The Stewardship Agreement]] §16 individually, as it applies to Ecosystem Company operations.
- The detailed provisions of the **Asset Lock** (§10.2) other than the absolutely entrenched no-extraction principle.
- The **Purpose Lock** (§10.3).

(c) **Special Resolution** required for: substantive amendments to other Articles of this Charter (member constituency structure, Weighted Governance Dynamics percentages, Capital Gains Dynamics, Dividend Sharing Dynamics, the bounded-ratio cap, Stewardship developmental thresholds).

(d) **Ordinary Resolution** sufficient for: operational provisions, Standards adoption, and matters expressly delegated to the Board.

10.2 **Asset Lock.** On dissolution, all assets of the Ecosystem Company, after satisfaction of genuine creditors, shall flow to other FSx-compatible entities in service of the same Driver. **No capital contributor, Member, or officer shall receive distribution exceeding the return of invested principal (for Investors) or honest service compensation (for officers).** The no-extraction principle of this Article is absolutely entrenched per §10.1(a) and cannot be reversed, weakened, or circumvented by any Resolution or amendment. The detailed flow-through provisions may be refined by Entrenched Resolution.

10.3 **Purpose Lock.** The Ecosystem Company's resources, assets, and operational capacity are committed to the pursuit of its Driver. They may not be redirected to any other purpose by any Resolution available under this Charter. The Purpose Lock is entrenched per §10.1(b).

## 11. Termination, Dissolution, and Succession

11.1 **Termination of Federation Agreements.** A Federation Agreement may be terminated by either party per the exit terms specified. Termination does not terminate the underlying Member entity; the Member ceases to hold Ecosystem Company Membership. Switchboard contributions cease. Access to the Knowledge Commons continues under CC-BY-SA 4.0 as for any public participant. Specific obligations arising during the Federation period are settled per the Agreement.

11.2 **Dissolution.** The Ecosystem Company may be dissolved only by Special Resolution with unanimous Steward consent, reflecting a determination that the Ecosystem Company's form no longer serves its Driver. On dissolution, the Asset Lock applies. The Knowledge Commons, the Protocol Commons, and the Developmental Commons released to CC-BY-SA 4.0 persist independently of the Ecosystem Company's existence.

11.3 **Merger and Succession.** The Ecosystem Company may merge with another FSx-compatible body by Special Resolution. The Driver, the Stewardship Principles, and the entrenched provisions must be preserved in any merger. Succession is guided by the principle that the Driver is served, whatever form best serves it. The Ecosystem Resourcing Pool at Layer IV is a **distinct sibling entity** with its own dissolution, merger, and succession provisions per [[IV - The Ecosystem Resourcing Pool Charter]] §15; dissolution of the Ecosystem Holding Company does not, of itself, dissolve the Resourcing Pool, and vice versa.

## 13. Amendment of This Charter

13.1 This Charter may be amended at the Resolution tier appropriate to the provision being changed, per §10:

- **Operational provisions** — by **Ordinary Resolution** under §5.6.
- **Substantive provisions** not entrenched — by **Special Resolution** under §5.6.
- **Entrenched provisions** under §10.1(b) — by **Entrenched Resolution** under §5.6, including the one-year reflection period.
- **Absolutely entrenched provisions** under §10.1(a) — only through dissolution and reconstitution of the superorganism.

13.2 Any Member or the Board may propose an amendment. Proposals shall include the intention, the section(s) affected, the proposed revision, and the Resolution tier required.

13.3 Amendments are shared with the Steward Assembly and the relevant Constituency Representatives, reviewed through dialogue, and refined where needed.

13.4 Until an amendment is formally adopted, the current Charter remains in effect. Once adopted, the updated version becomes the active Charter.

13.5 No lower Resolution tier may override the requirements of a higher tier. An amendment proposed under the wrong tier is invalid.
