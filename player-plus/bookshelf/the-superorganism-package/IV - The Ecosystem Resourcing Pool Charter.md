# III - The Ecosystem Resourcing Pool Charter

> *The Articles of the Ecosystem Resourcing Pool — the (Currently Nameless) Superorganism's fund-of-funds and internal bank, constituted as an FSx, federating sub-fund FSx instances under cross-fund ergodic pooling, in service of the Driver and the Stewardship Principles. Released to the Knowledge Commons under CC-BY-SA 4.0.*

---

## 1. Form and Naming

1.1 The **Ecosystem Resourcing Pool** (hereinafter the *Resourcing Pool*) is the **capital coordination entity** of the (Currently Nameless) Superorganism. It is a **fund-of-funds** — a single FSx parent body federating multiple **sub-fund FSx instances** that pool risk and reward with one another and deploy capital across the federation in service of the Driver. It is the ecosystem's **internal bank**.

1.2 The Resourcing Pool is itself constituted as an **FSx** — a FairShares Commons-type fractal sovereign legal person with agency independent of its members, with dynamic weighted governance and economic rights — operating at the capital-coordination scale of the FSx pattern (see [[VII - The FSx Organism Template]]).

1.3 The Resourcing Pool's primary jurisdiction of incorporation is to be specified in final filing during the Founding Phase. Parallel-compatible forms in other jurisdictions will be established by Ordinary Resolution to support participants in those jurisdictions.

1.4 The Resourcing Pool has full legal personhood and agency. It can hold property, enter contracts, deploy capital, receive returns, and exercise the powers of a natural person consistent with this Charter, the Stewardship Principles, the Driver, and applicable law.

1.5 **No party owns the Resourcing Pool.** Federated participants — sub-funds, investors, the Ecosystem Holding Company, federated operating entities, Stewards, and others — hold standing as Members in the constituencies specified at §4. Capital placed in the Resourcing Pool is held in fiduciary trust under the Asset Lock at §10, not as ownership.

1.6 The Resourcing Pool operates **under and in service of** the Stewardship Agreement at [[II - The Stewardship Agreement]] and **in coordination with** the Ecosystem Holding Company at [[III - The Ecosystem Holding Charter]]. It is a sibling entity to the Ecosystem Holding Company at the federation level, distinct in function (capital coordination rather than governance coordination) but coupled in governance via interlocking representation. Where this Charter and the Stewardship Agreement appear to conflict, the Central Animating Spirit, Driver Statement, and Stewardship Principles of the Stewardship Agreement govern.

## 2. Purpose and Driver

2.1 The Resourcing Pool's purpose is to **deploy and steward capital across the federated ecosystem** in service of the **Driver Statement** of the superorganism (entrenched at §2.3 of [[II - The Stewardship Agreement]] and reproduced in [[Appendix A - The Driver Statement]]):

> *The superorganism exists to unite and cohere the consciousness, capacities, and activities of sovereigns into a higher-order functional unity in service of Life and Source — capable of regenerating Earth, harmonizing her inhabitants into a world that works for All, and supporting the flourishing of all generations of life across all realms and worlds we are part of.*

2.2 The Driver is **entrenched**. It cannot be amended through any process available in this Charter.

2.3 The Resourcing Pool fulfils its purpose by:

- Operating a portfolio of **sub-fund FSx instances** (§7), each itself a federated FSx with its own Driver compatible with the superorganism's.
- Operating the **Cross-Fund Ergodic Pool** (§8) by which sub-funds share a portion of risk and reward with one another, applying the variance-reducing wisdom of ensemble pooling at the fund-of-funds level.
- Holding **investor capital accounts** (§9) for investors who place capital with the federation, with capped returns per the Investment Standard (IS-1).
- **Deploying capital to federated operating entities** at Layer VII (§10), as the **primary** — and where so designated by Federation Agreement, **sole** — resourcing source for those federated entities.
- Coordinating with the **Switchboard** of the Ecosystem Holding Company (II §7) so that capital flows are integrated with multi-capital flows across the broader federation.

2.4 **Application of Ergodic Investor Principles.** The Resourcing Pool is the structural realization of the ergodic investment wisdom in the FSC Pattern Language lineage (see *The Ergodic Investor*): pooling outcomes across multiple plays reduces ensemble variance and improves long-run flourishing for all participants. Cross-fund ergodic pooling at §8, capped returns at §9, multi-capital deployment at §10, and the structural separation between investors and operating entities together enact this wisdom at the federation level.

## 3. Relationship to Other Layers

3.1 The Resourcing Pool sits at **Layer IV** of the (Currently Nameless) Superorganism's constitutional package:

- **Layer II** ([[II - The Stewardship Agreement]]) — the spiritual association.
- **Layer III** ([[III - The Ecosystem Holding Charter]]) — the federation spine and governance coordination.
- **Layer IV** (this Charter) — capital coordination; the fund-of-funds; the internal bank.
- **Layer VII** ([[VII - The FSx Organism Template]]) — the federated operating entities.
- **Layer VIII** ([[VIII - The FSx Individual Template]]) — the irreducible scale; FSx Individual.

3.2 **Capital flows through the Resourcing Pool, not around it.** The Resourcing Pool is the **primary resourcing source for federated operating entities** at Layer VII. The Ecosystem Holding Company at Layer III (which coordinates governance and value flows) and the Resourcing Pool at Layer IV (which coordinates capital) are distinct entities so that no operating entity is exposed directly to individual investor pressure, and no individual investor is exposed directly to any single operating entity's outcome. This **one-degree structural separation** protects both sides:

- **Investors** are insulated from any single operating entity's specific risk; their returns flow from the diversified, ergodically-pooled portfolio of the fund-of-funds.
- **Operating entities** are insulated from individual-investor pressure on operations, governance, or extraction; they receive capital from a multi-stakeholder, capped-return, mission-aligned source.

3.3 **Sole-Source Designation.** A Federation Agreement between the Resourcing Pool and a federated operating entity at Layer VII may designate the Resourcing Pool as the **sole** capital source for that entity. Where this designation applies, the operating entity may not accept investor capital from any source other than the Resourcing Pool (it may still receive grants, donations, revenue, member contributions, and Switchboard flows from the Ecosystem Holding Company; "capital" here means investment capital as defined in IS-1). The default Federation Agreement at Layer VII adopts the Sole-Source designation; entities may, by mutual agreement and Ordinary Resolution of both bodies, federate without it.

3.4 **Membership in upper layers.** Membership in the Stewardship Agreement at Layer II is **separate from and prior to** membership in the Resourcing Pool. Membership in the Ecosystem Holding Company at Layer III is **separate from but coordinated with** membership in the Resourcing Pool. A sub-fund or investor may federate into the Resourcing Pool without participating in the Holding Company beyond the standing automatic by federation; reciprocal governance terms at §5 connect the two.

3.5 The **Threshold of Federation** (II §3.3) applies fully to the Resourcing Pool: only sovereign legal persons constituted as FSx may federate as sub-funds. Sub-fund admission is sovereign-to-sovereign throughout, structurally protecting the Pool from capture, predation, enclosure, and the smuggling of extractive forms across the line.

## 4. Member Constituencies

4.1 The Resourcing Pool has the following Constituencies of Membership. Per the Threshold of Federation, every Member entity is itself a sovereign legal person of the FSx pattern. References to "individuals" in any Constituency denote **FSx Individual** Members (per [[VIII - The FSx Individual Template]]), not flesh-and-blood persons in their unconstituted capacity.

- **Class A — Stewardship Members.** FSx Individual serving as Stewards of the Driver and the entrenched Stewardship Principles, satisfying the developmental Qualifying Criteria of [[III - The Ecosystem Holding Charter]] §4.2.
- **Class B — Sub-Fund Members.** **The federated sub-fund FSx instances themselves** — see §7. Each sub-fund holds standing as a Member of the Resourcing Pool by virtue of its Federation Agreement.
- **Class C — Investor-Individual FSx Members.** FSx Individual who have placed capital with the Resourcing Pool through one or more sub-funds.
- **Class D — Investor-Company Members.** FSx-compatible institutional investors (foundations, endowments, mission-aligned funds, family offices constituted in FSx-compatible form, etc.) who have placed capital with the Resourcing Pool through one or more sub-funds.
- **Class E — Investee-Company Members.** FSx-compatible federated operating entities at Layer VII that have received capital deployment from the Resourcing Pool. This Constituency gives operating entities a voice in the capital-coordination layer that funds them.
- **Class F — Donor Members.** Contributors of capital without expectation of financial return, where the contribution is to the Resourcing Pool rather than to the Ecosystem Holding Company.
- **Class G — Living Systems Members.** Holders-in-trust for specific natural domains and the more-than-human kin, with standing to represent those domains in capital-deployment decisions of the Pool.
- **Class H — Society Members.** Holders-in-trust for specific cultural, social, and community domains, with standing to represent those domains in capital-deployment decisions.
- **Class I — Ecosystem Holding Company.** The Ecosystem Holding Company at Layer III holds standing as a single Member entity, ensuring reciprocal governance coupling with the federation spine.
- **Class J — Superorganism Member.** The **(Currently Nameless) Superorganism** itself — in its spiritual aspect at [[II - The Stewardship Agreement]] — represented by the Stewardship Council or such other representative as the Federation Agreement specifies. Carries the **Superorganism Dynamic Stake** in this Resourcing Pool: by default a **10% Dynamic Governance Stake** and a **10% Dynamic Value Stake**. Distinct from Class I (the Ecosystem Holding Company as an operating sibling FSx); Class J represents the body of the whole.

4.2 **Stewardship Member candidacy** uses the same developmental Qualifying Criteria as [[III - The Ecosystem Holding Charter]] §4.2. Founding-Phase Stewards may be the same persons serving Stewardship roles at Layer III, given the close coupling between governance and capital coordination during the Founding Phase. Post-Founding, the bodies may diverge as appropriate.

4.3 **General Membership** in any Constituency other than Stewardship does not require a developmental threshold. All entities that consent to this Charter, sign the relevant Federation Agreement, and meet Constituency-specific requirements may become Members.

4.4 **Capital Gains Dynamics.** Capital appreciation across the Resourcing Pool's holdings is allocated across Member Constituencies as follows (sample defaults; adjustable by Special Resolution):

- **Class J (Superorganism): 10%** *(Superorganism Dynamic Value Stake)*
- Class B (Sub-Funds): 25%
- Class E (Investee-Company): 15%
- Class C (Investor-Individual FSx): 15%
- Class D (Investor-Company): 15%
- Class G (Living Systems): 7.5%
- Class H (Society): 7.5%
- Class F (Donor): 0% (by Donor election)
- Class A (Stewardship): 0%
- Class I (Ecosystem Holding Company): 5%
- **Total: 100%**

*Rebalancing note: Class J's 10% is drawn 5 from Sub-Funds (formerly 30%) and 5 from Investor-Individual (formerly 20%) — the two largest capital-side stakes. Living Systems, Society, Investee-Company, Investor-Company, and the Ecosystem Holding Company's standing are preserved untouched.*

The differences from II §4.5 reflect the capital-deployment focus: Investor and Investee Constituencies carry more weight here, while general Organization Membership (which dominates II) is replaced by Sub-Fund Membership.

## 5. Governance

5.1 **Governance pattern.** The Resourcing Pool is governed by the **same FSx pattern** that governs the rest of the federation: multi-stakeholder Constituencies; **Integrative Decision-Making (IDM)** as the default decision method; Weighted Governance Dynamics on fallback; tiered Resolutions (Ordinary, Special, Entrenched, Constituency, Emergency); Stewardship Constituency veto on Special and Entrenched Resolutions touching the Stewardship Principles. There is no specialized governance for capital coordination; capital coordination is held within the same multi-stakeholder pattern as everything else, ensuring that no investor class can capture the capital flows.

Both Constituencies represent the broader purpose; Stewardship enacts the Driver within the Pool, Superorganism represents the body of the whole. The Stewardship + Superorganism combined weight (40) is unchanged.*

The Investor Constituencies (C + D = 20) and Investee Constituency (E = 12) together hold a substantial but not dominant share, balanced by Stewardship (40) and the structural representation of Sub-Funds, Living Systems, Society, and the Ecosystem Holding Company.

Major decisions affecting the federation as a whole require coordinated resolution between the two bodies, conducted through the Standards (GS-1, DS-1) and the Stewardship Council at Layer II.

5.7 **Termination of Membership.** A Member may resign per their Federation Agreement, or be removed for cause per the same procedures as [[III - The Ecosystem Holding Charter]] §5.7. **Removal does not claw back honest past contributions; investor principal remains protected per §9.**

## 6. Six Capitals Accounting

6.1 The Resourcing Pool adopts **six-capital accounting** per the Six Capitals Standard (SCS-1), tracking Natural, Individual, Social, Intellectual, Manufactured, and Financial capital across the Pool itself, all sub-funds, and all Investee operating entities receiving capital deployment.

6.2 **Investment performance is reported in all six capitals**, not solely in financial returns. An investee company that produces strong Natural and Social capital outcomes alongside modest Financial returns is honored as a high-performing investment under the SCS-1 framework, even if conventional financial metrics alone would discount it.

6.3 The Resourcing Pool's annual report includes consolidated six-capital reporting across all sub-funds and investees, presented at the Annual General Meeting alongside financial statements audited per applicable jurisdiction.

## 7. Sub-Funds (Fund-of-Funds Architecture)

7.1 The Resourcing Pool federates a portfolio of **sub-funds**, each an FSx in its own right with its own Articles, Driver Statement (compatible with the superorganism's), Member Constituencies, Capital Gains Dynamics, Dividend Sharing Dynamics, and Asset/Purpose Locks. Sub-funds use the [[VII - The FSx Organism Template]] as their starting point with Resourcing Pool-specific adaptations specified in this §7.

7.2 **Sub-fund categories** include, by way of illustration and not limitation:

- **Bioregional sub-funds** — capital deployment focused on a coherent ecological and cultural region.
- **Sectoral sub-funds** — regenerative agriculture; renewable energy; shelter and village development; technology; education; health; cultural and indigenous lifeways; etc.
- **Purpose / Quest sub-funds** — time-bounded, mission-specific, often coupled to Layer III Quests.
- **Vintage sub-funds** — annual or multi-annual cohorts (Year-1 vintage, Year-2 vintage, etc.) supporting time-batched capital deployment.
- **Strategic Ecosystem sub-fund** — what was the Ecosystem Fund at [[III - The Ecosystem Holding Charter]] §7.5 may, by Special Resolution of both bodies, migrate into the Resourcing Pool as a sub-fund.
- **Crisis-response sub-fund** — held with deliberately low utilization for rapid deployment in genuine crises.

7.3 **Sub-fund admission.** A sub-fund FSx federates into the Resourcing Pool by signing a **Sub-Fund Federation Agreement** with the Resourcing Pool. The Agreement specifies the sub-fund's Driver, Investment Mandate, Capital Gains Dynamics, Dividend Sharing Dynamics, Cross-Fund Pool contribution rate (default per §8), reporting cadence, and exit terms.

7.4 **Sub-fund creation.** New sub-funds may be created by:

- (a) Ordinary Resolution of the Resourcing Pool, where the sub-fund Driver is clearly aligned with the superorganism's Driver and the Pool's existing portfolio;
- (b) Special Resolution, where the sub-fund proposes new categories or significant scale;
- (c) External proposal — any qualifying FSx may propose itself as a sub-fund by submitting Articles meeting the FSx Compatibility Tests (per [[VII - The FSx Organism Template]]) and the Resourcing Pool's Sub-Fund Qualifying Criteria.

7.5 **Sub-fund dissolution.** A sub-fund may dissolve by Special Resolution of its own Members, with assets flowing per its Asset Lock to FSx-compatible bodies serving the same Driver — preferably to other sub-funds within the Resourcing Pool, otherwise to FSx-compatible bodies outside.

7.6 **Sub-fund autonomy.** Each sub-fund retains full sovereignty over its own operations, investment decisions, and internal governance, subject to the Driver compatibility and the Cross-Fund Ergodic Pool contribution. The Resourcing Pool does not micromanage sub-fund operations; it coordinates them.

## 8. The Cross-Fund Ergodic Pool

8.1 **Mechanism.** Each sub-fund contributes a default **30% of its periodic returns** (Capital Gains plus distributable Surplus) to the **Cross-Fund Ergodic Pool**, held by the Resourcing Pool. The Ergodic Pool is, by structure, **redistributed across all sub-funds in the same period** — partly equal-weighted (every sub-fund receives a baseline equal share), partly weighted by contribution / impact / strategic priority per the Resourcing Pool Standard (RPS-1).

8.2 **Why ergodic pooling.** This applies the central insight of *The Ergodic Investor* at the fund-of-funds scale: for non-ergodic processes such as capital allocation, **ensemble pooling reduces variance and improves long-run outcomes for all participants**. A sub-fund with an exceptional year contributes more to the ensemble; a sub-fund with a difficult year is buoyed by the ensemble. Over time, the mechanism dampens variance across the portfolio without erasing the rewards of distinctive performance.

8.3 **Net flows.** A sub-fund's net flow under the Ergodic Pool may be positive or negative in any given period. **Over time, the mechanism tends toward partial equalization** — high-performing funds contribute more than they receive; struggling funds receive more than they contribute. The structural commitment is mutual: every sub-fund accepts the Pool as a precondition of federation, knowing it will be both contributor and beneficiary across the long arc.

8.4 **Fraction adjustment.** The default 30% contribution rate may be adjusted by Special Resolution of the Resourcing Pool, in coordination with sub-fund Special Resolutions where individual sub-funds depart from the default. Sub-funds with explicitly higher-volatility mandates (e.g., crisis-response funds, frontier-research funds) may agree to higher cross-fund contribution rates as a condition of federation; sub-funds with explicitly lower-volatility mandates (e.g., long-horizon strategic funds) may agree to lower rates.

8.5 **Within-sub-fund distribution.** When a sub-fund receives its share of Ergodic Pool redistribution, that share enters the sub-fund's distributable Surplus and is allocated per the sub-fund's own Capital Gains Dynamics and Dividend Sharing Dynamics, with within-Constituency weighting per accumulated TEA and contribution weight per SS-1 and RPS-1.

8.6 **The Ergodic Pool is not a tax** and **not a subsidy**. It is a **risk-sharing covenant** among federated sub-funds. The math is the wisdom: pooled outcomes outperform un-pooled outcomes for ensemble participants in non-ergodic domains, particularly across long time horizons and adverse conditions.

## 9. Investor Capital Accounts

9.1 **Investor capital is held in sub-funds, not at the Resourcing Pool directly.** Investors place capital with one or more sub-funds whose Drivers and Investment Mandates align with their intentions. The Resourcing Pool acts as the **fiduciary parent** — administering investor relationships, consolidating reporting, enforcing capped returns and Asset Lock, coordinating cross-fund moves — without holding investor capital in a single undifferentiated pool.

9.2 **Investor admission.** An investor (Class C Investor-Individual FSx or Class D Investor-Company) is admitted by:

- (a) constituting itself as an FSx (FSx Individual for individuals; FSx-compatible Articles for institutions);
- (b) signing the Stewardship Agreement at Layer II;
- (c) signing a Federation Agreement with the Resourcing Pool, specifying capital amount, sub-fund placement, capital commitment period, and reciprocal terms.

9.3 **Capped returns per IS-1.** All investor returns are capped per the Investment Standard (IS-1). The default cap is **seven percent (7%) per investor per year** across all sub-fund holdings combined, not per fund. This prevents an investor from compounding above-cap returns by spreading capital across multiple sub-funds. Returns above the cap flow to other Constituencies (Investee-Company, Sub-Fund, Living Systems, Society, etc.) per Capital Gains Dynamics at §4.4.

9.4 **Asset Lock applies to investor capital.** Investors may receive (a) **return of invested principal** plus (b) **capped returns under IS-1**. They may not receive distributions exceeding return of principal plus capped returns. On withdrawal or death, principal is returned per the sub-fund's exit provisions; any further capital that the investor's FSx Individual Charter has committed under its Personal Asset Lock (per [[VIII - The FSx Individual Template]] §F) flows to FSx-compatible bodies serving the same Driver as the investor's Self-Driver.

9.5 **Inter-fund mobility.** An investor may move capital between sub-funds within the Resourcing Pool by signing a sub-fund Transfer Agreement. Inter-fund moves do not constitute withdrawal; they remain within the Resourcing Pool's federation. Frequent or destabilizing moves may be limited per RPS-1.

9.6 **Investor exit.** Upon notice and per the relevant Federation Agreement, an investor may withdraw principal from a sub-fund. Notice periods, redemption windows, and orderly-exit protocols are specified in IS-1 and per sub-fund. The Resourcing Pool may not redeem at the cost of operating entities in active capital deployment.

9.7 **Voluntary subordination.** An investor may, at the time of placement or by subsequent agreement, voluntarily subordinate their capital — accepting later return of principal, lower priority, or longer commitment — in exchange for greater impact alignment, a specific sub-fund's mandate, or other non-financial considerations. Such subordination is honored per the Federation Agreement and recorded in the investor's capital account.

## 10. Capital Deployment to Federated Operating Entities

10.1 **The Resourcing Pool is the primary capital source for federated operating entities at Layer VII.** Capital flows from investors through the Resourcing Pool's sub-funds into operating entities, in service of the Driver. Operating entities are insulated by structure from any single investor's pressure or extraction.

10.2 **Sole-Source Designation (Default).** The default Federation Agreement at Layer VII (per [[VII - The FSx Organism Template]]) adopts the **Sole-Source designation** at §3.3 of this Charter — the operating entity accepts investment capital exclusively from the Resourcing Pool. The entity may still receive grants, donations, revenue, member contributions, Switchboard flows, and cooperative trade flows from other federated entities; "investment capital" here means capital placed by an external party with expectation of financial return.

10.3 **Deployment instruments.** The Resourcing Pool may deploy capital to federated operating entities through:

- (a) **Equity** in the form of Investor Shares (Type A or B) per [[VII - The FSx Organism Template]] Article 11 and 12, with no ownership running in either direction beyond the share-holding relationship.
- (b) **Convertible instruments** — convertible loans, SAFE-equivalents, or convertible grants that may convert to Investor Shares per the operating entity's Articles.
- (c) **Loans** — at terms consistent with the Asset Lock and capped returns; non-recourse where appropriate.
- (d) **Grants** — non-returnable capital, where the sub-fund's mandate supports grant-making (typically Strategic Ecosystem and Crisis-Response sub-funds).
- (e) **Multi-capital deployment** — capital that includes natural, social, intellectual, manufactured, or other capital alongside or in lieu of financial capital, accounted per SCS-1.

10.4 **Compatibility.** No capital is deployed to any entity that is not FSx-compatible per [[VII - The FSx Organism Template]]. Where a candidate entity is in transition toward FSx compatibility, the Resourcing Pool may extend bridge capital under a **Pre-Federation Compatibility Agreement** that specifies the timeline and terms by which the candidate becomes federation-eligible.

10.5 **Reporting.** Operating entities receiving capital deployment commit, in their Federation Agreement with the Resourcing Pool, to six-capital reporting per SCS-1 at intervals specified in RPS-1, and to participation in the Resourcing Pool's portfolio review process.

10.6 **No micromanagement.** The Resourcing Pool does not direct operating entities' day-to-day decisions. It deploys capital with mission alignment confirmed at federation, and supports through reporting, mentorship, and ecosystem flows. Operations are sovereign to the operating entity per [[VII - The FSx Organism Template]].

## 12. Entrenched Provisions

12.1 Provisions of this Charter are protected by **tiered entrenchment**, mirroring [[II - The Stewardship Agreement]] §11.4 and [[III - The Ecosystem Holding Charter]] §10:

(a) **Absolutely entrenched** — changeable only through dissolution and reconstitution. No Resolution available under this Charter may touch:
- The spirit of the **Driver Statement** (Service to Life and Source) at §2.1.
- The **no-extraction principle** of the Asset Lock at §13: that no capital contributor, Member, or officer may receive distribution exceeding return of invested principal or honest service compensation. **This applies to every investor, every sub-fund, and the Resourcing Pool itself.**
- The **right of voluntary entry and exit** of every Member.
- The **structural separation between investors and operating entities** at §10 — capital flows through the Pool, not around it; this is what makes the Pool the body it is.

(b) **Entrenched at the Entrenched Resolution tier** — changeable only by Entrenched Resolution (85% Weighted Governance Dynamics majority on fallback, bare majority within each Active Constituency, unanimous Steward consent, one-year reflection period):
- The wording of the Driver Statement (§2.1).
- Each Stewardship Principle of [[II - The Stewardship Agreement]] §16 individually, as it applies to Resourcing Pool operations.
- The detailed Asset Lock provisions (§13) other than the absolutely entrenched no-extraction principle.
- The Purpose Lock (§14).
- The capped-return cap (§9.3) — protecting against gradual erosion by inflation of the cap.

(c) **Special Resolution** required for: substantive amendments to other Articles (constituency structure, Weighted Governance Dynamics percentages, Capital Gains Dynamics, Dividend Sharing Dynamics, Cross-Fund Ergodic Pool default contribution rate, Sole-Source designation as default).

(d) **Ordinary Resolution** sufficient for: operational provisions, Standards adoption, sub-fund creation within established categories, and matters expressly delegated to the Board.

## 13. Asset Lock

13.1 **Asset Lock.** On dissolution of the Resourcing Pool, after satisfaction of genuine creditors and return of investor principal under §9.4, all residual assets shall flow to other FSx-compatible bodies in service of the same Driver — preferably to the Ecosystem Holding Company at Layer III for redeployment, otherwise to FSx-compatible bodies serving the same Driver.

13.2 **No capital contributor, Member, or officer shall receive distribution exceeding the return of invested principal (for Investors) or honest service compensation (for officers).** The no-extraction principle is absolutely entrenched per §12.1(a).

13.3 The detailed flow-through provisions may be refined by Entrenched Resolution.

## 14. Purpose Lock

14.1 **Purpose Lock.** The Resourcing Pool's resources, assets, and operational capacity are committed to the pursuit of its Driver — the deployment and stewardship of capital across the federated ecosystem in service of regeneration. They may not be redirected to any other purpose by any Resolution available under this Charter.

14.2 The Purpose Lock is entrenched per §12.1(b).

## 15. Termination, Dissolution, and Succession

15.1 **Termination of Federation Agreements.** A Federation Agreement (sub-fund, investor, or investee) may be terminated by either party per the exit terms specified. Termination does not terminate the underlying entity; the entity ceases to hold Resourcing Pool Membership.

15.2 **Dissolution.** The Resourcing Pool may be dissolved only by Special Resolution with **unanimous Steward consent**, reflecting a determination that the Pool's form no longer serves its Driver. On dissolution, the Asset Lock applies. Investor principal is returned per §9.4 and the relevant Federation Agreements before any other distribution.

15.3 **Merger and Succession.** The Resourcing Pool may merge with another FSx-compatible capital-coordination body by Special Resolution. The Driver, the Stewardship Principles, the Asset Lock, the Purpose Lock, the structural separation between investors and operating entities, and the cross-fund ergodic pooling principle must be preserved in any merger.
