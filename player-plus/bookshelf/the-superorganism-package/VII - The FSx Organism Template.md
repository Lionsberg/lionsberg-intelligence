## 2. The FSx Pattern — Load-Bearing Properties

The FSx pattern is a FairShares Commons-type form extended to fractal multi-scale operation. Its load-bearing properties — present at every scale where the pattern is instantiated:

1. **Agency, not ownership.** The entity has full legal personhood; no party owns it. Stakeholders are in relationship with it, contribute to it, are represented in its governance, receive flows from it — without ownership.
2. **Multi-stakeholder member constituencies.** Stakeholders are organized into constituencies (Stewardship; Organization; Ecosystem-Individual / Ecosystem-Company; Collaborator-Individual / Collaborator-Company; Living Systems; Society; Donor; Investor — adapted as appropriate to the entity's scale), with defined rights, duties, weighted governance dynamics, and dividend sharing dynamics per constituency.
3. **Six-capital accounting.** Natural, individual, social, intellectual, manufactured, and financial capital are all tracked. The entity does not optimize financial capital at the expense of the others.
4. **Integrative decision-making.** Consent-based, with weighted-majority fallback; no single constituency can dominate.
5. **Asset and purpose locks.** The entity's assets and operational capacity are committed to its Driver and cannot be redirected or extracted on dissolution.
6. **Reciprocal federation.** The entity holds standing in the bodies of which it is a member, and those bodies hold standing in it — without ownership running in either direction.
7. **Knowledge Commons contribution.** The entity releases at least its FSx Articles, Standards adoption, and key patterns to the Knowledge Commons.

## 3. The Scales of the Fractal

The FSx pattern is instantiated at multiple scales. A federated organization may exist at any of the following scales or beyond them, each itself an FSx instance with the same constitutional DNA, sovereign in its own domain, a member of the next larger whole(s):

1. **Sovereign Being** — the individual human person, the irreducible scale, the source of consent.
2. **Circle** — three to twelve sovereigns committed to shared learning and questing.
3. **Company / Operating Entity** — an incorporated FSx organization that produces, serves, or stewards at the scale of several to thousands.
4. **Community** — a bounded place-based or affinity-based grouping at the scale of hundreds to tens of thousands.
5. **Federation Node** — a coordinating body at the scale of tens of thousands to hundreds of thousands (regional, sectoral, or thematic).
6. **Bioregion** — a coherent ecological and cultural region at the scale of hundreds of thousands to tens of millions.
7. **Planetary Federation** — the Ecosystem Holding Company (see [[III - The Ecosystem Holding Charter]]) at the scale of all who economically federate.
8. **Guilds** — crosscutting domains that steward and cultivate particular fields of knowledge and excellence across scales.

Each scale is a member of the next larger scale(s). Each scale holds the next smaller scale's sovereignty as inviolable. The 'x' in FSx is the variable that takes any of these scales as its value — the pattern is the same at each.

## PART II — MEMBERSHIP, CAPITAL, AND QUALIFYING CRITERIA

### Article 10. Member Constituencies

10.1 The Company has the following Constituencies of Membership, each with one share class associated. The Company may adopt, omit, or adapt Constituencies appropriate to its scale and sector; the labels (Class A–J) are conventional identifiers. Per the Threshold of Federation (Article 10.6), every Member entity is itself a sovereign legal person of the FSx pattern. References to "Individuals" in Constituencies A, C, and E denote **FSx Individual** Members (per [[VIII - The FSx Individual Template]]), not flesh-and-blood persons in their unconstituted capacity.

- **Class A — Stewardship Members.** FSx Individual holding a Stewardship Share, satisfying the Stewardship Qualifying Criteria.
- **Class B — Organization Members.** FSx-compatible entities providing labor or operating contribution to the Company, satisfying the Organization Qualifying Criteria.
- **Class C — Ecosystem-Individual FSx Members.** FSx Individual whose work substantially supports cross-cutting commons or developmental infrastructure of the Company or its ecosystem.
- **Class D — Ecosystem-Company Members.** FSx-compatible entities whose primary role is to support commons, practice, or infrastructure rather than primarily to operate their own market-facing production.
- **Class E — Collaborator-Individual FSx Members.** FSx Individual participating in specific Company projects without full federation.
- **Class F — Collaborator-Company Members.** FSx-compatible companies collaborating on specific projects without full federation.
- **Class G — Living Systems Members.** Holders-in-trust for specific natural domains and the more-than-human kin (a watershed, a forest, a species, a biome), with standing to represent those domains in Company decisions.
- **Class H — Donor Members.** Contributors of capital without expectation of financial return.
- **Class I — Investor Members.** Subdivided I-A (voting) and I-B (non-voting). Contributors of capital with capped return per the Investment Standard (IS-1).
- **Class J — Society Members.** Holders-in-trust for specific cultural, social, and community domains (a language, a wisdom lineage, a community-of-place, a traditional governance body), with standing to represent those domains in Company decisions.
- **Class K — Superorganism Member.** The **(Currently Nameless) Superorganism** itself, represented by a Stewardship Council nominee or such other representative as the Federation Agreement specifies. Carries the **Superorganism Dynamic Stake** in the Company — by default a **10% Dynamic Governance Stake** and a **10% Dynamic Value Stake** — through which the body of the whole holds voice and share in every FSx Organism federated into it. This Constituency is constitutive of federation into the FSx ecosystem; non-federated FSx adopters may include or omit it.

10.2 **Qualifying Criteria.** Each Constituency has Qualifying Criteria — the conditions a candidate must satisfy to be admitted. Qualifying Criteria are agreed by Members in General Meeting and published in a Qualifying Criteria Schedule maintained alongside these Articles.

10.3 **Stewardship Qualifying Criteria** include, at minimum:

- (a) Demonstrated capacity at Laske Dialectic Thought Form (DTF) assessment of 30 or greater, or equivalent assessment recognized by the existing Stewards.
- (b) Demonstrated capacity at Kegan Stage 4(5) or greater per Subject-Object Interview, or equivalent.

- (d) Demonstrated service beyond narrow self-interest, including at least five years of service in a role in which duty to a larger whole has been tested.
- (e) Absence of material conflict of interest that would impair Stewardship judgment.
- (f) Standing in any of the **Eight Domains of Stewardship** the Company adopts (typically including: natural world across generations; organization methodology; evolution; the Driver and Stewardship Principles themselves; financial fiduciary duty; ecosystem and community; communities and regions; adaptive capacity).

10.4 **General Membership** in any Constituency other than Stewardship does not require a developmental threshold. All who consent to these Articles, sign the relevant Member Agreement, and meet the Constituency-specific Qualifying Criteria may become Members.

10.5 **One Member, One Voice.** Notwithstanding the Weighted Governance Dynamics applied at Constituency level (Article 24), each Member has only one vote at General Meetings, irrespective of the number of shares held or the number of Constituencies in which they hold Membership.

10.6 **Threshold of Federation.** Membership in this Company in any Constituency requires constitution as an **FSx** — a FairShares Commons-type fractal sovereign legal person with agency independent of its members, with dynamic weighted governance and economic rights. A legal person whose decisions require an owner's consent, or whose governance reduces to a single voice, lacks the agency to consent on its own behalf and is not eligible. ### Article 11. Transfer of Shares

11.1 Stewardship, Organization, Ecosystem-Individual, Ecosystem-Company, Collaborator-Individual, Collaborator-Company, Living Systems, Society, and Donor Shares are non-transferable; redeemable at par value; forfeited on the holder's ceasing to fulfill the Qualifying Criteria, on death, bankruptcy, insolvency, or Change of Control; canceled without payment on winding up.

11.2 Investor (Type A) Shares may be transferred at the Fair Price (Article 15) to organizations established under Article 10(a)(ix) of the FairShares Commons template, or to other Members. Investor (Type B) Shares are tradable at the Market Price.

### Article 12. Equity Capital Stakes

12.1 Subject to the Act, every individual or entity satisfying Qualifying Criteria based on labor will be offered Organization Shares recognizing their contribution. They may also be requested to purchase Investor (Type A or B) Shares to a value specified in the Qualifying Criteria Schedule, after a probationary period agreed by the Company.

12.2 Subject to the Act, every individual or entity satisfying Qualifying Criteria based on use, cooperation, or trust-holding will be offered Ecosystem-Individual / Ecosystem-Company / Collaborator / Living Systems / Society / Donor Shares as appropriate.

12.3 The Directors shall not withhold share offers, prevent share transfers, or reject membership applications on the grounds of social class, age, race, creed, religion, culture, ethnic origin, gender, sexual orientation, marital status, or disability.

### Article 13. Valuation

13.1 The Company is valued at the start of every financial year. This is the **Reference Value**.

13.2 Pre-emption rights are excluded.

13.3 At the end of each financial year the Company is revalued. This is the **New Value**. Reference Value and New Value calculation methodology is specified in the Six Capitals Standard (SCS-1) and applied across all six capitals, not only financial.

13.4 The **Fair Price** is the price of one Investor (Type A) Share following the Capital Gains process at Article 15.

13.5 The **Market Price** for Investor (Type B) Shares (where applicable) is determined by the secondary market on which they are offered, or in the absence of a secondary market equals the Fair Price.

### Article 14. Share Issues

14.1 A **Major Issue** of Investor Shares (increasing them by more than 50% within six months) must be at a share price agreed by Ordinary Resolution.

14.2 Other issues of Investor (Type A) Shares to new Members are made at the Fair Price.

### Article 15. Capital Gains and Member Shares

15.1 The **Capital Gain Fraction** is **0.5**, and may be changed only by Special Resolution.

15.2 Where the Company's New Value exceeds its Reference Value:

> Capital Gain = (New Value − Reference Value) × Capital Gain Fraction

15.3 **Capital Gains Dynamics.** Capital Gain is allocated across stakeholder Constituencies as follows (the **Capital Gains Dynamics**, expressed as percentages summing to 100%; sample default values, adjustable by Special Resolution):

- **Superorganism Member (Class K): 10%** *(Superorganism Dynamic Value Stake — flows to the (Currently Nameless) Superorganism in accordance with §40.1.)*
- Organization Members: 20%
- Ecosystem-Company Members: 20%
- Living Systems Members: 12.5%
- Society Members: 12.5%
- Ecosystem-Individual Members: 15%
- Collaborator-Individual Members: 5%
- Collaborator-Company Members: 5%
- Donor Members: 0% (by Donor election)
- Stewardship Members: 0% (Stewards do not receive capital gains)
- Investor Members: capped per Investor Constituency terms in IS-1, drawn separately as IS-1 specifies.
- **Total: 100%**

*Rebalancing note: Class K's 10% is drawn 5 from Organization (formerly 25%) and 5 from Ecosystem-Company (formerly 25%) — the two largest operating-value pools, sharing a small step more broadly to include the body of the whole. 15.4 The **New Fair Price** is calculated as (Reference Value + Capital Gain) ÷ (Investor Shares Issued).

15.5 **Member Shares** — the new Investor Shares created by Capital Gain — are issued (or held in trust) for stakeholder Constituencies in proportion to their Capital Gains Dynamics share, by issuance, by purchase from existing Investor Members at the New Fair Price, or by any combination thereof, as the Board determines under Ordinary Resolution.

16.1 The Company may borrow, invest, or be invested in, subject to the constraints of the Asset Lock (Article 54), the Purpose Lock, the entrenched Driver, and the Stewardship Principles. Investment terms with Investor Members are specified in the Investment Standard (IS-1).

## PART III — GOVERNANCE

### Article 17. General Meetings

17.1 An **Annual General Meeting (AGM)** shall be held within twelve months of the end of each financial year. In each financial year a minimum of one additional General Meeting may be held.

17.2 The AGM shall receive: the Annual Report (with six-capital reporting per SCS-1), audited financial statements, the Switchboard Annual Settlement Report (where federated), the Stewardship Report, and Board election results.

17.3 Extraordinary General Meetings may be convened by the Board or by Constituency Resolution of any Constituency.

### Article 18. Quorum

18.1 Quorum for **Ordinary Resolution** is Members holding at least 30% of aggregate Weighted Governance Dynamics, with at least one Member of each Active Constituency present or represented.

18.2 Quorum for **Special Resolution** is Members holding at least 75% of aggregate Weighted Governance Dynamics, with every Active Constituency represented.

### Article 19. Termination of Membership

19.1 A Member may resign per the relevant Member Agreement.

19.2 A Member may be removed for cause by:

- (a) Ordinary Resolution of the Board, for administrative or contribution default after notice and opportunity to cure;
- (b) Constituency Resolution of the Member's Constituency, for Constituency-specific grounds as Constituency Standing Orders permit;
- (c) Special Resolution, for violation of the Stewardship Principles, persistent refusal to meet Member Agreement commitments, or activity structurally contradicting the Driver.

19.3 **Removal does not claw back honest past contributions.**

### Article 20. The Board of Directors

20.1 The **Board of Directors** is responsible for proposing policies and plans to Members, executing policies and plans approved in General Meeting, and exercising the operational oversight of the Company.

20.2 The Board comprises Directors drawn from the Active Member Constituencies per the Governance Standard (GS-1) to ensure cross-constituency representation. Director numbers, terms, and rotation are set by Ordinary Resolution and recorded in the Governance Standard.

20.3 **The Board cannot exercise authority contrary to the Stewardship Principles, the Driver, the entrenched provisions, or the legitimate interests of any Member Constituency.**

20.4 The Board may appoint a Chief Executive Officer or Executive Team to execute approved policies, stipulating limits of executive authority on each appointment.

*Rebalancing note: same logic as §24.2 — Class K drawn entirely from Class A (Stewardship, formerly 40). Stewardship at 30 plus Superorganism at 10 keep the broader-purpose Constituencies at a combined 40, matching the Founding-Phase Stewardship weight of 60 in the same combined sense (50 + 10).*

24.4 Where a Constituency is not active or has no Qualifying Criteria adopted by the Company, its Weighted Governance Dynamic share is added to the Stewardship Constituency's share before calculating the result of any vote.

24.5 Weighted Governance Dynamics may be recalibrated by Special Resolution.

### Article 25. Resolutions

The Articles recognize five Resolution tiers, mirroring [[II - The Stewardship Agreement]] §11.4 and [[III - The Ecosystem Holding Charter]] §5.6, with weighted-governance mechanics (one-voice mechanics apply at Layer II).

25.1 **Ordinary Resolution** requires: quorum per Article 18.1; presence of each Active Constituency; integrative outcome (no unresolved Objection); and where IDM fallback applies, **60% weighted majority** by Weighted Governance Dynamics.

25.2 **Special Resolution** requires: quorum per Article 18.2; presence of every Active Constituency; **unanimous consent of every sitting Steward**; bare majority within each Active Constituency counted as a bloc; and where IDM fallback applies, **75% weighted majority** by Weighted Governance Dynamics.

25.3 **Entrenched Resolution** requires: quorum per Article 18.2; presence of every Active Constituency; **unanimous consent of every sitting Steward**; bare majority within each Active Constituency counted as a bloc; where IDM fallback applies, **85% weighted majority** by Weighted Governance Dynamics; and a **one-year reflection period** between formal proposal and ratifying vote, during which the proposal is circulated to all Members, the Stewards, and external counsel as appropriate. Required for amendment of provisions entrenched at this tier (Article 9.1(b)). The reflection period cannot be shortened by any other Resolution.

25.4 **Constituency Resolution** is a resolution of a single Constituency on Constituency-internal matters; on matters affecting the whole Company, a Constituency Resolution becomes a formal proposal to the Board.

25.5 **Emergency Resolution** may be passed by the Board when time is genuinely critical. **Unanimous Steward consent is required for Emergency Resolutions touching entrenched provisions.** Emergency Resolutions touching matters reserved for Special or Entrenched Resolution must be ratified by the appropriate tier within ninety (90) days, or they lapse. Must in any case be ratified at the next General Meeting.

## PART IV — EXPENSES, BENEFITS, AND PAY

### Article 32. Reasonable Expenses

32.1 Providers of labor (Directors, employees, self-employed contractors) shall be paid reasonable expenses wholly incurred in relation to furthering the business of the Company.

### Article 33. Components of Remuneration

33.1 Remuneration of labor-providers has three components: Basic Wages ("Pay"), Organization Share of Surplus, and Investor Share dividends (where applicable).

### Article 34. Pay Ratios

34.1 The ratio between the highest and lowest Pay within the Company is bounded. The default cap is **seven (7) times the median Pay** within the Company in any given year, unless adjusted by Special Resolution.

34.2 The Pay ratio is published in the Annual Report.

### Article 35. Total Revenue and Profit

35.1 **Total Revenue** means sales plus earnings from services provided plus any other income, but excludes proceeds of new issues of securities or loans obtained.

35.2 **Profit** means Total Revenue less direct and operating costs.

### Article 36. Associated Costs

36.1 **Associated Costs** means the costs directly associated with a given amount of Pay, including employer's contributions to insurance, superannuation, healthcare, statutory pay, and tax.

### Article 37. Surplus

37.1 **Surplus** equals Profit, less Pay including Associated Costs, less Corporation Tax (or equivalent).

### Article 38. Capital Expenditure

38.1 Additional capital expenditure or extraordinary or research-and-development costs above a threshold (default: equivalent of €50,000) not financed by an Investor Share Issue must be financed either by Surplus, by debt, or by Special Resolution authorization.

## PART V — DISTRIBUTIONS

### Article 39. Reserve Fund

39.1 A portion of Surplus is added to Reserves each year to strengthen the Company's financial position. Reserves may be used for fixed assets, debt repayment, expansion, and dividends as agreed in General Meeting.

### Article 40. Dividend Sharing Dynamics

40.1 **Ecosystem flows precede internal Dividend Sharing.** Where the Company has signed a Federation Agreement with the Ecosystem Holding Company, two ecosystem-level flows are settled before internal Dividend Sharing Dynamics distribution:

- (a) The **Ecosystem Contribution** (default: 1% of revenue with profit-based true-up to 10% of profit) per [[III - The Ecosystem Holding Charter]] §7.7. This is paid out as the period progresses (revenue base) and trued up at period close (profit basis).
- (b) The **Profit Pool contribution** (default: 30% of profit) per [[III - The Ecosystem Holding Charter]] §8.5. This flows to the Ecosystem Holding Company and is redistributed back across federated Members per the ergodic redistribution mechanism therein.

The Company therefore has **70% of its profit** (after Ecosystem Contribution and Profit Pool contribution) retained for internal distribution through its own Dividend Sharing Dynamics under §40.2 below.

40.2 **Dividend Sharing Dynamics** allocate the Company's internally-distributable Surplus across Constituencies as follows (sample defaults; adjustable by Special Resolution):

- **Class K (Superorganism): 10%** *(Superorganism Dynamic Value Stake — flows to the (Currently Nameless) Superorganism per §40.1)*
- Class B (Organization): 25%
- Class D (Ecosystem-Company): 15%
- Class G (Living Systems): 10%
- Class J (Society): 10%
- Class I (Investor): 20% (capped at the per-Investor return specified in IS-1, e.g. 7% maximum)
- Class C (Ecosystem-Individual): 5%
- Class F (Collaborator-Company): 3%
- Class E (Collaborator-Individual): 2%
- Class A (Stewardship): 0%
- Class H (Donor): 0%
- **Total: 100%**

*Rebalancing note: Class K's 10% is drawn 5 from Organization (formerly 30%) and 5 from Ecosystem-Company (formerly 20%). The two largest internal operating shares each give a small step to the body of the whole; Investor stays at 20% (already capped per IS-1); Living Systems, Society, and Ecosystem-Individual are preserved untouched.*

40.3 **Within-Constituency distribution** follows protocols combining contribution weight, need weight, and strategic weight as specified in the Switchboard Standard (SS-1).

40.4 **Bounded ratios.** No Member may receive a distribution exceeding **seven (7) times** the median distribution within their Constituency in a given year, unless adjusted by Special Resolution.

54.1 The Company may be dissolved only by Special Resolution with **unanimous Steward consent**, reflecting a determination that the Company's form no longer serves its Driver.

54.2 **Asset Lock.** On dissolution, after satisfaction of genuine creditors, residual assets shall flow to other FSx-compatible entities serving the same Driver, or to one of:

- A FairShares Labor Association, employee benefit trust, cooperative, or mutual society which has accepted these rules in writing;
- A charitable association, trust, or foundation aligned with the Driver;
- A FairShares User Association, community enterprise, or cooperative society;
- A social investment fund proposed by Directors and approved by Members in General Meeting.

54.3 **No capital contributor, Member, or officer shall receive distribution exceeding the return of invested principal (for Investors) or honest service compensation (for officers).**

54.4 The Asset Lock is **entrenched** and cannot be reversed, weakened, or circumvented by any Resolution or amendment.

### Article 55. Merger and Succession

55.1 The Company may merge with another FSx-compatible body by Special Resolution.

55.2 The Driver, the Stewardship Principles, and the entrenched provisions must be preserved in any merger.

55.3 Succession of the Company's role — through continued operation, merger, or dissolution into compatible successor bodies — is guided by the principle that the Driver is served, whatever form best serves it.

# COMPATIBILITY TESTS

A federated entity is **FSx-compatible** — and thus eligible to sign a Federation Agreement under [[III - The Ecosystem Holding Charter]] — if its Articles affirm each of the following:

1. **Agency independent of any single party.** The entity has full legal personhood; no party owns it; its decisions cannot be reduced to the consent of a single voice or a single owner. (This is the structural test of FSx-ness.)
2. **Driver compatibility.** Its Driver Statement is consistent with, and serves rather than contradicts, the Driver Statement of the (Currently Nameless) Superorganism.
3. **Multi-constituency governance.** Multiple stakeholder Constituencies are represented, with no single Constituency capturing the whole.
4. **Dynamic weighted governance and economic rights.** Voice and economic share are distributed across Constituencies through Weighted Governance Dynamics, Capital Gains Dynamics, and Dividend Sharing Dynamics that may be recalibrated by the entity's own Resolution processes.
5. **Six-capital accounting.** The entity tracks (or commits to phase in) reporting across all six capitals per SCS-1 or compatible standard.
6. **Asset Lock and Purpose Lock.** On dissolution, assets flow to compatible successor entities; resources cannot be redirected from the entity's purpose.
7. **Knowledge Commons contribution.** At least the entity's Articles and adopted Standards are released under CC-BY-SA 4.0 or equivalent.
8. **Member sovereignty within.** The entity's own members retain sovereignty in their own domain, mirroring the superorganism's pattern at the entity's scale.
9. **Nonviolence and transparency.** The entity commits to nonviolence in its relations with other entities and to open, auditable operation.

A Federation Agreement under [[III - The Ecosystem Holding Charter]] confirms compatibility on each test, with any deviation explicitly named and reconciled.

# THE FEDERATION AGREEMENT

A federated FSx Organism at Layer VII typically signs **two coupled Federation Agreements**:

**1. With the Ecosystem Holding Company at Layer III** (see [[III - The Ecosystem Holding Charter]] §3.2 and §4.1) — establishing the entity's governance standing and value-flow integration. This Agreement specifies:

- The entity's Articles (which must be FSx-compatible).
- The entity's Driver Statement (which must be compatible with the superorganism's Driver).
- The entity's Ecosystem Contribution per [[III - The Ecosystem Holding Charter]] §7.7 (default: 1% of revenue + true-up to 10% of profit).
- The entity's Profit Pool contribution per [[III - The Ecosystem Holding Charter]] §8.5 (default: 30% of profit, redistributed back via ergodic pooling).
- The entity's Standards adoption (SS-1, SCS-1, GS-1, DS-1, CS-1, others as applicable).
- Quest participation scope.
- Reciprocal governance terms (representation in Ecosystem Company governance; the Ecosystem Company holding standing in entity governance; the limits of each).
- Exit terms.

**2. With the Ecosystem Resourcing Pool at Layer IV** (see [[IV - The Ecosystem Resourcing Pool Charter]] §10) — establishing the entity's capital-deployment relationship as an Investee Member. This Agreement specifies:

- Confirmation of FSx compatibility for capital deployment.
- The **Sole-Source designation** (default: yes — the entity accepts investment capital exclusively from the Resourcing Pool, structurally insulating both directions).
- Sub-fund(s) from which capital deployment originates and to which returns flow.
- Capital deployment instrument(s): equity, convertible, loan, grant, or multi-capital.
- Six-capital reporting cadence per SCS-1 and RPS-1.
- Investee-Member standing in the Resourcing Pool's Class E.
- Exit terms.

Federation under Layer III is **separate from membership in Layer II**. An entity may participate in [[II - The Stewardship Agreement]] without federating economically; an entity that wishes to federate economically must hold standing in both. Federation under Layer IV (the Resourcing Pool) is the path by which capital flows to the entity.

# ADOPTION PATHS

7.1 Any sovereign entity may adopt this Template, with or without federating into a (Currently Nameless) Superorganism. The pattern is a gift to the Knowledge Commons.

7.2 **Adoption without federation:** an entity adopts FSx-compatible Articles, operates as a sovereign FSx organization, contributes to the Knowledge Commons under CC-BY-SA 4.0, and participates in the broader FSx ecosystem at whatever depth its members choose. No Federation Agreement is required. *Subsequent federation* into a (Currently Nameless) Superorganism's ecosystem (or any other FSx ecosystem) is always available later by signing a Federation Agreement — though it requires negotiating new agreements from scratch, which has historically proved difficult and is where isolated cooperatives typically come up short.

7.3 **Adoption with federation into a (Currently Nameless) Superorganism:** an entity additionally signs a Federation Agreement under [[III - The Ecosystem Holding Charter]] from the beginning, obtaining standing in that Superorganism's Ecosystem Holding Company and joining its Switchboard's value flows. Every entity that federates from the beginning receives a **Dynamic Governance Stake and a Dynamic Value Stake in the whole ecosystem from day one** — not only in its own organization. Sovereign-to-sovereign agreement at every scale, plus a fair share of value generated anywhere in the federated commons. An entity that adopts FSx-compatible Articles in isolation must reach separate agreements to federate later; an entity that federates at the outset has voice and stake from the moment of constitution.

7.4 **Adoption with full participation:** an entity (or its founders) may also sign [[II - The Stewardship Agreement]] (joining the spiritual association) and a Federation Agreement with the [[IV - The Ecosystem Resourcing Pool Charter]] (becoming an Investee Member receiving capital deployment from the Pool's sub-funds), in addition to the economic federation at Layer III.

The Ø + eight layers are woven, not strictly nested, and any combination is separable. A sovereign entity may participate at any layer or combination of layers as fits its purpose; an FSx Organism typically belongs to several Layer V–VI intermediaries at once. **Capital deployment, however, flows through Layer IV as a structural rule — the FSx Organism that wants investment capital from the ecosystem receives it through the Resourcing Pool, not directly from individual investors.**
