## After the Founding

You have constituted the entity. You have signed the Federation Agreement. The Articles are filed. Now actual work begins — and now the question shifts from *what are we agreeing to* to *how do we actually do this together, day by day*.

This Part is the practitioner's most-often-asked-about layer: what about money, what about roles, what about how decisions actually flow, what about the messy week-to-week reality of running an entity that is neither a conventional company nor a charity nor a co-op nor a household nor any other inherited form alone.

The good news: the answers are workable, even simple, when you hold the pattern. Most of what follows is the unfolding of two ideas — *agreement, not ownership* and *flow, not enclosure* — into the practical mechanics of working life.

## The No Owner, No Employee, Zero Blame Co-Working Agreement

A workable opening agreement for a small operating circle:

> *There are no guarantees. There are no owners. There are no bosses. There is no one to blame. There are only mutually agreed roles and responsibilities entered into via the voluntary agreement of sovereign legal persons.*

Reading this carefully: every word of it is doing work.

- **No guarantees.** The pattern does not promise anyone a paycheck, a return, a seat, or success. It promises mutual agreement and good-faith collaboration.
- **No owners.** Per the Asset Lock and the Stewardship Principle of *federation, not ownership*, no person owns the entity. The FSx Individual that energizes a role does not own it; the role does not own them.
- **No bosses.** There are roles, and there is mutual accountability within them — but there is no boss-as-such. Authority within roles flows from the agreements made, and is bounded by them.
- **No one to blame.** Zero blame environment. Universal responsibility and accountability. When something goes wrong, the question is what the system needs to learn, not whose head goes on the spike.
- **Mutually agreed roles and responsibilities entered into via voluntary agreement of sovereign legal persons.** The functional unit of accountability is the **role**, agreed between FSx Members, with the role itself being a thing energized by the avatar of one or more FSx Individual.

> *We are co-operating this up together. We are measuring our time via the Galactic TEA Bank. We understand that there is no "them" "out there" to guarantee our success or security. We understand that monetary compensation will only be available as we co-create it and it filters through the Value Pools. As we produce Value together, we will wisely distribute it. We intend to care for one another. We understand we all need to survive. As rapidly as possible and wise, we aim to co-create abundant flow internally by co-creating abundant value and flow for the good of All.*

Some circles will sign a written Co-Working Agreement that names this explicitly. Some circles will hold it as the implicit basis of every subsequent decision. Either is fine. The agreement is what matters; the document is its echo.

## Roles, Functional Accountability, and Size of Role

The pattern works through **roles**, not job descriptions. A role is a defined function with a clear scope, an expected output, and a mutually agreed interface with adjacent roles. A FSx Individual may energize multiple roles; a single role may be energized by multiple FSx Individual in coordination.

The body holds three load-bearing concepts together: **size of role**, **size of person**, and how an omni-federation **auto-nominates** the right people into the right roles.

### Size of Role and Size of Person

Each role has a **size** — the level of consciousness, complexity, and care required to hold it well. Each person (or FSx Individual) has a **size** — the level of consciousness and competence they can presently bring to bear. The body's working principle:

> **Size of person should match size of role within roughly ±10%.**

A role much larger than the available person produces overwhelm, hidden failure, or burnout — and the work suffers. A role much smaller than the available person produces frustration, atrophy, and disengagement — and the person suffers. Both are forms of waste the body actively works against.

A useful scale (1–10 for simplicity):

- **Level 1 roles** — well-defined, repeatable, low novelty. Can be held by anyone competent at the task.
- **Level 5 roles** — significant ambiguity, multiple stakeholders, real consequences for misjudgment. Requires established adult capacity to hold complexity and uncertainty.
- **Level 10 roles** — wicked complexity, long time horizons, high stakes for the whole, irreducible novelty. Requires the highest available consciousness, competence, and care.

Most starting circles are working at levels 3–6. Stewardship roles run higher. Operating-entity Driver-holders, Layer IV sub-fund Stewards, and Federation-Node coordinators typically run at 7+. Match the size of role to the size of person, and provide the Mentor / Council of Peers relationships that let people **grow into** larger roles over seasons rather than being thrown into them unprepared.

### How an Omni-Federated Body Auto-Nominates the Right People to the Right Roles

A claim worth pausing on: **a federation that is structured rightly does not need a central authority to assign roles**. The pattern itself, at scale, tends to put the right people into the right roles by emergent process. The mechanisms:

- **TEA Ledger transparency.** Every Member's accumulated Time, Energy, and Attention contributions across roles are visible across the federation (with appropriate privacy at individual scale). Contribution patterns reveal where each person has *already* shown up over time. Roles tend to flow toward those with track-record alignment.
- **Mentor-and-Council vouching.** Larger roles require nomination supported by Mentors / Council of Peers who know the candidate developmentally. The vouching is a constitutive constraint on role assignment — no one is dropped into a level-9 role without the people who know them deeply having said *yes, this is appropriate*.
- **Reciprocal federation visibility.** A FSx Individual federated across multiple operating entities (multiple Circles, multiple Layer VII companies) accrues a multi-dimensional record of competence and care. The federation can see, across the body, who has demonstrated capacity in which kinds of roles.
- **Consent at every level.** No role is filled by appointment alone. The person consents to take it; the relevant Constituencies consent to their taking it; the Mentors / Council consent. Three voices at minimum, often more.
- **Founding-Phase Stewardship developmental thresholds** ([[III - The Ecosystem Holding Charter]] §4.2) — Laske DTF 30+, Kegan 4(5)+, ten years of practice in a recognized lineage, five years of tested service in a role with real stakes — these are not gates against persons; they are *truthful descriptions* of what the highest-consciousness roles in the federation actually need. The body does not pretend that everyone is equally prepared for level-10 roles.

This emergent pattern is more reliable, more flexible, and more resistant to capture than any centralized HR department. It is also more demanding of practitioners — because it depends on the **continuous developmental practice** described in Chapter 1. Roles cannot flow rightly if practice is absent.

### The Functional Accountability Chart

Map the roles needed for the entity to do its work, and the relationships between them. This is *not* an org chart in the conventional sense — there is no above-and-below. It is a map of who has agreed to be accountable for what, and how the roles depend on one another. The chart is **living** — it evolves as the entity learns what work it actually needs to do.

The chart for each FSx records, for each role:

- **Role name and scope** — what this role exists to hold.
- **Size of role** (1–10).
- **Energizing party** — the FSx Individual (or sometimes multiple) currently holding the role.
- **Adjacent interfaces** — which roles this role depends on, and which roles depend on it.
- **Mentor / Council vouching** — who has consented to support the role-holder developmentally in this work.

The chart is reviewed at each Annual General Meeting and at significant transitions. It is not secret. It is part of the body's transparency commitment.

A small starting circle might have:

- A **Driver-holder** role (someone who carries the active expression of the Driver into operations)
- An **Outward-facing** role (relationships, communication, partnerships)
- A **Resource-flow** role (the Switchboard interface, accounting, value-pool stewardship)
- A **Inner-life** role (culture, conflict, mentorship, rhythm-keeping)
- A **Production** role (whatever the entity actually makes or stewards)

Larger entities elaborate the chart. Smaller entities collapse roles into single people. Both are fine.

### Dojo Practice at the Collective Scale

The dojo and safe-sparring practices named at individual scale in [[1 - Becoming - The Sovereign Individual]] apply at the collective scale too. A Circle, an operating entity, a sub-fund, the Resourcing Pool, the Holding Company — each is a **collective practice space** for the body, and each benefits from a regular dojo rhythm.

What this looks like in practice:

- **Weekly Circle gatherings** with structured time for honest reporting, sparring on real tensions, integrating Objections, and collective Working from Beyond on whatever is hardest.
- **Monthly retrospective** on what worked, what did not, what the body is being asked to learn.
- **Quarterly omnispection** at each solar cardinal — see Chapter 5.
- **Annual** full review of the Functional Accountability Chart, role-fit, developmental progress of each Member, and Driver-alignment.

Dojo at collective scale is what makes the body **adaptive**. Without it, the FSx Articles will hold the form but the substance will quietly drift. With it, the body learns its way through whatever the years bring.

## TEA — Time, Energy, Attention

A core practitioner habit is to track what each Member is investing of themselves into the entity. The unit of account is **TEA** — Time, Energy, and Attention — measured per role, weighted by size of role.

A simple calculation:

> *Points contributed = (% of TEA dedicated to role) × (size of role on a 1–10 scale)*

Examples:

- 100% TEA on a Level 1 role = 100 points / day
- 100% TEA on a Level 10 role = 1,000 points / day
- 50% TEA on a Level 5 role = 250 points / day

These points are logged in the entity's **TEA Ledger** (sometimes called the "Galactic TEA Bank" in lineage practice — keep the playful name or rename to taste; the function is what matters). In the LIØNSBERG cross-vault tradition, the same time-times-size-of-role unit is referred to as **LIØNSBERG Units of Value (LUV)**; TEA points and LUV are kindred and operationally equivalent — federated entities may use either name. The Ledger is auditable by all Members. It is the working record of who has contributed what to the entity over time.

**Why bother measuring this?**

- It makes contribution **visible** in a way that prevents resentment and free-riding without resorting to surveillance.
- It feeds the **Within-Constituency distribution protocols** of the Switchboard Standard (SS-1) — when surplus is distributed within a Constituency (e.g., Class C Ecosystem-Individual FSx Members, where individuals' contribution-weight is at issue), the contribution-weight portion is calculated from accumulated TEA. The same logic applies within Class A (Stewardship) and Class E (Collaborator-Individual FSx). Within Class B and D (organizations), TEA is contributed by the individuals working within those organizations, who are themselves FSx Individual in their own right.
- It allows **internal economic exchange** to happen in TEA points before any conversion to fiat is necessary. *Whenever possible, participants secure everything they need internally using as much internal currency from the TEA Ledger as possible.* Conversion to fiat is the last resort, not the default.
- It provides a **portable record** of contribution that travels with the FSx Individual across federated entities.

Commitments made and kept should be measured in TEA points. Promises broken without integration via the conflict-transformation process degrade trust and may lead to Member review.

## Getting in Flow: Value In, Value Out

The first order of business in working together is to **get in motion** — to produce value for others and for the whole, and to receive value flows in turn. The pattern recognizes several inflows and several outflows, and offers a default ordering for distributing what comes in.

### Inflows (Sources of Value)

- **Revenues** — value produced and exchanged for compensation in the wider economy.
- **Grants** — third-party funding aligned with the Driver.
- **Donations** — gifts received from those who wish to support the work.
- **Superorganism Allocations** — flows from the Ecosystem Holding Company through the Switchboard, including from the Ecosystem Fund (§7.5 of the Charter) and Developmental Infrastructure Funding (§7.6).
- **Member Contributions** — capital, in-kind, or TEA contributions from Members.
- **Reinvestments** — surpluses Members elect to leave in the entity rather than receiving as distribution.

All inflows enter the **Entry Pool**, where they are accumulated for distribution down the waterfall.

### The Waterfall — Fourteen Pools

When the entity is ready to distribute, value flows down a prioritized waterfall. **Each pool is satisfied before any surplus flows to the next.** The defaults below are starting points; each FSx adjusts them by Resolution as needed and as it learns its own homeostasis.

The waterfall has three movements: **off-the-top** (Pool 0, paid as revenue arrives); **operations and pay** (Pools 1–7, the day-to-day running of the entity); and **period-close settlement** (Pools 8–13, settled at accounting period close on a profit basis).

#### Pool 0 — Ecosystem Contribution: Revenue Base (1% of revenue, off-the-top)

The first cut of revenue, paid continuously as revenue arrives, flows to the Ecosystem Holding Company per [[III - The Ecosystem Holding Charter]] §7.7(a). One percent (1%) of revenue, denominated in revenue rather than profit so that pre-profit and non-profit federated entities contribute on the same footing. This is the federated entity's **base ecosystem dues** — strengthening the commons regardless of profitability. *Remaining flows to Pool 1.*

#### Pool 1 — Basic Operating Expenses
Space, utilities, water, general administration, baseline overhead. *Surplus flows to Pool 2.*

#### Pool 2 — Key Trade Partners
Suppliers, contractors, and partners whose continued resourcing is critical to operations. Resourcing those who resource us is upstream of resourcing ourselves. *Surplus flows to Pool 3.*

#### Pool 3 — Co-Worker Baseline Downpayment
The minimum each Member needs to get through the period (week, fortnight, month) — survival floor. Not a salary; a baseline. *Surplus flows to Pool 4.*

#### Pool 4 — All Vendors
Commitments made to other parties that need to be kept before discretionary distribution. *Surplus flows to Pool 5.*

#### Pool 5 — R&D, Learning, Security Nets, Reserves
Long-term needs invested in consistently. Maintaining six months of operating reserves is a workable target in uncertain environments. *Surplus flows to Pool 6.*

#### Pool 6 — Investor Baseline Allocation
Capped at the Investor Constituency terms in IS-1 (default: 7% maximum return). 4.2% as a working norm in stable conditions; 0% if necessary. *Surplus flows to Pool 7.*

#### Pool 7 — Co-Worker Balances to Fair Local Living Standards
Internal distributions that bring Members up to fair local standards of living. Distinct from Pool 3 (survival floor) — this is the level at which Members can live with dignity in their context. *Surplus flows to Pool 8.*

*[At period close — profit is calculated. Pools 8 and 9 are the ecosystem flows that settle at period close. They are the principal mechanism by which the federated entity participates in the FSx ecosystem economically.]*

#### Pool 8 — Ecosystem Contribution: Profit True-Up

Per [[III - The Ecosystem Holding Charter]] §7.7(b), at period close the federated entity tops up the Ecosystem Contribution to **10% of profit** when 10% of profit exceeds the cumulative Pool 0 contributions paid for the period. The combined Ecosystem Contribution (Pool 0 + Pool 8) therefore equals the greater of 1% of revenue or 10% of profit. *Surplus flows to Pool 9.*

#### Pool 9 — Profit Pool Contribution (Ergodic Redistribution)

Per [[III - The Ecosystem Holding Charter]] §8.5, the federated entity contributes **30% of profit** to the Ecosystem Profit Pool. This is a redistribution mechanism, not a fee: the Profit Pool is immediately redistributed back across all federated Members per the Dividend Sharing Dynamics (II §8.1) × within-Constituency TEA. **The federated entity therefore receives back its share of the Profit Pool**, which may be greater or lesser than its 30% contribution depending on the ecosystem's overall conditions. The entity's *net* ecosystem-wide flow tends toward equalization across the federation over time — this is the **ergodic pooling** wisdom: pooling reduces variance and improves long-run outcomes for ensemble participants. *The remaining 70% of profit flows to Pool 10.*

#### Pool 10 — Downstream Impact Investment

Recommended baseline (typically **5–10% of profit**) to entities, causes, and initiatives **outside the federated ecosystem** that the entity wishes to resource out of its abundance. Distinct from Pools 0, 8, and 9, which are flows *inside* the ecosystem. Pool 10 is the entity's contribution to causes beyond the federation — community foundations, traditional philanthropy, mutual aid networks, watershed groups not yet federated, and the like. *Surplus flows to Pool 11.*

#### Pool 11 — Legacy Pool
Distributions to Members against their Day-One Legacy Pool entries (see Part II), made pro-rata so that all Members are made whole at the same time. A small interest rate on outstanding balances may be approximate. *Surplus flows to Pool 12.*

#### Pool 12 — Discretionary Pool
Before stakeholder bonuses, what Pools above might want to be strengthened or invested in further? Counter-cyclical reinvestment, anticipatory capacity-building. *Surplus flows to Pool 13.*

#### Pool 13 — Stakeholder Bonuses
If surplus remains after all prior Pools, distribute across stakeholder Constituencies per the Dividend Sharing Dynamics in Article 40 of [[VII - The FSx Organism Template]]. This is the entity's **internal Dividend Sharing** — the 70% of profit (after Pool 9) being distributed among the Constituencies of the entity itself. The user experience: each Member, across the federation, receives a flow that is **70% from the entity's own Dividend Sharing Dynamics + their share of the ecosystem-wide Profit Pool redistribution from Pool 9**. The math is: 70% of profit on the FSx(s) you contribute to directly + 30% of your flow based on the success of the whole.

### Pool Caps and Homeostasis

Most Pools should have **caps** that are adjusted to keep each Pool in *homeostasis* as the entity grows. Examples:

- 6 months of operating reserves at Pool 5 in uncertain environments.
- 30% of profits cap to stakeholder bonuses at Pool 13.
- General 50% reinvestment into strength and growth of capacity (across Pools 5, 10, 12).

When a Pool is at its cap, surplus continues flowing to the next Pool. Caps are recalibrated annually or as the entity learns.

### Why Two Distinct Ecosystem Flows (Pools 0+8 vs. Pool 9)

A common practitioner question: *why distinguish the Ecosystem Contribution (Pools 0 + 8) from the Profit Pool (Pool 9)?* They both flow up to the Ecosystem Holding Company.

The reason: they do **different work**. The Ecosystem Contribution **funds the ecosystem itself** — the Ecosystem Fund (II §7.5), Developmental Infrastructure Funding (II §7.6), Ecosystem Company operations, the strategic initiatives the ecosystem undertakes as a whole. The Profit Pool is a **redistribution mechanism** — value flows up and immediately flows back down to federated Members on an ergodic basis.

Distinguishing them:

- **Ecosystem Contribution** stays at the ecosystem level (after dividend-sharing of any net surplus per II §8.1). It is "ecosystem dues."
- **Profit Pool** flows through the ecosystem level back to federated Members. It is "ergodic equalization."

Both are part of being federated. Their effects are complementary: the Ecosystem Contribution makes the ecosystem strong and capable; the Profit Pool makes every federated Member more resilient than they would be alone.

### Reinvestment

Members of any Pool who do not need or want a scheduled distribution may **forgo** the distribution and reinvest it back into the entity to strengthen other needs. All such reinvestments are tracked and added to the Investment Capital balance of the Member.

## Internal Service Providers

> *Anytime an organization finds itself having to contract externally for services, it should encourage the members of the broader service organism to stand up an Internal Service Provider.*

When multiple federated entities share a need (legal counsel, accounting, design, technology, hospitality, transport, learning facilitation, conflict mediation, etc.), the answer is rarely *each entity contracts the service externally* and rarely *each entity builds the capacity in-house*. The pattern's answer is to **stand up a federated FSx Internal Service Provider** that serves the broader ecosystem.

This solves several issues at once:

- The cost of the service is shared across multiple entities, reducing the burden on each.
- Value generated stays inside the federated commons rather than flowing out to extractive providers.
- The provider organization itself is FSx-compatible and federated, so it is held to the same standards as the entities it serves.
- Capacity grows where it is most leveraged.

A young ecosystem will, over its first few years, accumulate a portfolio of Internal Service Providers across the most common needs. This is a healthy sign — and a pattern to actively cultivate.

## Centers of Abundance and Dynamic Distribution

The aim is not for any one entity to maximize its own retained surplus. The aim is for each FSx to **become a center of abundance** as quickly as possible, so that each one of the all becomes a generous source of distribution and flow beyond itself.

> *Each Fractal Sovereignty has more than enough as quickly as possible so that each one of the all becomes generous centers of abundance, distribution, and flow beyond themselves. Each center creating as much value as possible, using as much as they need, and distributing the rest of the flow back into the ecosystem.*

This reverses the conventional optimization. The conventional firm hoards what it can; the FSx releases what it can, trusting that releasing creates more flow than hoarding does. This is a testable hypothesis, not a moral assertion. The pattern's case is that, over time, federated centers of abundance generate more value for everyone than competitive accumulators do. Practice will demonstrate this, or the pattern needs to learn.
