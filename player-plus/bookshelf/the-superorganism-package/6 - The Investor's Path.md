# 6 - The Investor's Path

*For those bringing capital to the New Earth Operating System. The Ecosystem Resourcing Pool as the federation's internal bank. Capped returns and what they actually mean. The cross-fund ergodic pool. One-degree structural protection in both directions. Exit terms. The investor as a federated member.*

---

## Why a Different Path

If you are arriving with capital — your own, or capital you steward as a fiduciary — and want to deploy it in service of the regeneration of Earth and the harmonization of all generations of life, this Part is for you.

You are arriving into a system that is structurally different from venture capital, private equity, conventional impact investing, family office direct-investing, or philanthropic endowments. The differences are not cosmetic. They are constitutional.

A short list of what is different:

- **No ownership.** You will not own anything in the federation. You will hold standing as a Member, with capped returns, governance voice, and the protections of the Asset Lock and Purpose Lock at every layer.
- **Capped returns.** The default cap is **7% per investor per year** across your aggregate sub-fund holdings — not per fund, not on individual investments, but across your total federated capital. Returns above the cap flow to other Constituencies (Investee-Company, Sub-Fund, Living Systems, Society, Stewardship). This is not a tax; it is a structural commitment to non-extraction.
- **Capital flows through the Pool.** You do not invest directly in operating companies. You place capital with the **Ecosystem Resourcing Pool** at Layer IV, which holds your capital in one or more sub-funds and deploys it across federated operating entities at Layer VII. This is **one-degree structural separation**, and it protects both you and the operating entities.
- **Cross-fund ergodic pooling.** Sub-funds within the Resourcing Pool share a portion of returns with each other (default 30% via the Cross-Fund Ergodic Pool). Your fund's outcome is partly buffered by other sub-funds' outcomes. Variance is reduced. Long-run flourishing is structurally favored over individual maximization.
- **Multi-capital reporting.** Returns are reported in all six capitals (Natural, Individual, Social, Intellectual, Manufactured, Financial), not financial alone. An investee that produces strong Natural and Social capital outcomes alongside modest Financial returns is honored as a high-performing investment under SCS-1.
- **Voice that is not vote.** You will have governance standing in both the Holding Company (Layer III) and the Resourcing Pool (Layer IV), but you will not have controlling votes at either. The federation is sovereign-to-sovereign throughout; the Threshold of Federation prevents capital from concentrating control.

The deepest difference: you are participating in a **regenerative covenant**, not a financial product. Your returns are capped not because the system is failing, but because the system is succeeding — surplus value flows to the operating entities and constituencies doing the actual work of regeneration, with you as one valued participant rather than the primary beneficiary.

## The One-Degree Structural Protection

This is the architectural insight that makes the model work, and it is worth pausing on:

> **You are insulated by the Pool from any single operating entity's specific risk. The operating entities are insulated by the Pool from your direct pressure or extraction. Both sides are safer.**

Concretely:

- **You are safer** because your capital is deployed across an ergodically-pooled portfolio of FSx-compatible operating entities, with cross-fund pooling buffering variance further. No single entity's failure exposes you. The capped return is the price of the diversification and protection.
- **Operating entities are safer** because they receive capital from a multi-stakeholder, capped-return, mission-aligned source rather than from individuals who may apply pressure for outsized returns, control, or premature exit. They can do their work in service of the Driver without being whipsawed by individual investor preferences.
- **The federation is safer** because no operating entity is exposed to enclosure, capture, or hostile takeover via concentrated investor pressure. The Threshold of Federation and the Sole-Source Designation work together to prevent that vector.

This is why the Resourcing Pool is the **primary** — and, by default Federation Agreement, the **sole** — capital source for federated operating entities. Capital flows through the Pool, not around it. (See [[IV - The Ecosystem Resourcing Pool Charter]] §3.3 and §10.2.)

If you have a preferred operating entity you'd like your capital to support specifically, the path is to deploy through a sub-fund whose mandate covers that entity, or to propose a new sub-fund whose mandate names it. Direct investment in operating entities is structurally prevented in the federated model — for everyone's protection.

## The Investor's Journey

A workable sequence for an individual investor (institutional investors follow the same arc with appropriate adaptations):

### 1. Constitute your FSx Individual

You enter as your **FSx Individual** — your legal-person avatar in the federation, distinct from your flesh-and-blood self. Per [[VIII - The FSx Individual Template]], this requires the Charter to be signed with at least two **Mentors** or a **Council of Peers** (the multi-voice constitutive requirement). Capital decisions of significance require Mentor consent.

For institutional investors (Class D Investor-Company Members), the analog is constituting your institution in FSx-compatible Articles per [[VII - The FSx Organism Template]] — multi-voice governance, no ownership, agency independent of any single party.

### 2. Sign the Stewardship Agreement at Layer II

Join the spiritual association per [[II - The Stewardship Agreement]] §17, with a sponsoring Member. You are a Member of the body before you are a financial participant in the economy.

### 3. Sign a Federation Agreement with the Resourcing Pool

Per [[IV - The Ecosystem Resourcing Pool Charter]] §9.2, your Federation Agreement specifies:

- Capital amount and capital commitment period.
- Sub-fund placement (one or more — bioregional, sectoral, Quest-based, vintage, strategic).
- Capped return (default 7% per year, adjustable by mutual agreement subject to the IS-1 cap).
- Voluntary subordination, if any (longer commitment, lower priority, in exchange for greater impact alignment).
- Reporting cadence and detail.
- Exit terms.

You become a Member in Class C (Investor-Individual FSx) or Class D (Investor-Company) of the Resourcing Pool, with corresponding governance standing.

### 4. Optionally, sign a Federation Agreement with the Ecosystem Holding Company

Class I (Investor) standing at Layer III per [[III - The Ecosystem Holding Charter]] §4.1 gives you governance voice in the Holding Company itself, separate from your capital placement. Most investors will sign both Layer III and Layer IV Federation Agreements; some may sign only the Resourcing Pool Federation Agreement and remain Layer-I-only at the Holding Company level.

### 5. Place capital. Deploy. Report. Live in the rhythm.

Your capital enters the sub-fund(s) you've selected. The sub-fund deploys to federated operating entities at Layer VII per its Investment Mandate. Returns flow back per Capital Gains Dynamics, Dividend Sharing Dynamics, and Cross-Fund Ergodic Pool redistribution, capped at IS-1's per-investor maximum. Reporting reaches you per the cadence in your Federation Agreement, in all six capitals.

You participate in Annual General Meetings of the Holding Company and the Resourcing Pool at Summer Solstice. You witness the federation's growth across seasons. You may choose to add capital, redirect across sub-funds, withdraw at the agreed cadence, or remain placed across long arcs.

## What "Capped Returns" Actually Means in Practice

The 7% cap is a structural commitment to non-extraction. In practice:

- **Where the federation generates more than 7% return on your capital**, the excess flows to other Constituencies (Investee-Company, Sub-Fund, Living Systems, Society, Stewardship, Donor pass-through, etc.) per the Resourcing Pool's Capital Gains Dynamics at §4.4 and Dividend Sharing Dynamics under §8 of the same Charter.
- **Where the federation generates less than 7%**, you receive what is generated, plus the Cross-Fund Ergodic Pool's buffering. The cap is a ceiling, not a floor — there is no guarantee.
- **You may voluntarily subordinate** to a lower cap, longer commitment, or different priority in exchange for impact alignment, sub-fund mandate, or other non-financial considerations. This is honored per Federation Agreement.
- **Aggregation across sub-funds** prevents the cap being gamed by spreading capital. The cap applies to your total federated holdings, not per fund.
- **Asset Lock applies.** You may receive (a) return of invested principal plus (b) capped returns. You may not receive distributions exceeding these. On withdrawal or death, principal is returned per exit provisions; any capital you've committed under your Personal Asset Lock (per [[VIII - The FSx Individual Template]] §F) flows to FSx-compatible bodies serving your Self-Driver.

The 7% cap is the ceiling at which **mission alignment outranks return maximization**. If you find this cap unacceptable, the federation is not your fit — and that is honored. There are many vehicles in the world that maximize returns. The federation is structurally not one of them.

## The Cross-Fund Ergodic Pool

This is the structural innovation that enacts *The Ergodic Investor*'s wisdom at the fund-of-funds scale.

Each sub-fund within the Resourcing Pool contributes a default **30% of periodic returns** (Capital Gains plus distributable Surplus) to the Cross-Fund Ergodic Pool. The Ergodic Pool is then **redistributed across all sub-funds in the same period** — partly equal-weighted, partly weighted by contribution / impact / strategic priority per RPS-1 (the Resourcing Pool Standard).

Why this matters mathematically:

- For non-ergodic processes such as capital allocation, **ensemble pooling reduces variance and improves long-run outcomes for ensemble participants**. This is a precise mathematical claim, not a sentiment.
- Each sub-fund participates in the long-run flourishing of the whole, not only its own performance. A struggling fund is buffered; a thriving fund contributes more than it receives. Over time, the mechanism dampens variance without erasing distinctive performance.
- **For investors**, this means: even if you place capital in one sub-fund, your effective portfolio includes a partial slice of every other sub-fund's outcome. Diversification beyond the diversification you actively chose.
- **For sub-funds**, this means: federation is a real risk-sharing covenant, not just a brand affiliation. Sub-funds federate knowing they are both contributors and beneficiaries.

The 30% rate may be adjusted by Special Resolution. Sub-funds with explicitly higher-volatility mandates (crisis-response, frontier-research) may agree to higher contribution rates as a condition of federation; sub-funds with explicitly lower-volatility mandates (long-horizon strategic) may agree to lower rates.

The Ergodic Pool is **not a tax** and **not a subsidy**. It is a **risk-sharing covenant** among federated sub-funds, applied at the architectural level, with mathematics behind it.

## What You Receive by Federating

By signing the Federation Agreements and placing capital, you receive:

- **Capped returns** per IS-1 (default 7% per year aggregate).
- **A diversified portfolio** across the sub-fund(s) you've chosen, ergodically pooled with all other sub-funds.
- **Multi-capital reporting** in all six capitals — your Natural, Individual, Social, Intellectual, Manufactured, and Financial returns, separately tracked.
- **Governance voice** in both the Holding Company (Layer III) and the Resourcing Pool (Layer IV), at the weights specified in §5 of each Charter.
- **Membership in the spiritual association** at Layer II.
- **Participation in Quests** that align with your interests, where you may bring not just capital but knowledge, relationships, mentorship.
- **The Knowledge Commons** — full access to the federation's intellectual contributions under CC-BY-SA 4.0.
- **Internal Service Providers** as the ecosystem matures — legal, accounting, advisory services at internal-economy rates.
- **The FSx breakthrough**: your capital is part of an ecosystem that has dynamic governance and economic stake in itself from day one — not an island that has to negotiate every relationship from scratch.

## Exit Terms

You retain the right to exit at any time, per the terms in your Federation Agreement. In practice:

- **Notice periods and redemption windows** are specified in IS-1 and per sub-fund. Most sub-funds use quarterly or annual redemption windows, with longer notice for larger withdrawals to avoid destabilizing operating entities mid-deployment.
- **Principal is returned**, less any losses booked at the sub-fund level, plus your share of cumulative capped returns.
- **Asset Lock applies.** Capital you have committed under your Personal Asset Lock (the portion of your capital you have committed to commons-strengthening purposes per [[VIII - The FSx Individual Template]] §F) does not return to you. It flows to FSx-compatible bodies serving your Self-Driver upon your death or formal release.
- **Inter-fund mobility.** You may move capital between sub-funds within the Pool by signing a sub-fund Transfer Agreement. This does not constitute withdrawal.
- **Exit is not betrayal.** Forcing yourself to remain federated when continued federation harms your work or your people would be the betrayal. Where exit is in response to harm or value misalignment, the conflict-transformation provisions at [[II - The Stewardship Agreement]] §13 apply.

## For Institutional Investors

If you are deploying institutional capital — a foundation, an endowment, a mission-aligned fund, a family office — the path is the same with adaptations:

- **Constitute as Class D (Investor-Company)** rather than Class C (Investor-Individual FSx).
- **Your institution must itself be FSx-compatible** to federate. Many foundations and family offices are nearly compatible already (multi-voice governance, asset locks, mission constraints); some require restructuring. The federation does not accept owner-controlled institutional investors, since this would re-introduce extraction at one remove.
- **Larger commitments** typically use vintage sub-funds (Year-1 cohort, Year-2 cohort, etc.) for orderly capital deployment alongside other institutional investors.
- **Strategic Ecosystem sub-fund** (the migrated Ecosystem Fund per [[III - The Ecosystem Holding Charter]] §7.5) may be appropriate for institutional capital with the broadest mandate.
- **Reporting** can be customized per Federation Agreement, including consolidated reporting that integrates with the institution's existing reporting frameworks.

If your institution's mandate cannot accept capped returns or asset locks, the federation is not an investment fit. Consider Layer II membership only — participation in the spiritual association and the Knowledge Commons without economic federation — as a meaningful alternative.

## The Investor as a Federated Member

A theme worth ending on: in this architecture, you are not an *outside* investor putting capital into an organization that you remain external to. You are a **federated Member of a body that includes you**.

That changes the nature of the relationship. You are not extracting; you are participating. You are not optimizing your return; you are participating in the ecosystem's flourishing. You are not exiting at the maximum; you are exiting (or remaining) per a covenant you entered freely.

This is a different mode of being a person-with-capital. Not all who hold capital will find this fit them. Those who do will find that what they receive — capped returns, multi-capital outcomes, ecosystem voice, structural protection, the participation in something larger than any one investor's return — is, on the long view, more than what conventional models offer.

The investors who thrive in this model are those for whom capital is a means of contribution, not a measure of self. The federation will hold that contribution with care.
