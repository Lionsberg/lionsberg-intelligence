# 2 - Coming Together - The Constituting Circle

*When two or more gather. The opening agreement. Discerning the Driver. The simplest possible form. Founding circles of three to thirteen. Constituting the FSx legal person. Federating everything from the beginning.*

---

## The Move Outward

There is an arc to becoming. First the inner work (Part I). Then the move outward, toward others, toward shared work, toward something that has the chance to be *more than the sum of us*. This Part is about that move.

> *When two or more gather and we come together to co-create — what is needed and what is offered (internally and externally), and what are the wise right next steps?*

This Part is for circles already forming, founding teams, families considering how to constitute the household side of their life, communities of practice ready to formalize. The work is similar at every scale.

---

## Why Three to Thirteen

Most of human time has been organized in small holons of three to thirteen people, with a sweet spot around four to seven. Below three, agency is too concentrated; above thirteen, intimate trust and teamwork frays. Most of us will participate in several small holons at a time over the course of a life — household, work circle, learning circle, neighborhood, council of friends.

The first organizational scale of the FSx fractal is the **Circle**: three to thirteen sovereigns committed to shared learning, work, or Quest. Practical guidance:

- **Three** is the smallest stable circle. Two can collapse into a dyad; three can hold tension.
- **Four to seven** is the practical working size for most circles — small enough for everyone to contribute and be heard, large enough for diverse perspective.
- **Eight to thirteen** stretches the circle but remains workable with clear roles and rhythms.
- **Above thirteen**, you are no longer in a circle — you are in something else, and probably need to subdivide into linked circles and restructure as a larger holon.

Most circles will choose to bond with other circles to become *communities of friends* (Section §6.2 of the lineage tradition). In scenarios of collapse and re-villaging, communities will choose to form commons, pool resources, and revillage themselves into shared physical spaces. This is the shape of the larger pattern. But it begins, again, with the Circle.

## The Opening Agreement

Before the legal-person paperwork, before the bank accounts, before the websites, there is an **opening agreement** — a substantive conversation among the people considering coming together. The opening agreement is not signed; it is *reached*. Without it, everything that follows is fragile.

The opening agreement asks roughly these questions:

1. **What is being called forth, and why?** What is the Driver — the higher purpose transcendent of self-interest — that is impelling us to co-create something that makes our lives and world better? Is there genuine coherence on what we are gathering for?
2. **What are our fundamental values?** Truth. Love. Justice. Stewardship. Wisdom. The Golden Rule. Service to Life and Source. Do No Harm. Love ONE, One Another, and All. Foundational ground of conduct that we can name simply and live by.
3. **What does this co-create, in the world?** What changes if we succeed? What value flows are we hoping to generate, and to whom?
4. **What is the means-as-ends commitment?** What is the way of being together that we are practicing *now*, not only what we hope to produce later? Because the means are the ends. This is about embodiment, not just outcomes.
5. **Are we agreed that the entity that emerges through us has its own agency, independent of us?** Or are we trying to build something we will keep controlling? If we try to own or control it, it is not an FSx and cannot federate. 
6. **Are we agreed to federate everything?** Or are we trying to emerge in isolation? Isolation may be suicidal at this scale and at this hour.
7. **Are we starting from love and abundance, or from fear and scarcity?** This affects every decision. The pattern presupposes the former.

Spending three or four sessions on the opening agreement before any legal work is normal. Spending none of them is a recipe for an organization that becomes unworkable in its first six months.

## Discerning the Driver

The **Driver Statement** is the entrenched purpose of any FSx — the first and last reference for every decision, the thing the entity exists to serve, the thing that does not change even as everything else evolves.

A wise Driver Statement is:

- **Grounded in service** to something larger than the founders' self-interest.
- **Specific enough** to make decisions actually different (vs. a generic "make the world better").
- **Open enough** to allow strategic and operational evolution without amendment.
- **Compatible with** the (Currently Nameless) Superorganism's Driver if you intend to federate (see [[Appendix A - The Driver Statement]] and [[II - The Stewardship Agreement]] §2.3).

A useful exercise: each founder writes a draft Driver Statement separately, in their own words, then the circle compares and weaves them. The shared draft will usually surface within an hour or two. Refine it across two or three sessions, separated by sleep. Then commit it as the entrenched purpose of the entity to be constituted.

The Driver is hard to change later (Entrenched Resolution tier — 85% supermajority, unanimous Steward consent, one-year reflection period). Get this one mostly right.

## The Simplest Possible Form

> *What are the basic needs and minimum requirements? What is good enough for now, safe enough to try, that we can take today? How do we avoid becoming paralyzed by projections into past or future fears or needs?*

The temptation, when constituting something new, is to try to design for every contingency at the start. Resist this. The pattern is *designed to evolve* — sovereignty and agency are protected at every scale precisely so the DNA of the emerging organism can change its shape, forms, systems, and structures over time as needed.

What is **load-bearing** at the opening:

- A clear Driver Statement (entrenched).
- Mutual recognition among founders that the entity has its own agency (no one owns it).
- A small set of **opening principles** — drawn from the Stewardship Principles at [[II - The Stewardship Agreement]] §16 and from your tradition.
- A working agreement on **how decisions are made** (typically: IDM with weighted-governance fallback as you scale; one-voice while you're three to seven).
- A working agreement on **how value flows** in and out (see Part III).
- An agreement to **federate**, not to emerge in isolation.

What is **not** load-bearing at the opening, and can be deferred:

- The exact percentages of Capital Gains Dynamics or Dividend Sharing Dynamics (start with the Charter defaults; recalibrate at year-end).
- Detailed operating procedures.
- Long-term strategic plans.
- Most policies most groups think they need on day one.

> *Staying present, inquiring together into what is needed next, and taking the wise right next step enables us to work together from a standpoint of emergence into the unknown — working from spirit, source, and emergence; transcendent of self; in service of the whole; creating as much value as we can together in each moment toward the greatest good of all.*

This is an entirely different mode from "design the whole org chart in week one." Practice the mode. The mode is part of what you are constituting.

## Constituting the FSx Legal Person

Once the opening agreement is solid and the Driver is clear, the founding circle moves to constitute itself as an **FSx** — a FairShares Commons-type fractal sovereign legal person with agency independent of its members, with dynamic weighted governance and economic rights.

The structural moves:

### 1. Choose your jurisdiction (or jurisdictions)

The FSx pattern is agnostic to legal form. It can be implemented as a limited liability company in most U.S. states, as a community-interest company or company limited by guarantee in the U.K., as a coopérative in France, as similar forms elsewhere. **Most jurisdictions will let you implement an FSx if you write the Articles right.**

For most starting circles, the simplest form is a multi-member limited liability company (LLC) or equivalent, with the [[VII - The FSx Organism Template]] adapted as the Operating Agreement / Articles. Some jurisdictions require one or more members to interface with the regulatory state — this can simply be a compensated role, not an ownership stake.

### 2. Adapt the Template

Use [[VII - The FSx Organism Template]] as your starting point. Adapt the variable provisions:

- **Article 3 — Name.** Choose, register, or work with a placeholder.
- **Article 4 — Jurisdiction.** Specify.
- **Article 5 — Driver Statement.** From your discernment above.
- **Article 10 — Constituencies.** Adopt the full pattern (A through J) or a subset appropriate to your scale and sector. A small starting circle may begin with a smaller set; the pattern is fractal and will grow with you.
- **Article 24 — Weighted Governance Dynamics.** Use the Founding-Phase defaults until you have reason to recalibrate.
- **Schedules A, B, C** — Qualifying Criteria, Founders and Initial Stewards, Initial Capital Structure.

A small village FSx will look different from a continent-spanning manufacturing FSx. What they share is the pattern, not the line-by-line text. Local adaptation is expected.

### 3. Constitute with the multi-voice requirement

Per the Threshold of Federation, the FSx must be **multi-voice**. For organizational FSx instances this is structurally satisfied by the Member Constituencies at Article 10 — you cannot have a one-voice FSx organization. For FSx Individual instances (Layer VIII), the multi-voice requirement is satisfied by the Mentors / Council of Peers. For each scale the pattern protects against ownership concentration.

### 4. Sign and file

File the Articles with your jurisdiction. Convene the founding circle as the initial Member roster. Adopt opening Standards (SS-1, SCS-1, GS-1, DS-1, IS-1, CS-1) as Knowledge Commons references; refine them as you go. Open a bank account in the entity's name.

### 5. Honor the Founders' Contributions on Day One

On the day the new entity is constituted, log the **Legacy Pool** for each founder — the contributions they bring to the starting line that pre-date the organization itself. For each Member, record:

- **Approximate hours of uncompensated time** working and training toward this, scaled by size of role.
- **Opening capital contribution** (cash, equipment, real estate, technology, etc.).
- **Value of other opening contributions** (intellectual property, relationships, brand equity, etc., as the founders mutually recognize).
- **Requested return to make whole** — the level at which each founder would feel reasonably honored and made whole for what they brought.

No external audit or authority is required. This is a product of mutual agreement and consent among founders. It will be drawn down at Pool 11 (Legacy Pool) of the Value Waterfall as the entity generates surplus over time (see Part III).

In a context of collapse, future return may not be denominated in the same currency in which the contribution was made — but should be of equivalent *real wealth value*, understood (as a Guidebook framing, not a constitutionally defined term) as the ability to provision oneself and one's loved ones into the future at a given standard of living.

## Federating from the Beginning

Here is where the Guidebook diverges most clearly from a generic FSx guidebook. Any group can constitute an FSx in isolation. But the **core enabling breakthrough** of the FSx ecosystem is that federation can be undertaken *from the beginning* — and when it is, the new entity receives **dynamic governance and economic stake in the whole ecosystem from day one**, rather than only in its own organization.

The path to federation from the beginning:

1. The founding circle has each constituted themselves (or are in the process of constituting themselves) as FSx Individual per [[VIII - The FSx Individual Template]].
2. The founding circle has constituted the new organizational FSx per [[VII - The FSx Organism Template]].
3. The founding circle (or a designated representative) signs [[II - The Stewardship Agreement]], joining the spiritual association.
4. The new FSx entity signs a **Federation Agreement** with the Ecosystem Holding Company per [[III - The Ecosystem Holding Charter]] §3.2 and §4. This Agreement specifies the entity's contribution commitments (default: first tenth into the commons), Standards adoption, governance terms, and exit terms.
5. From the moment of signature, the entity has standing in the Ecosystem Holding Company, receives a fair share of value generated anywhere within the federated commons (per Capital Gains Dynamics and Dividend Sharing Dynamics), can access the Switchboard's multi-capital flows, and has voice in the governance of the whole.

A group that adopts FSx-compatible Articles in isolation must reach separate agreements to federate later, agreement by agreement, after the fact. A group that joins an FSx ecosystem at the outset has it built in.

> *No sovereignty in isolation. Each fractal sovereign is also a part of one superorganism, collaborating and supporting one another in service of the whole.*

This is the breakthrough. Most of what follows in this Guidebook is the practical question: *given that we want to federate from the beginning, how do we then actually work?*

## A Practical Sequence for a Founding Circle

A workable rough sequence (months are illustrative; honor your circle's actual pace):

- **Month 1:** Opening Agreement sessions. Each founder begins their FSx Individual Charter. Mentors are discerned individually.
- **Month 2:** Driver Statement drafting. Initial naming. Initial set of Stewardship Principles for the new entity. First read-through of [[VII - The FSx Organism Template]].
- **Month 3:** FSx Individual Charters signed with Mentors. Articles drafted from the Template, adapted to the specific entity.
- **Month 4:** Articles filed. Bank account opened. Legacy Pool entries logged. Federation Agreement signed with the Ecosystem Holding Company.
- **Month 5+:** Operating begins. First value flows. First Pools formed. First Quests undertaken. First seasonal review at the next Solstice or Equinox.

This is fast, by conventional standards. The pattern is designed to be fast. The slowness, when slowness is needed, is in the *first month* — the opening agreement and the inner work — not in the legal paperwork.
