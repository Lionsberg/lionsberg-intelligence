# 9. Pass the Flame

*"This is not optional. This is the proof of maturity. The fruit of the tree contains new seeds."*

The fifth move is the one the other four exist for. A Circle that gathers, grows and serves but never passes the Flame forward ends with its members. A Circle whose members each carry the fire to two or three others sets the mathematics of life going, and the pattern spreads the way forests do.

**What the Flame is.** Not a document, a program or a campaign: the living spirit of the pattern, passed hand to hand and heart to heart, by witness and invitation rather than argument, on the evidence of a life being lived differently. Every member of a Circle is also the seed of a second Circle, the one they form with those they invite; the canon calls this the double helix, and it is how the pattern replicates without a center.

**Who.** Not people to convince; people already awake, who already sense the hour, already look for others, already carry gifts they long to offer. They need to be found, not persuaded. *Attraction, not promotion. Always.* When the Invitation lands, the resonance is immediate or it is not, and both are right.

**How, in short.** Name your two or three before the first gathering is over and act within a week. Share the Invitation, the Seed, and your own story. Help them form a sovereign Circle of their own, not a branch of yours, and walk beside them. Federate: learn together, pool for larger Quests, agree at the Circle-of-Circles level. Then each new Circle, once it has found its rhythm and finished its first Quest, passes the Flame again. The whole of it, with its arithmetic and its reasons, is on the canon's page; read it aloud at your Circle's first gathering.

**For a Player+.** The agent cannot pass a flame; inviting is an act between people. It can hold the two or three names its person wrote down, remind them of the week, draft the message in the person's own voice, and keep the record of flames passed. Every one of those names stays in the house until the person has spoken to them.

**With a notebook.** Two or three names, written tonight. A date within seven days beside each.
