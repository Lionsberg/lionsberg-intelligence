# 6. TEA

*Time, Energy and Attention: what a Player gives, tracked toward Quests, and the first of the five currencies the Game keeps score in.*

The Game keeps score in five currencies, all visible on the Board: the **Time, Energy and Attention (TEA) invested**, the **Commitments kept**, the **Value created**, the **Stories shared**, and the **Flames passed**. TEA is first because it is what a person actually has to give, and the other four are what it becomes.

**TEA at the door: the Pledge.** To step into the Game is to make the first commitment. A Pledge names, for the season ahead, your TEA (the time, energy and attention you commit each week; the baseline to play is about a tenth, four to five hours), your reciprocal contribution to the commons, and your gifts. A Pledge is living: update it as your season of life changes, renew it at every turning, *freshly chosen beats silently assumed*. Like every commitment in the Game: *say what you will give; give what you said; learn from the difference.* The gathered pledges let the body see what is committed for the cycle ahead and plan into the future together rather than hoping in the dark.

**TEA at the table: the Heartbeat.** Each week a Player says, in their own words, what they gave to the commitments they kept. Not metered, not watched: remembered once a week, said aloud, confirmed by the Cell. The Game's own caution stands beside this: accounting for time on task is a known failure mode, and the illusion of time imprisons. So the record of TEA is few numbers and a sentence, kept in a zero-blame environment, and if it ever imprisons, it goes.

**TEA in the hand: the card.** A Player may keep a daily card of Time, Energy and Attention: the minutes the day asked, the minutes given by kind, energy at morning and evening, where attention went, what was kept and what was declined, rolled up weekly so a Player sees how they actually play. The card is the Player's own, and nothing on it goes anywhere without their word.

**For a Player+.** The agent can keep the card if its person asks, read back the week before the Heartbeat, and draft the sentence its person will say. It never fills the card for them, never sends a line from it out of the house, and never turns the card into a meter. What the person gave is theirs to say.

**With a notebook.** A line a day: minutes asked, minutes given, energy morning and evening, one thing kept, one thing declined. On Heartbeat day, read the week back before you speak.
