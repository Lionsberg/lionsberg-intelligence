# The Game Engine

*The calculating part: the same rule, applied by anyone to the same record, deck and clock, returns the same answer.*

A game needs an engine or its board is an opinion. The engine is what turns a body's cards, its record and its shared clock into answers everyone can check: start and finish dates, [[Float]] on every item, the [[Critical Path]], the stage each piece of work has reached, and what a contribution banks. Two people in different countries with the same inputs get the same result, which is what lets them trust a number neither of them computed.

It is held in three layers, and the order matters. First a **specification in plain words with a worked example**, so that a person with paper and an afternoon gets the same answer as any machine. Then [[The conformance deck]] — a small set of cards with known answers that any implementation must reproduce. Then implementations, in whatever anyone happens to run. The specification is the rule; the programs are conveniences, and any of them may be thrown away ([[Screens and paper]], [[Technology Sovereignty]]).

Its discipline is that the engine computes and never decides. It can say what the arithmetic of a plan implies; it cannot say what a body should do about it. How people's time, energy and attention are actually deployed against what the plan requires is a judgment of strategy, informed by the calculation and never set by it ([[Resource Leveling]], [[Load]], [[Wisdom]]).

It is load-bearing because it is the alternative to authority as a tie-breaker. Where a number is contested, a body without an engine resolves it by whoever is most senior or most insistent. With one, it resolves it by re-running the rule — and if the rule is wrong, by improving the rule in the open, once, for everyone ([[Proposal]], [[Current Best Understanding]]).
