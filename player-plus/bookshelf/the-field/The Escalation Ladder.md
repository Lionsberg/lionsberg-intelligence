# The Escalation Ladder

*Where an issue goes when the body that holds it cannot resolve it — one rung up, sparingly.*

The default is local. An issue is named, given an owner and a date, talked through, and solved by the body whose work it is ([[Issue]], [[Subsidiarity]]). Only when that body genuinely cannot resolve it does the issue travel: to the stewardship body of the [[Supercell]] it belongs to, and, if it must, one rung further ([[Stewardship body]], [[Guidance]]).

Two disciplines keep the ladder from becoming a chain of command. It is climbed sparingly, because attention at a higher scale is scarce and every escalation spends some of it. And it carries an issue rather than a person: what goes up is a question the body could not answer, not a complaint about who answered it wrong ([[Zero Blame Environment]], [[Proportionality]]).

What comes back down is also bounded. A higher body offers direction, and at times a decision, only where its wider view genuinely serves; it pushes decisions back out as far as they will go and no farther, and it stays out of as much as it can ([[Subsidiarity]], [[Consent-Based Decision-Making]], [[The Return]]). Sovereignty is not suspended by escalation: the body that raised the issue keeps its own agency throughout ([[Sovereignty At Every Scale]], [[Agency, not ownership]]).

Beside the ladder stands the other motion, which is not escalation at all: anyone at any level may say *stop*, and it takes effect at once ([[Whistleblower Protection]], [[Stop]]).
