# Cross-Functional Integration

*Dissolving the walls between domains: one team, organised by what is being made rather than by what each person is called.*

Work is ordinarily divided by function — design here, money there, operations somewhere else — and each division hands the next a finished decision it had no part in. Integration reverses the order: the people who will build, fund, run and live with the thing are in the room from the beginning, and the team is organised around the outcome. What was a sequence of handovers between departments becomes one conversation with several kinds of expertise in it.

It is load-bearing because it is what [[Integrated Delivery]] actually does. Fragmented delivery produces fragmented results — a million organisations, none coordinated, the whole far less than the sum of its parts. The remedy is not a better handover but fewer of them, and the handovers that remain carry [[Conditions of satisfaction]] written by the receiver. In the canon's lattice this is also what an Integration Domain is for: the trades are integrated within a place and a tradition so that the Whole can function, rather than each trade perfecting itself alone.

In a small body it looks like nothing special: the person who will use the thing sits in the planning session; the one who will pay for it hears the constraint when it is found. That is the whole of it at the scale of a Cell, and it is the same move that, at the scale of a programme, decides whether a hundred groups build one thing or a hundred incompatible ones.
