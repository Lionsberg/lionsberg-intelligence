# Guardrails Encountered

*Recording where an inquiry was refused, throttled, or steered — the refusal kept as data in its own section, rather than as grievance in the argument.*

An inquiry that hits a wall usually produces one of two bad outcomes. Either the wall is forgotten and the finished account silently omits what could not be looked into, or the wall becomes the argument and the account turns into a complaint about being blocked. Giving the encounter **its own section** avoids both: the obstruction is recorded, in place, as part of the record of the inquiry ([[The seven-part UnVeiling form]], [[Provenance]], [[Honesty about time]]).

What belongs in it is specific and checkable: what was asked, what was refused, by whom or what, and in what terms. What does not belong is the inference — *therefore they are hiding something* — which is a claim and belongs where claims go ([[We Do Not Know]], [[Navigating Towards Truth]], [[Discernment]]).

That discipline is the whole value, because an obstruction is genuinely ambiguous evidence. A refusal can mean concealment; it can equally mean policy, liability, exhaustion, or a rule applied without thought. Recording it without concluding from it is what keeps the record usable by someone who does not share the author's suspicions ([[Second reader]], [[Control Of Information]], [[No False-Oppositional Framings]]).

It generalises well beyond contested history, and that is where most readers will use it. Any body should be able to say where its own inquiries were stopped — which questions it could not get answered, which records it could not obtain, which rooms it was not admitted to — and a body that never records this is either never asking or never saying ([[Gaps Not Yet Closed]], [[Requirement Of Transparency]], [[The Record]], [[Keepers' notes]]).

In its source the section's contents are assertions about named platforms and services; none of that is carried here. The practice crosses; the contents do not.
