# Maximum reuse

*Write most carefully what is used most often: the wisdom invoked by twenty Plays deserves twenty times the care of the one invoked by none.*

It is an authoring-priority rule, and it answers a question every language of this kind eventually faces — with limited attention and a great many things unwritten, what do you write next? The answer here is not *what is most interesting* and not *what is most overdue*. It is *what carries the most weight in the lived body*.

The reasoning follows from the architecture. Because a [[Play]] invokes a [[Pattern]] rather than restating it, a Pattern sits underneath every Play that calls it. Deepen that one Card and every Play it serves deepens at once, without any of them being touched ([[Plays Are Riffs; Patterns Are The Notes]], [[One canonical home per concept]]). Deepen a Pattern nothing invokes and the improvement reaches exactly one page. The difference is not a matter of degree; it is the difference between leverage and diligence.

It also gives a body a way to see what it is actually running on. Count the invocations and three things become visible: which wisdoms bear weight, which are named and never called, and which Plays rest on Patterns that were never written ([[The Playbook Chart]], [[Gaps Not Yet Closed]]). That census is only possible where composition is declared — a body of work whose parts do not say what they are made of can be neither weighed nor audited, and so cannot be prioritised at all.

The discipline has a guard. High reuse is a claim about what is being used now, not about what matters most, and a rule that only ever funds the well-used will starve everything a body has not yet learned to need — which is how a language ossifies around its first season's habits ([[Everything Is Current Best Understanding]]). Reuse decides the order of the work. It does not decide what belongs in the language ([[Notes, Riffs, Songs, And The Symphony]], [[How A Pattern Enters The Language]]).
