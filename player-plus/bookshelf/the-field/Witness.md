# Witness

*The second voice: the line that vouches for what was seen — and, where two accounts contradict, the line that settles which one holds here.*

To witness is to put your own name beside something somebody else said happened. It costs the witness something, which is exactly what makes it worth anything: a record in which only the doer ever speaks is a record of claims, and a record in which a second person will stand behind a claim is a record of facts ([[Event]], [[The Record]], [[Testimony]]).

It does two distinct jobs. The first is **vouching**: the work was done, the word was kept, the thing was seen. A promise is closed by the one who was owed, and the closing may be witnessed so that the wider body can rely on it without having been present ([[Committee]], [[The Life of a Commitment]], [[Kept as a share of made]]).

The second is **settling**. In a record where nothing is erased, two contradictory accounts can both stand — and should, because the disagreement is real information. What resolves it is not deletion but a witness written by the body whose scope it is, saying which account holds *here*. Another body may witness differently, and the record carries both. Truth is not enforced from a centre; it is vouched for locally and read with its provenance attached ([[Provenance]], [[Navigating Towards Truth]], [[We Do Not Know]]).

The third thing it does is more human than either. Being witnessed is how work that nobody would otherwise see becomes real to a body — and what a person cannot do for themselves, however honestly, is say that their own contribution counted. A body that witnesses generously is not being sentimental; it is collecting the only evidence it will ever have ([[Attribution]], [[Honor]], [[Gratitude]]).

Its guard is that names are witnessed and never credentialed. Nobody issues standing; people vouch for one another, and the vouching accumulates into something anyone can read and anyone can dispute ([[Trust]], [[The Roll]], [[Brought-by · witnessed-by]]).
