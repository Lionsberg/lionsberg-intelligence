# How A Pattern Enters The Language

*The self-maintenance question: by what process does a new Pattern become settled, an existing one improve, and an exhausted one retire — and who holds that judgment.*

A living Language needs a door, a workshop, and a grave, and it needs them written down. The door: a Pattern is proposed because something was lived, and it is tested against the two filters — is this general rather than an application ([[General Pattern, Specific Application]]), and is it a Pattern rather than a container ([[Patterns And Containers]])? The workshop: an existing Card deepens as practitioners feed back what the field taught them. The grave: a Pattern that no longer carries transmissible wisdom is retired rather than defended, which is [[The Triadic Discipline]]'s third move applied to the Language itself.

Without a written process, the Language can only grow by whoever happens to be writing, and this is named plainly as a gap in the Language, alongside the question of who sits in judgment — a Pattern Council is named and nothing describes it. What exists in practice instead is the wider discipline: Patterns are validated by embodiment, not by assertion; what works is preserved, what fails is refined or retired.

The shape a written process would have to honor is already visible elsewhere. A change travels as a [[Proposal]] with its why and its source, it soaks before it binds, an objection names a harm rather than a preference ([[Meaningful Objection]]), and changes gather at a turning and land by [[The edition]]. What is missing is the application of that shape to the Language's own body.

Until it is written, the honest posture is the one already taken here: name the gap, keep the backlog visible, and let embodiment rather than argument decide.
