# The Asymmetry of Consequences

*The argument that makes acting correct without first winning the argument about how bad things are: compare what each mistake costs, and one column is recoverable while the other is not.*

Set the two uncertainties against each other. The danger is either real or overstated; we either move or we don't. Four outcomes follow. **Move, and the danger was real** — some of what could be saved is saved, and something new is standing. **Move, and it was overstated** — the effort went into healing, beauty, justice and stronger communities, which are worth having in any world; the loss is small and partly a gain. **Don't move, and it was real** — the cost is unrecoverable, and it is not paid by the ones who chose. **Don't move, and it was overstated** — the old arrangement goes on decaying at its own pace, and we were spectators who could have built something and didn't.

The force is in the comparison of *worst cases*, not of likelihoods. The worst case of acting unnecessarily is recoverable and partly productive. The worst case of not acting when it mattered is neither. That is why the usual dismissal — *alarmist* — does not reach it: the argument concedes in advance that the alarm may be wrong and still holds ([[Advantage Based Decision Making]], [[Decision Making]]).

It changes what invitation has to accomplish. Nobody has to be persuaded of a diagnosis before they can be asked in. *Even if this is overstated — and we hope it is — what we are building is worth building. Will you build it with us?* ([[The Invitation]], [[Attraction, not promotion]], [[Demonstrate before advocating]].)

There is a moral edge the tidy version misses: whoever waits for more evidence is not risking only their own time. They are staking the cost on people who did not get to choose ([[Omniconsideration]], [[Rights extend first to the weak; responsibilities first to the strong]]).

And it scales down intact. A Circle deciding on a quest under uncertainty, a household deciding about a school, a person deciding about a livelihood — the same shape. *If being wrong one way is cheap or even good, and being wrong the other way is large and irreversible, act* ([[The Voluntary Leap Forward]], [[Possibility Of Reversal]]).

Its honest limit: the matrix does not tell anyone *what* to build, and it can be run for any proposal whose advocates call its failure cheap. The asymmetry is an argument for moving, never a substitute for judgment about the move ([[Discernment]], [[Spontaneous Wise Right Action]]).
