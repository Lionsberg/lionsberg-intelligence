# Limit Statement

*Naming the edge of what can be said, inside the saying of it.*

Some of what this language reaches for cannot be contained by it. ØNE, the Source, the beginning and the end, the good beyond better and best: every word offered for these is a fingerpost, and a fingerpost that forgets it is one becomes an idol. A limit statement is the sentence that keeps the reader oriented — *this points; it does not contain* — said plainly rather than left for the reader to infer.

It is not a refusal to speak. The discipline is to say the most that can be truly said and then to mark the edge, rather than either falling silent or letting a confident sentence imply more precision than exists. The Lexicon's outermost section works this way: it holds the words for what stands beyond every category — transcendent and manifest, beginning and end, deepest and highest — and says of the whole layer that each entry is a humble rendering of something that lies beyond our current best understanding, and further beyond our current best articulation of it.

A limit story does the same work in narrative form: a telling that carries a true shape while announcing that it is a telling. Myth is honest when it says what it is, and dishonest when it claims the precision of a measurement.

The practical use is small and constant. A page about something ineffable says so once, near the top, and then gets on with saying what can be said. That single sentence is what lets the rest of the page be bold.
