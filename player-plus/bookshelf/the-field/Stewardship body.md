# Stewardship body

*The small group where a decision can finally sit when process cannot produce one — staying out of as much as it can, deciding when it must, and pushing decisions back out as far as they can go, and no farther.*

Every group eventually meets a question its ordinary process cannot answer: consent stalls, the matter is urgent, or the body is genuinely split. Without somewhere for such a question to land, it lands on whoever is willing to act alone — and that person becomes, without anyone choosing it, the body's real government. A named stewardship body makes that landing place visible and small.

Its definition is almost entirely a set of restraints, and that is the design. **Small.** **Stays out of as much as it can.** **Decides only when it must.** **Pushes decisions back out as far as they can go, and no farther** — which is [[Subsidiarity]] written as a duty of the body most tempted to ignore it. It also holds the covenant the group has made with those who fund it and those who follow it: prescribed trust where it is needed, discretionary trust everywhere else, and the discipline, when things go wrong, of not drowning good actors in new prescription.

It is load-bearing as the honest alternative to two fictions: that a sufficiently good process never deadlocks, and that a body can have no final decider. Both produce the same outcome — an informal one.

In play, a [[Cell]] does not need one; its final decider is the table. A [[Supercell]], a body with employees or money, or a federation does — and the test of its health is how rarely it decides anything.
