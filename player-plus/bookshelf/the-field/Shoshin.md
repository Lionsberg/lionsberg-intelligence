# Shoshin

*Beginner's mind: open, eager, curious, in awe, and without preconception about what it is approaching. Not a starter's quality to be outgrown — the deepest discipline of the master.*

The moment a being believes they *know* the Way, the possibility of knowing the Way is removed in that instant. This is why the discipline grows more critical the further one goes. The one most exposed is not the newcomer, whose actual beginner-state holds this naturally and whose work is to preserve it, but the long-walking master, the established teacher, the honored elder, the certified practitioner, and any tradition that has lived long enough to produce fixed orthodoxies.

The failure it protects against is corruption by one's own mastery — the closure to what is trying to reveal itself. It keeps the conduit open across an entire walk, so that cultivation deepens without ossifying.

The forces are real on both sides and the Pattern does not pretend otherwise. Competence built through long practice is legitimate, and so is the certainty required to act decisively under pressure; what is refused is competence mistaken for completeness and the false certainty of *I know*. Honoring mastery is right; identifying with the mastered position is the trap.

At the scale of a Body this is the same discipline that keeps an articulation from hardening into doctrine — the posture underneath [[Current Best Understanding]] and the personal form of the refusal of [[Ideological Capture]]. It is also what keeps [[Lineage]] honorable: a lineage held with beginner's mind can be loved and still questioned.

The related image the canon keeps beside it is the uncarved block — the state in which nothing is fixed and anything is still possible. And the discipline that makes it practicable rather than merely admired: *break the form only after you can hold it* ([[Shu-ha-ri]]).
