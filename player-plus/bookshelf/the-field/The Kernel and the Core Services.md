# The Kernel and the Core Services

*The immutable core — ontology, ethic, authority — that cannot be changed without the thing ceasing to be itself; and the six services that thread every instance of it, wherever it is running.*

Anything that replicates needs two parts: what must stay identical or the copy is not a copy, and what every copy needs in order to run. Confusing the two is how living systems die — either by freezing what should adapt, or by amending away what made them themselves.

The **kernel** is the first part, and it is deliberately tiny: an ontology (what is held to be real), an ethic (what is held to be right), and an authority (where legitimacy comes from). These three cannot be modified without the result ceasing to be this way of living at all. Everything else — every structure, practice, instrument, and word — is revisable ([[Everything Is Current Best Understanding]], [[The Golden Seed]]).

The **core services** are the second part: six functions every instance runs, however small. **Time and cycle** — the seasonal rhythm, the calendar, the jubilee, the ritual year. **Identity and stewardship** — recognizing a sovereign being and holding a role accountable. **Commons registry** — what is held in common, by whom, under what stewardship. **Value exchange** — how contribution becomes something that can move between people. **Knowledge graph** — the pattern language held as a live structure rather than a shelved book. **Rites engine** — the wheel of rites as a working system rather than an anthology of ceremonies.

Naming these as *services* rather than as institutions is the useful move. A service can be run by a village with a notebook or by software on a phone; it can be replaced, reimplemented, or carried by hand when the power goes out, and the body is the same body either way. The kernel names what must never change; the services name what must always be present. Everything between them is free.
