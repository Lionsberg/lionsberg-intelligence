# Pattern · Play · Tool · Embodiment

*The architecture in one sentence: the Pattern is the why beneath, the Play is the way through, the Tool is the thing in hand — and embodiment is the point of all three.*

Four layers, and knowing which one you are holding is most of the craft. A **[[Pattern]]** is timeless wisdom about a recurring situation — what the forces are and what resolves them — written once, at the general level, on its own [[Pattern Card|Card]]. A **[[Play]]** is choreography for a particular operational moment: when this happens, do this, then this, and here is where you improvise. A **[[Instrument|Tool]]** is the artefact you actually pick up — a template, a tracker, a guide, a deck. And **[[Embodiment]]** is what all three exist to produce: the move done rightly, by a person, without having to look anything up.

The layers are not a hierarchy of importance. They are a division of labour that keeps each one honest. Keep the wisdom in the Pattern and a Play stays short enough to run; keep the choreography in the Play and a Pattern stays general enough to serve twenty Plays; keep the craft in the Tool and neither has to explain how to hold a pen. Collapse any two and the corpus begins duplicating itself, which is the failure the architecture exists to prevent ([[Plays Are Riffs; Patterns Are The Notes]], [[One canonical home per concept]]).

There is a practical rule for telling them apart, and it is taken from how each is used in life rather than from any theory. **Patterns are spoken** — so they carry bare names that live inside sentences: *field of agreements*, *pass the flame*. **Plays are called** — so they carry the call in the name: *Play — Decide by Consent*. **Tools are picked up** — so they carry their craft in the name: *Template For…*, *…Tracker*, *…Guide* ([[Naming Forms]]). Where a bare name and a Play share their words, the bare name is the timeless Pattern and the Play is its runnable version. That one convention resolves nearly every collision the corpus produces.

The fourth layer is the one most easily forgotten, and the canon puts it last on purpose. Handling a good instrument is how a person actually learns — the Pattern settles into the body by doing the thing, not by reading about it — and a Pattern that has not reached anybody's hands has not yet happened ([[Learning By Doing]], [[Embodying the Way]], [[Praxis]], [[Spirit Precedes Pattern]]).
