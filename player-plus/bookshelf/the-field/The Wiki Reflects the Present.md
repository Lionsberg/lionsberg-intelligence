# The Wiki Reflects the Present

*Every page carries the present sense of its concept, and only that. A reader meets the chart of the present course, not the log of the voyage.*

No page keeps a history section, a struck draft, or a paragraph about what it used to say. What a reader meets is what is currently held, stated once, cleanly — which is what makes a page usable at all ([[Current Best Understanding]] · [[Current Best Articulation]] · [[Bottom Line Up Front]]).

**Nothing is lost.** The history is kept whole where a steward can find it: in the version record beneath the wiki, in the decision record for *why* a thing changed, and in the improvement log for what was learned ([[Decision Records]] · [[Improvement log]] · [[Change log]] · [[Provenance]]). A page may close with the date it is true as of, and a reader who asks why is sent to the decision record. The rule is about where history lives, not about whether it is kept.

When a page is retired the practice is exact: **it is mined first.** What still lands is set down — with its date, its source, and where it went — and only then is the page deleted. Nothing of meaning is left behind, and nothing dead stays in the reader's way ([[Weave, do not delete]] · [[Compost]] · [[Prune]] · [[Elision]]).

The reason given is navigational. We are steering a vessel toward a destination, and those aboard do not need to see the previous, less good versions; found without their context, old drafts do not inform, they confuse — and a reader cannot tell which of four versions is the live one ([[Navigation]] · [[One canonical home per concept]] · [[Comment]]).

The discipline this really demands is of the writer rather than the reader: to actually write the present sense, rather than layering a new paragraph on top of an old one and letting the page become a sediment of every hand that touched it ([[Be the canon, do not narrate it]] · [[Progressive canonization]] · [[Second reader]]).
