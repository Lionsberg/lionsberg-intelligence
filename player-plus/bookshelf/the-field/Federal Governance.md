# Federal Governance

*How we govern ourselves at the highest level of abstraction — as a whole — decided consciously, because the alternative is someone else's default.*

The canon's argument here is not a design; it is an insistence. However uncomfortable it is to think about governance at the widest scale, the choice is not between deciding and not deciding. A decision not to decide is a decision to accept randomness, or whoever else has already decided. What is left unclaimed does not stay empty.

It is load-bearing for exactly that reason. A movement built entirely from the bottom up can be tempted to treat the top as unnecessary, and then discover that the coordination problems it cannot solve locally — planetary commons, shared standards, defence of the vulnerable, anything that crosses every membrane — are being answered by the old world's institutions by default.

What this layer may do is tightly bounded by everything else in this Pillar. It is not a government over the parts: [[Subsidiarity]] keeps decisions at the smallest competent scale, [[Sovereignty At Every Scale]] keeps the parts whole, [[Federations vs Empires]] draws the line it may not cross, and service flows downward while authority flows upward. Its legitimacy rests on the same [[Consent]] as a Cell's, and its powers are the ones lent to it, revocably.

In play, almost no player touches it. Its practical face for a [[Cell]] is the upper half of the card — the plan of the whole, broadcast downward — and the [[Proposal]]s that travel upward at a turning to change what is shared.
