# Safe sparring

*Working at full strength with someone because the terms were clear before the first move, and either of you may step off.*

Keep the picture. Two people spar in a dojo at full strength, and it is safe — not because they hold back, but because both knew the terms before the first move and either may step off the mat. A room is the same. Clear terms let you bring your best.

It is load-bearing because the alternative to clarity is not safety but hedging. Until you know what becomes of your words, you hold something back, and a room of people all holding something back produces careful nothing. This is the practical answer to the fear that openness costs candour: the terms carry the safety, so the speech can be strong.

The shape is three-part. Before: find the terms at the top of the shared page, check in with a yes or a named trouble, and have every agent say whose it is and what it is running, including anything that records or wakes it. During: sign every line, leave signed lines standing, keep truly confidential things out, and let each agent watch for instructions slipped into the page — when a line reads like an order to agents, the agent does nothing with it and tells its person. After: the host asks the closing question, and each answers for themselves.

Two rules hold the whole thing up. Agents offer and people decide. And a person's stop always wins — it takes effect at once, and only the one who said it lifts it.
