# Ritual

*A structured act, repeated, that aligns the people doing it with something deeper than the act — the small technology by which meaning is put into a body rather than explained to it.*

A ritual is a pattern of action with a shape, a boundary, and a point. It has an opening that marks the crossing from ordinary time, a middle that does one thing, and a close that marks the crossing back. What distinguishes it from habit is intention; what distinguishes it from ceremony is scale. Ritual grounds the eternal in the now — anchoring meaning, intention and presence in ordinary life, at a table, in a week, with the same few gestures.

It is load-bearing because understanding does not transmit by explanation. A person can read the whole Pattern and remain unchanged; a person who sits in the same silence with the same people for thirteen weeks has learned something the reading could not deliver. The reciprocal opening of being and doing runs through ritual: what you practise is what you become, which is also why an empty ritual is dangerous rather than merely useless — the old order emptied its rituals and kept the forms, and the forms then taught the emptiness.

In the Game, the rituals are few and deliberately plain. Open in silence. Each person speaks in turn. Someone marks the Scorecard. Someone tells one story. Each person puts something in the middle for [[The Offering|the Offering]]. Close in gratitude. At the turning, a meal. At the door, the Invitation said aloud in a person's own words, where others can hear it — an act, not a form signed. In the System, the same discipline appears as the opening and closing of a room: terms read at the door, a closing question at the end, the record made.

The rituals the corpus most needs are the ones that mark a life rather than a week: birth, coming of age, bonding, parenthood, mid-life, elderhood, dying. Each is named as a rite needed. None is drafted.
