# Screens and paper

*Nothing here needs a screen; where screens exist, the same cards continue there — and the notebook is the floor the network stands on.*

A group with a notebook and a group with a network are playing the same game. The plan can be computed by a script or walked by hand in an hour; the cards can be a database or index cards on a wall; the record can be a file or a page in a book. What must not differ is the object: the same cards, the same halves, the same lines in the same order, so that the two groups can read each other.

It is load-bearing because it decides who may play. A system that requires a device excludes the places that need it most, fails in an outage, and quietly hands whoever controls the software a veto over whoever uses it. Making the paper primary removes all three at once: the tools make things faster and wiser, never required. A hand copy on any paper counts exactly as much as a printed one, and if two records disagree, the paper with two signatures is the record.

It also sets the honest order of building. Build the paper form first, prove that three people with a pencil can run it, then let a screen carry the same object faster for those who want one. Anything a screen does that the paper cannot has quietly left the standard — and will be the part that breaks the day the power does.
