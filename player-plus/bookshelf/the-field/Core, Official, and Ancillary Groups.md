# Core, Official, and Ancillary Groups

*A three-stage ladder for a group's standing in the whole — fringe, recognised, critical — with recognition granted by the assembly rather than claimed.*

Anyone may start a group. That is the first rule, and the ladder exists because of it: if entry is free, standing cannot also be free, or the words *part of this* mean nothing.

**Ancillary** groups operate at the edge of the network, without official recognition or any role in governing the whole. This is not a demotion; it is where every group begins, and many stay there usefully forever. **Official** groups have developed to an appropriate maturity and have been formally recognised by the assembly as an integral part of the whole's governance and functioning. **Core** groups are those the whole actually depends on — critical to its proper function and accountability.

Two conditions attach to the upper rungs and they are the point of the ladder. Official and Core groups must have their domains of authority and responsibility, and their flows of information and energy, **clearly articulated** — because a body cannot be accountable for what has never been described ([[Accountability]] · [[Necessity Of Accountability]]). And anyone in the system may request and receive information about how any of them functions ([[Requirement Of Transparency]]). Standing is therefore paid for in legibility. The more the whole relies on you, the more of you must be visible.

Movement up the ladder is granted, never taken: an evaluating body assesses, the assembly approves ([[The LIØNSBERG General Assembly]] · [[Legitimacy]]). The page says of itself that this is a work in progress to be grown into as the system matures, which is the honest posture for a structure nobody has yet run.
