# HoloFractal Governance

*Governance that mirrors the holofractal architecture of Creation: each part reflects and serves the Whole through nested sovereignty and [[Subsidiarity]].*

Holofractal is the compound of two truths. *Holarchic*: every body is a whole that is also a part. *Fractal*: the same pattern appears at every scale. Governance built this way has one grammar rather than many — what a [[Cell]] of eight does on a Tuesday is recognizably what a [[Community]] of ten thousand does at a turning, and what a [[Bioregion]] does across a decade.

The economy of this is the point. A civilization needs a governance a person can learn once and use everywhere; if the rules change at each level, only specialists can move between levels, and specialists become a class. One grammar means a newcomer at any table already knows how things are decided, and a Cell that federates does not have to learn a second system to take part in the larger one.

It is also what makes the architecture legible to inspection. Because each level is the same shape, a distortion at one level is visible against all the others: a table that has stopped taking [[Consent]], a level that has begun issuing instructions instead of [[Guidance]], an office that no longer rotates. [[Structural Immunity]] depends on this legibility.

The canon names the principle clearly and leaves the mechanics thin — the Lexicon defines it, the Pattern Language lists it, and no page yet sets out how two same-shaped bodies at different scales resolve a genuine conflict. Read this as a settled orientation with an unbuilt procedure.
