# The Commander And The Soldier

*A parable: the one who knows the Intent and follows a contrary order anyway has already lost the thing the order was given to win.*

The Commander of the forces of good sends out the Intent — the purpose of the work, and what the finished thing must look like — with one instruction above all the others: *ensure that every means you employ is entirely consistent with the end we must bring about* ([[Commander's Intent]] · [[Means]] · [[Conditions of satisfaction]]).

Weeks later the Commander walks among the work and finds a company preparing to do harm. He asks one of them what he is doing, and the man answers with the company's plan. *No — what are you, individually, doing?* He describes his own small task. *Why?* Because he was told to, by the one put in charge.

Then the questions that are the whole parable. *Did you read the Intent?* Yes. *Did you understand it?* I think so. *Is this the wise right thing to do to accomplish the Goal?* No. *Is this what I would want?* No. *Is this what ØNE would want?* No. *Then why are you doing it?*

Because he was afraid of the one put in charge.

The Commander's answer is a release and a command at once: **you are free now; you are no longer bound by the rules and the false hierarchies of the old world.** There is only ØNE to be ultimately feared and obeyed, and until that is so, the great battle cannot be won. Stand on the ground you have levelled and resist, with everything you have, any attempt to violate what you know in your soul the Intent to be. I will stand with you — and I suspect most of the company will stand with you too ([[Restraint As The Refusal To Act]] · [[Meaningful Objection]] · [[Courage]] · [[Withdrawal Of Consent]] · [[The Old Authorities]]).

And the closing line that relocates the whole conflict: **the great battle is not against an enemy out there.** Winning it requires first winning the battle within — for one's own soul, one's own integrity, one's own coherence ([[The Sacred War]] · [[Integrity]] · [[Coherence]] · [[The Warrior's Vow]] · [[Composure Under Threat]]).

The parable's hardest clause is the one about cost: be willing to be cast out by your own company before adding your strength to what you know to be wrong. That is the price the story names, and it does not soften it ([[Sacrifice]] · [[Honor]]).
