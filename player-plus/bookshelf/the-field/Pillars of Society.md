# Pillars of Society

*The load-bearing parts of any society, named so that each can be looked at, assessed, and repaired — governance, economy, society, and spirituality.*

The point of naming pillars is diagnostic. A society is too large to hold in mind at once, and a people that cannot name its structural parts will argue endlessly about symptoms. Four names give somewhere to stand.

**[[Governance]]** — how a society is steered toward its aims: institutions, courts and regulatory bodies, the laws and policies that guide them, and equally the self-governance of individuals, families, groups and localities at every fractal level. Its job is fairness, justice and accountability, and the protection of everyone's rights and wellbeing.

**[[Economy]]** — how a society makes, distributes and uses what it needs: enterprises, markets, financial systems, and the rules that shape them. Its job is provision — that families and communities have what they need and that nobody's basic needs go unmet.

**[[Society]]** — the social structures and norms that shape how people actually treat one another: families, communities, schools, and the cultural and ethical values inside them. Its job is cohesion, beauty, and a shared sense of belonging and purpose ([[Culture]] · [[Belonging]]).

**Spirituality** — the inner life of people and communities: what they value, believe, and find meaning in, and how they stand in relation to the Divine. Its job is inner growth, and the orientation of a people toward something beyond itself ([[Spirit]] · [[Theology]] · [[Reverence]]).

The load-bearing claim is that all four must be sound at once. A society can have excellent governance and a hollow inner life, or great wealth and no cohesion, and it will fall down anyway — which is why single-pillar reform movements keep failing and keep being surprised. Repair has to be whole ([[Design]] · [[Integrated Delivery]] · [[The Meta Crisis]]).

This four-part naming is the first pass, and the coarsest. The Twelve Pillars are the same instinct worked out further, and a reader who wants the finer grain should go there ([[Pillar I — Sacred Ontology and Metaphysics]] and its eleven siblings).
