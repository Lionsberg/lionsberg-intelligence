# Linking (as curation)

*Tying scattered things together so that others can build on them — the true strength of a shared wiki, and the smallest complete act of curation.*

A link is a claim that two things belong near each other, made by someone who noticed. That is more than navigation. The set of links a body has made *is* most of what it collectively understands, because meaning lives in the relations between things at least as much as in the things themselves ([[Link concepts, not coordinates]], [[Curation]], [[The Incipient Link]]).

It is load-bearing because it is the cheapest possible contribution and one of the most valuable. Writing a page is hours of work. Noticing that four scattered mentions are about the same thing, gathering them onto one page, and saying a sentence about what you saw, is twenty minutes — and it makes all four usable by everyone who comes after ([[Knowledge as commons]], [[Stigmergy]]).

Two disciplines make it work. **Link generously**: an unwritten link is a connection lost, and a link to a page that does not exist yet is not an error — it marks something worth writing, and marks it where someone will trip over it. **Link concepts, not coordinates**: link the idea by its name, not a path, a file, or a location, so that the link keeps meaning when everything around it moves ([[Link concepts, not coordinates]], [[An address]]).

And the human half is not optional. What a person adds when they link is a *reading* — not only that these things are related, but how, and why it matters now. Links placed without that reading accumulate into a dense, useless web; links placed with it are an argument anyone can follow ([[Author's Voice Is Sovereign]], [[Sensemaking]], [[Second reader]]).
