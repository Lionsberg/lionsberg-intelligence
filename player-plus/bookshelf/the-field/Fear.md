# Fear

*The body's report that something is at stake. Five states are kept distinct — fear, dread, terror, worry, nervousness — because they report different things and ask for different responses.*

**Fear** is the direct signal: something here threatens what matters. It is fast, specific, and usually accurate about the presence of stakes even when wrong about their size. **Dread** is slower and anticipatory — the weight of something approaching that has not arrived. **Terror** is fear at the intensity where capacity begins to fail. **Worry** is fear that has entered thought and is circling, no longer reporting but rehearsing. **Nervousness** is the body's activation before something that matters, which is as often about significance as about danger.

Collapsing them costs precision. Worry treated as fear sends a Sovereign looking for a threat that is not in the room; the response it actually asks for is settling, not vigilance. Dread treated as worry gets argued with when it should be heeded — the canon names foreboding as a warning to be read rather than dismissed. Terror asks first for the breath and the ground, not for analysis.

The discipline is [[Mastery Of Emotions]] applied here: feel it fully, read what it reports, respond to the field rather than to the feeling, release when its work is done. The canonical move is *integration* rather than conquest — fear is not defeated, it is received, understood, and carried. A Sovereign who has integrated fear can act inside it, which is what [[Composure Under Threat]] rests on. A Sovereign who has suppressed it will meet it again at the worst moment, arriving as terror.

Fear is also the oldest instrument of domination: a frightened people is governable, and manufactured fear is one of the ways consent is bypassed. Reading one's own fear accurately is therefore not only an inner discipline but a defence.
