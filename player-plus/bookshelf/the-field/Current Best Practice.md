# Current Best Practice

*A recommendation at the top of the holarchy, never a law — refined by the experience of everyone who works under it.*

It is a status rather than a thing: the standing that every convention in the [[The LIØNSBERG Book of Conventions|Book of Conventions]] is held at, and by extension the standing of any practice this body asks anyone to follow. Two clauses carry it. *At the top of the holarchy* — this is the best known way, offered by those who have gone furthest, and it is not a suggestion you may ignore without cost ([[Holarchy]], [[Our Core Ways]]). *Never a law* — it does not bind, it has no enforcement, and the moment something better is found it is replaced rather than defended ([[Everything Is Current Best Understanding]]).

Holding both at once is the difficulty, and the reason the phrase exists. A practice held too loosely is not a practice: if every writer settles naming for themselves there is no shared body of writing, only adjacent ones ([[One canonical home per concept]], [[Name the Concept in the Singular]]). A practice held too tightly becomes a law, then an identity, then something a body protects instead of improving ([[Institutional Idolatry]], [[Idolatry (of the word and of the doctrine)]]). The status is a deliberate middle: follow it, because it is the best we know; and be the one who improves it when you find better ([[Every Defect Is A Gift]], [[Continuous Improvement]]).

The second clause tells you who improves it. *Refined by the experience of all who write here* — not by whoever wrote it first, and not by a committee. A convention earns its place by being what a new writer would otherwise get wrong, and it is retired the day it stops being that ([[The LIØNSBERG Workshop]], [[How A Pattern Enters The Language]], [[Prune]]).

It belongs to a family of three and the differences are worth keeping. [[Current Best Understanding]] is what we presently hold to be true. [[Current Best Articulation]] is the best wording we have yet found for it. Current best practice is what we presently recommend *doing*. A thing can be well understood, badly worded, and well practised, and knowing which of the three is failing is most of the work of improving anything ([[Soak]], [[Progressive canonization]]).
