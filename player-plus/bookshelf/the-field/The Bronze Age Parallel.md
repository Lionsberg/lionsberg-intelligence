# The Bronze Age Parallel

*The closest historical analogue: an interconnected world of great sophistication that came apart in about fifty years — not from one cause, but because every part depended on every other.*

The case is well documented and the corpus uses it carefully. A set of Late Bronze Age powers around the eastern Mediterranean traded, corresponded, kept bureaucracies, and depended on long supply routes — tin from far inland, copper from the islands, grain by sea. Then, within roughly two generations, it ended: cities burned, trade dissolved, literacy lost in places, populations fallen, and one empire so completely destroyed that it was forgotten until archaeologists found it again.

The mechanism is the reason it is cited. No single cause suffices. Drought produced famine, famine produced movement, movement produced war, war broke the trade routes, and broken routes meant economies that could no longer produce the one metal everything depended on — at which point they could not arm, maintain, or feed themselves. The feedback loops closed. Historians of the period call it a *systems collapse*: not the failure of a part, but the simultaneous failure of an arrangement in which every part relied on the others ([[Systemic Failure Modes]], [[Multi-Polar Traps]], [[Supply Chains]], [[The Endgame Cascade]]).

What makes the corpus's use of it sound is that the **disanalogy is stated as carefully as the analogy**. That collapse reached a few million people across one region. Elsewhere, entire continents were unaffected and carried on. It was regional, and the world had an outside ([[Probability, Severity, and Scope]], [[The Planet]]).

Which is precisely the point being made. The claim is not *this will happen again*; it is that the one near-precedent we have was smaller in every dimension that matters, so it understates rather than predicts ([[Collapse]], [[The Meta Crisis]], [[The Three Converging Cycles]]).

And the useful residue for a body reading it now is not fear but design: the collapse ran along dependencies. What is worth knowing is which of yours are long, thin, and shared with everyone else ([[Supply Line Supremacy]], [[Self-Sustenance and Self-Replication]], [[Anti-Fragility]], [[Structural Immunity]]).
