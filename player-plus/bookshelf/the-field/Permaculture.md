# Permaculture

*Designing with nature's intelligence rather than against it — reading what living systems already solved, and building on that answer.*

The core move is humility applied as method. Before designing a food system, a settlement, a water system, or a building, look at what the place already does: where the water runs, what the sun does across the year, which species already cooperate, how the soil is fed. Then design so the flows do the work. Biomimicry is the same move in other trades; biophilia is the affection that keeps a designer looking long enough to see.

It is load-bearing well beyond agriculture. The structural commitments are the same lessons read from ecosystems: stack functions rather than dedicating each element to one job; catch and store what arrives rather than importing continuously; put the intensive work close to the centre; value the edge, where two systems meet and most of the productivity is; let the [[Waste]] of one process be the input of another, which is what [[Compost]] does with death and what [[Fostering Ecosystemic Resource Flows]] does between Circles. [[Ecology]] supplies the reading; permaculture is what you do with it.

In play it is one of the most tractable first [[Quest]] shapes a [[Cell]] can choose. It is local, physical, visible, cheap to begin, and it produces something to point to at the end of a season — a garden, a swale, a fixed roofline, a restored stretch of bank. It teaches the Circle its place at the same time: you cannot design with a system you have not observed, so the design work forces the [[Watershed]] and [[Ecoregion]] literacy the Pillar asks for anyway.

Two cautions. Permaculture is a design discipline, not a doctrine, and its answers are local — a technique that works in one ecoregion can fail in the next. And this territory is marked thin: the seven pattern cards of place are named among the work not yet written, so what stands is the orientation rather than a worked LIØNSBERG method.
