# Span of Control

*How many a node can answer for at once — three to seven, five about right — before the body adds a branch rather than asking one node to stretch.*

Anything that carries others beneath it has a ceiling: a person, a Cell holding subcells, a seat holding a fleet. Past the ceiling the carrying does not merely get harder, it silently stops working — messages are missed, states go stale, and the node becomes the place where the body's information dies. The answer is structural: when the number is exceeded, a branch is added. Nobody is asked to try harder ([[Subsidiarity]], [[The three charts]]).

It is load-bearing because it is a ceiling placed on *design* rather than on willpower, and design ceilings are the only kind that hold. What it protects is the scarce human at the centre of things — the person everyone reasonably wants an answer from, who becomes the [[Bottleneck]] of the whole precisely because they are willing ([[A role held forever quietly becomes a throne]], [[Size of role, size of person]]).

The same shape recurs at every scale: a body of Cells gathering three or more rather than thirty, and reading only what its parts publish rather than everything inside them ([[Supercell]], [[Nucleus]], [[Selectively Permeable Membrane]]).

It must be kept apart from a [[Cell]]'s own size, which looks similar and is not. Three to thirteen souls, best at four to seven, is a covenant number — how many can cohere as one body and know one another by name. Span of control is a carrying number — how many a single node answers for. A Cell of seven answers to one another as equals; there is no supervisor among them ([[Cell]], [[Levels Of Scale]]).

The number itself is a proposal borrowed from bodies that coordinate under emergency, and it is held as an estimate rather than a measurement. The field's own caution belongs on the page: it is only as good as the hands that know it, and its most-cited failures were people who did not respect it rather than the design itself. It is settled only after some seasons' running.
