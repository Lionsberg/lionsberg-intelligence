# Consent-Based Decision-Making

*How a Circle decides without a boss: a proposal is brought, objections are heard, and it passes when no one has a reasoned, paramount objection.*

The movement is short. Someone frames the question cleanly and brings a [[Proposal]]. The circle asks clarifying questions, then reactions, then the one question that decides it: *does anyone have a [[Meaningful Objection]]?* If no one does, it is decided. If someone does, the objection is a gift of information — the proposal is amended until it falls inside everyone's [[Range Of Tolerance]], not until the objector gives up.

It is load-bearing because it is the only deciding method that is fast *and* preserves [[Sovereignty]]. Voting is fast and leaves minorities bound by something they refused. Consensus honours everyone and can stall a body indefinitely. Consent splits the difference honestly: the standard is workability rather than agreement, so a body moves at the speed of *good enough for now, safe enough to try*, with a review date attached.

Three disciplines keep it from degrading. **Objections are about the aim**, not about preference — framed as harm to the body's ability to advance. **Span of impact sets the pace**: the wider a decision reaches, the more time a body should spend moving past consent toward the higher standard of consensus. And **the reasons are written down** ([[Decision Records]]), so a decision is not re-litigated by accident and successors can see why.

In play, this is the deciding movement of the weekly [[The Turn]] and of every [[Council]]. Alongside it sits [[Advantage Based Decision Making]] for choosing between alternatives, and [[Wise Eldership]] for the rare case where consent stalls.
