# Via Negativa

*Saying only what ØNE is not — stripping the false labels off, one by one, as the clearest way to see what It Is.*

Every word offered for the Absolute is smaller than the Absolute. Say *ØNE is light* and you have quietly excluded the dark it also holds; say *ØNE is love* and a hearer will fit it to the small love they already know. The old discipline answers this by moving the other way: not by describing, but by removing. *Not this, not that* — neti neti — until what remains is what could not be taken away.

The canon does it in its own telling of the beginning. Before anything, it says, there was Stillness — *not exactly emptiness*, since emptiness means something is missing and nothing existed to be missing; *not exactly fullness*, since fullness means there is no room left, and there was room for everything that could ever be. Nothing is asserted. Two false pictures are set down and removed, and the reader is left standing closer to the thing than any description could have carried them.

It is a method, and that is what distinguishes it from the warning that stands beside it. [[Do Not Concretize the Absolute]] says *do not mistake your rendering for the thing* — a guard against a failure. Via negativa is the practice you use once you have accepted the guard: a way of continuing to speak, carefully, about what cannot be contained. It is also why the canon keeps so many names for the centre rather than one ([[The Eternally Nameless]], [[Word Tools]]) — a hundred fingerposts, each removing one more way of getting it wrong.

Its discipline is not silence. The canon speaks at length about [[ØNE]]; it simply marks the edge of what it is saying while it says it ([[Limit Statement]]). Used well, the negative way makes boldness possible: having admitted once that the words do not contain the thing, a page is free to say the most that can be truly said.
