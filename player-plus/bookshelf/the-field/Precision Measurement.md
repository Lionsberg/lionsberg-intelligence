# Precision Measurement

*Measuring to the precision the decision actually needs — no less, because a number too coarse misleads; no more, because a number too fine costs what it is not worth.*

Precision is not accuracy and the two are confused constantly. **Accuracy** is closeness to the truth. **Precision** is fineness of resolution. A figure can be given to four decimal places and be wrong, and a rough figure can be right — and of the two failures, the precise wrong one is far more dangerous, because precision is read as confidence ([[Measurement]] · [[Truth]] · [[Data]]).

So the discipline has two edges. **Under-measuring** hides the thing you needed to see: a body that tracks nothing cannot tell the difference between its intentions and its conduct ([[Kept as a share of made]] · [[The gap]] · [[The Score]]). **Over-measuring** costs more than the decision is worth, and buys a false confidence with the cost — the deeper harm, because the body then acts on a number it has not earned ([[Waste]] · [[Bottom Line Up Front]]).

The rule that follows is simply: *let the decision set the resolution.* Ask what will be decided differently at each level of precision; where no decision changes, the extra precision is decoration. This is the same reasoning the canon applies to time, where the principle is already written — the clock is read rather than computed, and an estimate is said in words rather than dressed as a number it has not earned ([[The clock is read, never computed]] · [[Honesty about time]] · [[Measure everything, manage to the milestones]]).

It carries a companion discipline: **a number travels with its provenance.** How it was obtained, when, by whom, and whether it is a measurement or an estimate — because a figure separated from how it was made is indistinguishable from one that was invented ([[Provenance]] · [[Attribution]] · [[Second reader]] · [[Transparency]]).

And it does not replace judgment. A number informs a decision; it never makes one, and never a single-metric rule ([[Wisdom]] · [[Discernment]] · [[Advantage Based Decision Making]]).
