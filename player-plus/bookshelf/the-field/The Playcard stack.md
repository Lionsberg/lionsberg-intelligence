# The Playcard stack

*One card per Turn, kept in a pile, so a season can be read backwards.*

In this reading the Playcard is a card a Player fills. Its upper half arrives from above — the Goal, the moment, the milestones, the stretch of road this player's part touches. Its lower half is written by the player at each [[The Turn|Turn]]: what I said I would do, what I did, what I learned, what I will do next. At the next Turn a fresh card is taken and the old one goes on the pile.

The pile is the point. A single card says what this week is. Thirteen cards in order say what a season *was* — where the Quest actually went, which promises kept coming back unkept, the week everything changed, the stretch where nothing moved and nobody noticed. No summary produces that, because a summary is written by someone who already knows how the story ended ([[The Record]], [[Retrospective]], [[Look-back]]).

It is load-bearing because it is the cheapest honest memory a person can keep. A stack of cards on a shelf needs no device, no account, and no permission, and it cannot be quietly edited after the fact. At the turning a Player reads their own stack before saying anything about the season, and a Cell that reads its members' stacks together learns things about itself that no one member could have reported ([[The Season Sheet]], [[Kept as a share of made]], [[The gap]]).

The discipline is that it is filled at the Turn, not reconstructed at the turning. A card written from memory three months later is a story about oneself; a card written on the day is evidence. And the stack belongs to the player who wrote it: nothing on it travels without their word ([[Sovereignty]], [[TimeEnergy]]).

**Open:** The canon holds two Playcards under one name and they are not the same object. Two term pages state flatly that the Playcard is *received with the Flame and never filled in*; the seed and the Operating System describe one filled at every Turn and stacked. This page carries the seed's reading and names the collision: either one reading goes, or the two senses are named as two objects — a card that is received and a card that is kept.
