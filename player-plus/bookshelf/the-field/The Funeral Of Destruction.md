# The Funeral Of Destruction

*Every act of destruction is followed by a funeral — witness, mourning, report, abstention, return — and the guardian who skips it is the one whose acts accumulate.*

What is owed afterward is not a feeling. It is a sequence, and it is owed whether or not anyone would have known.

**Witness.** The one who acted does not return to ordinary activity as though nothing happened. What was done is looked at, plainly, and not narrated into something easier.

**Mourning.** What is mourned is larger than the loss itself: also the field that could have existed if right relationship had held and the destruction had never been required. The one destroyed is held in ØNE's keeping regardless of what they had become ([[Grief]], [[Love Freely Withheld]]).

**Report.** To the [[Circle]], to the Elder, and to the [[Wisdom Council]] that holds authority over the engagement. The account is given to people with standing to judge it, not to sympathetic listeners ([[Accountability]], [[Transparency]], [[Oversight]]).

**Abstention.** A period set apart before ordinary life resumes — fasting, sleeping apart, and staying away from ordinary work for days, weeks, or months according to what was required. Lineages hold their own protocols of reintegration: practices, ceremonies, witnessed accountings ([[Rites of Passage]], [[Sabbath]]).

**Return.** The guardian is brought back into the body deliberately rather than drifting back.

The mechanism is stated as plainly as the sequence: what is not digested accumulates as residue and eventually compromises everything. And the diagnostic is the one that matters most — **numbness is drift, not resilience.** The one who has been required to act many times over decades is expected to become *more* present to it, not less; coherent grief is itself a discipline. The day it stops costing anything is the day the capacity has begun to change hands.

This is the specific mechanism that keeps a protective body from becoming a militarised one. A culture that celebrates those who destroyed produces more of them; a culture that mourns with them, holds them accountable, and honours them by sustained presence rather than applause produces fewer destructions and more healings ([[Restraint's Two Tells]]).
