# The smoke test

*One account is an anomaly. Two, pay attention. Three, look into it. Five or ten of the same shape about the same body, and there is almost certainly a fire.*

It is a rule of thumb for a hard problem: how to weigh reports that are individually deniable. Any single account of harm by an institution can be explained away, and usually is — the person was difficult, the circumstances were unusual, the account is one-sided. All of that may even be true. The test says the deniability of each one is not the question; the **shape and number** of them is ([[Testimony]], [[Sensemaking]], [[Pattern Recognition]]).

Its discipline runs in both directions, which is what keeps it from being a licence to believe anything. It refuses the demand for proof before attention — one account is enough to attend to. And it refuses conclusion from a single case — one account is not enough to conclude ([[Navigating Towards Truth]], [[We Do Not Know]], [[Discernment]], [[Second reader]]).

What it does for a person inside the situation is different from what it does for an observer. Inside, it is what lets someone stop treating their own case as a verdict on themselves: the same thing happening to strangers is information about a body, not about a character ([[The Accuser]], [[Shame]], [[Structures of Injustice]]).

What it does for a body is set a duty. If five accounts of the same shape have arrived and nobody has looked, the not-looking is now the finding ([[Necessity Of Accountability]], [[The Lahaina Principle]], [[Whistleblower Protection]], [[Requirement Of Transparency]]).

The numbers are a rule of thumb and nothing more — offered by their source as judgment, not as a threshold anyone derived, and they will be wrong for a small body and wrong again for a very large one ([[Honesty about time]], [[Proportionality]]).
