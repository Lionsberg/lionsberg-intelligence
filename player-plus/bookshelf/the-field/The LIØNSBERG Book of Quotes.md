# The LIØNSBERG Book of Quotes

*Wisdom compressed into lines short enough to be read aloud in a Circle, written on a wall, and carried in the heart.*

Some things are best held as a paragraph and some as a sentence. The Book of Quotes is where the sentences live: lines gathered from every age and tradition that say something true in a form a person can actually carry — into a hard week, into a gathering, into stillness. It is one of the entries in the library, and it is meant to be used aloud rather than studied.

It is gathered without attribution, and the reason is stated on its face: not to erase lineage, but to honor the wisdom itself. The wisdom is the wisdom whether or not we name who carried it; every line in it has been borne forward through many hands across many ages, and what matters is whether it speaks true, and whether it speaks into this moment, this Circle, this turning of the world.

That practice is the same instinct as Chatham House pointed at the past instead of the present, and it sits in tension with the discipline of provenance that governs everything else here. The tension is worth naming rather than resolving too quickly: a quotation used as an authority needs its source, and a quotation used as a spark does not.

Used well, the book is a practical instrument. A line read at the opening of a gathering does work that an explanation cannot, because it arrives already compressed and leaves room for the room to fill it.
