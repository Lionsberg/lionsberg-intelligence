# Narrative Lens

*The story-shaped frame through which a person or a people perceives — not what they look at, but what they are able to see.*

Perception is not reception. Everyone arrives midway into a [[Story]] already unfolding and reads what happens next through the frame that story supplies: which events count, who the actors are, what a setback means, what would constitute a win. Two people watching the same hour can honestly report different worlds, and neither is lying. The lens is doing the work.

It is load-bearing because it explains why argument so often fails and why [[Story]] is treated as the operating layer rather than as ornament. You cannot correct a lens with a fact; the fact is read through the lens and comes out the far side changed. What does change a lens is a better story that accounts for more of what a person already knows — which is why [[Testimony]] and [[Forward-Looking Story]] are the instruments they are, and why the old order's grip was maintained by captured stories rather than by argument. It is also why [[The Lens]] exists as its own named discipline: if everything is seen from somewhere, the honest move is to say where you are standing rather than to claim you are not.

In play it becomes a habit of asking. When a Circle cannot agree, the disagreement is often not about the facts but about which story the facts are in — a missed [[Commitment]] read as betrayal by one member and as ordinary human weather by another. [[Sensemaking]] and [[Dialogos]] are the instruments for surfacing that; [[OmniSpection]] is the seasonal version, deliberately done in company because no single vantage can see its own frame.

The discipline that keeps this from relativism: lenses differ, reality does not. Held as [[Current Best Articulation]], a lens is improvable, and it is improved by contact with what actually happens and with people who see differently — not by deciding that all seeings are equal.
