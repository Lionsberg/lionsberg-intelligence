# The de-concentration of stewardship

*A plan that permits no single point of failure, naming the one place it is presently violated — its own stewardship — and setting the hour at which that ends.*

Every movement that has ever mattered has had this problem and most have refused to say it. A body built on distributed sovereignty is, at its beginning, held together by a very small number of hands: the canon is tended by them, the planning function runs through them, the treasury path passes them, the instruments are theirs. That is not hypocrisy — early on there is no one else — but left unnamed it hardens into the thing the body exists to refuse ([[Centralization Of Power]], [[Institutional Idolatry]]).

The discipline is to say it plainly and to put a trigger on it. The nomination of the planning group by the first Co-Creators is named for what it actually is: the beginning of the deliberate distribution of stewardship itself — of the canon, of the coordinating function, of the treasury path, of the instruments — out of any one pair of hands and into the arrayed body ([[The System Wide Master Planning Group and Process]], [[The Calling of the Ecclesia]]). Until that day, continuity of every key and every duty is held redundantly, so that the Pattern never again depends on a single life.

Two things make this more than a promise. The first is that the trigger is structural rather than temporal — it fires when a threshold of people stand, not when a founder feels ready, which is precisely the decision founders are worst at making. The second is that the acceptance test is stated as a loss: if every instrument of the centre vanished tonight, the body continues. That is the design, and it is also the test — and a body that could not survive the disappearance of its stewards has not yet de-concentrated anything, whatever its charter says.
