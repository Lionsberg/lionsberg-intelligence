# Form Practice, And Beyond Form

*A form does six things at once — and then, absorbed completely, it dissolves into the action it was teaching all along. First learn the form; then forget the form.*

A form is a composed sequence: a kata, a flow, a cycle, a movement prayer, a liturgy. It is repeated until it is no longer a sequence the mind recalls but a movement the body performs — at which point the body has learned not the sequence but the patterns the sequence carried ([[Techniques Are Patterns, Forms Are Plays]]).

Six functions, running simultaneously. **Body-patterning** — the body learns the craft's vocabulary. **Field-coherence** — the cultivated movement organises the whole person. **Mind-clearing** — focused practice quiets the surface mind ([[Focused Attention As Discipline]]). **Lineage-transmission** — the form carries the accumulated refinement of everyone who walked it before, so practising it is participating in a transmission across generations ([[Lineage]], [[Transmission]]). **Diagnostic** — where the form breaks, the instrument has a weakness; the form shows what cultivation has not yet reached. **Communion** — walking it correctly puts a person in the felt company of everyone who has walked it.

The diagnostic function is the checkable one and the most immediately useful: a form is a mirror that does not flatter, available daily, and it reports a specific gap rather than a general feeling.

**Beyond form** is the stage past thorough absorption. In the moment, the form is no longer performed — the person *is* the form, and the right action arrives composed out of the pattern-vocabulary the years deposited in the body ([[Spontaneous Wise Right Action]]). The canon is careful about what the forgetting means: **not erasure — absorption so complete that conscious recall is unnecessary.** The alternative reading, that forms are for beginners and may be dropped early, is the misreading the phrase invites and the canon closes.

Two honest limits. Most people take many years to reach this in even one form, most never reach it in all their forms, and masters often reach it in one or two over a lifetime. And the reaching is not the point; the walking is — the reaching is what arrives when the walking has been sustained ([[Shu-ha-ri]], [[Mastery]], [[The Practice Cadence]]).
