# The Limit Story

*A story pushed all the way to the conceptual absolute — and then offered to you as your own aim.*

Something particular is noticed here that the great texts do. They do not offer a good example; they push the story to the limit — to perfection, to the end of the line, to the thing beyond which nothing further can be conceived — and then invite the reader to adopt that as their aim.

The reasoning behind it is a chain made explicit here. If a person is going to generate an array of possible stories, evaluate them, and consciously choose one to live toward, then there is only one thing it makes sense to aim at: **the best and highest.** Anything short of it was chosen by picking a stopping point, and the stopping point will be wherever imagination or nerve ran out ([[Worthy Goal]], [[Vision]], [[Forward-Looking Story]]).

Its own inherited texts read this way: the limit story of *being* is the Absolute itself; the limit story of *doing* is that Absolute's intention fully realised here; the limit story of suffering and triumph is the one who embodied it under the worst conditions. You cannot push any of them further — that is what makes them limit stories ([[Ideal]], [[Archetype]], [[The True Myth]]).

**Why aim at something unreachable.** Because an aim is a direction, not a destination, and the direction is what organises everything below it. An aim set at what seems achievable becomes a ceiling the moment it is met; an aim set at the limit never stops calling ([[The Sacred Ascent]], [[Continuous Improvement]], [[The Zone Of Optimum Development]]).

And the invitation at the end, made carefully: as imperfect as anyone presently is, each is invited to share in it and in the making of the best possible future for all. The limit story is not held over a person as a standard they are failing. It is handed to them as an aim they are permitted to take ([[Co-Creating Heaven]], [[Your Story Woven Into Meta Story]]).
