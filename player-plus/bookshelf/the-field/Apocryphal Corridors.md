# Apocryphal Corridors

*A register kept deliberately apart from the claims: what is not established, what is only suspected, and — the part that makes it honest — what would establish it.*

Every inquiry accumulates material that is interesting and unsupported. Two things are usually done with it, and both are bad. It is **discarded**, and with it the leads that might have gone somewhere. Or it is **smuggled in**, phrased carefully enough to sit beside the evidenced material, where it quietly borrows that material's credibility ([[Provenance]], [[We Do Not Know]], [[Elision]]).

The register is the third option: keep it, name it, and put a wall around it. Inside the wall, a thing may be recorded because it is worth following; outside it, nothing may be asserted that is not supported ([[The seven-part UnVeiling form]], [[Limit Statement]], [[Patterns Pending Articulation]]).

The requirement that makes it a discipline rather than a dustbin is the second half: **what would establish it**. An entry that names what evidence would settle the question is a research task. An entry that does not is a rumour with a better filing system ([[Hypothesize]], [[The Five Verification Filters and the Spectrum of Knowing]], [[Navigating Towards Truth]]).

It is a near relative of the open ground this corpus keeps for what is true and homeless — with one difference that matters. That ground holds what is *believed true and has no home*; this holds what is *not established at all*. Merging them would destroy both ([[The Field (of the Book), and the law of no loss]], [[Gaps Not Yet Closed]], [[Work In Progress]]).

Its own source is the strongest argument for it and against its use there: the corridors in those pages hold the least supported material in the whole corpus, and the wall is not always kept. A register that leaks is worse than none, because it launders ([[Second reader]], [[Discernment]], [[Semantic Capture]]).
