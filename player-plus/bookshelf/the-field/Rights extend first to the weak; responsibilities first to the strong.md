# Rights extend first to the weak; responsibilities first to the strong

*The double inversion that makes rights-talk operational: the vulnerable are covered first, the capable are burdened first.*

All individuals flow from one [[Source]] and are members of one family and one community of life; the rights and responsibilities that come with that membership are equal and inalienable to [[All]]. That is the ground. What is unusual is the order in which they are applied, and the order is deliberately the reverse of the old world's.

**Rights extend first to the weakest and most vulnerable.** Not to those best able to claim them, best able to afford the claiming, or loudest in the room. A society is measured at its edge, and a right that arrives last where it is needed most is a privilege with a right's name on it.

**Responsibilities extend first to the strongest and most capable.** It is the moral duty of those able to exercise power to do so in service of helping, protecting and lifting up all, beginning with the weakest. **The strongest, most intelligent and most capable bear the overwhelming burden of the responsibility and accountability that all flourish, without exception.** Capability is not a claim on more; it is a claim on you.

The two halves are one move, and they are load-bearing for a reason that is easy to miss. Consent-based governance, voluntary association, sovereignty at every scale — every instrument this body uses is neutral about power, and a neutral instrument in an unequal room serves whoever already has more. This is the rule that stops that: it says in advance which way the weight leans, so that [[Consent]] cannot quietly become a shelter for the powerful and [[Sovereignty]] cannot become the right to be left alone with one's advantages. It is the unstated assumption beneath the [[The waterfall|value waterfall]], the [[The asset lock|asset lock]], and the seat held for those who cannot speak.

It also answers the ordinary objection to strength. Strength is not suspect here and not levelled; it is *directed*. Whoever is capable of great power is not asked to be less, but to carry more — which is the same posture the Guardian holds under [[Dangerous But Good]], stated as a rule of society rather than of character.
