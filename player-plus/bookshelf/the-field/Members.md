# Members

*A word the canon has retired: the early name for the people of this body, since replaced by Co-Creators.*

The page exists to record a change rather than to define a term, and the change is worth keeping visible because the reason for it is the substance.

*Member* is the word an organisation uses for the people on its roll. It describes a status — you are in, or you are not — and it implies a body that exists independently and admits you to it. Everything that follows from that framing is slightly wrong here: membership can be granted and revoked by someone; it invites the question of who is in charge of the register; and it says nothing whatever about what the person does.

*Co-Creator* names an act rather than a status. It says the body is being made, that the one so named is making it, and that there is no version of belonging that consists only of being on a list ([[Co-Creation]] · [[Participation]] · [[Meaningful Work]] · [[Player]]). It also fits the rest of the structure: a [[Cell]] has no membership office to admit anyone, and belonging there is constituted by a [[Field of Agreements]] and by the work, not by a record ([[Belonging]] · [[Agreement]] · [[Consent]]).

The retirement is partial in practice and honesty requires saying so. *Member* is still the ordinary word in law and in most bodies a reader will meet, the canon's own association form carries it, and older pages across the corpus still use it ([[Private Ecclesiastical Voluntary Membership Association]] · [[Citizenship]] · [[Living Systems Member]]).

Recording a superseded word rather than deleting it is itself the practice: the wiki holds the present sense of a concept, and where a word has been replaced, the page says so and points onward, so that a reader meeting the old word in an old document knows what happened ([[The Wiki Reflects the Present]] · [[Weave, don't delete]] · [[Naming]] · [[A Word on Words]]).
