# The one question

*Before every move: does this make life a little more like Heaven? If it does, make the move. If it does not, stop, discern, and choose a better one.*

One sentence, asked of the actual move in front of you. A ten-year-old can ask it; so can a tribe. It is a compass that always points toward [[The Goal]], and it does not change when the fashions, the strategies, or the leaders do. *A little more* is deliberate: the question does not ask whether a move achieves Heaven, only whether it moves in its direction, which is what makes it askable of a bus timetable and a peace treaty alike.

It is load-bearing because it is the whole decision procedure a player must carry. Everything else in the Game — the moves, the cards, the sheets — assumes that the people using them are pointing the right way. Without the question the instruments will faithfully deliver something nobody wanted. With it, a table needs no policy manual to refuse a move that is obviously wrong, and no permission to make one that is obviously right.

How it is played. Ask it before, not after. Ask it of the specific move, not of the general aim, because the general aim always sounds good. Take the *no* seriously: stop, discern, choose again is a three-part instruction and the middle part is where the work is. And when two moves both pass, the older players ask a second question — *is this the highest thing we could do right now, for ourselves and for the whole?* Not every good move is the next move; that is where [[Critical Path]] and the zone of optimum development enter what is otherwise a child's question.
