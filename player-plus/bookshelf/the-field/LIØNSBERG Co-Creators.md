# LIØNSBERG Co-Creators

*The plural authorship this work is attributed to and continues through — an array of people who brought lifetimes of work to one table, and through whom it goes on after any of them.*

The word is doing something specific. Not *founders*, which implies a moment and a claim. Not *members*, which implies a list. **Co-Creators** names the relation: people who brought what they had already spent a life making, put it on a shared table, and let it be changed by what else was there ([[Co-Creation]], [[Convergent Streams of Discovery]], [[Synergy]], [[Weaving vs. Starting New]]).

That plural is load-bearing for the trust argument rather than decorative. A body attributed to one person is inherited by whoever holds that person's place; a body attributed to many, none of whom owns it, has nothing at its centre to inherit — which is exactly what the corpus means when it says the work survived its founder ([[Dissolution by prior design]], [[The Foundation of Trust (the four promises)]], [[LIØNSBERG Is A Prototype, Not The Eternal Essence]]).

It also sets how credit works here. Ideas travel and attribution does not accompany them as ownership; what a person brought is honoured, and what it became belongs to whoever takes it up ([[Attribution]], [[Honoring Lineage Without Lineage-Capture]], [[Author's Voice Is Sovereign]], [[Truth To ØNE, Errors To Us]]).

And it is open rather than historical. The term reaches forward as much as back: whoever takes the work up is one, and the stewardship keeps distributing itself away by design so that no single life is ever again a single point of failure ([[The de-concentration of stewardship]], [[Pass The Flame]], [[The Charge Of The Receiver]], [[Keepers Of The Way]]).

No roster is kept here, and that is the rule rather than an omission: a list of who counts is exactly the thing a body attributed to nobody must not have, and naming people is theirs to do and not ours ([[Consent]], [[Disclosure tier]], [[The Atlas, Commentaries, and Codex Keepers]]).
