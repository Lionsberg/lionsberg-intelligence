# The 7 Commitments of Every Co-Creator

*What a person affirms on entering — seven, and each of them a doing rather than a belief.*

**One: join as a sovereign individual.** Stand as free and autonomous, and contribute what only you can ([[Sovereignty]], [[Inexhaustible Individuals]]). **Two: hold the shared values and the shared form.** Truth, love, justice, stewardship, wisdom, co-creation — and the small set of conventions that let separate bodies read one another ([[Values]], [[Minimum standards of interoperability]]). **Three: form a Cell, keep the shared rhythm, and tell what happened.** ([[Cell]], [[The Clock of the Game]], [[Share the Stories]]). **Four: give the first tenth to the commons.** A share of your time and of your resources, so that mutual aid and shared services are actually funded ([[Voluntary Tithing]], [[The Offering]], [[Commons]]). **Five: pass the Flame.** Find those who are waking, tell them, invite them ([[Pass The Flame]]). **Six: walk the ascent.** Develop toward the fullness of your potential by helping everyone else do the same ([[The Sacred Ascent]], [[The Sovereign Path]]). **Seven: federate everything, and act with integrity.** Pursue what you pursue through connected bodies rather than alone, so that every success enriches the whole — and do the wise right thing, love one another, do no harm ([[Federation]], [[Integrity]], [[Spontaneous Wise Right Action]]).

It is load-bearing because it is the shortest complete answer to *what am I actually agreeing to*. A person can read it in a minute, weigh it honestly, and say yes or no with their eyes open — which is the only kind of yes worth anything ([[Consent]], [[The Invitation]], [[Field of Agreements]]).

Its tone is deliberate, and the canon states it: these are not burdens but invitations, and what is offered alongside them is the tools, the guidance, and the company needed to keep them. A list of obligations handed to someone standing alone is a different document entirely ([[Mutual Aid]], [[ØNE Body]], [[The Force For Good]]).
