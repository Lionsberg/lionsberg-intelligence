# A3 Reports

*The named instruments for learning from what happened: one page per problem, the five whys, stop–keep–start, lessons written where the next work will meet them.*

An A3 is a whole problem on a single page — background, current condition, the goal, the analysis, the countermeasure, the check. Its discipline is the size of the paper: a problem that cannot be stated on one page has not yet been understood. Beside it sit the other instruments. **The five whys**, asking *why* down through the symptoms until a cause is reached that can actually be acted on. **Root cause analysis**, its wider name. **Stop, keep, start**, the three columns a body fills together at a turning. **Lessons learned**, which only count once they are embedded.

They are load-bearing because a gap that is felt and not examined teaches nothing. [[The gap]] supplies the material; these supply the method; [[Every Defect Is A Gift]] supplies the posture that makes it safe to bring one. Cause is sought in the set-up before the person — a missed commitment traced honestly usually lands on a constraint that was never logged, and blaming the committor would have hidden it.

They are held lightly. Asking *why* five times by rote stops at symptoms; investigators find only causes they can already imagine; and insisting on a single root cause can be wrong where several are real. These are means, not ends — instruments that challenge a body to look deeper, and that fail whenever they become a form to fill in.
