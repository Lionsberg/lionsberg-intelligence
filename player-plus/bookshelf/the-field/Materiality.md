# Materiality

*What is worth counting, and what is not — the threshold beneath every measure.*

Before any measurement there is a prior judgment nobody usually makes out loud: does this matter enough to track? Everything cannot be counted, and a body that tries drowns in its own bookkeeping; a body that counts too little cannot see itself at all. Materiality is the name for that threshold, and for the act of setting it consciously rather than by habit ([[Measurement]], [[Waste]], [[Minutes are the one unit]]).

It is load-bearing because thresholds decide outcomes far more quietly than measures do. What falls below the line becomes invisible, and what is invisible is treated as free — which is precisely how the old economy came to treat soil, water, air, care, and attention. Nothing was decided against them; they simply never crossed anyone's threshold ([[Extraction Of Wealth]], [[The six capitals]], [[Tragedy of the Commons]]).

The canon's own use of the word runs deeper than accounting. It asks that the whole material basis of our existence be re-examined, re-imagined, and re-integrated within the boundaries and principles of the living system — and that this requires consciously balancing energy within each locality, rather than importing it from somewhere nobody looks ([[Living System]], [[Bioregion]], [[Regeneration]], [[Energy]]).

Its discipline is that the threshold is stated. A measure whose materiality is unwritten looks objective and is not: someone chose what counts, and if they did not say so, nobody can argue with them. Saying it converts an invisible judgment into a decision a body can revisit ([[Transparency]], [[Decision Records]], [[Honesty about time]]).
