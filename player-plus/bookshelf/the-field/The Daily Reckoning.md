# The Daily Reckoning

*Three questions at the close of the day: where did I walk in alignment, where did I walk out of it, and what does tomorrow ask of me.*

A minute or two, costing nothing. The first question is asked first on purpose — a day is not accounted for honestly by a person who only looks for what they got wrong, and a practice that becomes self-accusation is abandoned within a month. The second is asked plainly, without excuse and without shame; naming a drift is what makes it a small thing. The third turns the whole reckoning forward, so that the day closes by handing something to the next one rather than by scoring the one just spent.

What makes it structural rather than merely wholesome is what it is the seed of. **This is the simplest form of what at scale becomes the body's [[Networks of Commitments]]** — the discipline of noticing what was promised and what was kept, what was given and what was withheld, what is asked next. Done daily by one person it tightens that person's relationship with their own [[Integrity]]; done in [[Cell|Circle]] it tightens the body; done across a whole body it is the entire said-and-did ledger the [[The Turn|weekly Turn]] and the [[The Commitment Tracker|commitment tracker]] formalise. The practice and the instrument are the same shape at two scales.

It pairs naturally with the morning's [[Silence And Stillness As The Ground|silence]] — the two can be stacked into one rhythm with a day between them — and it belongs to the family of small repeating disciplines that do, over years, what no single dramatic act can.

The temptation is to skip it when the day was hard. Hard days are precisely when the reckoning yields the most.
