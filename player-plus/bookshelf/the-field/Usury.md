# Usury

*Parasitic extraction through interest-bearing debt — the mechanism by which claims grow faster than the capacity to satisfy them.*

Usury is not simply a high rate. It is the structural arrangement in which money lent must return more money than was lent, compounding, without limit, and without the lender bearing the risk of what the borrowing was for. The arithmetic is the problem: claims grow by a percentage every period, while soil, hours, and living systems do not. Over enough periods the claims exceed the real capacity of the body they are claims against, and something must give — default, inflation, enclosure, or the quiet transfer of everything unpledged into the hands of whoever holds the paper.

The old traditions named this before anyone had a model of it, and they named it as a moral matter because its effects are moral: it converts misfortune into permanent dependency, it makes a debtor's time the property of a creditor, and it requires perpetual growth of a finite world to stay upright. [[Manufactured Dependency]] and [[Extraction Of Wealth]] are its two visible faces. The demand that the economy grow forever is not greed but arithmetic, and it is why the old order cannot stop.

Nothing here forbids one person helping another with resources and being made whole. The line the Pattern draws is between **rent and reign**. Capital that helped may be honoured and repaid with a fair, limited return — and then it stops. It never converts into ownership, never into control, never into an unbounded claim. That line is held structurally, not by good intentions: [[The asset lock]] caps what anyone may take out, [[The waterfall]] pays the people who did the work before any return to capital, and [[Capital Subordinate To Purpose]] states the principle the other two enforce.

[[Jubilee]] is the release valve the tradition supplies for what accumulates anyway, and the reason it is periodic rather than occasional: a system that compounds needs a reset built into its calendar, not left to catastrophe.
