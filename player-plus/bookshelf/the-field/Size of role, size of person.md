# Size of role, size of person

*Match a role to the time and capacity the person actually has this season, and keep the two within about a notch of each other.*

A role two sizes larger than the person holding it fails them and fails the body — not through any lack of will, but because the hours were never there. A role two sizes smaller wastes a gift the body needed. So the sizing is done out loud, at the season's start, against real capacity rather than hoped-for capacity, and it is redone each turning because people's seasons change ([[Roles]], [[Season]]).

This is a kindness with a structural purpose. It removes the most common cause of a body quietly stalling — one overloaded person who cannot say so — and it puts the cause where it belongs, in the set-up rather than in the person ([[Zero Blame Environment]]). It also makes rotation survivable: a role scaled to an ordinary person can be handed to another ordinary person, which a heroic role cannot ([[Term Limits And Rotation]], [[The Two-Coordinator Rhythm]]).

The sizing is inseparable from the currency of the thing. What a role costs is time, energy, and attention, and those are the irreducible stock of an embodied life ([[TimeEnergy]]). Asking for more than someone has is asking for a [[Commitment]] that cannot be kept, which spends the one thing the Game runs on — a word given and a word kept.
