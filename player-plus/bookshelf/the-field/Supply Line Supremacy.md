# Supply Line Supremacy

*Empire's actual object is not land but lines. Whoever controls what moves, and the routes it moves along, governs everyone who depends on the moving — without needing to govern them.*

The reading is historical and the pattern is the point. Legions did not make an empire; the ships and routes that kept the legions supplied did. Sea power displaced land power once ships could carry trade further than armies could march. The age of conquest opened again when navigation improved enough to reach the whole of a round world. Each time, the contest was over the lines ([[The Game of Empire and Oligarchy]], [[Supply Chains]], [[Power]]).

Why it works so well is that it needs no consent and produces no visible ruler. A people whose food, fuel, medicine or money arrives along a line somebody else owns is governed by whoever owns it, whatever its own constitution says — and will experience the governing as weather ([[Manufactured Dependency]], [[Power Over]], [[Centralization Of Power]]).

The counter is not a better line. It is **local capability**: a place that can grow, make and mend enough of what it needs is not available to be held this way, and the bar is lower than it sounds — a body does not have to be self-sufficient, only un-strangleable ([[Self-Sustenance and Self-Replication]], [[Individual And Local]], [[Bioregion]], [[Structural Immunity]], [[Anti-Fragility]]).

Which is also the test to run on any arrangement offered as convenient. What would fail here, and how fast, if that line stopped? A dependency nobody can name is a dependency nobody is managing ([[Limiting Factor]], [[Bottleneck]], [[Theory Of Constraints]], [[Technology Sovereignty]]).

And it is why this corpus keeps building things that degrade to paper and hand, and why value is designed to move peer to peer rather than through a centre ([[Centers of Distribution]], [[Minimum standards of interoperability]], [[Screens and paper]]).

The source tells this as narrative history, naming monarchs, firms and battles. The pattern crosses; the particulars are the source's and are not carried here.
