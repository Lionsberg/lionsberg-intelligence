# First, Do No Harm

*The first and surest way to become better is to cease from doing harm.*

It is a claim about order. Improvement is usually imagined as addition — new practices, new projects, new good works — and the chapter says the first move is subtraction, because it is surer. Ceasing a harm is reliable in a way that adding a good is not: you know it worked, it takes effect immediately, it costs nothing but the stopping, and it does not depend on anyone else's cooperation. Most people and most bodies have something available to them under this heading right now.

The chapter names five fields, and the sequence runs from the innermost outward. Stop poisoning the **spirit** — what you take in, what you say, what you consent to be shaped by. Stop poisoning the **mind** — the streams that fragment attention and leave it operated by someone else ([[Focused Attention As Discipline]], [[Gathering and Integrating Energy]]). Stop poisoning the **body** — what is eaten, and what is not moved ([[Nutrition]], [[Moving]], [[Tending]]). Stop poisoning the **society** — the small betrayals, the words that corrode trust, the arrangements that quietly extract ([[Speaking Truth In Love]], [[Usury]], [[Right Relationship]]). Stop poisoning the **air, water and soil** ([[Earth]], [[Watershed]], [[Regeneration]]).

Then the chapter's own conditional, which is where the hope in it sits: *if only we could cease doing that which harms, and find a way to help — a little at first, and then a little more* — all things would become a little better every day and flourish in harmony. Note the modesty of the second half. Cease first; then help, small, and growing ([[Continuous Improvement]], [[The Living Gradient]]).

Two things keep it from curdling. It is not a doctrine of inaction: ceasing harm is the floor, not the whole, and a body that only ever stops things has not begun ([[Throughput]], [[Restraint As The Refusal To Act]]). And it is directed at oneself first. Turned outward as an accusation it becomes the most efficient instrument of self-righteousness there is; turned inward it is the cheapest improvement available ([[Zero Blame Environment]], [[We Do Not Know]]).

The phrase is inherited — it is the oldest of the healing oaths, said in many forms in many places — and it is used here at the scale of a whole life rather than a consultation.
