# Ontological Shock

*What happens to a person, and to a people, when the pillars of their understanding — history, science, religion — are unmasked as partial truths or as deliberate veils.*

It is not the shock of learning a new fact. It is the shock of learning that the frame the facts were held in was wrong: that the account of where we came from was edited, that a field of knowledge was steered, that an institution trusted with the sacred was also managing it. What gives way is not one belief but the floor under a whole set of them, and with it the ordinary confidence that the world is roughly as described.

The two outcomes are direct. The same revelation either fractures a collective psyche or ignites its rebirth, and which one happens is not determined by the content of the revelation. It turns on preparation — whether there is a story large enough to receive the new information, a body of people to hold the person while the floor is replaced, and a way forward that does not require the old frame ([[Story]], [[Cell]], [[The Way]]).

This makes it a design constraint rather than only a feeling. Anyone who intends to unveil anything is responsible for the landing: what is said, in what order, to whom, and with what accompanying. Disclosure without a container produces casualties and then a backlash that closes the door for a generation ([[The Great UnVeiling]], [[Disclosure tier]], [[Guidance]]).

It is distinct from the trauma named elsewhere. [[Trauma]] and the engineered use of it describe harm done *to* a person to control them; ontological shock is the injury of finding out — the cost of the veil coming off, borne even where no one intended harm. The counsel is to treat the shocked with the care given to the wounded: pacing, company, honesty about what is not yet known ([[We Do Not Know]]), and the plain statement that the ache of it is passage rather than pathology ([[The Refining]], [[Coming Home]]).
