# Fibonacci

*The sequence in which each number is the sum of the two before it — one, two, three, five, eight, thirteen — and the shape this body uses for how it grows and how it makes.*

Two uses run side by side. As proportion, the ratio the sequence approaches appears throughout growth and packing in the natural world and has been used by makers for millennia; it is the working core of [[Sacred Geometry]] and the most portable answer available to *what size should this be relative to that*. As a growth model, the sequence describes propagation by relationship rather than by broadcast: each carrier, having received, passes to the next, and the total rises by addition at every step while no centre grows at all.

It is load-bearing because it is the arithmetic of [[Pass The Flame]]. One, two, three, five, eight, thirteen, twenty-one, thirty-four, fifty-five, eighty-nine, one hundred forty-four — the image of living growth, and the measure of it: each term is a ring of the [[LIØNSBERG Cycles of Growth and Cycle Time|Cycles of Growth]], and the ring the body stands on, with days per ring, is read daily. For the first three percent the tactic that moves the body through the rings is a plainer rule, every new Player bringing their three, as their first passing of the Flame — about two and a quarter rings for every generation of three ([[29 The Plan — the first three percent#The chart|the chart by phase]]). That is why the instruction is *propagate, do not scale*: nothing grows by making the centre larger, and one [[Cell]] planting the next is the only mechanism that ever could reach [[The First Three Percent]].

In play, the sequence is also the shape of a [[Cell]] itself — three to thirteen people, optimally four to seven, dividing at thirteen the way a cell does. And it is a caution as much as a promise. The arithmetic assumes faithful passing; real Circles miss, stall, and stop. The model describes the ceiling of what is possible, not a forecast, and timing estimates written from it have already been overtaken.

Held honestly, then: a proportion worth building to, a growth shape worth organizing around, and a set of numbers that should never be mistaken for a plan.
