# Scarcity

*The imposition or illusion of lack — most often manufactured, and always useful to whoever is imposing it.*

Scarcity is the belief that there is not enough, and the arrangements that make the belief true. It is named here as a lie not because limits do not exist but because the lack most people live inside is produced rather than found: food destroyed to hold a price, housing empty beside people sleeping outside, knowledge fenced behind a paywall it costs nothing to copy, medicine priced at what desperation will bear. The world's real constraints are seldom the ones pressing on a given life.

Manufactured scarcity is a control technology, and it is worth seeing the mechanism plainly. Scarcity makes people compete instead of combine. It makes a wage into a threat. It converts a [[Commons]] into a market by first enclosing it. It licenses hoarding as prudence and calls circulation naivety. And it is self-confirming: once enough people believe there is not enough, they behave in ways that ensure there is not — which is the real content of [[Tragedy of the Commons]], a story about unowned resources with no governance, not about sharing as such.

The counter is structural, not attitudinal. Believing in [[Abundance]] changes nothing by itself; building the arrangements that produce it does. [[Commons]] with real governance, so the shared thing has stewards rather than free riders. [[Voluntary Tithing]], so every table has something to draw on. [[Fostering Ecosystemic Resource Flows]], so surplus moves to need instead of piling at a centre. [[Jubilee]], so accumulated claims do not compound past the point of repair. Each one removes a condition scarcity needs.

The everyday form is worth naming too. A Cell holds this test: before accepting that something cannot be done for lack of resources, ask whether the resource is truly absent or merely stuck. Most of the time it is stuck — held, unnoticed, unrecognized, or unasked for. That is the honest starting point, and it is also the hopeful one.
