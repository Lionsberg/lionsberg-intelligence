# The names of the interface's parts

*One short list of plain words, each naming one thing a hand can point at, so that what a Player saw can be said exactly.*

The rail is the column down one side, always there, and the way to every panel. A rail item is one entry on it; a badge is the small count beside that entry saying how many things there need you. The foot of the rail carries the name — who you are in the Game, which every action you take carries with it.

A panel is one section of the main area. Its panel head is its title line, with the few actions that belong to the whole panel. Inside a panel sits a row: one item — an answer, a reply, a sighting, a conversation, a commitment, a card — acted on where it is. A pill is a small word on a row saying its state or its kind. A tile is a big number with a caption: days remaining, minutes on a chain, commitments overdue. A form is where you write.

Beneath all of it: the deck is every piece of work as a card, and every panel that shows work is a sort of the deck. A card is one piece of work with its record and its thread. A thread is a conversation anchored to a card, a page, or to nothing at all. A [[Sighting]] is what you saw on the road, handed in from the panel head where you saw it.

No word here is coined and none is clever. That is the point: the list is taught on the surface itself, and it is short enough that a person can hold the whole of it after one reading. A vocabulary a Player must study is a wall ([[A Word on Words]]).
