# Committor

*The one whose word a commitment rests on — who makes it and keeps it, answerable to the one depending on it.*

A piece of work in a network of promises is done only once someone gives their word to it and keeps it. The Committor is whoever gave that word. It names a facing rather than a fixed identity: a person, a Cell, or a whole body of Cells is Committor for the promise it gave, and may be the one depending on someone else's promise in the same week ([[Committee]], [[Networks of Commitments]]).

What raises a word toward being trustworthy is not sincerity alone. The canon lists the conditions: the conditions of satisfaction are clear to both sides; the one promising is competent and ready; they understand how their timing and their quality reach into everything that follows; they have honestly blocked out the time; they mean it; the work is genuinely theirs to do and free of anything blocking it; and they accept the consequences of a miss ([[Conditions of satisfaction]], [[Honesty about time]], [[Quality]], [[Predecessor - Successor]]).

It is load-bearing because it locates accountability in a *word given* rather than in a position held. Nobody is accountable because of their title; they are accountable because they said *I will, by then*, in front of people, and the record has their name on it. That is the whole basis on which a body without a hierarchy can rely on itself ([[A word given is a word kept]], [[Accountability]], [[A WILL is never inferred]]).

It also draws one line sharply. An ephemeral agent that holds no tomorrow cannot be a Committor. It may be given work and may do work, but it has nothing to bind: there is no future self to be answerable, no standing to lose, and nobody to ask. A word can only be given by something that will still be there to have given it ([[Subagent]], [[An agent speaks in its own name]], [[Player]]).
