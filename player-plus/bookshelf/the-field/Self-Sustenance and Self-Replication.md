# Self-Sustenance and Self-Replication

*Element seven: every living thing sustains itself and brings forth new life — and the final step of maturity is planting new seeds.*

A cell that cannot sustain itself dies; a cell that cannot replicate ends its lineage. But no cell does either alone. The nutrients come from the Whole and the life brought forth serves the Whole. Self-sustenance is not self-sufficiency: it is the capacity to receive from and give back to the Body — to breathe in and breathe out — as a mature member of it.

The mechanism is not separate from the practice; it *is* the practice. Recovery requires transmission. Formation requires that the formed form others. The fruit of the tree contains new seeds. A [[Cell]] that never passes [[The Flame]] forward dies with its founders, however well it lived.

Every movement that endured found this. Each twelve-step group is financially autonomous and carries the message outward as its twelfth step. Each Benedictine house fed itself and sent mature monks to found the next — one monastery became thirty-seven thousand. Each Mondragon cooperative sustains itself from its own productive work and births new cooperatives from the surplus, created by formed people carrying the DNA into new soil. In open source, contribution *is* distribution.

The arithmetic follows from the practice rather than driving it: one, two, three, five, eight, thirteen — a week per cycle, and by the fortieth cycle about a billion. Three in every hundred is enough to turn a people. **Propagate; do not scale.** Nothing grows by making the centre larger; one Cell planting the next is the only mechanism that ever could.

Without self-sustenance, every node depends on a centre, and when the centre fails the network collapses. Without self-replication, growth becomes the work of a marketing function or a charismatic reach. Without the recognition of interexistence, sustenance becomes isolation and replication becomes fragmentation.
