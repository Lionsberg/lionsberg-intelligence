# Authority derived from responsibility

*Nobody gave us permission. The authority to act comes from the responsibility we have to [[ØNE]] and [[All]] to do the wise right thing — and it grows in direct proportion to how many choose to participate.*

Asked *who gave you permission*, the honest answer is nobody, and the honest follow-up is that the question assumes a world in which legitimacy is issued. This is a different derivation. Where a body sees a need it can meet, a problem it can solve, or a goal this moment in history plainly sets, the responsibility to act is already present; the authority to act is what that responsibility carries with it. No office confers it and no office can withdraw it.

Three things keep this from becoming a licence.

**It is bounded by the deed.** The authority extends exactly as far as the responsibility actually taken — to organize ourselves to meet *these* needs, solve *these* problems. It is not a general warrant, and it never reaches across another's own domain.

**It is bounded by [[Consent]].** Permission and authority derive from ØNE *and the voluntary consent of participating citizens*. Nobody is governed who has not agreed; decision-making is distributed rather than centred in any person or group. An authority that no one has consented to is not this.

**It scales only with participation.** Authority and capability to act at greater scales grow *in direct proportion to the number of people participating* — not by claim, not by recognition from an existing power, and not by the persuasiveness of the writing. This is a slow, self-limiting rule, and deliberately so: it makes overreach structurally impossible before the body that would have to carry it exists.

The corpus carries a second derivation beside this one — that authority is legitimate power derived from [[Sovereignty]] and bound by the [[Logos]]. The two are not in conflict so much as answering different questions: *by what right does a sovereign being act at all* (sovereignty) and *by what right does this body act on behalf of others who did not appoint it* (responsibility). Both are live.
