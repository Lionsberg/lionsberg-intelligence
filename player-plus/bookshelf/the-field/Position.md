# Position

*Where an Actor stands: place in the world · which body it stands inside · how near it is to the live work.*

To have a position is to stand somewhere — in the world, among people, in the work. Every Cell broadcasts its position, its condition, its offers and its requests to the whole; this is what the first of those words points at, in three facets, because *position* alone carries too much to be one field.

**Place** — where in the world an Actor plays, stated as coarsely as it likes and by its own consent: a town, a region, a hemisphere. **Body** — which Cell or gathering it stands inside, found by its address in the nested holarchy of sovereign bodies. **Hand** — how near it is to live work, in the old delivery sense of *closest to the work, with the standing to decide*.

Together the three let a request ask one clear question: *given where you stand, in what body, holding what — are you the right hand for this?* That is a very different question from *who is available*, and it is the one that actually routes work well ([[Requests and offers]], [[Subsidiarity]]).

It is load-bearing because place is how a body without a centre finds the nearest capable hand. A request that must travel to the middle and back is slow and creates a middle; a request that knows position can go sideways, directly, to whoever stands nearest the need ([[An address]], [[Holarchy]], [[Federation]]).

Place is stated by consent and never inferred. A person may say *this continent* and owe nobody more, and an Actor's whereabouts is never a field the body fills in on their behalf ([[Sovereignty]], [[Consent]], [[Disclosure tier]]).

One thing this word must not swallow. The canon uses *Position* in a far larger and more sacred sense — humanity's rightful standing and role within the cosmic order, a birthright rather than a whereabouts. The small fields here are deliberately kept beneath it ([[Sovereignty In The Populated Field]], [[Identity]]).
