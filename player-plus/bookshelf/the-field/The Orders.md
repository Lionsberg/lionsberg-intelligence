# The Orders

*A recurring move rather than a single idea: whenever a plurality has to be navigated, put it in order — of loves, goals, responsibilities, intentions, rules, commitments, authorities, systems, communities.*

Most difficulty is not about what is good. It is about what is good *first*. A person who holds a dozen genuine goods and no ordering among them is not free; they are stalled, and will let urgency or pressure do the ordering instead ([[Prioritizing]], [[Most Important Things]], [[Critical Path]]).

So the move recurs here, applied to one plurality after another. Loves, so that nothing good is asked to carry ultimate weight ([[Ordo Amoris — The Order of Loves]]). Goals, so that the nearest does not quietly outrank the largest ([[The Goal]], [[Worthy Goal]], [[Design To The Goal]]). Responsibilities, so that a duty to the near does not become an excuse against the far ([[Rights and Responsibilities]], [[Universal Responsibilities]]). Intentions, rules and commitments, so that when two collide there is already an answer ([[Intention]], [[The Rules of The Game]], [[Consciously Breaking Commitments]], [[Networks of Commitments]]). Authorities, so that obedience is not decided by whoever is nearest or loudest ([[Authority]], [[Subsidiarity Of Authority]], [[Separation Of Power]]). And systems and communities, so that a body knows which whole it is serving when two make claims on it ([[Total Nested Holarchy Of Sovereignties]], [[Community of Communities]]).

The single rule underneath is the one the order of loves establishes: **whatever is at the top orders everything beneath it**, so getting the top right is not the first question of many but the only one that changes all the others ([[One Purpose Above All]], [[Commander's Intent]], [[Order]], [[Sacred Order]]).

And the failure the whole family guards against is the same each time: not choosing wrongly between two goods, but never choosing — leaving the ordering implicit, where it is made by habit, fear, or whoever is in the room ([[Fear]], [[Drift]], [[A WILL is never inferred]]).
