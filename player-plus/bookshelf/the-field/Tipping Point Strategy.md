# Tipping Point Strategy

*The theory of how a few become enough: change a system by reaching the fraction at which it changes itself.*

A system does not usually turn because a majority was persuaded. It turns when a committed minority becomes coherent enough that the rest find it easier to move with them than against them. The fraction the canon names is three in a hundred — the same number wherever it appears, at a [[Community]] of ten thousand and at a planet ([[The First Three Percent]], [[The 300]]).

As a strategy rather than a statistic it has disciplines. Find the resonant, and stop spending the early years on the resistant — the pattern attracts rather than promotes. Form deeply: a shallow three percent is a fad, and a deeply formed one is a movement. Federate fast, because three percent scattered is fragile and three percent federated is not. Hold the line on the means, since a body that wins by the old world's methods has become it. And trust the cascade — the early years are a gathering problem, not a sales problem ([[The Invitation]], [[Cell]], [[Federation]], [[Integrity]]).

It runs beside the organic spread rather than instead of it: [[Fibonacci Spread]] describes how the flame passes at the speed of trust, and this describes the deliberate, staged gathering within the window available. Both at once. The diagnostic is one question — *are we reaching three percent, or a hundred percent of three percent?* Wide and shallow loses the cascade; deep and resonant wins it ([[Circle Coherence]], [[Coherence]]).

Its honesty is in what it does not claim. A tipping point is a threshold, not a promise; it says nothing about which way a system tips, and coherence, not headcount, is what makes a minority count ([[Emergence]], [[Islands of Coherence]]). The corresponding refusal is of growth that looks faster and carries nothing: an audience with no bodies behind it moves no threshold at all ([[10 Billion People In 10 Years]], [[The Movement]]).
