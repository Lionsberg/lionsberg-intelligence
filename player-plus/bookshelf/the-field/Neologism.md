# Neologism

*A newly coined word — minted deliberately, where the language available has no word for the thing that needs saying.*

Sometimes a concept has no name, and the nameless concept keeps getting collapsed into the nearest word that already exists, which is not it. Coining is the repair. A good deal is coined here — the Lexicon carries many of them — because much of what is being described has no settled English name ([[The LIØNSBERG Lexicon]] · [[Language]] · [[Working Exploration of Concepts Missing From English]] · [[Word Tools]]).

Coining is powerful precisely because it is dangerous, and the dangers are worth naming. A new word can **hide** rather than reveal — inventing a term instead of understanding the thing. It can **exclude**, since every coinage is a small barrier at the door for someone who does not have it yet ([[The vocabulary ladder]] · [[The grandmother test]] · [[The Floor And The Ceiling Of The Kit]]). It can **capture**, quietly taking an old meaning and putting a new one in its place ([[Semantic Capture]]). And it can simply be an author's pleasure in their own invention ([[Author's Voice Is Sovereign]] · [[Elision]]).

So disciplines are kept around it rather than forbidding it. Coin only where no existing word carries the sense, and say what the existing words fail to do ([[A Word on Words]] · [[Naming]]). Give the new word one clear home page rather than letting it live in many partial uses ([[One canonical home per concept]] · [[Name the Concept in the Singular]]). Gloss it at first meeting, every time, so the barrier is low. Treat it as current best understanding and be willing to retire it ([[Members]] · [[The Wiki Reflects the Present]] · [[Everything Is Current Best Understanding]]). And remember that a coinage in one language is not a coinage in another — the sense, not the letters, is what travels ([[On translating]] · [[Cross-Cultural Pattern Translation]] · [[Essences, Renderings, And Ineffable Referents]]).

A coinage is also not the same as a [[Word Tools|Word Tool]], though the two are often confused here. A neologism is a *new* word; a Word Tool is any word consciously used as an instrument pointing past itself, old or new.
