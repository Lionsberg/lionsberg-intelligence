# The Steering Committee that dissolves its own founding authority

*The small founding circle that brings a body into being and then hands the body its own authority — scaffolding, named as scaffolding from the first meeting.*

Ten thousand people are a population, not a body. Something has to begin, and it cannot be one person and cannot be a hundred. So a founding circle of four to seven forms, takes a clear and temporary mandate, and holds the first gathering. It may convene and invite; it may *draft* — never ratify — the [[Driver]], the [[Field of Agreements]], the [[Charter]]; it may hold early shared resources in trust, transparently; and it may speak for the founding effort, never for the community, which has not yet consented or found its voice. Every founding decision is provisional until the wider body ratifies it.

What makes this a concept rather than a committee is the second half. **The day the wider body can govern itself, the founding mandate is complete and the founding authority dissolves back into the body.** The founders remain members; what ends is the temporary power. This has to be said out loud at the first meeting and written into the record, because a founding body that forgets it is temporary becomes exactly the ossified centre the whole [[Pattern]] exists to prevent. Almost every movement that hardened into an institution hardened here, at the point where the people who did the unglamorous early work quietly became the people who decide.

The founding circle is chosen for commitment, not prestige: the ones who show up every week. Its first act of unity is to ask who is missing and reach across the lines that have divided the place before. Among the roles that tend to emerge are a [[Driver]] who will not let the work die, a weaver who knows who knows whom, a [[Keeper]] of dates and money and follow-through, an elder who lends standing, a builder who gets concrete things done, and a questioner who is willing to say *are we sure?*

The rhythm that protects the handover is provisional-now, ratified-later: adopt the Charter provisionally, live under it for a full year, and ratify only after four seasons have shown where it holds and where it chafes. A constitution earned through a year of practice is worth more than a flawless one written in a weekend.
