# The four fates

*What a page can meet when a body tends its own writing: kept · archived · discarded · synthesized.*

**Kept** — it crosses whole into the living body of work, read in full and made coherent with what surrounds it. **Archived** — it has been superseded, and its provenance is preserved while it is unlinked from the living pages. **Discarded** — nothing of unique weight in it survives current best understanding; the version history remembers what the page cannot. **Synthesized** — it is folded into a page that says the same thing better, and its meaning continues under another name ([[Weave, don't delete]], [[One canonical home per concept]], [[Current Best Understanding]]).

Naming four is what makes the tending honest. With only *keep* and *delete*, everything becomes a verdict on whether a page was good — and so bodies hoard, because deleting feels like erasure and keeping feels safe. Four fates let the decision be about what serves a reader now, and let a page that was genuinely valuable still stop being carried ([[Prune]], [[Drift]], [[Compost]]).

Each fate leaves a mark. A crossed page names where it went and when; an archived page keeps its lineage; a discarded page lives on in history; a synthesized page points at the page that absorbed it. What is never acceptable is silent disappearance — a reader following a link into nothing learns nothing, while a mark tells them exactly what happened ([[Provenance]], [[Change log]], [[Decision Records]]).

And the crossing is done by a mind, never by a script. A page is read in full and made coherent with its neighbours; moving text mechanically is how a body acquires a library that looks tended and is not ([[Curation]], [[Second reader]], [[Semantic Capture]]).
