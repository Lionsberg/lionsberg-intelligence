# The Rite Wheel and the Canon of Rites

*The year held as a wheel — anchored to the solstices and equinoxes and turning with the body's own cycles of growth — and the handful of rites by which a life's thresholds are marked upon it.*

People do not remember what they were told; they remember what they stood up and did, together, at a moment that mattered. Every culture that has lasted has kept two things: a year with shape, so that time is not an undifferentiated stream, and a small canon of rites by which the passages of a life are witnessed rather than merely undergone.

The **wheel** is the first. Its fixed points are the ones the sky gives everybody — the two solstices and the two equinoxes — so that the rhythm belongs to no authority and can be kept anywhere on Earth without permission ([[The LIØNSBERG Calendar]], [[Season]], [[Rhythm]]). Onto those turnings the working rhythms are laid: the daily practice, the weekly gathering, the seasonal review, the jubilee, and the body's own cycles of growth. The result is that the year itself carries the practice; nobody has to remember to schedule it.

The **canon of rites** is the second: birth · initiation · union · service · death · renewal · and the refounding. Six of them mark thresholds in a life — coming into the world, becoming an adult member, binding oneself to another, taking up a charge, leaving the world, and beginning again after breaking ([[Rites of Passage]], [[Initiation]], [[Union]], [[Giving Birth]], [[Dying]]). The seventh marks a threshold in a body rather than a person: the hour when a people must be reconstituted from its own seed. A rite is not decoration on a decision. It is how a community makes a change real to everyone present, and how the one passing through is carried rather than left to do it alone.
