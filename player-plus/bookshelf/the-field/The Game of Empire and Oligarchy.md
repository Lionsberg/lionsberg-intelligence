# The Game of Empire and Oligarchy

*The anti-pattern named as a game, because that is what it is: power-over pursued by controlling the lines everything moves along, and by bringing every lesser body under one hand.*

It has a shape that repeats across centuries and continents, which is what makes it a pattern rather than a period. Take the lines rather than the land ([[Supply Line Supremacy]]). Keep the conquered divided so the conquest need not be repeated ([[Divide and Conquer · Keep Divided To Keep Conquered]]). Bring every organisation of any power inside the arrangement, or under it. Control what is known about all of it ([[Control Of Information]], [[Centralization Of Power]], [[Regulatory Capture]]).

Naming it as a *game* does specific work. A game has rules, a scoring system, and players who are playing it whether or not they would describe themselves that way — which is more useful and less flattering than a story about villains. It also means it can be declined: the move available is not to beat this game but to stop playing it and to build somewhere the rules are different ([[The Rules of The Game]], [[Withdrawal Of Consent]], [[New Wine Requires New Wineskins]]).

Its opposite is not a nicer empire. It is federation — bodies that keep their own sovereignty and join for what none can do alone, with authority flowing from responsibility rather than from position ([[Federations vs Empires]], [[Federation]], [[Subsidiarity]], [[Authority derived from responsibility]]).

And the counter to its central move is local capability: a body that can make, grow, and repair enough of what it needs is not available to be held by whoever holds the lines ([[Self-Sustenance and Self-Replication]], [[Supply Chains]], [[Individual And Local]], [[Structural Immunity]]).

The source tells this as history, naming actors. The pattern crosses; the accusation does not, and none of the particulars is carried here.
