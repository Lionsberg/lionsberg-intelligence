# Covenant Across Time

*The seventh of the Constitutional Recognitions: accountability extends forward to the seventh generation and backward to the ancestors.*

A decision made only among the living is made among a minority. Those who will inherit its consequences are not in the room, and those whose work made the room possible are no longer in it. Covenant Across Time is the recognition that both are parties to the agreement anyway, and that a body's obligations run in two directions along time as well as outward across a table.

It is load-bearing because almost every failure named here as an anti-pattern is a form of borrowing from the future: extraction spends soil, centralization spends trust, debt spends work not yet done. A body that only optimizes its own season cannot see any of these as costs. Seven generations is the chosen horizon — long enough that no living person will collect, short enough that a real human being can imagine it.

Its practical faces are concrete. Seats for future generations and for living systems at the governing table. [[The asset lock]] that keeps what was built from being cashed out by whoever holds it last. Review dates and [[Decision Records]] written so that successors can understand *why*, not merely what. Provenance that never thins. And the older half: crediting what was received, so the ancestors' work is not quietly absorbed as one's own.

In play, a [[Cell]] meets it at a turning, when someone asks whether this season's [[Quest]] left anything more capable than it found — and whether what is being written will be legible to whoever picks it up in ten years.
