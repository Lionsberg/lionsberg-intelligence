# Universal Rights, and Universal Aspirations

*Rights flow from the Universal Law and Values derived from the [[Logos]]; aspirations are the Ideal we are striving toward and are not guaranteed. Keeping the two apart is the whole discipline.*

Recognizing the inherent dignity, worth and value of [[All]] as members of one family of life, a basic list of universal rights can be articulated. The substance for that list is the United Nations Declaration of Human Rights, named as such: thirty general concepts, from *all human beings are born free and equal* through freedom from slavery and torture, recognition before the law, freedom of movement, conscience, expression and assembly, work and rest, education, participation in cultural life, and the closing rule that no individual, group or state has the right to destroy universal rights. That attribution travels with the list; the native contribution here is the framing around it and the questions it ends on.

**The distinction that does the work.** A right is a claim that is *guaranteed* — something a society owes and can be held to. An aspiration is the ideal being striven toward, and is not guaranteed. Collapsing the two is the common failure of rights language: a list that mixes what can be owed with what can only be hoped for is enforceable nowhere and honoured selectively, and it teaches people that declarations are decoration. Held apart, each becomes usable — the first as an obligation with a bearer, the second as a direction.

**And the honest admission.** These rights are not manifest in the old world. In every country there are massive inequalities, corruption and unequal treatment in society and in law, and a share of every society struggling to survive rather than to flourish. However well the articulation reflects what a heart of love would want for one it loved, we have struggled to operationalize it.

So the page ends not in a claim but in three questions, and they are the ones worth carrying: *what kind of society could actually guarantee such rights? what would we have to design and build for them to manifest for all? and what way of co-operating would give every being the genuine freedom and opportunity to develop into the fullness of their potential as part of a harmonious whole?* A right without a society that can deliver it is an aspiration wearing a right's clothes — which is exactly why the answering half of this is [[Universal Responsibilities]].
