# Listening

*The first discipline of voice. Before anything is said well, something has to be heard fully.*

Ordinary listening waits. Deep listening receives — the words, and what is underneath them, and what the speaker has not found words for yet. Heart-listening receives the person rather than the content: what they are carrying, what it costs them to say this, what they are asking for that they have not asked for. The three are not stages of politeness; they are different acts, and a moment usually asks for one of them in particular.

It is the first discipline because every other voice practice depends on it. Truth cannot be said in love by someone who has not heard what the other can receive ([[Speaking Truth In Love]]). A difficult conversation that begins with the prepared sentence is not a conversation ([[Having Difficult Conversations]]). A question worth asking comes out of what was just heard ([[Asking Questions]]). An apology that has not listened first repairs nothing ([[Apologizing]]).

At the table it is structural rather than merely kind. The Turn goes in turn, and each person says what they said they would do, the gap, what they will do, what they offer and what they ask — which only works if the others are receiving rather than composing. [[Dialogos]] is listening made collective: shared sensemaking, where the aim is to find what none of them could see alone. And a [[Meaningful Objection]] can only be met if it was first genuinely heard; a Body that argues with objections rather than listening to them has kept the form of consent and lost the substance.

The canon also holds its counterpart: that not every moment asks for speech, and that a word should improve the silence it breaks. Listening is what makes that judgment possible.
