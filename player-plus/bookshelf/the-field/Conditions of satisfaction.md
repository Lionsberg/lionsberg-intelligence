# Conditions of satisfaction

*What a commitment must meet to count as kept — written by the one who will receive the work, before the work begins.*

A request means nothing precise until both sides can say, in the same words, what satisfying it looks like. So the receiver writes it: *by clean I mean swept, dishes done, counters clear.* Named at the making of the [[Commitment]] rather than read in afterwards, it leaves committor and receiver holding one picture of *done* instead of two that diverge. It is the first of the conditions of a reliable promise and it carries the others — competence, time, readiness cannot be judged against an unnamed target.

It is load-bearing because the handoffs are where systems fail. The successor is the customer; the doer checks the conditions before handing the work over; quality enters at design rather than at inspection, so no later pressure can quietly trade it away. This is the same move as [[Quality]] defined before the work and tested at the milestone under real load, said at the scale of a single exchange.

They nest. A task's conditions belong to the Quest or [[Milestone]] it serves, a project's to the programme above it, all answering upward into the one condition the whole is measured by: that what is co-created measurably serves the Goal. A condition met while working against that has not truly been met. At a kitchen table this is one sentence before the word is given; at a world's scale it is the same sentence in a plan, letting thousands who will never meet verify their piece against a standard anyone can read.
