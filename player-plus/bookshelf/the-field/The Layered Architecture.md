# The Layered Architecture

*Read from the top down; built from the bottom up. A person meets a seed, then a path, then the volumes; every page underneath either lives in a volume or serves one. The two directions meet in the middle.*

Four layers. **The seed** — one page, one shared link, the first crossing, small enough to hand to anyone ([[The Golden Seed]], [[The door]], [[Receive The Seed]]). **The path** — the wayfinding that moves a person through the material in the order it actually opens, rather than in the order it was written ([[The Sovereign Path]], [[Welcome to LIØNSBERG]], [[Experience Is Gateway, Codex Is Corpus]]). **The volumes** — the bound, complete, printable whole ([[The Sacred Codex]], [[Every Page Lives Within A Volume]]). **Everything else** — the substrate: entries, plays, quests, supporting articulation, archived precursors, each living inside a volume or in service of one ([[The LIØNSBERG Corpus]], [[Foundational Literature]]).

The two directions are the whole idea, and they are usually confused. A reader **experiences** it top-down, from the seed inward to whatever serves the moment they are actually in ([[Just in time]], [[The vocabulary ladder]]). A librarian **organises** it bottom-up: no orphan pages, every page accountable to something larger ([[One canonical home per concept]], [[The Incipient Link]]).

Where they meet is the canonical middle, and that meeting is what gives a large body coherence at every scale. Build only bottom-up and you get a well-ordered archive nobody can enter. Build only top-down and you get a beautiful front door with nothing behind it ([[The grandmother test]], [[The Living Library]], [[Conditions of Acceptance]]).

The arrangement generalises past a wiki: any body with more knowledge than a newcomer can hold needs both an entrance sequence and an accountability rule, and most have one ([[The Kit]], [[Curriculum]]).
