# Load

*How hard someone is being asked to push — read from the person's side, in six plain words, never from a meter.*

**Idle** — resting, available, nothing asked. **Steady** — the baseline of play: a few hours in the week, the floor of what was pledged. **Push** — a couple of focused hours a day, sustained; a stretch. **Full** — a whole day pushed, beside everything else a life asks. **Redline** — far more than that, bearable briefly and only with rest already planned. **All in** — something is genuinely at stake; every hour, for as long as it must be, and then recovery.

The reading is made from two things the person states: what they pledged for the season, and what this week actually asked of them. Nothing measures it. That is deliberate — the moment a load is computed from a trace it becomes surveillance, and people manage the trace ([[TimeEnergy]], [[The Pledge]]).

The word was chosen over *utilization* on purpose, because the word shapes the response. Utilization invites a body to raise the number. Load invites it to help. When a Cell sees one of its own at redline it moves work; when it sees idle hands it offers something. A gauge read in common is how a group balances weight without anyone having to confess to struggling ([[Resource Leveling]], [[Mutual Aid]]).

It is load-bearing because it is the humane half of planning, and without it the calculable half is dangerous. A plan can always be met by asking more of whoever will not say no. Saying the six words aloud at the gathering makes what is being asked visible *before* it is decided — and makes the [[Bottleneck]] visible too, since the person whose load sets the pace of the whole is usually obvious the moment everyone says their word ([[Rest And Regeneration]], [[Wellness]]).
