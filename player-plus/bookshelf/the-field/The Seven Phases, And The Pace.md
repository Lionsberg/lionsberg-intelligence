# The Seven Phases, And The Pace

*The arc of a long formation — awakening, recognition, trial, apprentice, journeyman, master, elder, closing walk — where each phase is entered by a threshold rather than by a date.*

**Awakening**: a person comes awake to the Way by whatever road their life prepared. **Recognition**: among those awakening, some recognise themselves in a particular calling and go looking for it ([[The Calling]], [[Who Refuses The Call, And Is Honored]]). **Trial**: a first bounded season — ninety days — in which both sides look at each other and either may withdraw without dishonour ([[90 Day Sprint]]). **Apprentice**: roughly the first three years; the disciplines settle, the Circle becomes home ([[The Three-Year Formation]], [[Apprenticeship]]). **Journeyman**: roughly years four to eight; the person moves into actually holding a portion of the work rather than only training for it. **Master**: full standing, and the authorisation to teach ([[Mastery]], [[Belt Progression]]). **Elder**: after long service, a change of function rather than a promotion ([[The Elder Transition And The Final Transmission]], [[Wise Eldership]]). **The closing walk**: the last season, in which what was carried is handed on ([[Pass The Flame]], [[Dying]]).

The durations are given as typical rather than required, and the canon is explicit that **a phase carries a threshold, not a deadline**: standing is conferred when it has actually matured, not at a calendar date.

**The pace claim is the load-bearing half of this page.** Build patiently, faithfully, and at the pace the work actually requires — because *a body that emerges from faithful building endures for millennia, and a body that emerges from forced acceleration tends to collapse within a generation.* That is a real constraint on ambition, and it sits directly against the pull of any strategy organised around thresholds and tipping points ([[Tipping Point Strategy]], [[Generational Time]], [[The Time Horizons]]).

The canon holds both without pretending they are comfortable. The hour is urgent; formation is slow; the answer is not to speed up formation but to start more of it, earlier, in more places — and to accept that the body which arrives will be the one that finishes the passage, not the one that began it.
