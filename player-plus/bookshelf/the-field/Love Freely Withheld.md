# Love Freely Withheld

*Love that cannot be freely withheld cannot be freely given. That one sentence is the canon's answer to why the door was left open.*

It is asked, in the canon's telling, by a child: *if the ØNE knew — and it had to have known — why not make us so we could not turn away? Why not make us so we would not harm?* The question is not answered quickly, and the canon says plainly that whoever answers it quickly has not understood it. The whole long night of history hangs on that one hinge.

The answer comes as a picture rather than an argument. When your mother puts her arms around you — what would that be worth if her mind were cold and her mechanical arms could not do otherwise? The warmth is in the choosing. A being that cannot do otherwise is not loving; it is functioning. So the Creator chose to create co-creators, and to give them freedom, and to invite rather than to compel ([[The Two Gifts]], [[Sovereignty]]).

The cost is stated without softening. The journey through the long night that the choice enabled has been painful beyond imagination, and nothing here pretends otherwise or asks anyone to be grateful for what was done to them. What the canon claims is narrower and harder: that freedom was not a design flaw tolerated for some other benefit — it was *the only way Heaven could ever be made*. A world of beings who could not have chosen otherwise would be a well-run machine with no one home in it.

Two things follow in practice. Every structure the canon builds refuses compulsion at its root — consent at the door, the freedom to leave, no one governed who has not agreed ([[Consent]], [[The Way Out]], [[Voluntary Association]]). And the standing posture toward anyone still turned away is an unbarred door rather than a wall: grief, vigilance, and a way back that is never closed.
