# Wise Right

*A compound adjective marking the thing that is both wise and right in this situation — it exists because a list of values, however good, cannot be that.*

It is reached by a specific road. After years of articulating core values in many organizations, what came out was not a longer list but one principle: ***do the wise right thing, at the right time, for the right reasons, to progress toward the Goal.*** The point of that single line is stated plainly — it guides action within a shared aim **without letting any one articulated, and therefore inherently limited, set of values distort behaviour** ([[Values]] · [[Principles]] · [[The Goal]]).

That is what the compound is for. *Right* alone invites rule-following: correct by some standard, regardless of whether it helps. *Wise* alone invites cleverness: effective, insightful, and possibly a betrayal. Welded together they name the act that is both — fitted to the totality of this context, and answerable to the aim ([[Righteousness]] · [[Wisdom]] · [[Discernment]] · [[Spontaneous Wise Right Action]]).

The four qualifiers are not decoration. *At the right time* — timing is part of rightness, and the same act early or late is a different act ([[Kairos]] · [[Just in time]]). *For the right reasons* — motive is inside the act, not beside it ([[Means]] · [[Integrity]]). And the aim clause keeps it from floating free of anything checkable ([[Throughput]] · [[The compass question]]).

As a convention, it travels. It compounds onto whatever needs the same standard — *wise right speech*, *wise right discernment*, *wise right thing*, *wise right people*, *wise right reasons*, *wise right time* — and in each case it is doing the same work: replacing a rule with a judgment held to a standard ([[Wise Right Speech]] · [[Wise Eldership]] · [[Word Tools]] · [[Naming Forms]]).

Its cost should be said. A phrase that cannot be checked from outside can excuse anything, and *it was the wise right thing* is available to anyone. What keeps it honest is everything around it: the compass question, a body that can object, a record of what was actually decided and why ([[Meaningful Objection]] · [[Decision Records]] · [[Accountability]] · [[Second reader]]).
