# Empirical Resonance

*The test applied to any rendering: how far does it actually align with the lived experience of the people who carry it?*

When several articulations of the same thing are in hand, something has to decide between them, and two of the usual answers are refused. Not authority — the most credentialed rendering is not thereby the truest. Not internal elegance — a formulation can be beautifully consistent and still describe nothing anyone has met. What is left is the evidence a community actually has: does this rendering ring true against what these people have lived through, worked with, suffered, and tested?

*Empirical* is doing real work in the phrase. The reference class is experience, not sentiment. A rendering that resonates only because it flatters, or only because it is familiar, has not passed; what has passed is a rendering that keeps proving accurate where a body's life presses against it. And *resonance* is doing work too: this is a matter of degree, not a pass/fail gate. Renderings are more or less resonant, and the more-resonant one is preferred without the less-resonant one being destroyed ([[Resonance]], [[Current Best Articulation]]).

Its second half is collective. Different communities and cultures, each with its own experience and its own pressures, will find different facets of the same text load-bearing — so a multiplicity of readers amplifies individual discernment and builds a wider wisdom than any reader could reach. This is why reading happens in company, and why foreign lamps are kept rather than extinguished ([[Progressive Illumination]], [[Cross-Cultural Pattern Translation]]).

It is also the honest limit of the whole method. Resonance is evidence, not proof; a whole community can resonate with something false, which is why the test sits beside the others rather than above them — coherence with the [[Logos]], the ordinary fruit over time, and the willingness to revise ([[Navigating Towards Truth]], [[Everything Is Current Best Understanding]]).
