# The Ø Convention

*The slashed letter is the canonical spelling, and it is not decoration. It is a signature that survives being copied, printed, translated, and read by a stranger.*

One character, held on purpose. The **Ø** marks the name of the Source and the name of the body, and a reader meeting it knows immediately that this is not ordinary text ([[ØNE]], [[LIØNSBERG]], [[Naming Forms]]).

Four things are said to be carried in it, and they are worth knowing whether or not one finds them persuasive: the empty seat beneath the layers from which the rest emerges ([[FSx]]); a visual mark of something set apart, recognisable across languages and ages; the zero point, the still centre from which the spiral opens ([[Sacred Geometry]], [[The Fundamental Tone]]); and the eternal present, the only moment anything is ever actually lived ([[Nøw]], [[Here and Nøw]]).

The practical half is tolerance. Both spellings refer to the same thing, both resolve, and neither breaks the other; older writing is not hunted down and corrected ([[Interoperability]], [[Weave, do not delete]], [[Minimum standards of interoperability]]). What is fixed is what goes into anything meant to last — what is printed, what is carried in the seed, what is meant to be remembered ([[The Golden Seed]], [[Every Page Lives Within A Volume]]).

The cost is worth naming, since a convention that ignores its own cost is a preference. A non-ASCII character is harder to type, unreliable in some systems, awkward to search for, and invisible to anyone reading in a font that does not carry it — which is a real tax on a body that means to be reachable by everyone ([[Conditions of Acceptance]], [[Universal-Accessing]], [[The grandmother test]]). That the mark is kept anyway is a judgment that the signature is worth the friction, and it should be held as a judgment rather than as a law.
