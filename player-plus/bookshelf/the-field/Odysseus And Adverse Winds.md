# Odysseus And Adverse Winds

*A parable: the crew who tore open the sack in sight of home, and lost in one greedy hour everything a decade of hardship had won.*

Odysseus was almost home. The smoke from the sailors' own families could be seen rising from the shore like a beacon. The warmth of love and safety lay within reach and the loneliness of the sea was behind them. He breathed out, closed his eyes, and slept.

With the end in sight, his men found their chance. They crept to the sack their captain had guarded so carefully all the way, and, imagining riches, tore it open. It did not hold gold. It held the adverse winds, bottled and given to Odysseus by the king of the winds. They rushed out at once and drove the whole fleet back across every ocean the crew had struggled so hard to cross.

The parable's teaching is about the last stretch rather than the first. Most things are not lost at the beginning, when everyone is alert and nothing is in reach; they are lost near the end, when the goal is visible, vigilance drops, and a small self-interested act costs everything ([[Drift]] · [[Discipline]] · [[Focused Attention As Discipline]] · [[The Final Test]]).

Three things in it are worth keeping. **The sack was the captain's care, not his secret hoard** — the crew misread protection as privilege, which is what suspicion does to a body that has stopped trusting its own ([[Trust]] · [[Transparency]] · [[Stewardship]]). **The harm came from inside**, not from any sea or enemy ([[The Dark Initiate Failure Mode]] · [[The Anti-Patterns]]). And **one hour undid ten years**, because a gain that is compounding can be reversed faster than it was built ([[Spiraling Up · Spiraling Down]] · [[Possibility Of Reversal]]).

The canon applies it in its last line, and the application is the reason the parable is carried: after millennia of peril the human race is almost home to a land of safety, freedom and abundance — and the crew has its hands on the sack ([[The Window]] · [[The Chasm]] · [[The Meta Crisis]] · [[Extraction Of Wealth]]).
