# Festival

*A communal celebration of sacred rhythm, remembrance, and meaning through art, ritual, and joy — the day a people becomes visible to itself.*

Festivals are not rewards for work completed. They are the anchors that hold a year together, and the moments at which a community that is otherwise dispersed across households and quests assembles and sees its own size. They are where the young learn what the body believes, faster and more durably than any teaching does, because they learn it by eating, singing, and staying up.

It is load-bearing because rhythm without celebration decays into schedule. The turning points are put in the calendar by the sun, which no one owns — solstice and equinox, four each year — and a [[Community]] that federates its Circles makes those the largest gatherings in its life. Around them run the lunar inflections and the [[Season]]'s four faces. At the turning a Circle does its accounting, tells its best [[Story]], chooses the next [[Quest]], and fills its one page; the festival is what makes that a celebration rather than a review. [[Celebrate, Learn, and Improve]] sits before *Share the Stories* in [[The Seven Moves]] for the same reason: what is celebrated is what becomes worth telling.

In play, this asks very little and gives a great deal. Food, music, the naming of what was done and who did it, the welcoming of those who arrived this season, and genuine recognition of real work by real people rather than false praise. Existing cultural feasts are not replaced — the Calendar is explicitly a universal frame that local and indigenous festivals hang within, not a substitute for them.

Two honesties. A festival that becomes obligation is no longer one. And this territory is marked thin: sacred play and festival are named among the partial gaps, with the specific forms left for each community to grow and write down.
