# Grief

*The body's report of loss. Eight states are kept distinct in the suffering family — grief, anguish, misery, sadness, despair, hopelessness, depression, discouragement — because each reports something different and each asks for something different.*

**Grief** is loss registering in a being that loved. **Anguish** is grief at an intensity that overwhelms; the canon's own naming holds it as something to be sat with rather than solved. **Misery** is suffering sustained over time. **Sadness** is the lighter, wider tone of loss. **Despair** is loss that has reached the future — not only what is gone but what will not come. **Hopelessness** is despair that has become a belief about how things are. **Depression** is the flattening of capacity itself, which is why it cannot be met with the moves that meet sadness. **Discouragement** is the loss of courage for the work, and is specific to effort rather than to a person.

The distinctions are practical. Discouragement met as depression is over-treated; depression met as sadness is under-treated. Despair met with reassurance is not met at all. Grief met as a problem to be finished is the most common error the canon points at — what it asks for is company and time rather than a solution.

The canon treats grief as initiatory: something one passes through and is changed by, rather than a malfunction to be corrected. This is why [[Dying]], hospicing, and the marking of passages sit beside it, and why the naming of a gap matters here — mental health, addiction, dying, and being born are each flagged in the Language as territory the corpus has not yet properly patterned.

The discipline remains [[Mastery Of Emotions]]: feel fully, read accurately, respond to what is reported, release when the work is complete — knowing that here the work is long, and that a Body's first response to a grieving member is presence.
