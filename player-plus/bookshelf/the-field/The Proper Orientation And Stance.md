# The Proper Orientation And Stance

*Eyes up. Shoulders back. Spine straight. Attention outward and forward. Forward of centre — physical and dispositional at once, because they are not two things.*

The list is deliberately mixed, and that is what makes it teachable. **Balanced. Eyes up. Shoulders back. Spine straight.** Then, without changing register: **focused outward from the self; focused forward from the self; attending to others; aware; paying attention; receptive in all directions; attending to what is emerging; perceiving the patterns and the potentials; picking up the cues; expanding outward; spiralling upward; forward of centre, advancing toward what is worth advancing toward; embodying wise ways of being; loving, serving, encouraging, and empowering** whoever is present.

The claim in the mixing is that posture and disposition are one arrangement. A body curled inward is not merely expressing a state; it is holding one, and a person cannot attend outward from a stance that is attending to itself. So the list can be entered from either end — correct the spine and the attention follows; turn attention outward and the body follows ([[Embodiment]], [[The Dual-Axis Energy Model]], [[The Warrior Is The Instrument]]).

**Forward of centre** is the phrase that carries the whole. Not braced, not leaning back on the heels waiting to see what happens: weight slightly ahead, already moving toward the thing. It is a stance, a disposition, and a strategy in one word ([[Wayfaring]], [[Worthy Goal]], [[Do the Work]]).

It is paired with an explicit inverse — the improper orientation, item by item — which is what makes it practicable rather than aspirational. A person can check themselves against either list in a second, anywhere, and the check is honest because the items are observable ([[Check In]], [[Day shape]]).

And the instruction is the simplest thing on the page: **every time we advance or move, we move this way.** Not a posture adopted for important occasions — the ordinary way of crossing a room.
