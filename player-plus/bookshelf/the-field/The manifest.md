# The manifest

*Every scene declares what it carries — so nothing enters the Story unaccounted, and every reader has a trapdoor into depth.*

Below a rule at the foot of each scene sit three things. **Carries** — the concepts this scene actually transmits, each one linked, which is both the coverage record and the reader's way down into whatever caught them. **People, places, things** — everyone and everything the scene introduced, which compiles into the indexes a reader can wander. **Sources** — the passages of the wider corpus this scene renders, so that its lineage is never lost ([[Provenance]], [[The Core Concepts]], [[The Concept Card]]).

It is load-bearing for two reasons at once. For the reader, it is the joint between the myth and the library: the fiction stays clean above the rule, and the depth stays exactly one step down, available at the moment of interest rather than in advance ([[Just in time]], [[The Zone Of Optimum Development]], [[The Living Library]]). For the body making the Story, it is the only way to know what has actually been transmitted — a story that teaches by showing cannot report on itself, and a manifest lets the coverage be seen without any scene ever explaining itself ([[Curriculum]], [[Curation]]).

Its hard rule is about voice. The back-matter is **part of the Book** and speaks in the Book's own voice: no design intent, no *left mysterious on purpose*, no workshop talk, no attributions. Where a note is needed it is phrased in-world — *the counselor, whose name is refused*. Everything that belongs to the making lives in the workshop's notes and never on the page ([[Be the canon, don't narrate it]], [[Elision]], [[The LIØNSBERG Voice]]).

And it makes the whole body of scenes recomposable. Because every piece is self-contained and declares what it carries, the same modules can be shelved as one book, several books, or none — and served one at a time to a person at the moment their life asks for that one ([[Module]], [[Kit of Parts]], [[The Guide's four renderings]]).
