# The Concept Card

*One idea of the language in card form — its name, its one-line sense, its neighbours, and where it is discussed — so a concept can be dealt and sorted beside the work that uses it.*

A page explains. A card is held. The Concept Card is the card face of a page: the smallest thing you can put in someone's hand that still carries a whole idea, pointing back at the page where the idea is actually written. The page is the source; the card never becomes a second, drifting copy of it.

It is load-bearing because ideas and work are usually kept in separate places, and the separation is expensive. A library of concepts that nobody consults while working is an ornament; work that proceeds without the language it was designed in reinvents things badly. Putting a concept in the same shape as a piece of work means it can sit on the same table, be picked up in the middle of a decision, be sorted next to the Quest that needs it, and be handed to a newcomer who has three minutes ([[Card Types]], [[Work Deck]]).

It also changes how a language is taught. Fifty cards can be spread on a table, grouped by hand, argued over, and rearranged by people who would never read fifty pages — and the rearranging is itself a way of finding out what a body actually understands ([[Pattern Card]], [[The LIØNSBERG Lexicon]], [[Curation]]).

The card's discipline is its brevity and its honesty about being partial. One line of sense is not the concept; it is the handle. A reader who wants the thing follows the link, and a card that begins to grow paragraphs has stopped being a card and should go back to being a page ([[Limit Statement]], [[Essences, Renderings, And Ineffable Referents]]).
