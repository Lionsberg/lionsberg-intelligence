# Sacrifice for Something Better

*Getting to where we are going requires leaving behind where we are.*

Five lines, and the whole of a path in them. Arriving somewhere else means departing from here; becoming someone else means the end of who you have been; and laying hold of something better requires letting go of what the hands are already full of. The chapter makes no attempt to soften this. Its last line is *even if it is our life*.

What makes it more than a truism is the word *better*. Sacrifice here is not privation, not discipline for its own sake, and not the buying of favour. It is exchange, and it is only worth making when the thing received is genuinely greater than the thing given up — which means the whole weight of the move rests on the judgment beforehand ([[Discernment]], [[Quality]], [[Worthy Goal]]). A sacrifice made for something no better is simply loss, and the traditions that went wrong here mostly went wrong by keeping the giving-up and losing the *better*.

It is also a diagnosis of why change stalls. Most people know what they would rather be doing and are not doing it, and the missing piece is almost never the plan. It is the grip — the arrangement, the identity, the comfort, the story about oneself that is genuinely valuable and that will not survive the move ([[The Way Out]], [[Release]], [[The Act Of Release]], [[Prune]]). Naming it as a sacrifice rather than as an obstacle changes what is being asked: not *overcome this*, but *decide what it is worth*.

At the scale of a body the same arithmetic runs. A Cell cannot take up a new Quest while still holding the last one; an institution cannot become something else while protecting what it currently is ([[Disbanding]], [[Turn the Season]], [[Weaving vs. Starting New]]). The wilderness crossings the canon tells are all of this one shape — a people leaving an arrangement that fed them for something they had only been promised ([[Liberation and Exodus]], [[The Refining]], [[Threshold]]).

And it is the pivot of the way itself. The canon's account of transformation is not accumulation but exchange, repeated: something is laid down each time, and what is received is the next stretch of the road ([[The Sacred Ascent]], [[Sacrifice]], [[Memento Mori]]).
