# The Accuser

*The interior voice that takes up what was said about you and asks, in the sleepless hours, whether you deserved it.*

Slander from outside is survivable. What breaks people is the second voice — the one inside, that repeats the charge in your own words, at three in the morning, with the authority of self-knowledge behind it: *maybe they were right; maybe I did ruin everything; maybe this is what I deserve.* The wound is not the accusation. It is that the accusation finds an ally ([[Shame]], [[Grief]], [[Mental Health]]).

It is worth naming for the same reason [[Anxiety as Default-On]] is worth naming: what is named can be regarded, and what is regarded stops being simply the truth about you ([[Emotion as instrument reading]], [[The emotional bands and their gradients]]).

Three things answer it, and none of them is argument.

**Evidence from elsewhere.** The voice is unanswerable from inside one life and loses its footing against pattern — when the same thing turns out to have happened to others, it stops being a verdict about a person and becomes information about a situation ([[The smoke test]], [[Structures of Injustice]], [[Testimony]]).

**Honest self-examination that is allowed to finish.** Searching for every real misstep is not the same as accepting a charge, and the difference is that the search ends. What actually was yours is owned and repaired; what was never yours is set down ([[Apologizing]], [[Forgiveness]], [[Integrity]], [[Retrospective]]).

**Not being alone with it.** This voice does its work in isolation and loses most of its power in a room with people who know you. That is the whole practical case for a Circle before anything goes wrong ([[Circle]], [[Support Group]], [[Witness]], [[There Is No Solo Phase]]).

It appears in its source inside one person's account of a specific ruin. The particulars are theirs and are not carried here; the voice is not particular to anyone.
