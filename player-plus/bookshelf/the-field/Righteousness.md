# Righteousness

*Doing the wise right thing, at the right time, for the right reasons, on the totality of the context — the highest action available to someone in the situation they are actually in.*

The word carries a plain and demanding definition, and every clause in it is load-bearing. **The wise right thing** — not the rule-following thing, and not the well-intentioned thing. **At the right time** — the same act is right in one hour and wrong in another. **For the right reasons** — an outwardly good act done to be seen has not met the standard. **On the totality of the context** — which means the answer cannot be looked up, because no code contains this situation, these people, this hour ([[Wisdom]] · [[Discernment]] · [[Spontaneous Wise Right Action]]).

So righteousness is not moral correctness, and it is emphatically not self-righteousness, which is its counterfeit and its opposite: the counterfeit is certain, comparative and pleased with itself, while the real thing is attentive, specific, and usually uncomfortable. That same standing humility applies here as hard as anywhere — [[We Do Not Know]], and a person acting rightly is acting on their best current reading, not on a verdict ([[Current Best Understanding]] · [[Shoshin]]).

It is also not a private achievement. What is right is answerable to everyone the act touches, including those not in the room and those not yet born, which is why the compass question is asked outward rather than inward ([[The compass question]] · [[Omniconsideration]] · [[Covenant Across Time]]). Where that widening is refused, righteousness narrows into piety and then into cruelty — the oldest failure mode there is ([[The Dark Initiate Failure Mode]]).

In play it shows as a habit rather than as a stance: pausing long enough to see the whole picture, naming the reason you are actually acting on, choosing the higher of two goods when you can tell them apart, and repairing quickly when you were wrong ([[Apologizing]] · [[Atonement]] · [[Reconciliation]]).
