# Profound Questions

*The questions a body returns to rather than answers — kept open on purpose, because the returning is what does the work.*

*What is thought? What is mind?* The canon's page holds two such questions and no answers, and the form is the content. A profound question is not an unsolved problem waiting for someone competent. It is a question whose value lies in being asked again — by each person, at each stage, and in each generation — because the asking changes the asker, and the answer that satisfied at twenty does not at fifty ([[Mind]] · [[Consciousness]] · [[Asking Questions]] · [[Mystery]] · [[The Ineffable]]).

Keeping them open is a discipline, not laziness. A body that answers its profound questions has usually just stopped asking them, and the settled answer then quietly governs everything downstream without anyone re-examining it ([[We Do Not Know]] · [[Do Not Concretize the Absolute]] · [[Shoshin]] · [[Unlearning]] · [[Drift]]).

They are distinct from the questions the Game actually runs on, and the distinction is worth keeping. *What is the most important thing this week* is meant to be answered, and answered again next week ([[Most Important Things]] · [[The one question]] · [[Closing question]]). *What is mind* is not. Confusing the two produces either paralysis — no decision without metaphysics — or shallowness: a body that decides everything and wonders about nothing ([[Decision Making]] · [[Wisdom]]).

Their proper home is a room and a rhythm rather than a document. They are what a gathering turns to when the work is done and there is still light; what a Jam is for when nothing needs deciding; what an elder is asked ([[Dialogos]] · [[Jam]] · [[Council]] · [[Wise Eldership]] · [[MindWeaving]] · [[Sacred spaces]]).

And a body should keep its own list. Not to close it, but so that it can see what it has been returning to, and notice when it has quietly stopped ([[Register]] · [[Retrospective]] · [[Gaps Not Yet Closed]]).
