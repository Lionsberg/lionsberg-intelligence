# Naming

*A constitutive act: to name a thing is to bring it into shared existence, where it can be spoken of, agreed about, and worked on.*

Before a thing has a name it can be felt but not handled. Two people who have both noticed it cannot check that they noticed the same thing; a Body cannot decide about it; nothing can be linked to it. Naming is therefore the first move of co-creation and not a label applied afterward — which is why the first shared act of a new Cell is giving itself a name, and why the naming begins in the first gathering rather than later.

The same act, done carelessly, does damage. A name claims an address, and in a living corpus the page standing at a name *is* the meaning of every link pointing there ([[Semantic Capture]]). A name given to a person or a group sticks and is hard to unstick. Naming a tension accurately is repair; naming a person as the tension is the beginning of a different kind of trouble.

The disciplines that keep it honest are held elsewhere and belong here. The name is a tool pointing at something real and never the thing itself ([[Word Tools]]). One working name per wisdom, the others honored as aliases. The shape of the name is a claim about what kind of thing it is ([[Naming Forms]]). And when two of us find we mean different things by a word, we fix the word rather than each other.

Naming is also how the unmapped becomes mappable. A concept the corpus lacks gets named as we cross it, linked before it is written, and paid for later ([[The Incipient Link]]). The honest naming of what is missing is itself part of the map.
