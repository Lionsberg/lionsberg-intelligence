# Decline

*An addressed, spoken* no *— a legitimate outcome of being asked, and never a failure.*

Someone asks; the answer is no; the no is said out loud, to the one who asked, and the asking is then free to find another hand. That is the whole of it, and the whole of its difficulty is cultural rather than procedural. The alternative to a decline is silence, and silence is the most expensive answer there is: the asker waits, the work ages, and nobody learns anything.

It is load-bearing because a body of volunteers runs entirely on freely given words, and a word is only worth something if *no* was genuinely available. Where declining costs standing, people stop declining and start agreeing quietly to things they will not do — and a register full of soft yeses is worse than an empty one, because it looks like capacity ([[A WILL is never inferred]], [[Sovereignty]], [[Consent]]).

Declining what should not be carried is work done correctly. A person who says *not this week, and here is why* has given the body real information. When several people decline the same request, the request has found a genuine gap in the body rather than a shirking one — and the gap is the finding ([[Limiting Factor]], [[Issue]]).

It is kept apart from its neighbours. A decline is given *before* any word is given, and is terminal. Putting back something already committed is a [[Release]]. Failing to do what was promised is a broken commitment, named as such and learned from ([[Consciously Breaking Commitments]], [[The states of a commitment]]).

One collision is worth naming because both senses live inside the same body of writing: a body's own lifecycle names a season called *decline*, between maturity and senescence. That decline is a passage a whole organisation moves through. This one is a single spoken answer by one Actor on one Tuesday ([[Organizations The LIØNSBERG Way]]).
