# Meaningful Objection

*An objection names harm to what the body serves — and no proposal passes over one. Every member has the moral obligation to object when they reasonably believe the body will be unable to achieve its aim.*

The word *obligation* is the surprising one and the important one. Objecting is not a right one may exercise if one feels strongly; it is a duty owed to the body. A member who sees a harm and stays quiet — for peace, for speed, for fear of being the difficult one — has withheld something the body needed, and the decision that follows is weaker for it.

Its discipline is in the framing. An objection addresses how the [[Proposal]] would harm the group's ability to advance toward its aim, in the context of the whole's progress toward [[The Goal]]. It is not *I would prefer otherwise*; preference lives inside [[Range Of Tolerance]], and members are asked to use [[Wisdom]] to discern when something merely varies from what they would have chosen and when it genuinely threatens the aim.

This is what makes [[Consent]] rigorous rather than merely polite. Any member may stop progress and require a proposal improved before it advances — real power, held by each person, and therefore held carefully. A body where objections are common and cheap cannot move; a body where they are rare because they are unwelcome cannot see. The health of a circle can be read from how its last objection was received.

In play, this is the single question at the centre of the deciding round: *does anyone have a reasoned, paramount objection?* When one comes, the proposal is amended toward the objector's concern rather than argued past it.
