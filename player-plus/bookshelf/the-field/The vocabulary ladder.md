# The vocabulary ladder

*Which words a Player may meet at which step of the road, and who hands each one — so that no term ever arrives undefined.*

A body building a new way inevitably builds new words with it, and the words are not the problem. Words arriving unannounced are the problem. The ladder is the ordered answer: at the plain door, a handful of everyday terms with a gloss in the sentence that uses them; in the Story and the little book, twelve words with one line each; at the threshold, the six words that threshold actually uses; at the first gathering, the few the gathering needs; and beyond that, the whole library, entered by anyone who wants it. Each rung names what a Player may meet there, and nothing further up.

It is load-bearing because the alternative is the failure every specialised culture makes and cannot see itself making. A surface built by people fluent in their own vocabulary reads perfectly to them and is a closed door to everyone else — and the people it closes out do not complain, they simply leave, which means the failure never reports itself ([[The grandmother test]]).

The governing doctrine is gentler than a curriculum: *each new word will find you exactly when its meaning becomes your need*. A ladder is not a syllabus to be completed before play, and nobody is examined. It is a discipline on the builders, not on the Player: whoever makes a surface is responsible for the words that surface uses, and for handing each one at the moment it first appears ([[The Guide's four renderings]], [[The Twelve Words]]).

Its counter-law is held beside it and belongs on the same page: [[Sensing comes before understanding]]. A Player may walk a long way with a word only half-held, and that is not a deficiency. The ladder governs the *arrival* of a word, not the moment of its comprehension, and a body that made every term precise before anyone was allowed to feel anything would have built a seminary rather than a way in.
