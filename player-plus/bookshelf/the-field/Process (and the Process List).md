# Process (and the Process List)

*The named, repeatable way a function is performed — and the list of them, so a body can see what it actually does.*

A process is the how. Not a policy and not an intention: the sequence someone can follow to get a thing done the way this body does it — meeting, onboarding, resolving an issue, tracking commitments, handling a submittal, answering a request for guidance, filling up what is lacking, removing what does not belong. Written down, it can be handed to a newcomer, criticised, improved, and inherited. Unwritten, it lives in whoever has been doing it longest and leaves when they do.

The **Process List** is the plain index of them. Its value is not the individual entries but the act of enumeration: a body that writes out everything it repeatedly does can see, often for the first time, what it is actually for — and can see what it claims to value and has no process for at all. The canon's own list opens with the Way itself and with the process by which processes are made, which is the right order ([[Continuous Improvement]], [[Current Best Understanding]]).

It is load-bearing because it is the third of [[The three charts]]. Functions say what must be done; roles say who holds that seat this season; processes say how, so that a seat changing hands does not mean the body forgetting how to do the work. It is also where improvement lands: a defect fixed once is a repair, a defect fixed in the process is a [[The guard|guard]], and a lesson written where nobody looks has not been learned ([[Every Defect Is A Gift]]).

Two disciplines keep processes from becoming bureaucracy. A process is [[Current Best Understanding]] and is revised the moment a better way is found — by whoever found it, not by whoever owns it. And a process exists for its function; when the function goes, the process is retired out loud rather than left running, because a procedure nobody can account for is how every body eventually acquires work that serves nothing ([[Prune]], [[Waste]]).
