# Card Types

*A card is the smallest complete unit of the record that fits one hand. A Card Type names what kind of thing it holds — and every type shares one anatomy, so whoever can read one card can read them all.*

**The anatomy.** A card has a **face** — its few fields: what it is, whose it is, where it stands. A **record** — every event that has happened to it, dated, nothing deleted. A **thread** — what has been said about it, by whom, when. And **links** — the cards and pages it depends on, serves, or is discussed in, which serve as its attachments. A card belongs to exactly one deck and one body's record; a view never copies a card, it points at it. The paper floor is a card file with the same four parts in ink.

**The first types.** [[The Playcard]] is what a Player or a Cell holds and plays. [[Work Card]] is one piece of work, with its address, its dependencies, its float and its stage. The TEA card is a Player's own daily account of time, energy and attention ([[TimeEnergy]]). [[The Concept Card]] is one concept of the language in card form, so an idea can be dealt and sorted beside the work that uses it.

It is load-bearing because it is what makes every surface of the Game the same object seen differently. The Board, a person's own hand, a wall of cards moved by hand, a dashboard: all of them are sorts of one deck rather than separate lists that must be kept in step. Keeping lists in step is where most of the administrative labour of an organisation goes, and it is entirely avoidable ([[Work Deck]], [[The Deck Standard]], [[The same card shape]]).

Types are added when the Game meets a thing that wants holding in one hand, and a type earns its place only when the same fields keep recurring and a hand wants to sort by them. That is a deliberately high bar: a type invented in advance of use produces fields nobody fills, and fields nobody fills are how a record quietly becomes fiction.
