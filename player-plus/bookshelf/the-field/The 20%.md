# The 20%

*To find which few to attend to, find which few causes are producing most of the results — in many complex systems, under a fifth produces over four fifths.*

[[A Few]] says to attend to a few of the highest quality. This chapter says how the few are found, and the method is to look at causes rather than at importance. Most of what happens in a complex system is produced by a small part of it. Locate that part and you have located where effort actually converts into outcome; miss it and you can work very hard on the other eighty percent for years.

The proportion is a rule of thumb with a long history in quality and reliability work, and it is offered here as one — *in many complex systems*, not in all, and *less than twenty producing more than eighty* rather than any exact split. It is an estimate about shape, not a measurement of any particular system, and the useful part is the asymmetry, which holds far more widely than the numbers ([[Theory Of Constraints]], [[Bottleneck]], [[Limiting Factor]]).

The chapter adds two things that make it more than a familiar maxim. The first is that it nests: *the twenty percent of the twenty percent are a few*. Apply the lens once and you have a fifth; apply it again and you have the handful that a person or a body can actually hold ([[A Few]], [[One moves a few, a few moves 3%, 3% moves 20%, 20% moves 80%]]).

The second is a chain of diagnosis that runs deeper than the results themselves: **results** are best read as **conditions**, which arise from **systems**, which are transformed by transforming **the few** that transform the twenty. That sequence is the whole difference between attacking symptoms and attacking causes. A result is an event and cannot be argued with; a condition is what keeps producing events of that kind; a system is what holds the condition in place; and the few are the points where the system will actually move ([[Root Causes]], [[Meta System]], [[The three nested holarchies — Issues, Opportunities, Goals]], [[Design To The Goal]]).

Used honestly it is a subtraction tool as much as a selection one. Finding the fifth that carries the load is also finding the four fifths that do not, and stopping those is usually the faster half of the improvement ([[Waste]], [[Prune]], [[Continuous Improvement]]).
