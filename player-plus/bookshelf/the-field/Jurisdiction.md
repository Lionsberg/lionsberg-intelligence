# Jurisdiction

*The scope and bounds of rightful governance or action, as defined by mutual agreement, alignment with [[Logos]], and functional [[Subsidiarity]].*

Jurisdiction answers the question *does this belong to us?* — and, just as importantly, *what is not ours to decide?* Where a [[Domain]] names the space a body tends, jurisdiction names the edge of what its decisions may bind, and therefore the edge of whose consent it must have.

It is load-bearing in two directions at once. Without a stated edge, bodies reach: a supercell begins deciding what a Cell should do at its own table, and federation quietly becomes rule. Without a stated edge, bodies also abdicate: a matter that visibly harms the whole is left unaddressed because nobody is sure it is theirs. Most governance failures in practice are one of these two, not malice.

In the Game jurisdiction is set by the same act that creates a body: sovereigns associate, a [[Domain]] appears, a [[Selectively Permeable Membrane]] bounds it, and a [[Field of Agreements]] says what the members have given the body leave to decide. Anything outside that is a [[Proposal]], a request, or an offer — never an instruction. When a matter genuinely spans two bodies, it goes to the smallest table that contains both, which is [[Subsidiarity]] doing its work.

The canon holds jurisdiction as a Lexicon definition rather than a developed practice; how disputes over jurisdiction are actually settled between neighbouring bodies is named across the corpus but not yet written. Treat what follows from the definition as sound and the procedure as unbuilt.
