# The Living Library

*The curated index of the wider body of work — what exists, what it carries, and how it relates to the spine.*

A corpus of millions of words is unusable without an index, and the index is itself a piece of work with a craft to it. The Living Library is that index: Volume XI of the Sacred Codex, listing every substantive work around the canonical spine, each with a brief account of what it carries and how it deepens the Volumes without replacing them. The library is larger than the Codex it indexes; the Codex is the centrepiece and the rest are the deepening companions.

Its inclusion criterion is what makes it a library rather than a list of everything. A work belongs if it is **substantive** (a body of work, not a fragment), **alive** (continuously developing, refined as the movement grows), **complementary** (deepening a specific aspect without trying to replace a Volume), and **not already a Volume** (no double-counting). Works that do not meet the criterion are not deleted — they are kept as historical procession, reachable through the wider corpus but outside the Library. *Weave, don't delete.*

The reason this sits under the learning Pillar rather than among the conventions is that indexing is a teaching act. What a library chooses to surface determines what a newcomer meets first, and in what order; an index is a [[Curriculum]] by other means. The same discipline applies as everywhere else in this Pillar: the whole shelf handed over at the door is a wall, and the Library's real work is making it possible to be handed one thing at a time, [[Just in time]], when the road asks for it.

It is also a commons obligation. What one Circle learns, every Circle may have — and can only have if it can be found. Cataloguing what exists under each Pillar, and marking honestly what is still to be built, is how [[Knowledge as commons]] stops being a sentiment.
