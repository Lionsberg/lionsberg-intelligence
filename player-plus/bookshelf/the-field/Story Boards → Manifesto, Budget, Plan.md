# Story Boards → Manifesto, Budget, Plan

*Stories of what was actually done, gathered in one place, turning over time into the three documents a movement usually has to write in advance — and doing it better, because they are made of things that happened.*

The sequence is the idea. A Cell finishes a quest and tells it ([[Share the Stories]], [[Every Story Is A Prophesy]]). The tellings gather where everyone can see them ([[The Board]], [[The Record]]). And the gathered body of them begins, without anyone drafting it, to do three jobs at once.

**A manifesto** — because what a movement is for is shown more convincingly by a hundred true accounts of what it did than by any statement of principles, and nobody argues with an account of a river that is running clean again ([[Demonstrate before advocating]], [[Forward-Looking Story]], [[Testimony]]).

**A budget** — because the stories carry what things actually cost and what they actually produced, which is the only honest basis for asking anyone for anything ([[The Offering]], [[Underwriting The New Economy]], [[Meaningful Monthly Contribution]]).

**A plan** — because a hundred accounts of what worked, read together, show what to do next more reliably than a forecast does ([[The Plan]], [[Pull planning]], [[The look-ahead]]).

Together these drive what the source calls the resourcing and engagement flywheel: stories attract people and resources; people and resources make more quests possible; more quests make more stories ([[Network Effects]], [[Attraction, not promotion]], [[The Tipping Point Cascade]]).

The reason to do it this way rather than the usual way is the failure it avoids. A manifesto written first commits a body to language it will have to defend; a budget written first commits it to a shape of work nobody has tested; a plan written first commits it to a world that has not arrived. Built out of what happened, all three stay answerable to reality ([[Everything Is Current Best Understanding]], [[Prototyping]], [[The load-bearing bets]]).

Four artefacts are named in the source and none of them exists.
