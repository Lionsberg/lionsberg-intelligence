# Advantage Based Decision Making

*Choosing between alternatives by isolating and weighing their advantages — and recording not only what was decided but why, and what was rejected.*

The method assumes something honest: no decision is truly objective. Rather than pretending to calculate net differences between advantages and disadvantages, it makes the comparison explicit — lay the real alternatives side by side, name what each is *good for*, and choose on the importance of those advantages. The best-known form is Choosing by Advantages, inherited from the production systems this Pillar borrows from.

It is load-bearing because [[Consent-Based Decision-Making]] answers *shall we do this* and not *which of these*. A circle that has only a consent process will serially approve or reject single proposals, which quietly biases it toward whatever was proposed first and loudest. Advantage-based comparison is how a body considers a real set before committing to one.

Its second half matters as much as its first. The record of the decision — what was chosen, why, what the next best alternatives were, and why they were set aside — is what makes learning and continuity possible between seasons and between generations of a body. Graphic records of the comparison are kept as part of the project record; the canon treats this as knowledge, not paperwork.

In play a [[Cell]] uses it when a [[Quest]] could be pursued three ways, or when a design decision commits the season. The instinct it trains is to write the alternatives down *before* the discussion, so the comparison is about the options rather than about the people advocating them.
