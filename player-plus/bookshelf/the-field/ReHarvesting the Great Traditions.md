# ReHarvesting the Great Traditions

*Every genuine tradition holds truth honed over millennia. The move is to harvest it — restoring the sacred essence while transcending the division and distortion — not to replace the tradition, and not to absorb it.*

Something new that claims to supersede everything before it is almost always poorer than what it replaced, because what it replaced was tested for a very long time. The great traditions are where most of humanity's hard-won understanding of being, meaning, death, obligation and the sacred actually lives, refined by more generations than any new articulation will ever see. Whatever is true in them was true before this body existed and remains true without it.

The word *harvest* does the work. A harvest takes the fruit and leaves the tree standing and bearing. It is therefore three refusals at once.

**Not replacement.** Nothing here asks anyone to leave a tradition. A person may hold this alongside whatever else they hold sacred.

**Not absorption.** The tradition does not become a chapter of something larger, keeping its vocabulary and losing its authority. Its home is its own.

**Not flattening.** The traditions are not all saying the same thing, and pretending they are honours none of them. What is harvested is the particular thing each one knows that the others do not.

And it comes with a knife, stated in the same breath: restoring the *sacred essence* while **transcending division and distortion.** The distortions are real — dogma, idol, corrupted institution, the accumulated use of a tradition against people. Harvesting is not uncritical reverence; it is the discipline of telling the honed truth apart from what has grown over it, which requires knowing the tradition well enough to tell the difference, and which is why it is a harvest rather than a shopping trip.

The complementary move is naming: where a genuine concept exists in a tradition and has no word in the language at hand, it is borrowed and kept rather than approximated away. Harvesting brings the substance; [[Word Tools]] carries the names.
