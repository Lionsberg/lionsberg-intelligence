# The 20-Risk-Factor Model

*Twenty structural risks in six families, enumerated — and the arithmetic of requiring all twenty to resolve well, which is what "business as usual" quietly assumes.*

The families, with what sits in each. **Environment** — a changing climate, biodiversity loss, depletion of finite resources. **Economy** — debt at levels that cannot be serviced, widening inequality, a financial system whose interconnection makes cascading failure possible. **Society and politics** — polarisation, geopolitical conflict up to the nuclear, and the erosion of trust in institutions. **Technology** — dependence on digital systems that can be attacked, displacement of work faster than it is replaced, and biological capabilities that lower the barrier to catastrophe. **Health** — pandemics in a world that moves everything everywhere, and a population-scale decline in mental health. **Inertia and cognition** — systems too large to turn quickly, a shortage of will to make hard choices, horizons shorter than the problems, and the human tendency to assume things continue because they have ([[Normalcy Bias and the Familiarity Trap]], [[Multi-Polar Traps]], [[Systemic Failure Modes]]).

Two of the six families are the ones usually left out of such lists, and they are the interesting ones. **Interdependence** is named as a factor in its own right — stress in one area cascading into others, with feedback loops closing. And **inertia** is named as a meta-factor that reduces the effectiveness of every intervention aimed at the other nineteen ([[The Endgame Cascade]], [[Theory Of Constraints]], [[Limiting Factor]]).

The arithmetic is where it bites, and it is an illustration rather than a calculation. Grant each factor an even chance of resolving well, treat them as independent, and the chance that **all twenty** do is vanishingly small. The source is explicit that the independence assumption is false and that reality is worse; what the illustration establishes is only that business as usual is not a neutral default but a compound bet ([[Probability, Severity, and Scope]], [[The Probability Calculation Method]]).

**Provenance, which governs every number here:** the source is a 2023 transcript of a dialogue between the book's questioner and an AI assistant whose own training data ended earlier. Every probability in it is that assistant's speculative estimate, elicited in 2023, and the book says so in its own words. These are estimates, not measurements, and the list is the durable part — a reader should redo the pricing themselves ([[We Do Not Know]], [[Honesty about time]]).
