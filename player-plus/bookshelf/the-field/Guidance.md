# Guidance

*Asking upward when a group cannot decide — and receiving one official answer among many, rather than an order.*

Decisions are made as close to the work as possible, by consent where consent is possible. Sometimes a body genuinely cannot resolve one: the question exceeds what it can see, or it touches others who are not in the room. Then it asks its home. What comes back is guidance: the considered answer of a level whose wider view may serve, offered as one input the asking body weighs — one official answer among many. The higher level offers direction, and at times decisions, where its abstraction and capability serve the whole.

It is load-bearing because it is the pressure valve that keeps subsidiarity honest. Without a way to ask upward, a stuck body either fractures or quietly invents a boss. With it — and with the answer framed as guidance rather than instruction — authority still flows from consent and service still flows downward. The asking is itself information: repeated questions of the same kind mean something above is missing, and usually the right response is a clearer agreement rather than a stream of questions.

It sits beside two neighbours. [[Stewardship body]] is where a decision finally rests when the process cannot produce one at all, and it decides as little as it can. [[Prescribed and discretionary trust]] says where specifying is wise and where trusting is. Guidance is the middle case: nobody is overruled, and someone who can see further has answered.
