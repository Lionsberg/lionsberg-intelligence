# The Realization Arc

*Recognition → Cultivation → Integration → Spontaneous operation. How a latent capacity becomes something a person simply does.*

**Recognition.** A person perceives a potential in themselves that has not yet been activated. Nothing has been gained at this stage and nothing can be forced; what has happened is that something previously invisible has been seen ([[Discernment]], [[Genius]]).

**Cultivation.** The disciplines wake the potential into a beginning capacity — clumsy, effortful, requiring attention ([[Discipline]], [[The Daily Way]]).

**Integration.** The capacity settles into the whole person and stops being a separate thing they do. It no longer requires a decision to deploy ([[Integration]], [[Embodiment]]).

**Spontaneous operation.** The capacity operates without deliberate intent: the person is the conduit and the thing simply happens through them, with the precision the moment needs ([[Spontaneous Wise Right Action]], [[Conduit]]).

The claim underneath the arc is worth stating plainly, because it changes what formation is *for*: nothing external is being acquired. What is called realization is the activation of what was already given — which is why the canon's vow begins *the power is not mine.* A person who claims the realized capacity as a personal possession has reversed the relation; a person who refuses their own potential has declined a gift ([[The Two Gifts]], [[Aligned Will]], [[Right Use of Power]]).

The arc is also a diagnostic. Something that still requires effort has not reached integration and should not be leaned on under pressure; something that operates spontaneously but was never cultivated is probably not what it appears to be. And it sets the honest expectation for any long formation: the stretch between cultivation and integration is where most people stop, because it is the part with no visible progress in it.
