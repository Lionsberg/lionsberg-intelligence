# Disbanding

*The just dissolution of councils, bodies, or structures that have become corrupted, obsolete, or misaligned — discrete birth, and discrete death.*

Bodies are meant to end. A council convened for a purpose dissolves when the purpose is met; a structure built for a season is not kept alive because dismantling it feels like failure. The canon pairs the two events deliberately: a body that is born on purpose can die on purpose, and one that was never consciously born is almost impossible to lay down.

It is load-bearing because institutional survival is the strongest drive any organization develops. Left alone, a body will outlive its purpose and then quietly acquire a new one — usually its own continuation. That is the root of [[Institutional Idolatry]] and a principal route into [[Manufactured Dependency]]: structures that must be fed regardless of whether they still serve. Disbanding is the immune response.

Its justice is in the manner. Dissolution done well names why, honours what was built, settles what is owed, returns what was entrusted, releases the people with thanks rather than with blame, and leaves the record intact so the work is not lost — the same discipline the Game applies to a person leaving in order ([[The Way Out]]). Dissolution done badly is a disappearance, and it teaches the members that nothing here is accountable.

In play, most Cells will retire a role, a practice, or a working group long before they ever consider ending themselves. The seed's founding instruction carries the pattern exactly: a steering committee that knows it is scaffolding and dissolves its own founding authority.
