# Wise Eldership

*Discernment held by those who have walked further — wisdom-based, not age-based, and sought because it is trusted rather than because it is in charge.*

An elder in this sense holds no office by virtue of being one. Their counsel is sought not because they hold power but because they have earned trust: they have been further along the road, made the mistakes, and can see a season ahead of the people asking. That is a different kind of standing from authority, and keeping the two apart is what makes eldership safe.

It is load-bearing as the counterweight inside [[Democratic Self-Governance Through Shared Agreements and Wise Eldership]]. Equal voice alone produces the tyranny of the majority — a body that is always as wise as its median member on its worst week. Elders alone produce the tyranny of the few. The two together give a body both legitimacy and memory.

Its practical rule is where stalled consent goes. When a [[Circle]] cannot resolve a [[Proposal]], it does not escalate to a boss; it goes to the elders for **counsel** — and comes back with counsel, which the body still weighs and decides. The canon's other guard is that eldership is *wisdom-based, not age-based*: years help and do not qualify, and a young member who has walked a particular road further is the elder for that question.

In play a [[Cell]] usually has no formal elders in its first season and should not invent them. It meets eldership as a person outside the deadlock whose judgment both sides trust — and later, if it grows, as a [[Wisdom Council]] reaffirmed every ninety days.
