# Maps of Content

*The index page form — a page whose content is the way into other pages, so that a body of writing can be entered rather than only searched.*

A wiki without maps of content is a pile. Search finds what you can already name; a map of content shows what is there, grouped so the shape of the whole is visible, so that a newcomer can find the door and a returning reader can find the room ([[The Hub]] · [[Navigation]] · [[The face (a holon's first page)]] · [[Orientation and Onboarding Process]]).

The form is simple and the discipline is in what it refuses. A map of content is **only links and the words needed to place them** — it does not carry meaning of its own, because meaning on an index page is meaning in the wrong place, and it will drift from the pages it duplicates ([[One canonical home per concept]] · [[Link concepts, not coordinates]] · [[The Wiki Reflects the Present]]).

There is usually more than one, and that is right rather than a failure of tidiness. The same body can be entered by role, by question, by order of reading, or by a personal path through it; several are carried at once, including a map of the maps ([[Room Types]] · [[The vocabulary ladder]] · [[The Guide's four renderings]]).

Two things keep them alive. They must be **maintained**, because an index that has fallen behind is worse than none — it is a confident list of what used to be there ([[Improvement log]] · [[Prune]]). And they must be **honest about coverage**, saying what they do not cover rather than implying completeness ([[Gaps Not Yet Closed]] · [[Provenance]]).

The near neighbour is [[Mapping]] and the two are not the same. Mapping, here, is a data practice — the gathering and collating of structured information from which views can be generated. A map of content is a hand-made page of doors.
