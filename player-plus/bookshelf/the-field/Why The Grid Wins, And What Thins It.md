# Why The Grid Wins, And What Thins It

*A distributed body answers each of a predatory order's four advantages with a property of its own — and weakens in five named ways, beginning with lapsed daily practice.*

**The four-against-four mapping** is the argued part, and it holds in ordinary terms. A predatory order's advantages are not superior individual capacity; they are **coordination**, **long-term planning**, **concealment**, and **institutional capture**. A distributed body answers each with a structural property rather than with force. Against coordination: coordination at every scale, so that organisation meets organisation ([[The Seven-Tier Engagement Escalation]], [[Federation]]). Against long horizons: a transmission lineage that operates across generations, so that a plan measured in decades meets a body measured in the same unit ([[Lineage]], [[Generational Time]], [[Pass The Flame]]). Against concealment: distributed attention, because what is watched from many places is harder to keep hidden ([[The Six Grid Disciplines]], [[Transparency]]). Against institutional capture: operating outside the captured institutions, so that what cannot be found inside them cannot be taken there ([[Structural Immunity]], [[Liberation and Exodus]], [[Islands of Coherence]]).

That is a real argument and it is the reason distributed bodies have historically outlasted the orders that hunted them.

**The five weakening conditions** are the more useful half, because they are the ones a body can check on itself. It thins when **people fall away from the daily disciplines** — the first named and the most ordinary; when **federations fragment** under dispute between parts; when bodies **collapse into militarism or faction** and lose federation with the wider whole; when **infiltration or targeted capture succeeds**; and when **catastrophe or displacement destroys concentrations faster than they can be replaced** ([[The Community's Three Vulnerabilities]], [[The Four Warrior-Guild Capture Modes]], [[Collapse]]).

Four of the five are internal. That is the finding worth carrying: a distributed body is mostly not defeated; it dissolves.

The canon's own summary claim — that a fully coherent lattice is **"virtually impenetrable"** — is an assertion with nothing behind it, and it is the kind of claim that makes a body complacent about the five conditions above, which are where its actual risk lives.
