# Spending Time · Units of Time

*Spend time as the sacrifice it is — and get free of its false constraints by cutting it into small units and being wholly present in each.*

The first half is the same arithmetic as attention: to spend an hour on this is to forgo spending it on everything else, so what you spend time on is a real moral choice and not a logistical one ([[Spending Attention]]). The second half is where this chapter goes somewhere of its own, and it is the useful part.

The canon holds time as subjective in a way that attention and energy are not — the hour is not the thing that is scarce, the presence within it is. From that comes a practice rather than a philosophy: cut time into progressively smaller units, and be fully, intentionally present to what matters in each frame. As a starting point, take fifteen minutes as a unit. Be entirely present to the most important thing in that frame. Then detach completely, empty out, re-prioritise, and re-enter another fifteen ([[Here and Nøw]], [[Focused Attention As Discipline]]).

Three things make this more than a productivity trick. **The detaching matters as much as the engaging.** The unit ends, you put it down, and you choose again with fresh eyes — which is what prevents a whole day from being spent on something chosen once, at nine in the morning, for reasons that stopped applying by eleven ([[Attend To The Most Important Things First]]). **The frame is small enough to be honest.** Nobody sustains a day of pure presence; almost anyone can manage fifteen minutes, and an astonishing amount of work, rest or relationship fits inside fifteen genuinely present minutes — or fifteen seconds. **And it dissolves the usual excuse.** *I do not have time* is answerable at this scale; *I have not been present in the units I had* is a different and more workable problem ([[Honesty about time]]).

The chapter's account of mastery follows from it: those who seem to move faster are often simply present in frames that others do not perceive, deploying attention and energy inside sub-units the rest of us pass through unnoticed ([[Mastery]], [[Kairos]], [[Nøw]]).

The practice sits under the Game's larger rhythms rather than beside them. The unit is the smallest frame; the day, the week and the season are the ones a body keeps together ([[The Daily Way]], [[The Turn]], [[Season]], [[Rhythm]]).
