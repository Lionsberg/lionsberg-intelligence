# Open a Communication Channel

*One channel your people actually open, with one agreement about how it is used: flame-tending, not noise.*

A body that meets once a week has six days in between. If nothing crosses them, the week's gathering spends its first twenty minutes rebuilding context that a single message could have carried. If too much crosses them, people mute the channel, and then the one message that mattered is missed too. Both failures are common and both are cheap to avoid.

The move has two halves, and the second is the one usually skipped. **Choose one channel your people actually open.** Not the best tool — the one already on their phones and already read. A Cell of five with one person who will not install an app has, in practice, a channel of four and a member who is out of the loop, which is a governance problem wearing a technical costume. Where there is a real choice, prefer what the body can keep: open protocols, self-hostable services, and data that does not become someone else's asset ([[Technology Sovereignty]], [[Technology Is Not Neutral]]).

**Then agree how it is used.** What belongs here — a heads-up, a change of plan, a question that cannot wait, something worth everyone seeing. What does not — the running commentary, the argument that should be a conversation, the decision that should be made at the table ([[Field of Agreements]], [[Forge Your Agreements]]). *Flame-tending, not noise* is the canon's phrase for the distinction: the channel keeps the fire alive between gatherings, and nothing more is asked of it. A hard thing said in a channel almost always goes worse than the same thing said to a face ([[Having Difficult Conversations]], [[Mend a Tension]]).

A body's own channel is not the same as the wider body's commons. Most Cells will want both: one small enough that everything in it concerns everyone, and one large enough to reach past the table ([[The Board]], [[Communication Debt]]).
