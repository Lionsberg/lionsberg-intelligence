# The Language of LIØNSBERG

*To know a craft is to speak it: the living vocabulary through which the work can be received, and through which resources flow.*

Every system is defined by its key terms. The sailor's few hundred words of sea and rigging, the healer's words of body and remedy — a bounded vocabulary that carries a whole art, so that whoever truly learns the words can receive the work. A civilization is no different. The old order knows this in shadow and bends thought by corrupting words; the new one knows it in light: to learn the language is to receive the system.

The language is spoken in word tools — words held consciously as pointers toward Reality, never mistaken for the Realities they name. That is its first grace: space between the word and the thing it reaches for, where speech about the most important things becomes possible again among people whose traditions have taught them to flinch at each other's vocabulary.

It is learned through three volumes, each holding its facing once — what a term means, how it is established in practice, when to invoke it — and at its heart stands a bounded core of concepts, enough for any family in any village, any Cell in any world, to receive the whole and regenerate it. Beyond that core opens the wider field: further word tools, growing wherever the body grows. The language is alive, and it is spoken into being by all who play.

Learning it is not a gate. Nobody is asked to carry a word they have not been handed, and the words arrive in the order the road needs them. Fluency is a consequence of playing, not a condition of entry.
