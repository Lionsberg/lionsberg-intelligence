# Servant Leadership

*Leadership in service of, rather than in power over — roles as service, and the Purpose governing rather than the person.*

The inversion is simple to say and hard to hold: the leader is not the one the body serves but the one who serves the body. A companion statement from the first irreducible element makes it structural rather than moral — **the Purpose governs; leaders serve** — which means a leader's standing is measured by how well the Purpose is advanced, not by their position relative to others.

It is load-bearing because leadership is unavoidable and therefore must be defined. Every group develops one, formally or informally; the question is only whether the form is chosen. Where it is not, the pattern that arrives by default is the one everybody already knows, which is command. Naming roles as *service* changes what a person is agreeing to when they accept one, and changes what the body may ask of them.

The disciplines that keep it real are structural: roles rotate, so that a role held forever does not quietly become a throne; there is no campaigning, so leadership emerges from demonstrated competence and trust rather than self-promotion; authority is delegated upward by the governed and remains revocable; and leaders are accountable to those they serve, not merely to those above them. Distributed leadership, in this sense, means many people holding small, named pieces rather than one holding all of it.

In play, this is the [[Host]] who prepares the room and hands the talking around, the [[Keeper]] who writes the record, the two coordinators who share a season and then pass it on. None of them decide for the Cell; they make deciding possible.
