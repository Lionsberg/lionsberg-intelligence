# The Gate

*The door of the Game, singular: where a person hears the Story, gives their word, and is written on the Roll.*

The Gate is one place and one moment. A person arrives, is greeted before they are given anything, hears enough of the Story to know what they are choosing, and says yes in their own words. Their line goes on [[The Roll]]; they are handed the little book and the [[The Playcard|Playcard]]; they name the few people they would most trust ([[The naming of the three]]); and they walk into the Game. That is the whole of it. There is no assessment, no probation, and no one with the power to refuse.

It matters that it is a *gate* and not a *filter*. Nothing is withheld from anyone who has not crossed; the library is open, the words are free, and a person may read as far ahead as they like. What the Gate marks is not permission but consent — the difference between having read about a thing and having given your word to it. And because consent can be withdrawn, the Gate does not close behind anyone: [[The Way Out]] is as plain as the way in, and leaving in order costs nothing ([[Consent]], [[Withdrawal Of Consent]]).

The word is easily confused with two others, and the canon separates them in a breath. The Gate is not [[The 144 Gates of Initiation]] — the twelve movements of twelve thresholds on the inner Path, walked over years. Nor is it [[The Gates of LIØNSBERG]]. Those are ascents; this is a door. A person crosses the Gate once, and then spends a lifetime on the Gates.

What the Gate must not do is hand a person words they have not been given. Its own few terms — Player, the Roll, brought-by, witnessed-by — are the first vocabulary anyone meets, and each is owed one plain line at the moment it appears ([[The vocabulary ladder]], [[The Guide's four renderings]]).
