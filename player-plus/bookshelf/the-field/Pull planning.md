# Pull planning

*Start at the finish and ask what must be complete immediately before it, and before that, until you reach today.*

Planning forward asks what we can do next and discovers the handoffs too late. Pulling backward from a [[Milestone]] makes the dependencies visible while there is still time to act on them, because each step is named by the step that needs it. It is done as a group, with the people who will do the work in the room — at least four hours for anything that matters. The milestone goes on the wall. Each person writes the activities they will own, one per card, with a duration and what must be done before it. The cards are arranged backward. The room argues about the handoffs: *what do you need from me, finished, so you can start?* — and the answer is written down as [[Conditions of satisfaction]]. When the wall reaches today, it is walked forward once and the dates are checked.

It is load-bearing because everything downstream is computed from it. Out of the session comes [[The activity table]]; out of the table, by two arithmetic passes, come [[Critical Path]] and [[Float]]; out of the chain come the finish-by dates that make a commitment honest. Without the pull, a plan is a list of intentions in date order and the first surprise re-writes it.

It is run again, not once: at each turning a body re-pulls from its milestones, and when a marker is missed it re-pulls before the milestone is moved. It is the SHOULD of the cycle — what the plan says should happen — and it hands its work to [[The look-ahead]], which asks what *can*.
