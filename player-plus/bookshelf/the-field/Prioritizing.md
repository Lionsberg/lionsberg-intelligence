# Prioritizing

*Deciding the order of the work against the Goal — the step in planning where resources, time and effort are pointed at what will actually move it.*

Prioritizing is determining relative importance and urgency among tasks, projects and initiatives, so that what a body spends itself on is what most advances [[Throughput]] of [[The Goal]]. It is the hinge of planning: everything before it is gathering possibilities, everything after it is execution, and a body that skips it executes whatever arrived most recently or shouted loudest.

The canon's standard is a single criterion and it is not *importance* in the abstract. It is **what moves the Goal** — which is why the practical instruments are the ones that reveal movement. The [[Critical Path]] shows which work, if late, makes everything late; a [[Constraint]] shows where the whole is actually limited, and effort spent anywhere else changes nothing ([[Theory Of Constraints]] · [[Bottleneck · Limiting Factor]] · [[Float]]). Those two turn prioritizing from a matter of taste into something a body can reason about together.

Importance and urgency are not the same axis and confusing them is the common failure. Urgent work announces itself; important work generally does not, and a body run by urgency will be busy, competent, and stationary ([[Waste]] · [[Quick Fixes]] · [[Just in time]]).

Two disciplines keep it honest. **The order must exclude.** A priority list that leaves everything on it in a different sequence has decided nothing; something has to be declined, out loud ([[Stop]] · [[Narrow]] · [[Most Important Things]]). And **it is not a single-metric rule** — the numbers inform a judgment about the wise right thing to do next, they do not replace it ([[Measurement]] · [[Wisdom]] · [[Advantage Based Decision Making]]).

It is done again every rhythm rather than once. What was most important last season is a fact about last season ([[The Turn]] · [[The look-ahead]] · [[Retrospective]] · [[Pull planning]]).
