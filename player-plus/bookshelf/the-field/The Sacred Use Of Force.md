# The Sacred Use Of Force

*Before reading: this page holds a doctrine of last resort, written down so that it can be examined and corrected, not so that it can be acted on. Nothing here is advice, a licence, or a call to act; the law of the place you live governs you; the Game asks nothing of anyone but consent. Read [[04 The Pattern and the Way|The Pattern and the Way]] first.*

*When force is permitted, and how little of it — the minimum necessary to restore [[Right Relationship]], and no more.*

Force is a last resort and never a first strategy. When it is used it is bounded by three things at once: its **purpose**, which is restoration rather than punishment or victory; its **quantity**, which is the least that will stop the harm; and its **alignment**, which means it is answerable to [[Love]], to law freely entered, and to the people it claims to be protecting.

The measure is not proportionality to the offence but sufficiency to the aim. Asking *how much does this deserve* leads to retribution, which escalates and has no natural stopping place. Asking *how little will restore right relationship* leads to the smallest act that works and ends the moment it has worked. This is why [[Justice]] here is restorative: the goal is a relationship put back, not a debt collected.

It is bounded before the moment as well as during it. [[The Warrior's Vow]] names what will never be done and when the engagement ends. Defensive force is distinguished from offensive force, and the discernment between them is trained rather than improvised — under pressure, almost everyone can tell themselves that what they want to do is defence. Engagement happens under witness, and afterwards there is a laying down: the capacity to destroy is put away deliberately, mourned rather than celebrated.

And the harder half is the refusal. [[Restraint As The Refusal To Act]] is the discipline that most often makes force unnecessary — because the earlier rungs of guardianship, naming the harm and withdrawing consent from it, resolve far more than force ever has.
