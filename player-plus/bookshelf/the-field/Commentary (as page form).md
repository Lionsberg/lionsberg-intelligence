# Commentary (as page form)

*A page that holds comment on another page without touching it — named for the page it comments on, with the marker `(commentary)` after the name.*

A concept page is meant to carry the present sense of its concept and only that ([[The Wiki Reflects the Present]]). But readers have things to say that are not the present sense: an objection, a reading, a note that this line came from somewhere, a question nobody has answered. Put on the page, all of that turns the page into a transcript. A commentary page is the answer — a separate page, at a predictable name, holding the remarks, quoting the lines they are about, and leaving the concept page clean.

What it preserves is the same thing the inline practice preserves: **named words stay where they were written.** A commentary quotes the line, says what it would change or what it noticed, and signs itself with who and when — so that disagreement is precise and located rather than a rewrite that quietly erases what someone meant ([[Comment]] · [[Author's Voice Is Sovereign]] · [[Attribution]] · [[Provenance]]).

The two forms are for different lifetimes, which is why both exist. A [[Comment]] is a note fastened to one exact sentence, meant to settle: when it does, the settled line goes into the page and the comment closes. A commentary page is for what is not going to settle soon — a standing reading, a scholarly note, a dissent held open on purpose ([[Meaningful Objection]] · [[Chatham House]] · [[Elision]]).

Its risks are ordinary and worth naming. A commentary can become the place where real disagreement is parked so that the main page need never change ([[Drift]] · [[Communication Debt]]). It can drift out of date while looking current ([[The Wiki Reflects the Present]] · [[Prune]]). And a reader who never finds it will read the concept page as though nothing were contested — which is why the concept page's own *Open* line does work the commentary cannot ([[Gaps Not Yet Closed]] · [[Second reader]]).

The form is its own experiment and says so: the one example carries a note that it is a test page, to see whether the form is liked. That honesty is the right status for it.
