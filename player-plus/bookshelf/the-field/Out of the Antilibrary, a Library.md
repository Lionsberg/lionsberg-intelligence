# Out of the Antilibrary, a Library

*Out of all the books you have not read, forge a small and carefully selected library — and read virtually nothing else until you have understood it.*

The *antilibrary* is everything unread: the shelves you have not reached, the whole of what is written, which is infinite for practical purposes and grows faster than anyone reads. It is not a failing. It is the permanent condition, and the chapter's first move is to accept it rather than to fight it. What is proposed is not reading more. It is choosing.

The instruction has two halves and the second is the demanding one. **Forge a small library** — deliberately assembled, few, chosen for quality rather than for coverage ([[A Few]], [[Quality]], [[Foundational Literature]]). **Then read virtually nothing else until you have understood it.** That is a refusal, maintained over a long time, against a current that is always offering the next thing. Most people do the first half, cheerfully, and never do the second, which is why most carefully assembled libraries remain antilibraries with better titles.

Why it works is the same reason a few things done well beat many done partially. Rereading is where a book actually opens; a text understood is an instrument you carry and can use, while twenty texts half-read are a set of impressions you cannot act on ([[Mastery]], [[Shu-ha-ri]], [[Learning]]). And the reasoning adds the moral edge: reading takes attention, attention is the scarce thing and the moral choice, so every act of reading — and every act of choosing what to read — is a moral decision and not a matter of taste ([[Spending Attention]], [[Attend To The Most Important Things First]]).

The chapter carries two old encouragements for the practice. That a small selected library holds a company of the wisest people from every civilisation over thousands of years, who set down in plain words for a stranger in another century what they might not have told their closest friend. And, again: read the best books first, or you will find you do not have time.

It is held here as a personal discipline, and it has a shape at the scale of a body too — what a Cell reads together, what a commons keeps, what is offered to someone arriving ([[The Living Library]], [[Ring 4 - The Library]], [[Curriculum]], [[Cohort-Learning]]).
