# Welcoming The Stranger

*What a body owes someone who arrives with no claim on it — the oldest obligation in the human record, and a named gap in the written Pattern.*

A stranger at the gate is the case the membrane was built for and the case that tests what the membrane is for. Consent at the door protects a body from capture; it was never meant to protect it from the arrival of a person in need. Hospitality is the practice that holds both: the door is real, the terms are read, and the person is fed first.

It is load-bearing because the arrivals are coming. As the old world comes apart, millions will move — some by choice, most not — and a body that has not decided in advance what it does when twenty people arrive at once will decide it under pressure, badly. The distinction that matters is between the terms of *membership*, which are consented and mutual, and the obligations of *hospitality*, which are owed before any agreement exists. Confusing the two produces either a body that will not open its door or a body that dissolves the first time it does.

In the Game, the smallest form is already present: no one is turned away, the Game is a free gift, and a newcomer is greeted before they are given anything — greeting is an office, not an afterthought. A Cell that receives someone with nothing is not running a programme; it is doing what the table is for. In the System, the questions get harder and are unanswered: how a community receives a number of people larger than itself, how re-rooting works, what a diaspora's Cells owe their scattered members, and how the commons is stretched without being drained.

This is honestly marked as a gap, and the gap is large. What exists is the posture and the door; what is missing is the practice at scale.
