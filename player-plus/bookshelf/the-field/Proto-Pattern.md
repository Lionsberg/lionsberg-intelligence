# Proto-Pattern

*A movement that keeps recurring across many Plays and has not yet earned a Card — named as a candidate, watched, and articulated only once it has proved itself.*

Check in. Make commitments. Harvest and close. These small movements appear inside the first gathering, inside the weekly Turn, inside the seasonal turning, and inside half the Plays a body will ever run. They are plainly the same thing each time. But a thing that is plainly the same is not yet a [[Pattern]] — it is a candidate, and the canon gives candidates a name so that they can be held honestly in the meantime.

The distinction earns its keep in two directions. It stops the Language from **inflating**: if every recurring movement were written up as a Card the moment someone noticed it, the [[The LIØNSBERG Pattern Language|Pattern Language]] would fill with articulations nobody had tested, and the whole point of a Card is that its wisdom is trustworthy enough to be composed from ([[Pattern Card]]). And it stops the Language from **losing** things: an unnamed recurrence is simply re-solved from scratch in each Play, and the fact that it was a recurrence is never noticed at all.

So a proto-Pattern is written down as what it is — *this keeps happening, we have not yet said why it works* — and it waits. What promotes it is use rather than argument: enough bodies run it, the variations show which parts are load-bearing, and only then is it articulated at the general level and folded into the Language as a note ([[How A Pattern Enters The Language]], [[General Pattern, Specific Application]], [[Patterns Pending Articulation]]).

The honesty of the device is that it makes a gap visible rather than papering over it. A Play whose movements are all proto-Patterns is a Play resting on nothing yet written, and saying so is more useful than pretending otherwise ([[Gaps Not Yet Closed]], [[The Playbook Chart]]). It is the same posture the canon takes everywhere about its own unfinished parts: name it, keep it in view, and let practice rather than assertion decide ([[Current Best Understanding]], [[Progressive canonization]]).
