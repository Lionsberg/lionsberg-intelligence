# Experience Is Gateway, Codex Is Corpus

*Two things that are always confused: the body of writing, and the way through it. The corpus is the bookshelf; the experience is the walking. Neither can do the other's work.*

A tradition that has lasted always has both. There is a corpus — scripture, canon, source texts, fixed enough to be printed and handed over ([[The Sacred Codex]], [[Foundational Literature]], [[Every Page Lives Within A Volume]]). And there is a path through it — a sequence of reading, practice and encounter, living enough to be revised as people learn how the walking actually goes ([[The Sovereign Path]], [[Curriculum]], [[Vol I — The LIØNSBERG Experience]]).

The distinction is worth a page because both collapses are common and both are costly. Collapse the path into the corpus and you get a reading list handed to a stranger — everything present, nothing enterable, the newcomer drowning in a library ([[The vocabulary ladder]], [[The grandmother test]]). Collapse the corpus into the path and you get a programme with nothing behind it — a sequence of exercises that cannot answer the question it provokes, and no deeper water for anyone who wants it ([[The Living Library]], [[Progressive Illumination]]).

So a person enters through the walking and reaches into the volumes as the walking calls for them. The corpus is fixed enough to be trusted and to be printed; the way through stays adaptive, because how people actually arrive keeps teaching us something ([[Everything Is Current Best Understanding]], [[Just in time]]).

The rule generalises past books. Any body with a store of knowledge needs both a keeping and a wayfinding, and the failure of most is that they built one and assumed the other ([[Knowledge as commons]], [[The Kit]]).
