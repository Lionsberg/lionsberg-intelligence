# Seats at the Table

*[[Consent]] answers how a decision is made; this answers the prior question — who is in the room when it is made.*

Four seats, and the first one is the bedrock: **one member, one voice.** No seat outweighs another by money, seniority or standing. Nothing that follows overturns that; what follows only widens who counts as having a voice at all.

**The Members** — the people who have consented to the [[Field of Agreements]], each one voice. **A Seat for Living Systems** — the land, the watershed, the soil, the non-human kin the body's choices reach. **A Seat for Future Generations** — those not yet born, who inherit what is built or broken. **A Seat for the Whole** — the wider body this one is a cell of.

The mechanism is what makes it more than sentiment. Each of the three widened seats is held **in trust by a named member**, charged to speak for what cannot speak and to put one question to every weighty decision: *what would this do to the living world we depend on? · would the people seven generations from now thank us or curse us? · does this serve the wider Whole, or only us?* The river does not get a vote it cannot cast; it gets a human voice sworn to represent it. A named holder can be asked to speak before the room closes a decision, and can be held to the charge afterward — which is precisely what a general commitment to "all generations of life" cannot do.

Not every seat need be filled on the first day. Name them in the [[Charter]] and fill them as the body matures. **A table that seats only its present members will, in time, serve only its present members** — and that is not a moral failing so much as a structural one: a room decides for whoever is in it.

The Seat for the Whole is the one most easily mistaken for control. It carries no ownership and cannot override the body's [[Sovereignty]]. It is the living reminder, present at the table, that [[Union]] was chosen over isolation — and it is answered by a reciprocal seat the Whole grants in return.
