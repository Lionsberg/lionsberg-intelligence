# Home

*Your own place inside your [[Home Cell]].*

Two words were doing one job, and people were losing track of which was meant. *Home Cell* is the body that holds you: it hears your season, keeps a copy of your sheets, and comes when something is wrong. *Home* alone is smaller and nearer — your seat at that table, the place inside the body that is yours. A Cell has a Home Cell; a person has a home.

The distinction earns its keep on the cards. Card one asks a Cell to name *our home*, meaning the body its season is sent to; a person filling the lower half of a [[The Playcard|Playcard]] answers for their own place in the Cell — who they are here, what they are carrying, what they offer and ask ([[Requests and offers]]). Keeping the two senses apart means a sentence like *send it home* has exactly one reading, which is what makes a fourteen-line page legible to a body that has never met the table that wrote it.

The word holds its ordinary warmth as well, and the Game keeps that on purpose. A home is where you are known, where your absence is noticed, where you are met before you are useful. A Cell that is a home to its people in that sense will be a home to other Cells when the time comes; one that is not will pass its sheets up faithfully and still hollow out. [[Belonging]] is the felt side of the arrangement, and it is not a decoration on the structure — it is what the structure is for.

Where it collides: in the wider world a home is a dwelling, and the Story says ØNE is the Source everything comes from and goes home to. Neither is this word. A home in the Game is a place among people, held by [[Consent]] and leaveable in peace ([[The Way Out]]).
