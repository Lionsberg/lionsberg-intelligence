# Moving

*Move in the morning to wake, through the day to circulate, in the evening to settle — because the body that does not move dies inside the body that does not move.*

Movement is not exercise. Exercise is an hour scheduled against a life otherwise spent still; movement is the ordinary condition of a living body, distributed across the whole day. The distinction matters because the harm of stillness is not repaired by a single hard session. Long sedentary stretches stiffen, dull thought, unsettle mood and spoil sleep, and they do it cumulatively over decades. What answers them is not intensity but frequency.

Three moments carry the day. **Morning** wakes the body: within the first hour of rising, something — joints articulated, a form practised, a run, a stretch, a [[Walking|walk]]. A day entered without it is entered with a slower mind and a shorter temper, and most people who try both can feel the difference within a week. **Through the day** keeps things circulating: stand and move at intervals, take the stairs, carry what you could have had delivered, hold meetings on foot, and sit in a way that does not compress the breath. **Evening** settles: gentler, more inward, releasing what the day accumulated so that sleep can do its work ([[Sleeping]]).

The forms are many and the body wants several. Energetic practice, which works the inner architecture directly. Form practice, for those walking a martial way ([[Martial Cultivation]]). Strength, so that the body can still lift and carry at seventy. Endurance, for the heart. [[Dance]] and expressive movement, which release what the body has been holding and which nothing else reaches ([[Mastery Of Emotions]]). Functional movement — the ordinary lifting, climbing and carrying of a day, done consciously rather than avoided. And stillness alternated with movement, which is itself the discipline: a body trained only to move cannot rest, and a body trained only to rest cannot move ([[Silence And Stillness As The Ground]]).

The diagnostics are honest and unflattering: stiffness, narrowed range, slowed thinking, poor sleep, and a startling loss of capacity the first time real physical demand arrives. Where they appear the instruction is the same as for any lapsed foundation — begin gently, build over weeks, and let the return be slow enough to hold.
