# Institutional Idolatry

*The sixth disease: the institution becomes the end. The vessel built to carry the purpose is preserved at the purpose's expense.*

An institution is a vessel — a legal form, a council, a school, a wiki, a movement's name. It exists so that the work outlasts the people doing it. Idolatry is the inversion: the institution's survival becomes the operative goal, and decisions that once served the purpose begin to serve continuity instead. The diagnostic question is precise: *would this Body dissolve itself if dissolving served the purpose better?* A Body that cannot answer yes has already made the exchange.

The signs come in order. First, defending reputation against legitimate critique. Then, treating loyalty to the institution as a virtue above [[Right Relationship]]. Then, operationalizing survival — funding, headcount, brand, roll — as the measure of health. By the time an institution is spoken of as a thing to be protected rather than a tool to be used, the exchange is complete.

The refusals are explicit. [[The Pattern Governs, Not People]]: no founder, and no structure, is elevated beyond the Pattern it carries. [[LIØNSBERG Is A Prototype, Not The Eternal Essence]]: what is being built is one instance of something older, and the instance is not the thing. [[Disbanding]] is named as a discipline — discrete birth and discrete death, the just dissolution of what is corrupted or finished — which is only possible in a Body that never made dissolution unthinkable. [[Term Limits And Rotation]] does the same work one role at a time.

This is [[Ideological Capture]]'s sibling: the same capture with a body rather than an idea at the centre. And it stands one step short of [[The Golden Calf]], where the visible thing has replaced not merely the purpose but the invisible Source.
