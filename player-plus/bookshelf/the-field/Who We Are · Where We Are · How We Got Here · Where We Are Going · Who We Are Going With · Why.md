# Who We Are · Where We Are · How We Got Here · Where We Are Going · Who We Are Going With · Why

*Six questions a people must be able to answer together; the Story is what answers them.*

A group that cannot answer these six is not yet a body, whatever else it has. It may have a budget, a name and a meeting schedule and still be a set of individuals standing near each other. The questions are the minimum orientation: identity, position, history, direction, company, and reason.

They are also the test of any story that claims to orient. *Who we are* asks for an identity that a person can actually stand in, not a slogan. *Where we are* asks for an honest reading of the present, including the parts nobody wants to say. *How we got here* asks for a history that includes the wounds, because a people that cannot account for its own arrival cannot trust its own direction. *Where we are going* asks for a picture vivid enough to be recognized when it arrives. *Who we are going with* is the question most strategies skip, and it is the one that determines whether anyone actually moves. And *why* is the only one that cannot be answered by the others.

Answered together, they do something no list of facts does: they make a shared frame, inside which people can disagree about almost everything else and still be walking the same road. That is the whole function of a story, said plainly.

They are asked at every scale and asked again as things change — a person at a threshold, a Cell at a turning, a community in a crisis. The answers are expected to move; the questions are not.
