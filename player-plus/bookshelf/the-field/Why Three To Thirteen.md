# Why Three To Thirteen

*The reasoning under a range used constantly and derived almost nowhere: three is the minimum, thirteen the maximum, four to seven the optimum, and past thirteen you spawn rather than enlarge.*

**Three is the floor for two reasons.** It is the smallest triangulated field — two can pair, and three hold something a pair cannot. And it is the smallest number at which deciding by [[Consent]] replaces bilateral negotiation: with two, every disagreement is a standoff between the only two positions in the room; with three, a third can hold the field while the other two resolve, and the question becomes *does anyone object* rather than *who gives way* ([[Consent-Based Decision-Making]], [[Meaningful Objection]]).

**Thirteen is the ceiling** because it is about the largest number at which every member can hold every other in direct relationship across an ordinary rhythm of meeting. Past that, the group does not fail — it quietly fragments into sub-groups, or holds a lower coherence than it could, which is worse because nobody notices.

**The remedy past the ceiling is the load-bearing part: spawn, do not enlarge.** The right move is a second body, not a bigger one — which is why growth here is measured in bodies birthed rather than members added ([[Grow Your Cells]], [[Self-Sustenance and Self-Replication]], [[Midwife a New Circle]], [[Supercell]]).

And the working range inside the bounds: **five to seven** is where most settle — small enough for depth, large enough for the full range of the practice to surface, sustainable across years ([[Cell]], [[Circle]]).

What makes this page worth having is that three-to-thirteen is stated everywhere and explained almost nowhere, so bodies adopt the number as a rule and then break it without knowing what they are giving up. Stated with its reasons, the range becomes a diagnostic: a body of two should notice that every decision is a negotiation; a body of sixteen should notice which relationships have quietly gone dormant.
