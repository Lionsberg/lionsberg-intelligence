# The Village

*Fifty to a hundred and fifty sovereigns — the last scale where direct knowing alone suffices.*

Below a couple of hundred people, everyone can still know everyone: name, family, craft, gift, grief. Decisions can be made in conversation. Trust is built face to face. Conflict can be resolved without intermediaries, and the whole can be held in ordinary human attention. Above that range it changes — names slip, faces go unfamiliar, and a body must start to cohere by representation, written agreement, ritual, and story instead ([[Circle Coherence]], [[Agreement]], [[Ritual]]).

That threshold is why the Village is load-bearing rather than merely charming. It marks where the Pattern must change its means: the same [[Twelve Irreducible Elements]] hold, but they are carried by structure rather than by acquaintance. Many of the most durable human forms sit at exactly this size — bands, hamlets, small monasteries, colonies that split near a hundred and fifty, effective companies — and several independent lines of evidence point to the same relational ceiling.

In the ladder, Cells do not federate straight into a [[Community]]; they federate through Villages — clusters that can still eat together, raise a barn together, hold a funeral together, and know one another's children ([[Levels Of Scale]], [[Holofractal Omnifederation]]). A Village holds place anchorage, direct trust, mutual aid as native function, and cultural texture: the songs, the foods, the seasonal rhythms, the local stories ([[Culture]]).

It is also the natural unit of standing alone. When something larger fails, a Village can carry on: it has the relationships, the land, and the seasons, and it is small enough to decide quickly ([[The Wilderness]], [[Islands of Coherence]]).
