# The United Front

*A coalition formed **across** existing bodies rather than a new body competing with them — individuals, communities, organisations and movements that already exist, holding one shared aim and keeping everything else that makes them themselves.*

The distinction is the whole of it. Most attempts at unity build a new organisation and ask existing ones to join it, which asks each of them to become slightly less itself and produces one more competitor in a crowded field ([[Weaving vs. Starting New]], [[Federations vs Empires]], [[Attraction, not promotion]]).

Five functions are named for it, and each is a thing a coalition can actually do that its members cannot do alone. **Unity and solidarity** — a shared aim and shared values that hold across real difference, built by the unglamorous work of understanding and respecting what each member is actually like ([[Navigating Difference]], [[Belonging]], [[Meta Unity of Meta Diversity]]). **Amplification** — a platform for voices that would not otherwise be heard, and deliberately for those least represented ([[Share the Stories]], [[Rights extend first to the weak; responsibilities first to the strong]], [[Representation]]). **Collective action** — the things only many bodies together can undertake ([[Polycentric Implementation of Shared Solutions]], [[Connect and Collaborate With Other Cells]]). **Accountability and transparency** — reporting, monitoring, and members answerable to one another, which is what makes a coalition more than a mailing list ([[Necessity Of Accountability]], [[Transparency]], [[The Record]]). And **resources and support** — what members share so that each is not rebuilding what another already has ([[Shared Services, Infrastructure, and Support]], [[Commons]], [[Kit of Parts]]).

What it is thin on is the part a coalition lives or dies by: **how a body joins, what joining commits it to, and how it leaves.** That is the question the corpus's own language of cells, membranes and federation now answers better than the chapter does, and the two should be read together ([[Anatomies of Association]], [[Guard the Membrane]], [[Federation]], [[Supercell]], [[The Way Out]]).

Its failure mode is the familiar one for coalitions: unity maintained by keeping the shared aim so general that nobody could disagree, at which point nothing is being coordinated ([[Meaningful Objection]], [[Commander's Intent]], [[Conditions of satisfaction]]).
