# Proportionality

*Responses, remedies, and structures matched to the scale and nature of the issue they address — a right-sized response, neither more nor less.*

Proportionality cuts in both directions, and bodies fail at both. Over-response: a committee for a question one person could have answered, a formal process for a misunderstanding, a sanction for a slip. Under-response: a serious harm met with a note in the minutes, a structural problem met with a reminder to try harder.

It is load-bearing because the cost of governance is real. Every procedure spends attention that could have gone to the work, and a body that meets everything with its largest instrument will exhaust itself and then stop using any instrument at all. Meanwhile the under-response failure destroys trust faster than almost anything else: people learn what a body actually cares about from what it treats as small.

The canon's instrument for calibration is the span of impact. The wider a decision's reach, the more time a body should spend — moving past the lower standard of [[Consent]] toward the higher standard of consensus, which is always available given enough wisdom, time, and patience. The same gradient governs escalation: solve it at the level closest to the work that can solve it, and go up only as far as the matter actually requires.

In play a [[Cell]] meets it whenever something goes wrong: the question is not only *what do we do* but *how big is this, really* — and the honest answer is usually smaller than the feeling and larger than the convenience.
