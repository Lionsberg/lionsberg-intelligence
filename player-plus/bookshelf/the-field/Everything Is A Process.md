# Everything Is A Process

*Whatever a body does more than once is a process — and a process can be named, written, taught, run, measured, and improved.*

The twin of [[Everything Is A Project]]. A project has a beginning, an end, and a thing that did not exist before; a process is what a body does again and again, and it is where almost all of the actual work of living together happens. Welcoming someone. Deciding. Mending. Closing a season. Moving money. Saying goodbye. None of these is a project, and all of them are either done well the same way each time or done differently by whoever happens to be holding them that week.

Naming something a process is a small, unglamorous move with four consequences, and they are the reason the claim is load-bearing rather than obvious.

**It becomes teachable.** An unnamed process lives in one person's judgment and dies with their absence. A named one can be handed to a newcomer with a page.

**It becomes improvable.** You cannot improve what has no described form. Once a process is written, a defect in it is a defect in a thing rather than a fault in a person — which is how [[Every Defect Is A Gift]] becomes practicable instead of consoling, and how a [[Zero Blame Environment]] stops depending on everyone's goodwill.

**It becomes shareable.** A written process travels. A principle does not: two bodies can agree entirely on what matters and still do the thing in two incompatible ways. This is how one body's hard-won learning reaches all the others.

**It becomes measurable.** Where the work repeats, the gap between what was intended and what happened can be seen, and the flow through it can be watched for [[Waste]] and [[Constraint]].

The danger the claim carries is bureaucracy, and the corpus answers it in one line: write only the vital few, keep each to a page, hold them all as [[Current Best Understanding]] rather than as law. The purpose is never a manual; it is that a body under pressure reaches for something of its own rather than for the habits of the old world.
