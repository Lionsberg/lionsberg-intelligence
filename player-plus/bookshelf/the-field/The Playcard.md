# The Playcard

*The one card you do not fill in: the aim, the compass question, the moves, and the words, received with the Flame and kept where you eat.*

Every other card in the Game is written on. The Playcard is received. It carries, on one side, the Game in a sentence, [[The one question]], [[The Five Moves]], the shape of the week and the season; on the other, [[The Twelve Words]] with one line each, the next turnings from [[The Clock of the Game]], the address of the library, and the edition. A hand copy on any paper is a Playcard, and copying it by hand is how most of the world will first hold one.

It is load-bearing because it is the survival kit. A Cell can lose its book, its sheets, and its screen and still play so long as one Playcard remains, because the card is the whole Game in a hand. It is also the instrument of one-ness: what is printed on it is the same at every table on Earth, which is what lets a stranger's Circle be recognisably the same game as yours. It changes only by [[The edition]], only at a turning, and the old card counts until the new one arrives — nobody is ever out of the Game for holding last season's card.

How it is used. Receive it with [[The Flame]] from your [[Carrier]]. Pin it where you eat. Read the words aloud with your people once. Give copies away freely — it travels under a share-alike licence and is meant to be reproduced. When fewer than two turnings remain on it, find the next edition, or simply look at the sky: the turnings will keep coming whether or not the book does.
