# Best Possible Future

*Out of every future that could be pulled into being, the one at the absolute top — approached as an image rather than as a proposition, and said out loud until it can be aimed at.*

There are an unbounded number of futures available to us. The question this page holds is the simplest and hardest one: *which is the highest, for All?* At this altitude the answer does not arrive as a proposition to be proved. It arrives as an image — something seen, heard, smelled, tasted — and the canon takes that seriously rather than apologising for it. A single design criterion narrows the search enough to begin: **love**. If we love, we want a future as close to Heaven as it is possible to get ([[Heaven On Earth]] · [[The Logic Of Love]]).

Its form in the canon is a litany, not a definition: a hundred and fifty lines of *we see · we hear · we smell*, each one naming a specific reversal. Waters clean. Weapons become tools ([[Weaponry To Livingry]]). Land returned to those it was taken from. Debts released ([[Jubilee]]). The displaced come home. Children playing where they were made to labour. Symphony where there was cacophony. Peace that outlasts the generation that made it. The litany works because it is concrete where abstraction goes vague — it is a sharper rendering of the Goal than any sentence about flourishing, and it can be read aloud to people who have never heard any of this before.

Two moves keep it from being a wish. First, the reader is handed the pen: *what evils would you see abolished, and what goods would you see burst forth?* The Vision is explicitly unfinished, improved by everyone who receives it — one fills up what is lacking, and removes what does not belong. Second, once seen it is **aimed at**, unwaveringly, and progressed toward a little at a time ([[Design To The Goal]] · [[Continuous Improvement]]). Without a vision and an aim, the canon's own warning is blunt: we perish wandering in the desert between where we are and where we are going ([[The Wilderness]]).

It is therefore the beginning of the compass rather than a decoration on it. Every move is checked against whether it makes life a little more like this ([[The compass question]]).
