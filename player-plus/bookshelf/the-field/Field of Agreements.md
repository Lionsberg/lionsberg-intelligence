# Field of Agreements

*The living system of interwoven agreements a body has freely chosen — voluntary covenants and never imposed law, read at the door, accepted once, honoured until renegotiated.*

This is what governs, instead of rulers. Every [[Domain]] has one, explicit or not; the discipline is to make it explicit so it can be examined, consented to, and improved. It holds space for the domain: it defines purpose, principles, practices, boundaries, and commitments; it governs the [[Selectively Permeable Membrane]] — what enters, what leaves, on what terms; and it governs the [[Commons]] — how shared things are resourced, stewarded, and accessed.

Without one, a body has no shared governance, only shared assumptions, and assumptions cannot be appealed to when they are broken. With one, [[Sovereignty]] is expressed through freely chosen structure rather than through imposed control — which is the difference between a body of peers and an institution with members.

It answers six questions: **Purpose** — what is this domain for, and does it align with [[The Goal]]? **Principles** — what do we commit to embody? **Practices** — how often do we meet, how do we decide, how do we handle conflict? **Boundaries** — who is in, how do new members join, how is the membrane governed? **Commitments** — what does each member contribute? **Stewardship** — how is the commons held? The same six work at every scale, from a person's agreements with themselves and [[ØNE]] to the planetary field.

In play, it is the first thing a player meets and the thing they keep meeting: read at the door before they say yes, invoked when something goes wrong, amended by [[Proposal]] at a turning. A Cell that writes one in its first season has done most of what governance asks of it.
