# The turn (of a Movement), and its chapters

*Two depths in one artefact: the **turn** is the reading you can take in minutes; the **chapters** behind it are the full articulation. Read the turns in an evening; read the chapters for the depth of a life.*

Most bodies of writing force a choice. A summary is short and loses what matters; the full text is complete and unreadable by anyone who has not already decided to read it. The usual answer — write both, keep them in different places — produces two documents that drift apart within a year ([[Communication Debt]], [[One canonical home per concept]]).

This is the other answer: **one artefact, two depths, joined**. Each Movement opens with its turn, which is whole in itself rather than a précis, and holds its chapters behind it at complete detail. Nothing is lost by folding into it, because the chapters are where each thing is said best, in full ([[The One Book]], [[Levels of Abstraction]], [[Progressive Illumination]]).

What it buys is that a reader never has to leave to go deeper, and a Movement never has to choose between brevity and completeness. Someone can take the whole thing in an evening and return for years to the same pages at a different altitude ([[The vocabulary ladder]], [[Just in time]], [[The grandmother test]]).

The word wants a warning, because the Game already has a **Turn** — the weekly gathering where a Cell meets, commits, and reckons. These are two different things sharing one word, and a reader who has met one will misread the other ([[The Turn]], [[The Heartbeat]], [[Semantic Capture]]).

The shape generalises past books. Any body that has more to say than a newcomer can hold needs a short whole and a deep whole with a real join between them — a face and its depths, not a summary and an archive ([[The face (a holon's first page)]], [[Bottom Line Up Front]], [[The Guide's four renderings]]).
