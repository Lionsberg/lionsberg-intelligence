# Tending

*The small daily care of the body and the space it lives in — kept clean, briefly, with attention, for the field rather than for appearance.*

Washing, teeth, hair, nails, skin, gut, clothes, the room you sleep in. These are the disciplines most easily dismissed as either vanity or trivia, and the dismissal costs more than it seems to. A body that is not cared for carries something that other people register before they can name it; and the person carrying it reads other people less cleanly in return. The claim here is modest and practical: cleanliness is not cosmetic, it is the maintenance of the instrument.

The Pattern is written against two opposite failures. One is the practitioner who lets basic care lapse and calls the lapse transcendence — as though the body were an embarrassment that spiritual seriousness outgrows. The other is the practitioner for whom presentation has quietly become the practice, so that grooming stands in for anything actually cultivated. Both put appearance at the centre; the correction in either direction is the same. Do the small things. Do them daily. Do them briefly. Do them with care and not for an audience.

In content it is unremarkable, which is the point: bathe, with hot and cold where conditions allow; clean the mouth well, including the tongue; keep hair and nails clean and trimmed, because they collect what the day leaves; attend the skin, which is the body's outer boundary and in constant conversation with everything around it; keep the gut moving, with water, fibre, movement and honest attention to what the body is asking for; wear clean clothes that serve what you are doing rather than perform who you are; and keep the space clean — bedding, cookware, air, order — because the room a person lives in is part of them. Five to fifteen minutes morning and evening, held consistently, does what occasional bursts of attention never do.

The diagnostic is what others notice and no one mentions: a staleness in the room, clothes that have stopped being washed, disorder in a space that mirrors disorder inside, and a cultivation that has stalled for reasons the practitioner cannot locate. Very often what had stalled opens again once the foundation is simply restored.

The word is used twice in this Field and the senses are not the same. Here it means the care of a body; in [[Commons-Tending]] it means the care of what a people holds together, and in [[Stewardship]] it is the general posture behind both.
