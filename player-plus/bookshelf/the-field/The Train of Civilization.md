# The Train of Civilization

*The image of the old order carrying its passengers at speed toward an edge — and a Guide appearing at the last possible moment with a forgotten way off.*

A train is exactly the right picture and no other would do. It is comfortable. It is going somewhere. It is not obviously dangerous. Everyone aboard is being carried by something they did not build, cannot steer, and mostly cannot see out of — and the vast majority of them are decent people going about their lives. Nobody aboard a train is to blame for the track ([[Collapse]], [[The Meta Crisis]], [[The Anti-Patterns]]).

The image does three things at once. It removes blame from the passengers, which is the precondition of anyone being able to hear it at all. It locates the problem in a *trajectory* rather than in any person or faction. And it makes the required act concrete and personal: not fixing the train, not arguing with the driver — getting off, with as many others as will come ([[The Way Out]], [[Liberation and Exodus]], [[Involuntarily Left Behind]]).

Its last beat is the one that makes it bearable. *Just as the first cars tumble into [[The Chasm]], a Guide appears, offering an ancient and forgotten way to avoid catastrophe* — and at the last possible moment humanity leaps off the trajectory toward ruin and begins a shared journey to the new, snatching freedom from the jaws of near-certain defeat ([[EuCatastrophe]], [[The Guide's four renderings]], [[The Promised Land]]).

It is load-bearing because it is the shortest telling of the whole Story, and it fits in a breath. A person who has heard it understands the situation, their own innocence in it, and what is being asked of them, before a single term of vocabulary has been handed to them ([[The grandmother test]], [[Metaphor]], [[Storytelling]]).
