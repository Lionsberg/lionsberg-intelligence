# The Core Concepts

*The short set a reader must hold before the rest of the vocabulary becomes usable.*

Somewhere between the twelve words a table needs to start playing and the full field of the language, there is a middle set: the concepts that everything else is built out of. One and All — individually sovereign, eternally interconnected, no separation but the illusion of it. Sovereignty and self-governance, authority arising from within rather than from an external system. Love as the ordering principle and truth as the instrument, neither of which works without the other: love without truth is weakness, truth without love is cruelty. And the work itself, which is not survival or obedience but the co-creation of Heaven here and now.

Naming such a set is a real act, not a summary. It says: if you learn nothing else, learn these, because the rest of the language assumes them. And it makes a falsifiable claim — that this set is sufficient — which can be tested by handing it to someone and seeing whether they can read what follows afterward.

The set is held as recognitions rather than as doctrines. They are offered to be tested against Reality, filled up where they are lacking and removed where they do not belong, in the same way every other part of this is held.

Two sets sit near this one and are not it: the twelve irreducible elements, which are the pattern by which bodies organize, and the twelve words, which are the floor of play. The core concepts are the orientation beneath both.
