# Mutual Aid

*Risk and value pooled across a body, so that no member faces alone what the whole can absorb easily.*

Misfortune is not evenly distributed in time. Any one household will have a few bad years across a lifetime; a hundred households will have a few bad years every year, and the cost of covering them is small and steady. Pooling converts an intolerable individual shock into an ordinary collective expense. This is the arithmetic behind every mutual society, every burial fund, every barn raised for the family whose barn burned — and behind [[The Offering]] being spent on a member in trouble before it is spent on anything else.

The canon names the two halves separately. **Pooling risk** is protection: a fund, a fund's equivalent in labour and food and shelter, and an agreement that it is drawn on without shame. **Pooling value** is capacity: tools, spaces, knowledge, and skills held so that each member can reach what none could afford alone. The first keeps people from falling out of the body; the second is most of what makes belonging to it worth anything. Both flow from the same commons and both are resourced by [[Voluntary Tithing]].

Mutual aid is distinguished from charity by direction of travel. Charity flows one way from those who have to those who do not, and it fixes the two roles in place. Mutual aid assumes the roles rotate: the one helped this season helps next, and everyone knows it, which is why [[Reciprocity]] and not generosity is the governing principle. It is also distinguished from insurance by governance — the pool is stewarded by the people who are in it, under their own [[Field of Agreements]], and no distant party profits from the claims not made.

Beneath it all sits the floor the Game states plainly: when a member is in danger, in body or in mind, the Circle acts first and records later. No card is more important than the person at the table.
