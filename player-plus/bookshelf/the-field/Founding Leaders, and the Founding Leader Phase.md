# Founding Leaders, and the Founding Leader Phase

*A named, temporary condition in which the person who founded a thing keeps special authority over its pattern integrity — held under oversight from the first day, and intended to end.*

Something new usually begins because one person would not let it die. For a while, that person's judgment genuinely is the thing's coherence: there is not yet a body that could hold the pattern, and pretending otherwise produces a committee governing a thing nobody yet understands. The canon does not deny this. It names it, bounds it, and puts a clock on it.

During the phase, a Founding Leader retains special responsibility and authority over the pattern integrity and governance of what they founded — and, from the beginning, governs **transparently and accountably under a council they themselves convene**, which reports to the wider body no less than quarterly ([[Wisdom Council]] · [[Oversight]] · [[Requirement Of Transparency]]). The council is deliberately not a legal board: early counsel was that installing formal governance too soon would freeze a form nobody has lived yet, and that a body of wise people acting the new pattern out is the better instrument while self-governance grows ([[Democratic Self-Governance Through Shared Agreements and Wise Eldership]]).

The phase is expected to last **two to five years**, depending on the complexity of the thing, and it ends by a positive act, not by drift: the founder, the core team, and the council pass a proposal activating full self-governance in accordance with the constitution and the interoperability standards ([[Proposal]] · [[Consent-Based Decision-Making]] · [[Minimum standards of interoperability]]). The holding body may send that proposal back for improvement — but only for violating the constitution or the standards, or for a reasoned belief that it will harm the entity's ability to accomplish its aim. And there is recourse during the phase: where there is conflict over the founder's leadership, the local core team may ask for a co-manager to be installed alongside them ([[Recourse]]).

This concept is load-bearing for an uncomfortable reason. The commonest way a movement dies is not conquest but a founder who never left, and the second commonest is a founder pushed out before anyone could hold what they were holding. The canon carries [[Term Limits And Rotation]] and [[Impeachment]] for ordinary offices; this names the extraordinary one, where the danger is greatest and the ordinary instruments do not apply ([[The de-concentration of stewardship]] · [[The Act Of Release]]).
