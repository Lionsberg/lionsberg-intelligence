# A Few

*A few of the highest quality are far superior to many that are mediocre.*

It is a rule about where to put finite capacity, and it is stated the same way in four places because the shape does not change with the subject. In **knowing**: far better to know deeply a few things that are true, wise and good than to know many mediocre things. In a **martial art**: far better to master a few techniques that actually work than to half-know many elegant ones. In **delivery**: far better to run a few basics well than to fail to operate a complex system beyond your capacity. In **reading**: far better to read, reread and genuinely comprehend the wisest books than to half-understand a wide array of ordinary ones ([[Out of the Antilibrary, a Library]], [[Mastery]], [[Our Core Ways]]).

The reason it needs saying is that breadth is what accumulates by default. Every additional thing is individually cheap to add and the cost only shows up in aggregate, as a person or a body that does many things at a depth that does not work. Depth, by contrast, must be chosen against something — you reach it by refusing the next thing, repeatedly, which nothing in ordinary life rewards ([[Quality]], [[Prune]], [[The Triadic Discipline]]).

Quality is the operative word and the one most easily lost. The claim is not that fewer is better; it is that a few *of the highest quality* beat many mediocre ones. A short list of poor choices is simply a small failure. So the rule cannot be followed without the harder discipline that sits under it — the judgment of what is actually good, which is itself something that only deepens with depth ([[Discernment]], [[Quality]], [[Wisdom]]).

Then the chapter turns outward with one line: *a very few things move nearly all things*. That is the leverage claim, and the canon runs it as a chain — [[One moves a few, a few moves 3%, 3% moves 20%, 20% moves 80%]] — while the chapter that follows explains how the few are found ([[The 20%]]). Read together the three make one argument: capacity is finite, effect is wildly uneven, and the whole art is locating the small set that carries the rest.
