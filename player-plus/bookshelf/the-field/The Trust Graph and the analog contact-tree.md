# The Trust Graph and the analog contact-tree

*The web of who named whom — the body's greatest treasure, and, in the wrong hands, a map for hunting it.*

When people name the neighbours they trust, the namings add up to something nobody designed: a living map of trust across a waking population. It shows where the real bonds run, which is exactly what makes it valuable and exactly what makes it dangerous. The same map that lets a body find its own people lets an adversary find them first. Both things are true from the first thousand names, so the guarding is built in before the graph exists rather than added after it is large enough to be worth stealing.

It is held in three tiers. A **public tier** — counts, Cells, Quests, stories: the field visible to anyone. A **recognized tier** — what Cells choose to share with one another, by mutual consent and no further. And a **private tier** — the person-graph itself, held locally by the very people it names, exportable and deletable by them, and never pooled at any centre. This is [[Selectively Permeable Membrane|the membrane]] applied to the body's own data as first-order law: what crosses is chosen, and disclosure defaults tighten as the danger of a place rises rather than loosening for convenience.

Beneath every digital layer runs the **analog contact-tree**: each person knowing their three and known by theirs, in memory and on paper. It is the reason the body survives the loss of its instruments. If the network falls, the accounts vanish, or the grid goes dark, the tree still reaches every person in it, one door at a time, at walking speed. No seizure reaches it because there is nothing central to seize.

The discipline this asks is unusual and worth naming plainly: the body deliberately declines to hold, at any centre, the thing that would be most useful to it. That refusal is the security. A graph nobody has gathered cannot be taken, subpoenaed, sold, leaked, or turned; and a movement whose map lives in ten thousand heads and notebooks cannot be decapitated, because there is no head.
