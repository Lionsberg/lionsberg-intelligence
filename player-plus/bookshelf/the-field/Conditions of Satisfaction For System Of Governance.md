# Conditions of Satisfaction For System Of Governance

*The acceptance test any proposed system of governance must pass: a coherent philosophy, sound theory under every element, concrete mechanisms — and nine things the three together must actually do.*

Most governance proposals are argued on whether they are appealing. This page asks instead whether they are **acceptable**, and sets the test in advance, which is the only time a test is honest.

Three levels must all be present. A **coherent and uniting philosophy** with design principles behind it — otherwise the parts will not compose. **Sound theory behind each element** — otherwise a good-looking whole rests on mechanisms nobody has thought through. **Concrete techniques and mechanisms** that put the theory into practice — otherwise it is a philosophy with a governance-shaped hole in it. A proposal that has two of the three fails; the failure mode of most political thought is the missing third, and the failure mode of most software is the missing first.

The three together are then held to nine requirements. They must serve the overarching and uniting highest mission and goal; uphold timeless wisdom, principles, and values through clear and wise mechanisms rather than good intentions; encourage just and beneficial participation among members; establish [[Right Relationship]] among all participants; protect both [[Values]] and [[Value]] for past, present, and future generations ([[Covenant Across Time]]); provide a template that can be instantiated across cultures, times, locales, legal systems, and organisational forms ([[Instantiation]] · [[General Pattern, Specific Application]]); enable sharing of resources, knowledge, and skill across every conforming instance ([[Minimum standards of interoperability]]); cultivate multi-generational mentoring and servant leadership ([[Wise Eldership]] · [[Servant Leadership]]); and bring the practices of every part of the community into alignment with its own highest purpose ([[Alignment]] · [[Drift]]).

The list is load-bearing precisely because it is written down *before* any particular system is judged by it — including the canon's own. A body that publishes its acceptance criteria can be told it has failed them.

This is a different concept from the delivery sense of [[Conditions of satisfaction]], where a receiver states what a piece of work must do before it will be accepted. Two senses, one phrase; this is the systems-acceptance one.
