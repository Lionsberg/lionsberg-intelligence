# The Test of Belonging

*One criterion for what is carried forward and what is left: if it belongs in the world we are making, it belongs here; if it does not, it does not — however familiar it has become.*

The test is deliberately blunt, and its whole usefulness is that it is **agnostic about where a thing came from and clear about where it is going**. A practice from an unexpected tradition passes if it belongs in the life we are trying to make. A practice at the centre of our own habits fails if it does not. Provenance earns nothing; destination decides ([[Heaven On Earth]], [[The compass question]], [[Discernment]]).

Applied, it cuts in both directions. A tool that watches people without their consent does not belong ([[Surveillance]], [[Consent]]). A practice that releases what was stored and returns a person to themselves does ([[Healing]], [[Trauma]]). An arrangement that manufactures scarcity does not ([[Scarcity]], [[Extraction Of Wealth]]). One that moves value toward the work that serves life does ([[LUV]], [[Proof-of-contribution]]). A religion's machinery for standing between a person and the sacred does not; that same tradition's deepest pointing toward direct communion does, and is folded forward ([[ReHarvesting the Great Traditions]], [[Reverence]]).

Two things it protects at once. It lets a body **harvest from everywhere** without dissolving into everything, because the criterion travels with it. And it stops old patterns being inherited **by accident** — which is how they are almost always inherited, carried in on the back of something comfortable ([[The Old Operating System]], [[The Anti-Patterns]], [[Weaving vs. Starting New]]).

It pairs with the discipline of doing: the test says *what* to fill and what to remove; the triadic practice of filling, improving and removing says *how* ([[The Triadic Discipline]], [[Prune]], [[Weave, don't delete]]).

Its honest danger is the danger of any clean binary. A confident body can use it to discard whatever is inconvenient and call the discarding discernment — so the test is worth most when it is run against something the one running it likes ([[Second reader]], [[Meaningful Objection]], [[We Do Not Know]]).
