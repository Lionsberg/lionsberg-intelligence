# Individual And Local

*Universal problems land differently in every life and every place, so a shared solution must be localised before it can work.*

The crises are global and nobody meets a global crisis. People meet a bill, a diagnosis, a dry well, a school that is failing their particular child. That is not a smaller version of the large problem; it is the only form in which the large problem is ever actually encountered — and therefore the only form in which it can be worked on.

The claim has a sharp edge for anyone trying to help at scale. A solution designed centrally and delivered outward addresses a situation nobody is in. What travels well is not the solution but the **pattern** — the general shape, which each place then instantiates in its own terms, conditions, and tongue ([[General Pattern, Specific Application]] · [[Instantiation]] · [[Cross-Cultural Pattern Translation]]). Six things the canon emphasises follow from that: personal responsibility for one's own life and place; real engagement between neighbours and local bodies; genuinely localised strategies that still contribute back to shared goals; building the local [[Capacity]] to act rather than delivering outcomes; empowerment in the plain sense of people doing it themselves; and cultural sensitivity that is not decoration but accuracy — the design is wrong if it does not fit how people actually live there ([[On translating]]).

And it cuts the other way too. Local work that never connects becomes a thousand isolated efforts re-learning the same lessons; the answer is grassroots initiative *plus* connection to the wider network, which is the mechanism [[Polycentric Implementation of Shared Solutions]] describes in full. This page carries the name people actually use and link to; that page carries the machinery.

The whole architecture of [[Subsidiarity]], the scale-honesty of [[Human-Scaling]], and the insistence that a [[Cell]] is the unit rather than a campaign all rest on this one observation.
