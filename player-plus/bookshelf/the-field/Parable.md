# Parable

*A short narrative that teaches by putting the hearer inside a situation rather than handing them a conclusion.*

A parable does not explain. It tells of a farmer, a debt, a road, a son — ordinary material, concretely — and stops. The hearer does the last step themselves, and because they did it themselves, they keep it. This is also why a parable can carry a hard truth past defences that an assertion would trigger: the hearer has judged the situation before realizing they are in it.

It is load-bearing as the teaching form that requires nothing. No literacy, no shared vocabulary, no prior agreement, no authority. It survives translation better than argument does, it is remembered after one telling, and it can be passed by someone who does not fully understand it — which is exactly the requirement for a pattern meant to travel hand to hand through people who will never meet its authors. Where [[Metaphor]] compresses a structure into an image, a parable compresses it into an event; where [[Testimony]] says *this happened to me*, a parable says *consider a person who*.

In play it is the register of the worked examples kept here: a circle of eight, a garden on a corner lot, a ring of ten circles — small concrete tellings that show the Pattern running rather than stating it. A [[Cell]] teaching a newcomer will reach for one of these before it reaches for a principle, and a [[Guide]] handing someone the next stretch of road does the same. Word pictures, parables, and analogies are named together as one instrument.

Two disciplines. A parable with its moral attached is no longer a parable; the gap where the hearer does the work is the form. And a parable is not evidence — it illustrates a claim that must be able to stand some other way, and a body that argues only in parables has stopped being checkable.
