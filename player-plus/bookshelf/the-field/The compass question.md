# The compass question
*Before every move: does this make life a little more like Heaven?*

If it does, make the move. If it does not, stop, discern, and choose a better one. A ten-year-old can ask it. So can a tribe, a guild, a bioregion, a planet. It never changes, it needs no measurement apparatus, and it always points toward [[The Goal]].

This is the whole of the Game's ethics carried in one portable sentence. Everything larger — the [[Twelve Irreducible Elements]], the Pillars, the Playbook, the Path — is the unpacking of what answering it well requires. Nothing larger replaces it. A body that loses the question can still run its rituals perfectly and drift for years; a body that keeps it can lose almost every instrument and still steer.

The question is asked before the move, not after. That placement is the load-bearing part: it makes the compass a practice rather than a judgment, and it puts the reading in the hands of whoever is about to act rather than whoever is in charge. It pairs with [[Hell On Earth]] to stay honest — a question with only one pole cannot be answered wrongly, and therefore cannot be answered at all.

When two moves both point Heavenward, the older players ask a second question: *is this the highest thing we could do right now, for ourselves and for the whole?* Not every good move is the next move. That second question is where [[Wisdom]], [[Discernment]], and the [[Critical Path]] enter; the first question sorts direction, the second sorts sequence.
