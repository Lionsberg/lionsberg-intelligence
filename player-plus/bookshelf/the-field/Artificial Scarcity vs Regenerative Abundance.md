# Artificial Scarcity vs Regenerative Abundance

*Scarcity as a condition maintained rather than a fact discovered — and the abundance that is available when the maintaining stops.*

Some scarcity is real: there is one of this hour, and a finite amount of arable soil. What this names is the other kind — scarcity produced and held in place because someone benefits from the competition it causes ([[Scarcity]], [[Abundance]], [[Tragedy of the Commons]]).

The mechanism claimed is that competition over artificially limited things converts natural collaboration into a contest for survival, fractures the bodies that would otherwise share, and makes everyone dependent on whatever institution rations the limited thing. The dependency is the product; the shortage is the means ([[Manufactured Dependency]], [[Multi-Polar Traps]], [[Usury]], [[Money]]).

The opposite it proposes is not infinite supply. **Regenerative abundance** means arrangements that produce more capacity than they consume — soil that improves under use, knowledge that costs nothing to copy, relationships that strengthen by being drawn on, energy flows that arrive continuously ([[Regeneration]], [[Permaculture]], [[Knowledge as commons]], [[Commons]], [[Ephemeralization]], [[Flow]]).

Where the claim is strongest is the case of things that are not rivalrous at all. Knowledge, patterns, methods and software can be given away without anyone having less, so any shortage of them is entirely a matter of arrangement ([[Knowledge as commons]], [[The open web]], [[CC BY-SA 4.0]], [[Commons-Tending]]).

Where it is weakest is the case of things that genuinely are limited, and the source does not go there. Land, an hour, a person's attention, the capacity of a watershed — these are finite, and an abundance frame applied to them becomes a way of avoiding the harder question of how limited things are shared ([[Air · Water · Soil · Microbiome]], [[Limiting Factor]], [[Proportionality]], [[Omniconsideration]]).

The source asserts this at length and gives no mechanism a Cell could test. The testable version is small and worth running: name one thing that is scarce where you are, and ask whether the scarcity is in the thing or in the arrangement around it.
