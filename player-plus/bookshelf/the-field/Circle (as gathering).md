# Circle (as gathering)

*A gathering of peers in a ring of trust — faces toward one another, no head of the table — and not yet a [[Cell]].*

People have gathered this way around every fire humanity has ever lit. The Circle is the oldest form we have for sovereign beings learning to trust, decide, and build together: the warmth, the witness, the belonging. It asks almost nothing to begin. Three or four people who want to meet are already a Circle, and that is the point of the word — it names what anyone can start on a Tuesday evening, before any commitment has been made and before anything has been promised to anyone outside the room.

What it is not yet is a functioning part of the whole. When a Circle takes up the whole code — covenanting in a [[Field of Agreements]], sustaining itself, choosing a [[Quest]], telling its stories, passing [[The Flame]] — it becomes a [[Cell]]. The Circle is the form; the Cell is the function. Holding the two words apart is load-bearing in both directions: it keeps *Cell* meaning something a stranger can rely on, and it keeps the door of *Circle* wide, so that no one has to promise a season before they have had an evening.

The passage between them is marked. [[Seal the Circle]] is the moment the gathered choose one another and the gathering becomes a body; before that, people are visiting, and after it, they are members with a way out that is also named ([[The Way Out]]). [[Circle Coherence]] is the condition a Circle reaches before it can move as one; a Circle that has not woven coherence will not choose its Quest well.

The word carries an older load. The scale ladder in [[Levels Of Scale]] names the three-to-thirteen rung *Circle*, much of the [[The LIØNSBERG Circle Guide|Circle Guide]] and the Plays say *Circle* where they mean a body that plays, and the Little Book's fourteen-line page is still called the Season Sheet of a Circle. Read those as the earlier word for what is now called a Cell; when a text means *not yet a Cell*, it says so.
