# Dangerous But Good

*Capable of destruction and choosing creation. Strength under discipline — the posture that keeps a guardian from becoming what they guard against.*

Two failures stand on either side of this, and both are common. The first is the gardener in a war: goodness that has no capacity, which cannot protect anyone and quietly leaves protection to whoever is willing to be ruthless. The second is the warrior who becomes the thing fought: capacity that has lost the purity of heart that made the fight worth fighting. **Dangerous But Good** names the narrow path between them — to become formidable, fearless and unbreakable *without* losing what the formidability was for.

It holds that true virtue is not weakness; that those who stand for truth and justice must be capable of great power and must wield it with wisdom and discipline; and that the distinguishing mark is not what a person can do but what they choose. Capable of destruction, choosing creation. Capable of violence, choosing peace. Capable of vengeance, choosing justice. Able to be terrifying, and becoming a protector instead.

What makes it a discipline rather than a boast is the restraint half, and the restraint is the harder half by a long way. Capacity that is never exercised atrophies into pretence; capacity exercised without a governor becomes the old world's logic wearing new clothes. So the posture requires both the real cultivation of [[Capability]] and a standing refusal — [[Restraint As The Refusal To Act]] — and it requires them at the same time, in the same person, permanently.

It is also quiet. *The lion does not need to announce that it is a lion.* Announced danger is a performance and usually a substitute for the thing; unannounced capacity is what actually deters, and what leaves room for a situation to resolve without force at all. That quietness is the tell by which the posture can be distinguished from its counterfeit.
