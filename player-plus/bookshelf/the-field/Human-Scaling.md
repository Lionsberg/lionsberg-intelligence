# Human-Scaling

*Designing at the size of a person and a place — buildings, streets, bodies, and institutions kept small enough to be lived in.*

Human-scaling is the discipline of making things the size that people actually are. A doorway a person passes through without shrinking. A street that can be crossed. A room where everyone can hear. A body of people small enough that each face is known. The failure it names is not ugliness but *overscale*: structures that work on a drawing and reduce whoever stands inside them.

The principle carries beyond building, and the field uses it there constantly. A [[Cell]] is three to thirteen, best at four to seven, and divides at thirteen — not for tidiness but because past that number the relationships thin and the thing stops being a circle of known people. [[The Village]] is named as the last scale where direct relationship alone suffices. [[Subsidiarity]] is human-scaling applied to authority: decisions belong at the smallest scale that can make them well. Each of these is the same claim, that the size of a thing decides what kind of thing it can be.

Its companion is the local material. Building from what the place has — its timber, its earth, its stone, its climate — ties the made thing to the ground it stands on, keeps the knowledge of how to repair it nearby, and produces the variety between places that uniformity erases.

A Cell applies this without any building work. Keep the gathering small enough that everyone speaks. Keep the [[Quest]] near enough to walk to. Keep [[The Record]] short enough that keeping it never becomes the work. When something grows past the scale a person can hold, divide it rather than thickening its machinery.
