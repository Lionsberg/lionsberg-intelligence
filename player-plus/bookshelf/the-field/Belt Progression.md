# Belt Progression

*Recognized embodiment across years, witnessed. A belt is not what you know; it is a mark that you showed you could do a thing when it counted.*

The distinction the canon draws is sharp and unusual: **meeting the requirements never confers it**. A record shows what you can do; a witness shows that you did. Someone must have been there. This removes the failure mode of every credential system — the person who has completed the curriculum and cannot do the thing — and it puts the burden on demonstration in real conditions rather than on assessment in arranged ones.

The unit is years, not modules. Embodiment is the word the canon uses, and embodiment is slow: it means the capacity is in the person rather than in their notes, available under pressure, and visible to those who train beside them. A belt marks a stage of a walk rather than the completion of a course.

Its structure is permissive like the rest of the Path. Some walk in days what others walk in seasons; the belt honors what happened rather than how long it took. And it composes with formation rather than replacing it — apprenticeship to those who have gone further, learning by doing, the Circle as the school, lessons delivered just in time rather than as a wall of curriculum at the door.

What it protects against is the two ways mastery is faked: the claim without the demonstration, and the demonstration without the witness. It also protects the honest practitioner, who otherwise has no way to be trusted by people who have not seen them work.

The canon names it in the Pattern Language and in the Player+ vocabulary and has not written the Card. What is stated firmly is the rule: a record shows capability, a witness shows that it was done.
