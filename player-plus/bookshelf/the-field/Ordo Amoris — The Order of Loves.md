# Ordo Amoris — The Order of Loves

*A life well lived consists of rightly ordered loves — and if anything but the most worthy thing conceivable sits at the top, everything below it is mis-ordered by that fact.*

Love is treated here as a force rather than a feeling: infinite, omnidirectional, and requiring direction to do anything at all. Ordered well it carries a life toward what it was for; ordered badly it carries it with equal energy somewhere else ([[Love]], [[The Logic Of Love]], [[Vector]], [[Alignment]]).

The argument for what goes at the top is short and does not depend on any tradition. **Whatever is highest sets the order of everything under it**; so if the thing at the top is not the most worthy that can be conceived, a person has guaranteed in advance that they will stand wrongly toward whatever actually is most worthy ([[One Purpose Above All]], [[Worthy Goal]], [[Commander's Intent]]).

The chapter then walks the candidates upward rather than asserting a conclusion. Direct love toward the most admirable person you could find — and ask what she came from. Toward the living world that sustains her — and ask what its source is. Toward the stars — and ask what made them, and what made the awareness that can wonder at them. Each step is the same move: what is behind this? The walk ends where the asking runs out ([[ØNE]], [[Source]], [[Mystery]], [[The Ineffable]], [[Awe]]).

What makes it practical rather than devotional is that a mis-ordered love is diagnosable from its effects. When something good is loved as though it were ultimate — a person, a body of work, a nation, a body of writing — it is asked to carry a weight it cannot, and it breaks, and so does the one who loved it that way ([[Idols Of The Pantheon]], [[Institutional Idolatry]], [[The Golden Calf]], [[Do Not Concretize the Absolute]]).

The Latin is kept on purpose, because the phrase is older than this corpus and carrying it under its own name credits where it came from ([[Attribution]], [[Lineage]], [[ReHarvesting the Great Traditions]]).

It is one of a family: the ordering of loves sits beside the ordering of goals, responsibilities, intentions, rules and authorities — the same move applied wherever a plurality has to be navigated ([[The Orders]], [[Order]], [[Prioritizing]]).
