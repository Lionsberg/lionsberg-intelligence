# The Annual Deep Review

*The long look, once a year: revisit the far light, and ratify any change to the founding agreements only after a full year of living under them.*

Four beats hold a body — the day, the week, the month, the season. The year is the fifth, and it is the only one slow enough to do two particular things.

**Revisit the [[The Beacon|Beacon]].** Ten years out is the horizon seasons are chosen against, and it is deliberately fixed so that a turning changes the work and not the direction. Once a year it is legitimate to ask whether the far light is still the one this place is walking toward — whether the decade has taught something the first writing could not know.

**Ratify what a year has proved.** This is the deeper function. A body does not write a perfect charter in its first season; it cannot, and the attempt paralyses it. So the founding rhythm is: adopt the [[Charter]] *provisionally*, live under it for four full seasons, and ratify — or amend — only after you have felt where it holds and where it chafes. A constitution earned through a year of practice is worth more than a flawless one written in a weekend.

That is why the annual review is the only place a change to the founding agreements lands. It puts a year between a tension and a permanent rule, which is exactly the interval that stops a hard season from rewriting a body's constitution, and stops a comfortable one from never revising it at all. No absolute is minted in the heat of the week it was felt.

The year is also where a body can see the things only a year shows: who walked the whole of it, what was actually built, what was released, and whether the place is measurably more like Heaven than it was four turnings ago.
