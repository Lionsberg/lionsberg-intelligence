# Release

*Putting committed work back before it is broken — by agreement, by a declared timeout, or by silence past the gathering — always with a reason.*

A promise ends in one of a few ways. It is kept. It is broken and said to be broken. Or it is let go before either, on purpose and in the open, so that the work returns to the body rather than quietly ceasing to exist in someone's week. That last one is a Release, named as an act because the failure mode it prevents is the commonest one there is: work that nobody is doing and everybody assumes someone is.

The reason travels with it. *Done elsewhere. Not the right hands. Conditions changed. No longer needed.* The reason is what makes the release useful rather than merely tidy: it is often the only evidence a body ever gets that its plan was wrong ([[The Record]], [[Every Defect Is A Gift]]).

Silence has its own release. When a gathering passes with nothing heard, the commitment is released on the silent person's behalf, with the reason recorded as *silent*, and the work goes back into view. That is a kindness rather than a reproach: it removes the accumulating weight of an undone thing from someone who has already stopped being able to carry it, and lets the body see the truth ([[The Heartbeat]], [[Zero Blame Environment]], [[Condition]]).

It is load-bearing because it makes honesty cheaper than pretending. A body with no release has only two honest states — kept or broken — and so people hold onto work long past the point of usefulness rather than accept the second. Adding a third, unshameful exit is what keeps the other two meaning what they say ([[The states of a commitment]], [[Consciously Breaking Commitments]], [[A word given is a word kept]]).

One name, two very different acts, and they must not be blurred. [[The Act Of Release]] is an inner discipline — consciously returning what flowed through you at the completion of any work. This is a line in a register. They share only the plain sense of letting go.
