# Kinship

*Familial relationship extended past the human — the recognition that the other beings of a place are relatives rather than resources or scenery.*

The word is chosen deliberately over *stewardship of nature* or *care for the environment*, both of which keep the human on one side and everything else on the other. Kinship says the relation is already family: the creatures, the waters, the forests, and the soil of a place are part of the same [[ØNE Body]], and a person is a member of that body rather than its manager. *All our relations* is the phrase many peoples already use, and it is taken as given rather than as metaphor.

It is load-bearing because it changes what consent and [[Right Relationship]] have to cover. If the other beings are relatives, then decisions that affect them are decisions taken about family who are not at the table — which is exactly why a community body seats [[Living Systems Member]] beside the seat for future generations and the seat for the Whole. Without kinship the seat is a gesture; with it, the seat is the minimum a family owes an absent member. The Playbook carries the same claim as a Play: honour inter-species relationship, with [[Reciprocity]] and Right Relationship as its Patterns.

In play, kinship is mostly attention. Learn the names of what lives where you live. Notice what is arriving and what has stopped arriving. Take what is needed and not more. Give something back to the system that fed you, which is what [[Compost]] does literally. At the table, let someone speak for the land when the Circle is deciding something that touches it, and let that voice be able to say no.

The discipline that keeps kinship from sentimentality is that families are honest with each other. Relatives kill and are killed, eat and are eaten, compete and depend. Kinship does not deny the eating; it denies the pretence that the eaten are nothing. [[Nutrition]] held as communion is this same claim said at a meal.
