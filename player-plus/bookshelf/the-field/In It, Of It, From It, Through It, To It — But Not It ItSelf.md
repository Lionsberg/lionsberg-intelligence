# In It, Of It, From It, Through It, To It — But Not It ItSelf

*The exact relation between ØNE and anything that manifests from ØNE — the line that keeps "one of the Ones of the ØNE" from collapsing into "I am God."*

The canon says the distinction is older than time and that it is exact. Each of the All is **in** It, **of** It, **from** It, **through** It, **to** It, breathed by It in every part, doing Its work and fulfilling Its intention — **and not It ItSelf**, whose Self no manifestation could ever contain.

The image given is musical. A chord is in the music and of the music; it could not exist apart from the music; and it is still, unmistakably, itself — distinct both from the Song and from the one composing it. Nothing is being held at arm's length here. The relation is as intimate as a relation can be; what is refused is only the last step, the one where the part announces that it is the whole.

The line does real work in both directions. Against the flattening that says creation is merely a product standing outside its maker, it insists on the five prepositions: you are not adjacent to the Source, you are inside it and made of it and headed back to it ([[Immanence]], [[Emanation]], [[Divine Spark]]). Against the collapse that says therefore *I am That*, it holds the sixth clause: the wave is not the ocean, and a being who forgets this has traded a relationship for a mirror. That is the same failure the canon names elsewhere as taking a rendering for the Reality ([[Do Not Concretize the Absolute]], [[The Golden Calf]]).

Held together, it is what makes the canon's central address possible — that a person can be spoken to as [[One of the Ones of the ØNE]], sovereign, precious, and carrying a real share of the Song ([[Lifelight]]), while the centre remains the centre and no one has to be worshipped.
