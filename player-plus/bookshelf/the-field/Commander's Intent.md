# Commander's Intent
*State the aim so clearly that those doing the work can adapt everything else, anywhere, without losing the thread.*

Commander's Intent is an old discipline with a precise function: it separates the WHY from the HOW so that judgment can travel outward. The one who holds the aim says what must be true when this is over and why it matters. Everyone downstream is then free — and obliged — to change the plan as conditions change, because they know what the plan was for.

It is load-bearing because the alternative does not survive contact with reality. Detailed instruction assumes the world will hold still; it will not, and a body that runs on instruction either freezes when the instruction no longer fits or follows it into harm. Judgment in service of intent over obedience to instruction is the only arrangement under which distributed, sovereign actors can move fast and still move together.

It is also the practical face of [[Sovereignty At Every Scale]] and [[Subsidiarity]]. If decisions belong at the smallest competent scale, the smallest competent scale must know the aim; otherwise subsidiarity is just devolved guessing. And it is what makes [[Networks of Commitments]] work between bodies: a [[Cell]] can be given an outcome rather than a task list, because the outcome is legible.

The discipline runs upward too. A player who cannot state the intent behind a request is not yet ready to make it; a player who receives a request whose intent they cannot see should ask before committing, because an unexamined WILL is not a word given. In play it appears as the line before the plan: *here is what we are trying to make true, and why; here is our current best guess at how.*
