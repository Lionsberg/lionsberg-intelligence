# Range Of Tolerance

*The band inside which a member can live with a decision that is not what they would have chosen — the space between preference and objection.*

Every person has three zones around any proposal: what they would choose, what they can live with, and what they cannot accept. [[Consent]] is defined against the middle one: a proposal has consent when it falls inside everyone's range of tolerance as the body advances toward its aim. Naming that band is what lets a group of sovereign people decide anything at all.

It is load-bearing because without it, consent collapses into either unanimity of preference — where nothing passes — or politeness, where people swallow real objections because they lack the language for *this is not my choice and I can carry it.* Range of tolerance gives that language, and with it a way to distinguish the two things members owe the body: [[Meaningful Objection]] when the aim is at risk, and generous tolerance when it is not.

It also explains dissonance. The closer a proposal comes to the wise right thing, the more harmony surrounds it; the more out of tune it is, the more dissonance is felt, until it falls outside someone's range altogether. So a rising chorus of discomfort is data about the proposal, not about the people feeling it — and a member who does not consent may require improvement until the proposal is inside everyone's band.

In play, the useful move is to ask it directly rather than infer it: *is this inside your range, or is it an objection?* The question separates grumbling from a stop signal, and most groups find the honest answer is *inside, and I would like it noted.*
