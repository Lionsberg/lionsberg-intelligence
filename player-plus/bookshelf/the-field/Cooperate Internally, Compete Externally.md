# Cooperate Internally, Compete Externally

*Cooperation and competition are not opposite moralities. They are postures for different relations — and a healthy body runs both, deliberately, knowing where its boundary is.*

Inside the body — the Circle, the Cell, the federation — the discipline is mutual aid: solidarity in the economics, [[Consent]] in the deciding, open information, aligned incentives, and the default that what helps the other helps me ([[Mutual Aid]], [[Reciprocity]], [[Zero Blame Environment]]).

At the boundary — in engagement with the wider market and with bodies that are not playing this way — the discipline is vigorous, skilled engagement. A cooperative federation that lasted seventy years did not pretend the global market was a cooperative arena; it competed there, well ([[Mondragon]], [[Quality]], [[Anti-Fragility]]).

The two together make a body both protected and alive. Internal cooperation produces the trust and shared infrastructure that let it take risks an isolated competitor could not survive. External excellence lets it sustain itself without depending on subsidy or goodwill ([[Self-Sustenance and Self-Replication]], [[Underwriting The New Economy]]). The cooperative cases are interesting precisely because the internal solidarity was not a handicap — it was where the performance came from: people who own the thing, decide its direction and share its returns outwork people who do not ([[Agency, not ownership]], [[Proof-of-contribution]], [[Compressed Pay Ratios]]).

It resolves a confusion common among those arriving from the old world, who have seen competition mostly in its predatory form and conclude the new thing must have none. There is competition that raises the floor — craft against craft, athlete against athlete, two articulations of the same pattern tested against each other — and competition that lowers the ceiling by extracting rather than building. The first is kept; the second refused ([[Mastery]], [[Extraction Of Wealth]], [[Multi-Polar Traps]]).

Two failures come from getting the sides backwards. A leader who competes *inside* — accumulating authority, extracting from members — destroys the body from within. A leader who is purely cooperative *outside* leaves it undefended ([[Right Use of Power]], [[Guardianship]]). And the boundary moves: what was external becomes internal as bodies join, so the question of where the line currently runs is re-asked rather than assumed ([[Guard the Membrane]], [[Federation]]).
