# Testimony

*The form a story takes when the teller is the evidence — first person, in front of people who can check it, about something that actually happened to them.*

A [[Story]] can be told about anyone. Testimony is told about oneself, by oneself, to a room. That is the whole distinction and it is what gives the form its weight: the speaker is not arguing for a claim, they are standing as the claim, with a face and a name and a life that the listeners can see. It cannot be refuted the way an argument can, and it cannot be borrowed.

It is load-bearing because it is the proof a movement built on trust actually runs on. Nobody is persuaded into a decade of hard work by a projection; they are moved by one person they know saying *this happened to me*. Stories of transformation are what make a [[Forward-Looking Story]] credible rather than wishful — a picture of the future carries no weight until someone present can testify to a piece of it having already arrived. The twelve-step rooms, one of the Pattern's named Earthly lineages, are built almost entirely of this form, and they have transmitted across a century without a centre, a budget, or a marketing apparatus.

In play, testimony has a shape worth keeping. It is bounded — one person, one experience, a set length, so it does not become the room. It is honest about the bad part; a testimony with no cost in it is a performance. It makes no demand on the listener. And it is voluntary: a Circle that expects testimony has begun to extract it, and extracted testimony is not testimony. Bearing witness is its receiving half — the listeners' work, close kin to [[Listening]], is to hear it without fixing it.

Testimony is also a governance instrument. A body that lets those it has affected testify at its own table, and records what was said, learns things that no report would ever surface. [[Reconciliation]] runs on it.
