# Music

*Harmonic expression of soul and spirit, able to tune, heal, and lift a being — and the fastest way a group of people becomes one.*

Singing together does something that talking together does not. Breath synchronizes, attention converges, and a room of individuals becomes, briefly and unmistakably, a single body. Every durable human institution discovered this and used it. That is the practical claim; the canon's deeper one is that harmony is not merely pleasant but structural — [[Resonance]] is how coherence spreads through living things, and music is resonance made deliberate.

It is load-bearing for the same reason [[Storytelling]] is. A pattern carried only in documents transmits to those who read; a pattern carried in songs transmits to everyone, survives the loss of the documents, and is remembered under stress when nothing else is. A [[Cell]] that sings has a second channel for its [[Culture]], one that does not depend on literacy, electricity, or anybody's archive. The canon's own architecture uses the vocabulary — notes, riffs, songs, and the symphony — to describe how [[Pattern]] composes, which is a strong hint about which art it takes as primary.

In play, this needs nothing. Any group can open or close [[The Turn]] with one song, sung badly, and receive most of the benefit. A [[Festival]] without music is a meeting with food. What a [[Community]] sings at a birth, a death, and a turning of the season is the shortest description of what it believes.

The canon names much and has written little. Sacred music, sacred sound, sacred frequency, sacred song, chant, toning, mantra, and drumming are all listed — and listed twice: once in the Pattern vocabulary and again among the significant gaps, as Patterns needed and not made. Claims in the wider corpus about specific tunings and frequencies are held loosely here; nothing in the canon establishes them, and the practice stands without them.
