# Dissolution by prior design

*A sound centre dissolving itself in advance — not because it failed, but so that there is nothing left to inherit, capture, or corrupt.*

The usual story of dissolution is a failure story: a body ends because it went wrong, or was ended by someone. This is the other kind, and the difference is the timing. The dissolution is decided **before** it is needed, while the ones deciding are the ones who would benefit from deciding otherwise, and it is executed regardless of whether the moment feels right ([[Disbanding]], [[The steering committee that dissolves itself]], [[Term Limits And Rotation]]).

What was dissolved, in the case this comes from, is stated as an inventory rather than a sentiment: no organisation, no accounts, no employees, no legal entity, no directive authority. Nothing remained that could be inherited, seized, sued, bought, or corrupted — because the work had already been given away as an open gift to anyone who takes it up ([[The asset lock]], [[Structural Immunity]], [[Gift]], [[Commons]]).

It is an **anti-capture instrument**, and it works on a different principle from every other one. Governance mechanisms make capture difficult; this makes capture pointless. There is no seat to take, no treasury to freeze, no succession to contest — which is the same reasoning as emptying a pool before it can be seized, applied to a whole body rather than to money ([[The anti-pooling law]], [[Centralization Of Power]], [[Regulatory Capture]], [[A role held forever quietly becomes a throne]]).

And it is what makes a particular kind of trust askable. Not *trust our institution*, because there is none; not *trust our leader*, because the work outlived its founder; not *trust our claims*, because every page invites its own correction. The trust on offer is smaller and more checkable than any of those: the gift is real, the givers sought nothing, the books are open, and the door is not locked from either side ([[The Foundation of Trust (the four promises)]], [[Every Defect Is A Gift]], [[Transparency]], [[LIØNSBERG Co-Creators]]).

Its cost belongs on the page. A body with nothing at its centre also has no one to hold accountable, no one to call when something breaks, and no way to refuse a bad use of its own name. What replaces the centre is stewardship that keeps distributing itself outward, so that no single life is ever a single point of failure — which is a design and not yet a demonstration ([[The de-concentration of stewardship]], [[Necessity Of Accountability]], [[Legitimacy]], [[Impeachment]]).
