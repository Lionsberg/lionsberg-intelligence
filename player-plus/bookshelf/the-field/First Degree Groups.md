# First Degree Groups

*The older vocabulary for composition: a group of groups is second degree, a group of those is third, and so on upward.*

Before the body had the words it now uses, the corpus counted degrees. A first degree group is people; a second degree group is first degree groups federated; higher degrees continue the same way. It is the same move the field now names [[Cell of Cells]] and [[Supercell]], expressed as an ordinal rather than as a living thing ([[Community of Communities]]).

The counting has one real virtue: it makes the arbitrariness of any particular rung obvious. Degrees keep going in both directions, which is exactly what the ladder says — no true bottom, no true top, only one Body ([[Levels Of Scale]], [[Holofractal Scaling]]). Its weakness is that a degree tells you nothing about what a body *is*: a second degree group could be a federation of sovereign Cells or a holding company, and the words do not distinguish them.

That is why the field carries this as a historical also-called rather than as live vocabulary. Where a page still counts degrees, read *supercell* and its subcells, and check that what is described actually holds the five functions rather than merely sitting one level up ([[Cell]], [[Agency, not ownership]]).
