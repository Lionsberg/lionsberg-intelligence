# Ring 4 - The Library

*The corpus ring: the whole body of work around the compressed articulations, organised so that you can find what you need when you need it.*

Three layers are named, and the distinction between them matters. [[The Sacred Codex]] is the bound spine — the twelve Volumes. The Library is the canonical-companion field around it: the substantive works that deepen particular aspects of the Way without being part of the spine. And the wider wiki is the procession out of which both grew, preserved with [[Provenance]] rather than tidied away.

Keeping the procession is the load-bearing choice. A corpus that deletes its own earlier thinking loses the ability to show how it arrived anywhere, and a reader who cannot see the road has to take the destination on trust. [[Weave, don't delete]] is the convention; earlier articulations are superseded in use and retained in record, each carrying its status — canon-grade, draft, ore, stub — so that no one mistakes an unfinished page for a settled one.

The ring is also where the honest warning about size lives. This is a body of millions of words, and parts of it were generated carelessly and repeat themselves. Weight by use and by what is settled, not by repetition; a page that exists is not thereby agreed; grandeur is not evidence. [[One canonical home per concept]] is the discipline that slowly resolves it, and the index at read.lionsberg.world is where that resolution is being worked out in the open.

In the Game nobody reads the Library. They are pointed into it — by a question at a table, by a Pattern a Play invokes, by a word whose meaning is at stake. That is what an organised library is for: not to be consumed, but to be entered at the right page.
