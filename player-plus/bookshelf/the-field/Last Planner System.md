# Last Planner System

*The lineage the commitment discipline comes from: the planning and promising of work is done by the people who will do it, on the front line, week by week.*

Its name is its argument. The last planner is the person closest to the work — the one whose hands will do it — and the promise that matters is theirs, not a schedule handed down. From that one move the rest follows: plan pulled backward from the milestone, a look-ahead window in which constraints are found and cleared, a weekly promise of what *will* be done, a record of what was, and a measure of promises kept. [[SHOULD-CAN-WILL-DID]] is its cycle; [[Kept as a share of made]] is its number.

It is load-bearing as a lineage rather than as a doctrine. It is named here because the discipline was not invented here: it was found in the production systems that build the largest works on Earth, tested on billion-dollar programmes, and carried across intact because it survives translation to a kitchen table. Honouring the lineage without owning it is the rule — the pattern is discovered, not invented, and the people who found it are named.

What it changes is where authority sits. A command loses fidelity at every level it descends; a promise made by a sovereign who understands the work and stakes their own word carries its full weight. That is why a body runs on [[Networks of Commitments]] rather than a chain of instructions, and why the same method that keeps a construction programme honest can be held in a notebook by three people.
