# Self Control

*Governing one's own impulse in the moment — the ability to not do the thing, so that what one actually does is chosen rather than triggered.*

Self control is the narrowest and most immediate of the self-governing virtues, distinct from [[Discipline|self discipline]] and self governance. **Self control** is the moment: the pause between impulse and act. **Self discipline** is the pattern: doing the thing that was decided, when the decision has gone cold. **Self governance** is the whole arrangement a person runs themselves by ([[Virtues]] · [[Discipline]] · [[Self-Governing, Self-Sustaining, and Self-Replicating]]).

It is not the suppression of the drives, and the difference matters. The drives are the motive power a person has; a life run by holding them all down has no fuel left ([[Motivational States]] · [[Mastery Of Emotions]]). What control buys is the gap in which a drive can be *placed* beneath something higher rather than obeyed outright ([[Higher Order Functional Unity]] · [[Aligned Will]] · [[Focused Attention As Discipline]]).

It is also a capacity rather than a trait, and it is depletable. It runs down under fatigue, hunger, fear and noise, which is why bodies that depend on everyone's continuous restraint fail, and why the practical answer is usually structural: rhythms and rest that keep the capacity charged, environments that do not provoke, and agreements that make the good move the easy one ([[Rest And Regeneration]] · [[Day shape]] · [[Field of Agreements]] · [[Addiction]] · [[Composure Under Threat]]).

And it is load-bearing far beyond personal conduct. Almost everything here depends on sovereigns who govern themselves, because nothing above them is going to: consent cannot bind someone who cannot keep a word, and a body of the unrestrained requires a ruler ([[Sovereignty]] · [[A word given is a word kept]] · [[Commitment]] · [[Consent]] · [[Restraint As The Refusal To Act]]).
