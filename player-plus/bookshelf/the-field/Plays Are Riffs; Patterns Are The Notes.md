# Plays Are Riffs; Patterns Are The Notes

*The composition rule between the two layers: a Play arranges; a Pattern carries the wisdom. The Pattern Language is the source of the music.*

A [[Play]] is a brief score for a recurring operational need — how to run a gathering, make a proposal, decide, welcome a newcomer, release a member with grace, choose a Quest, pass the Flame. It does not restate the wisdom it uses. It *invokes* Patterns, in sequence or in weave, with short notes about timing, transition, and improvisation. The deeper HOW of each move lives on its [[Pattern Card]], once, canonically.

It is load-bearing because it is what keeps the corpus from duplicating itself into incoherence. Every Pattern lives in exactly one canonical place; every Play arranges them. When a body opens a Play, the Play sends them to the Patterns for the wisdom; when a body deepens a Pattern, every Play that invokes it improves at once. This is [[One canonical home per concept]] applied to practice, and it is the difference between a living language and a pile of overlapping guides.

How it is held. Write a Play as choreography, never as doctrine: name the moment, the sequence, the variations, the outcome. Write a Pattern as context, forces, and solution, at the general level — most things that look like patterns are applications, and they should be folded back to the general. Keep both facings visible: [[The Playbook Chart]] shows which Patterns compose which Plays; the reverse facing, held in [[The LIØNSBERG Pattern Language]], shows each Pattern beside the Plays that call it. Neither is the master; they are two readings of one architecture.
