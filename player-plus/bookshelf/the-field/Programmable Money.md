# Programmable Money

*Currency that carries rules about how, when, where, and by whom it may be spent — so that the instrument of exchange becomes the instrument of permission.*

Ordinary money is indifferent. It does not know what it is buying, and that indifference is a large part of what makes it freedom rather than allowance. Programmable money is money that knows — and can therefore act ([[Money]], [[Exchange]], [[Trade]]).

The capabilities are not speculative; they are design features discussed openly in central-bank research and pilot programmes. Funds can be **frozen** without a court. Money can be made to **expire** if not spent, forcing consumption. Purchases can be **restricted** — a category of goods, a destination, a recipient. Every transaction can be **watched** in real time, producing a complete financial portrait of a person. And **negative rates** can be applied directly, taxing holding itself ([[Surveillance]], [[Control Of Information]], [[Technology Is Not Neutral]]).

Why it matters more than most technology arguments: money is the substrate almost everything else runs on. A restriction placed there needs no police, no court, and no enforcement, because it is executed by the instrument itself. Whoever sets the rules governs behaviour without ever issuing an order ([[Power Over]], [[Kontrolle]], [[Manufactured Dependency]], [[The Digital Panopticon]]).

It is also the thing likeliest to be offered at the moment of greatest vulnerability. When a monetary order fails, a replacement will be needed quickly and the pitch will be stability, efficiency and inclusion — all of which will be true, and none of which is the question ([[The Endgame Cascade]], [[The Race Between Liberation and Kontrolle]]).

Which is why a value instrument is designed *in advance* rather than accepted in an emergency, and why the tests that matter are structural: can it be used without permission; can it be used when the network is down; does anyone hold a switch ([[LUV]], [[Time Currency]], [[Proof-of-contribution]], [[Technology Sovereignty]], [[Off switch]], [[Screens and paper]]).

The source's figures on adoption are its own, from 2026, and are not carried here. The capabilities are the load-bearing part and are independently checkable.
