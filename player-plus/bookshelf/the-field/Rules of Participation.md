# Rules of Participation

*The guidelines, norms and expectations a particular body sets for how its people work together — written locally, assented to on joining, and improved over time.*

Every group already has rules of participation. The only question is whether they are written down and agreed, or unwritten and enforced by discomfort. Writing them is the move: it makes the expectations visible, checkable, arguable, and improvable, and it lets a newcomer know what they are walking into before they walk into it.

They are **defined locally**. No higher body issues them, because the specifics of how a given group communicates, decides, resolves conflict and allocates what it has depend on who is in it and what it is for ([[Sovereignty At Every Scale]] · [[Subsidiarity]]). They typically cover communication, decision-making, conflict resolution, resource allocation, and whatever else this group has learned it needs to say out loud.

They are **assented to**. Anyone who wishes to join must agree to them; that assent is what makes them binding rather than imposed, and it is why they can be binding at all among sovereigns ([[Consent]] · [[Voluntary Association]] · [[The Invitation]]). It also means that a rule nobody agreed to is not a rule, and that withdrawal remains available ([[Withdrawal Of Consent]]).

And they are **improved**. They are held at current best understanding, revisited when they stop serving, and changed by the same consent that adopted them ([[Continuous Improvement]] · [[Current Best Understanding]] · [[Proposal]]).

The near neighbour is the [[Field of Agreements]], and the difference is worth keeping. A Field of Agreements is the whole live set of what a body has agreed — including its aim, its roles, and its promises to one another. Rules of Participation are the conduct layer within that: how we behave here. In practice a small [[Cell]] writes one short page that does both; a larger body separates them.
