# Shoshin's Five Signs And Five Disciplines

*Beginner's mind made checkable: five observable signs that it is still present, and five practices that keep it so.*

[[Shoshin]] names the posture. This is the instrument — because the failure it guards against is invisible from inside, and a posture nobody can check is a posture that quietly departs.

**The five signs.** *Real surprise* — someone who has lost it no longer meets anything genuinely surprising, because everything is fitted into what is already known. *The willingness to be a beginner again*, in body, before any teacher, in any encounter — walking into another tradition's session as a novice, not symbolically. *The capacity to say "I do not know"* in a domain where one is acknowledged as expert — actual willingness to stay in an open question rather than collapse it into a remembered answer ([[We Do Not Know]]). *Continuing awe* — the one who has stopped falling into wonder has stopped walking ([[Awe]], [[Reverence]]). *Revising a long-held position without grief* — the captured defend old positions; the open update ([[Everything Is Current Best Understanding]], [[Every Defect Is A Gift]]).

**The five disciplines.** *Daily return to stillness* — the same practice at every level of standing; what deepens is what the sitting reveals ([[Silence And Stillness As The Ground]], [[The Practice Cadence]]). *Cross-stream training* — periodically studying under another tradition, and feeling what being a beginner is actually like ([[Progressive Illumination]]). *Witness from those younger in the path* — actually listening to those newer, because some things are clearer from where they stand ([[Learning]]). *Honest review*, held with others rather than alone, carrying the question *what have I closed to that I should be open to?* ([[OmniSpection]], [[Retrospective]]). *The constant question — what am I missing?* — not as anxiety, as practice.

The failure being guarded against is named exactly: **the corruption of the master by their own mastery.** The most exposed person is not the newcomer but the long-walking, the honoured, the certified, and any tradition that has lived long enough to produce fixed orthodoxies ([[Ideological Capture]], [[Honoring Lineage Without Lineage-Capture]]).
