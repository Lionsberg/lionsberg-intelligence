# It Has To Be Utopia Or Oblivion For Us All

*The stakes stated as a fork: at this level of capability and entanglement, a world that works for some is no longer a stable option.*

The line is Buckminster Fuller's, carried into this corpus because it says in one sentence what the Story spends chapters establishing. Once a species can reach every part of its own planet — and harm every part of it — partial solutions stop being partial. A safe enclave inside a destabilised world is a delay, not a destination. The entanglement that makes local catastrophe global also makes local flourishing insufficient.

The claim is load-bearing because it grounds the *for All* in [[The Goal]]. Universality here is not moral decoration added to a practical programme; it is the practical programme. If oblivion is shared, then so is any real alternative, and "make the world work for one hundred percent of humanity, in the shortest possible time, through spontaneous cooperation, without ecological offence or the disadvantage of anyone" becomes a specification rather than a sentiment. That sentence is carried in the corpus as the Earthly echo of the Intention.

It should be read as a statement of stakes and not as a mathematical proof, and the corpus holds it that way. Framed carelessly it becomes exactly the kind of false either-or the Way warns against — and the discipline of [[No False-Oppositional Framings]] applies to this line as to any other. What it legitimately rules out is not gradual work but *selective* work: a plan whose success requires that some be left behind is not a smaller version of the Goal, it is a different goal.

In the Game it shows up as the floor beneath the Way Out and beneath every commons: nobody is carried who has not chosen to walk, and nobody is left out who has. In the System it is why [[None Of Us Until All Of Us]] stands beside it, and why [[Omniconsideration]] is a named discipline rather than an aspiration.
