# A word given is a word kept

*...or a gap learned from, without blame.*

It is the seventh standard and the last, and it is the only one that is not about form. The other six could be met by a careful clerk; this one cannot be met by anyone who does not mean it. It says two things at once, and both halves are load-bearing. A word given is kept — so a promise is a real thing and a body can build on it. And where it is not kept, the gap is learned from without blame — so the truth about a miss can actually be told, which is the only way the first half survives contact with reality.

It is load-bearing because everything else in the system is denominated in this. [[Commitment]] is the unit of progress; [[Kept as a share of made]] is the number a body reads about itself; [[The Record]] holds the promise beside the outcome. Strip the seventh standard and the cards still line up, the addresses still resolve, and none of the numbers mean anything, because a promise that costs nothing to break measures nothing.

It is also the one value the seed names as making the Game possible at all: nothing binds without your own word, and your word is the whole Game. Which is why the blame clause is not softness. A body that punishes gaps gets fewer reported gaps and no fewer misses; a body that reads them for cause in the set-up rather than in the person gets the truth early enough to act on.
