# Mandate

*A legitimate and sacred charge to act on behalf of the Whole within a specific [[Domain]], conferred by recognition of [[Authority]] and alignment with [[Logos]].*

A mandate is narrower than authority and larger than a task. It is what a body hands a steward when it says: *this is yours to carry, on our behalf, for this while.* Because it is conferred, it can be withdrawn; because it is a charge rather than a possession, it comes with an account owed back to those who gave it.

The distinction matters most where good people accumulate quiet power. Work drifts toward whoever does it; over a few seasons, doing becomes deciding and deciding becomes owning. Naming a mandate — its scope, its term, its reporting — is the simple instrument that keeps competence from silently becoming entitlement. Where mandates are unnamed, [[Impeachment]] has nothing to point at, [[Accountability]] has no measure, and [[Term Limits And Rotation]] cannot be applied because no one can say what is being rotated.

At the largest scale stands the Cosmic Mandate: humanity's charge, as a maturing species, to co-create [[Heaven On Earth]] and beyond — stewardship of life, sovereignty honoured, co-creation with the Divine, and service rather than self-aggrandisement. This is a visionary articulation rather than an operational one; read it for orientation, not for procedure.

In play, a mandate is usually one line in a [[Field of Agreements]] or a [[Roles]] chart: what this role holds, to whom it answers, and when it is reviewed. Small bodies can carry mandates in their heads for a season; the moment a second table depends on the first, writing them down stops being bureaucracy and starts being kindness.
