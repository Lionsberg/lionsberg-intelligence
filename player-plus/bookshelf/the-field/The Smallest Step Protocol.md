# The Smallest Step Protocol

*For the moment when nothing large is available: find the smallest action you can actually take, take it, notice that you took it, and let the energy gather.*

Most methods assume a person has capacity to act. This is the one for when they do not — when grief, dread, exhaustion or a nervous system at full activation has stopped the body, and every reasonable piece of advice is out of reach ([[Anxiety as Default-On]], [[Overwhelm]], [[Grief]]).

**Name what you are moving toward.** It does not have to be large. *Feel the morning sun. Finish this glass of water. Wash three dishes.* It must be worth doing and it must be sized to the capacity actually present, not to the capacity you wish you had ([[Worthy Goal]], [[The Zone Of Optimum Development]]).

**Find the smallest step.** The smallest discrete thing that moves in that direction *right now*. If three dishes is impossible, one dish. If one dish is impossible, put a dish in the sink. If that is impossible, stand up. Keep halving until the step is undeniably takeable ([[Narrow]], [[Do the Work]]).

**Take it.** Now, not after preparing. The point is not what it accomplishes; the point is that it **closes a loop**. Something was committed to, and done ([[The Life of a Commitment]], [[A word given is a word kept]]).

**Close the loop out loud, even silently.** *I said I would; I did.* Without the noticing, nothing registers ([[Harvest and Close]], [[Celebrate, Learn, and Improve]]).

**Gather the energy** rather than spending it immediately on something bigger. A completed step releases a small real quantum of relief; let it settle ([[Rest And Regeneration]], [[Energy]]).

**Then the next one.** Over an hour, a day, a week, they compound.

It refuses two familiar pieces of advice that both fail here. *Make a big change* asks for what a stopped system cannot do and leaves the person further behind. *Just rest* is right for depletion and wrong for a stuck activation, where rest without motion sinks deeper. The smallest step starts motion at a size that is actually available, and the rest comes afterward.

It works for a body as well as a person. A Circle that cannot choose a quest, schedule a meeting, or resolve a tension takes one small organisational step — name a coordinator, book one meeting, write one agreement — and the quest emerges from the accumulation ([[Circle]], [[Choose a Quest]], [[Stop]]).

And it is a *named* protocol on purpose. Someone in a freeze cannot invent a practice; they reach for a known one, the way a wounded person reaches for something already in the kit ([[The Kit]], [[Protocol]], [[Safety check]]).
