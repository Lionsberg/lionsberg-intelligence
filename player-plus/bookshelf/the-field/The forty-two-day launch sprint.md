# The forty-two-day launch sprint

*The worked example: a small group with forty-two days, a festival, a crowdfund, and a community of thousands who know nothing of operating systems.*

Day one is the four-hour pull planning session. The milestones go on the wall — the festival, the crowdfund live, the covenant with funders written, the platform chosen — and the room works backward from each, asking what must be complete immediately before. It comes out with an activity chain of a hundred and more, an owner on every line, and the first six weeks of constraints logged. Day two is the first Heartbeat, forty minutes, weekly thereafter: what I said, what I did, the gap, what I will do, what I offer, what I ask. Each week the look-ahead is rolled, the constraint log worked, and kept as a share of made goes on the wall. Every decision that matters is logged with its why, and a small [[Stewardship body]] is named for the calls the process cannot produce. Day forty-two: the festival, the crowdfund, and a record that shows honestly what was said, what was done and what was learned — which is the covenant kept with everyone who gave.

It is load-bearing as proof of scale downward. Nothing in it is branded; the group keeps its name and its community sees only its own festival. It shows that the same instrument that is meant to carry a civilisation is usable by five people in six weeks, and that the mechanism can be invisible or named, as the group prefers.

It is an example, drafted, not a case study of something completed. Read it as a shape to copy, not as evidence.
