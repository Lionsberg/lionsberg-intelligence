# Idols Of The Pantheon

*A word-tool that personifies the whole set of primordial drives latent in a human being — read from any culture's gods, or from a two-year-old.*

Every human carries a set of basic drives that arrived before reasoning and do not answer to it: fear, aggression, sexuality, curiosity, interest, anxiety, happiness, and their kin. They animate, they energise, they aim at a specific goal, they persist until that goal is reached, and their relative strength shifts with the state of the body and the state of the environment. Naming them as a **pantheon** is the tool: it lets a person see the whole set at once, as characters with their own wants, rather than as one undifferentiated pressure called *how I feel* ([[Word Tools]] · [[Archetype]]).

The reading method is given twice, and both are checkable. Look at the gods of any culture's pantheon — war, love, wisdom, the hearth, the trickster, the harvest — and you are looking at a people's honest inventory of the drives they found in themselves. Or watch a two-year-old, who cycles through the same states openly, wholly possessed by whichever one has the floor. The adult difference is not that the gods have left; it is that they have learned to speak in the first person.

What makes it load-bearing is what it opens onto. Development, at its base, is the **integration of these drives into a whole personality moving toward a higher goal** ([[Higher Order Functional Unity]] · [[Integration]]) — and integration is not suppression. It works by modulating the innate lower-order drive against an abstract higher-order representation: the drive is not killed, it is *given a larger aim to serve*. A drive denied does not leave; it rules from underneath ([[Distortion]] · [[Addiction]]). A drive enthroned becomes an idol, which is the word's warning — the same failure, at the scale of an institution, is [[Institutional Idolatry]], and at the scale of a movement, [[The Golden Calf]].

So the question the tool puts to a person is precise: *which of these currently has the floor, and what larger aim could it serve instead of being obeyed or buried?*
