# Higher Order Agreements

*Begin at the highest. Never make a lower agreement that conflicts with a higher one.*

Most people's agreements accumulate in the order they were asked for — a job, a lease, a promise made at a hard moment, a membership never revisited — and the result is a life whose commitments were each reasonable and which together point in no particular direction. Higher Order Agreements invert the order of making. The foundational agreements come **first**: with the Source, with Creation, and with oneself. Everything after must stack up under them.

Three templates are live and they are deliberately distinct. An **agreement with ØNE and All, eternal** — one's core values, principles, and commitments in service of the Source, of Creation, and of right relationship with every being. An **agreement with self, eternal** — the lifelong commitment to one's truest and highest self. And an **agreement with self, temporal** — the short-horizon one: specific goals and milestones that serve the eternal one, rewritten as a person grows. Under these sit the ordinary collaborative agreements: to collaborate on a piece of work, between a person and a group, between two groups ([[Field of Agreements]] · [[Charter]]).

The critical principle is the ordering rule, and it is a refusal as much as a practice: *each sovereign person and each locality begins with its highest order agreements, and refuses to make any lower agreement that does not properly stack up in service of the highest overarching and uniting vision* ([[Stacking Up And Aligning]] · [[Commander's Intent]]). That refusal is what makes the stack real rather than aspirational. It is also what makes it costly — a great many ordinary opportunities do not survive the test, which is the point.

It stands beside, and is not the same as, [[The Three Supreme Agreements]]. Those name *what* sits at the top — love ØNE, love one another, love All. This names the *ordering discipline* by which everything beneath is made to descend from a top, whatever that top is. One is the content; the other is the structure.
