# Self-Governing, Self-Sustaining, and Self-Replicating

*The three-fold test of a mature body: it decides for itself, feeds itself, and plants the next one.*

Each term names a capacity a body either has or does not. **Self-governing**: it can make binding decisions by its own agreements, without appeal to any outside authority. **Self-sustaining**: it can meet its own needs from its own contributions, without dependence on a centre or a patron. **Self-replicating**: it can bring another body like itself into being.

The three are a test rather than a description because they are checkable. A body can be asked, at any turning, *which of these three can we actually do?* — and the honest answer usually names the next work. A body that governs and sustains itself but never replicates is comfortable and terminal; a Cell which never passes [[The Flame]] dies with its founders. A body that replicates but cannot sustain itself plants dependants. A body that sustains itself but cannot govern itself has a patron it has not named.

Self-sustenance is not self-sufficiency. No cell sustains or replicates alone: it draws from the whole it belongs to and gives back to it. The mark of maturity is being a contributing member of a living body, not an island — which is why this test sits inside [[Holofractal Omnifederation]] rather than beside it.

In play it is the standing question a [[Home]] asks of a young [[Cell]] and a Cell asks of itself at each [[Season]]'s turning. The final step of maturity, in every case, is planting new seeds.
