# Lapse Is Honored, Pretence Is Tragic

*Letting a practice go, knowingly, is honoured. Continuing to operate as though the capacity were still reliable when it is not — that is the tragedy.*

The line is not drawn where most bodies draw it. It is not between those who kept the practice and those who did not. It is between **knowing** and **not knowing** — between a person who can say *I have let this go* and a person still presenting as what they were.

**Lapse is honoured** because a practice released in clear sight is a sovereign act. Seasons change; a body fails; a family needs what the discipline was taking; the calling turns out to have been for a stretch rather than for a life. None of that is failure, and the canon refuses to treat it as one ([[Who Refuses The Call, And Is Honored]], [[The Act Of Release]], [[Consciously Breaking Commitments]]).

**Pretence is tragic** because of what it does to everyone standing behind it. Capacity degrades quietly when practice stops — the field clouds, the breath shortens, the attention drifts, and none of it announces itself ([[Drift]], [[The Warrior Is The Instrument]]). Someone still carrying the standing while the substance has gone will be relied on precisely when reliability matters, and the people relying on them will not know. The word *tragic* is exact: nobody chose the harm, and the harm is severe.

What follows is a practice, not a verdict. Notice honestly; say it plainly; let the body know; take up what this season actually permits. A body that makes honest lapse safe to declare will hear about it; a body that shames it will be told nothing and will discover the gap in the worst hour ([[Zero Blame Environment]], [[A word given is a word kept]], [[Every Defect Is A Gift]]).

The same distinction reads across everything anyone carries: a role no longer held, a skill no longer current, a commitment quietly abandoned. The remedy is never to pretend harder. It is to say so, and to let the standing follow the substance.
