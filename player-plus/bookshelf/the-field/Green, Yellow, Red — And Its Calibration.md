# Green, Yellow, Red — And Its Calibration

*A traffic signal the body runs whether or not you are watching — and it only works if it has been pointed at something worth moving toward.*

As a person moves through the world, the body and the emotions are continuously processing far more than awareness handles, and they report the result as a felt state that works like a signal. **Green**: proceed, explore. **Yellow**: slow, caution, pause. **Red**: stop, retreat, or fight. Most of the processing behind it is below awareness, which is why the signal arrives as a feeling rather than as a conclusion ([[Emotions Are Information, Gradients, And Never Enemies]], [[Sensing The Field]]).

**The calibration claim is the whole of this page.** A signal must be *relative to something*, and it is stated emphatically: **the system must be calibrated toward a worthy goal in order to function properly.** Absent that, it still runs — it is always on — but it runs relative to whatever lower drive happens to have the controls: hunger, safety, belonging, fear. The signal is not broken in that case. It is working perfectly, on the wrong reference.

That converts [[Worthy Goal]] from motivational advice into a **physiological requirement**. A person without one is not merely unmotivated; their entire navigational instrument is reading against an arbitrary baseline, which is why it will be nearly impossible to escape anxiety and reach sustained peace without one ([[Aligned Will]], [[Purpose]], [[Quest]], [[Commander's Intent]]).

The practical use is diagnostic in two directions. Read the light and ask what it is reporting ([[Feel → Allow → Read → Name → Ask → Move → Release]]). And when the lights make no sense — everything amber, nothing green, a red with no apparent threat — check the calibration before troubleshooting the signal. The instrument is usually fine; the reference is usually missing ([[The Dual-Axis Energy Model]], [[Worthy Goal]]).
