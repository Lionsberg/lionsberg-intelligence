# Coordinate · Facilitate · Empower

*Against "coordinate all the things": coordinate what needs coordination, facilitate what needs facilitation, empower what needs empowerment.*

The impulse to coordinate everything is generous and wrong. It assumes that the problem is disorder, and that the cure is a central view of who is doing what. What it produces is a middle — someone who must be informed, consulted, and kept current — and a middle is exactly what a body without a centre cannot afford ([[Centralization Of Power]], [[Subsidiarity]], [[Span of Control]]).

The correction is to notice that *coordinate* is one of three verbs, and usually the least appropriate. **Coordinate** where two efforts genuinely collide and must be sequenced — a shared resource, an interface, a date. **Facilitate** where people would do the thing themselves if a conversation were held, a room existed, or an introduction were made. **Empower** where people already know what to do and lack only the standing, the capacity, or the permission they should never have needed ([[Servant Leadership]], [[Delegation]], [[Capability]]).

Behind the triad lies an assumption worth stating: that people of good intent are ultimately working on one highest shared project. If that is true, then most apparent disorder is not disorder at all — it is many hands on one thing, seen without the frame that would make it legible. Coordination imposed on that is friction; facilitation and empowerment are what it actually wants ([[The Meta Project]], [[Meta Game]], [[Stigmergy]]).

It is load-bearing because it is a test that can be applied in the moment, by anyone, to their own instinct to organise: *which of the three is this, honestly?* The answer is coordination far less often than it feels ([[Silos No More]], [[Cross-Functional Integration]], [[Polycentric Implementation of Shared Solutions]]).
