# Cohort-Learning

*Learning in a named group that moves together — arriving together, struggling at the same place, and carrying each other through it.*

A cohort is a set of people who begin at the same time and walk the same stretch together. The difference from a class is that the group is the instrument, not the audience. People at the same stage can say the thing a teacher cannot: *I did not understand that either.* They normalize the difficulty, catch each other's misreadings early, and make it possible to admit not knowing — which is the precondition for learning anything and is socially expensive in a room where everyone else seems ahead.

It changes what can be attempted. A person alone abandons the hard stretch quietly; a cohort has momentum, obligation, and a shared memory of having got through the last one. It also changes what is learned: a group that struggles together builds the trust that later makes real work possible, which is why the formation of people and the formation of bodies are the same act here rather than two stages. Learning is co-creative and participatory — cultivated from within and among learners, not imposed from above.

The Movement's own shape is cohort-shaped. The plan anticipates waves departing from the old into the new, each separated by a few weeks, the first called the Pioneer Cohort and the [[Second Wave Cohort]] departing as the first passes a marked point in its Quest. Whether that particular arithmetic holds, the pattern behind it does: people cross in groups, and a group that crossed together can bring the next one across.

At the scale of a Cell, this is simply what a Cell is. Three to thirteen people, by name, walking a season together and learning from the same gaps — which makes [[Circle]] the ordinary form of cohort learning and means no separate structure is needed to have one.
