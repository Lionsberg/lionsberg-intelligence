# Walking

*Walk far enough every day that the legs remember they are for walking — on actual ground, with the breath aligned to the step.*

Walking is the oldest daily discipline a body has, and among the first that an industrial arrangement of life quietly removes. Cars, lifts, desks and deliveries make it possible to pass a week without walking any distance at all; what goes with the distance is not only fitness but contact — with the ground, with weather, with the pace at which a thought finishes itself. The practice asks very little and returns a great deal, which is exactly why it is so easy to let go of.

The practice has a floor and a shape. The floor is daily, with no exception: for most people half an hour at least, often more, whatever it takes for the legs to feel like legs again. The shape is four small disciplines. Walk on real ground when you can — soil, grass, stone — rather than always on manufactured surface, and barefoot when the ground is safe, because skin against earth does something that shoes prevent. Align the breath to the step; four in, four out, or two and two, or whatever your own rhythm turns out to be, so that the walk becomes one motion rather than legs carrying a distracted head. Vary the attention: held on one question, or open to the whole field, or turned toward [[ØNE]] as moving prayer, or simply watching thought arrive and go ([[Focused Attention As Discipline]]). And vary the company — sometimes in silence, sometimes with another, sometimes with the land, because each gives something the others do not.

Walk the land where you live, and walk it through the turnings. The route becomes familiar; the year becomes legible in it; the place stops being scenery and becomes a party to the practice ([[Right Relationship]], [[Earth]]). This is the substance behind every pilgrim road, forest walk, circumambulation and songline a people has ever kept: not one tradition's invention but one human birthright rendered many ways.

The tell that the discipline has lapsed is simple enough to check. The legs feel unfamiliar. The breath will not settle into the step. You cannot walk twenty minutes without something in your ears. When those appear, the answer is not intensity but return: start gently, build over weeks, let the body remember.
