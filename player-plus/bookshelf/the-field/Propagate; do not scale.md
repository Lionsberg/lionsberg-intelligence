# Propagate; do not scale

*Nothing grows by making the centre larger. One Cell planting the next is the only mechanism that ever could.*

To scale is to grow the thing at the middle until it can serve more people: more staff, more capacity, more reach. To propagate is to plant something complete which then plants another. The difference is not tactical. A scaled body has a middle that everything depends on, and so it can be captured, bought, overwhelmed, or simply outgrown. A propagated body has no middle to attack, and every new part is as complete as the first ([[Cell]], [[Nucleus]], [[Self-Sustenance and Self-Replication]]).

The arithmetic is what makes it credible rather than merely principled. One, two, three, five, eight, thirteen — a Cell forms, each member carries the Flame to two or three within a week, and their Cells are their own. A week per cycle: about a thousand by the tenth, a hundred thousand by the twentieth, ten million by the thirtieth. A hundred and forty-four Cells passing faithfully reach a quarter of a billion people inside nine months. Those are illustrative numbers from an unbroken sequence, not a forecast of anything, and the word *faithfully* is carrying all the weight ([[Fibonacci]], [[Pass The Flame]], [[The First Three Percent]]).

It is load-bearing because it is what makes the whole design coherent. Every other law here — that each Cell carries the whole code, that no centre is required, that a Cell divides at thirteen rather than swelling, that the mark of maturity is the Cells you helped birth — is a consequence of choosing propagation over scale ([[Grow Your Cells]], [[Holofractal Scaling]], [[Subsidiarity]]).

Its cost is honest and should be said: propagation is slower at the start, ungovernable in the ordinary sense, and impossible to report on as a single number. What it buys is a body that cannot be beheaded ([[Federations vs Empires]], [[Centralization Of Power]], [[Anti-Fragility]]).
