# The Game Interface

*The whole surface a Player acts on — a screen today, a room tomorrow — every part of it named, so that anyone can say exactly where.*

A Player does not meet the Game as an idea. They meet it as a surface: a column of headings, a panel of rows, a number with a caption under it, a box to write in. That surface is the Game Interface. It is not the Game — the Game is played at a table with paper and voices, and it runs complete with no screen at all — but wherever a surface exists, it is what most people will touch first and most often.

Its governing law is that everything is acted on **where it is**. A row is answered on the row; a defect is handed in from the panel where it was met; nothing asks a person to go somewhere else and re-describe what they are already looking at. The cost of breaking this law is not inconvenience but silence: a person who must leave what they are doing in order to report what they saw usually does not report it.

Its second law is that its parts have names. A [[Sighting]] is only useful if it says *where*, and *where* requires a shared word a hand can point at. So the interface carries a short, plain vocabulary — [[The names of the interface's parts]] — which is taught on the surface itself rather than in a manual, and which a grandmother can use on her first afternoon ([[The grandmother test]]).

Every surface of the Game is a sort of one underlying set of cards and one underlying record ([[Work Deck]], [[The Record]]). The interface shows; it does not hold. If a screen and the paper disagree, the paper is the record.
