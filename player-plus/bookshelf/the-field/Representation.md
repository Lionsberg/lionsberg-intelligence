# Representation

*Voicing the will or needs of others in an accountable way — always subordinate to direct [[Participation]] and [[Sovereignty]].*

The canon admits representation and demotes it in the same sentence. Someone must carry a body's voice to a table it cannot all attend; but the represented have not thereby handed over their standing, and the representative speaks *for* them only as far as they said, for as long as they say.

It is load-bearing precisely because it is the classic failure point. Representation is where nearly every democratic form has decayed: a voice carried becomes a voice owned, a delegate becomes a ruler, and the represented are reduced to periodic spectators. Naming it as subordinate — rather than as the normal mode of governance — is the canon's guard against that path.

The structural preference throughout is direct: one voice per person, decisions at the smallest competent scale, [[Consent]] taken from the people actually bound. Where representation is used, the protections are the ordinary ones made sharper — a narrow [[Mandate]], short terms with rotation, visible reasoning, and instant revocability. [[Servant Leadership]] and *no campaigning* belong here too: a representative who must campaign to be chosen has already begun to represent themselves.

In play, a [[Cell]] meets it when it sends someone to speak for it at its [[Home]] or a [[Council]]. The discipline is to agree beforehand what that person may say yes to, and to have them bring anything beyond it back to the table.
