# Federations vs Empires

*The line that keeps [[Federation]] from becoming empire: authority flowing from consent rather than command, service flowing downward, and no power accumulating at any intermediate level.*

The two forms look alike from a distance. Both coordinate many bodies. Both have levels. Both speak of unity. The difference is the direction of the arrow. In a federation, each part is whole, joins voluntarily, may leave in peace, and lends authority upward for named purposes. In an empire, the centre grants the parts whatever standing they have, and can revoke it.

Naming the distinction is load-bearing because the drift is one-directional and almost always well-intentioned. A coordinating body becomes useful; useful becomes necessary; necessary begins to instruct; instruction becomes obligation — and no one voted for any step. The canon's tests are blunt and usable: *Can a member leave without penalty?* *Does the higher body guarantee anything, or serve?* *Does power sit anywhere between the person and the whole?* *Is the centre small?* Any accumulation at an intermediate level is read as corruption to be corrected.

Two structural names carry the same guard: **federation without centre** and **federation without subordination**. The first says coordination need not have a capital; the second says joining need not mean kneeling. The arm's-length form — powerful help, no guarantee — is how both are made real, along with fork rights, freedom of exit, and a commons no member can enclose.

In play, a player meets this at the moment their [[Supercell]] proposes something their [[Cell]] does not want. The federation's health is measured precisely there: what happens when a member says no.
