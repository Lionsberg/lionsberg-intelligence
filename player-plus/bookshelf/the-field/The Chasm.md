# The Chasm

*The gap the old order is tumbling into — the third named place, beside the Caves and the Wilderness.*

[[The Caves]] are where a people lives without knowing it is in the dark. [[The Wilderness]] is the hard, open ground between leaving and arriving. The Chasm is neither: it is the drop, the discontinuity the existing arrangement is carried over by its own momentum, while everyone aboard continues about their business ([[The Train of Civilization]], [[Collapse]], [[The Meta Crisis]]).

What distinguishes it from ordinary trouble is that it is not survivable by improving the thing that is falling. A system in difficulty can be reformed; a system going over an edge cannot be reformed on the way down. That is the whole force of the image, and it is also its danger — because the same image, held badly, produces fatalism, and fatalism has never built anything ([[Hell On Earth]], [[The Way Out]]).

It is load-bearing because it names the stakes without naming a date. The Story's claim is not that a particular year is the end; it is that a direction is being travelled and that direction has an edge in it. What is asked of a person is not to predict the fall but to get off the trajectory and help build the thing on the other side ([[Liberation and Exodus]], [[The Promised Land]], [[The Timeline]]).

And it is the place the turn happens. In the Story the Guide appears at the last possible moment, *just as the first cars tumble* — not before the danger is real, and not after it is too late. Whatever else the Chasm is, it is the reason the Story is not a tragedy ([[EuCatastrophe]], [[The Guide's four renderings]], [[Kairos]]).
