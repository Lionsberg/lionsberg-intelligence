# Naming Forms

*Three shapes a Pattern's name may take — the noun, the verb made noun, and the composed phrase — each chosen because the wisdom it carries is that kind of thing.*

**Nominal** names states, aspects, and qualities: [[Awe]], [[Fear]], [[Love]], the Field. The thing named is a condition one can be in, and the name holds still.

**Verbal-gerund** names sustained cultivation: Eating, Walking, [[Listening]], Forgiving. The wisdom is not a state but a doing kept up over time, and the *-ing* says so. A gerund name is a quiet instruction.

**Composed phrase** is used where the bare noun would be too passive and the bare gerund forced: [[Speaking Truth In Love]], Channeling Fury, Sitting With Anguish. The wisdom lives in the qualification — truth *in love*, fury *channeled* — and stripping the phrase to one word would lose exactly the part that matters.

The form is not decoration; it is a compressed claim about what kind of thing the Pattern is, and choosing wrongly teaches wrongly. A cultivation named as a state invites the reader to check whether they have it rather than to practise it. A state named as a gerund invites striving where receptivity was the move.

The rule that governs all three is [[Word Tools]]: one working name per wisdom, the others honored as aliases, and the name always a pointer rather than the thing. Names are also the place where the Language is most easily inflated — a verb can be coined for anything, and a coinage with no Card behind it is a name without a note. Roughly five hundred and seventy-five such coinages sit in the inventory, named and unarticulated; they are a backlog, not a vocabulary ([[Patterns Pending Articulation]]).
