# The landing sequence

*What to do on arriving mid-story: remember who you are and what this is for, become present, orient, engage, plan and adapt, move, and learn — in that order.*

Nobody starts at a beginning. A person arrives partway through something already unfolding, in a particular place and time they did not choose, with no blank slate available. The sequence is what to do about that, and the order is the argument ([[Position]], [[Context · Internal Context · External Context]], [[Our Story]]).

**Remember** comes first — who we are, and what the whole thing is for — because the choice of what one is aiming at precedes and shapes what one is able to perceive. Orienting before remembering means orienting by whatever the surroundings suggest ([[Remembrance]], [[The Goal]], [[Narrative Lens]], [[Spacetime User Interface]]).

**Be present** second: the story being lived is not elsewhere or in the future; it touches ground here, now, through this person ([[Here and Nøw]], [[Nøw]], [[Silence And Stillness As The Ground]]).

Then the familiar four. **Orient** — where am I, what is actually going on ([[Navigation]], [[Sensemaking]]). **Engage** — enter it rather than survey it ([[Do the Work]], [[Participation]]). **Plan and adapt**, held as one motion because a plan that cannot adapt is a wish ([[Pull planning]], [[The look-ahead]], [[Anti-Fragility]]). **Move**, with everything you have, toward it ([[Commitment]], [[Critical Path]]). And **learn**, improving the quality of every one of the preceding steps ([[Continuous Improvement]], [[Retrospective]], [[Celebrate, Learn, and Improve]]).

Its distinctive contribution is the first two. The field already carries two cycles for acting well — one that begins with observing, one that begins with attending — and neither has a step for *remember who you are* or *arrive in the present* before the observing starts. That is what this adds, and it is the part most easily skipped by anyone competent ([[Observe · Orient · Decide · Act · Learn]], [[Attend, Plan, Act, Learn, Improve Cycle]], [[The Five Moves]]).

Three named cycles now exist, with overlapping steps and different names, and none is reconciled with the others.
