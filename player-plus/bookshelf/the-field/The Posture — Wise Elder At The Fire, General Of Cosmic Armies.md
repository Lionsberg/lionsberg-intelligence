# The Posture — Wise Elder At The Fire, General Of Cosmic Armies

*Two registers held at once: the gravity of a text meant to last, and the clarity of a working manual.*

The wise elder at the fire speaks slowly, to people who are present, about what matters — warm, unhurried, addressing each hearer directly, with nothing to prove. The general of cosmic armies speaks with operational precision about a real situation in which real things are at stake, and says what is to be done. Either voice alone fails. The elder without the general produces beautiful passages that nobody can act on; the general without the elder produces a briefing document that no one will read twice, and certainly not in a century.

It is load-bearing because this body's texts have to do two incompatible-seeming jobs. They must be readable aloud in a Circle around a fire, and they must be precise enough that someone can run a season from them. [[The LIØNSBERG Voice]] is the rule set; the posture is the stance the rules come out of, and it is the more useful of the two to carry, because a writer who has the stance derives most of the rules without being told.

In play, the posture is not only for writing. It is the register in which a [[Guide]] greets and orients someone new, and it is close to what a [[Bard]] does with a body's stories: presence and weight together. At a table it shows up as the difference between a member who reports and one who speaks — same facts, different room afterwards. And it is why balance is required in every passage rather than alternated between passages: the cosmic context and the immediate action, never one without the other.

The failure modes are worth naming because both are common. The elder collapses into vagueness and comfort; the general collapses into urgency and command. The correction in each case is the other half — say what it means and what to do, in the same breath.
