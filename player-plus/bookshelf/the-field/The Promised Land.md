# The Promised Land

*Where the Story is going — not a place we arrive at but a way of living we co-create, here and now, a little more each week.*

The name is borrowed from the oldest crossing story there is, and it is deliberately corrected in the borrowing. There is no territory waiting, already finished, for a people to occupy. The land has to be reimagined, redesigned, and rebuilt in order to be entered, and the entering is the building. This is the same claim [[Heaven On Earth]] makes and the same one [[Co-Creating Heaven]] makes: not a destination that arrives, but a relationship that is chosen and kept on being chosen.

The correction is load-bearing. A promised land held as a destination produces waiting, and waiting produces either passivity or the violence of people trying to force an arrival. Held as a way of living, it produces this week's work: one real thing, in one place, that makes life measurably more like Heaven — and then the next. It also disarms the false arrivals named among the failure modes of [[The Wilderness]]: no Circle can announce that it has got there, because there is no there to have got to.

What it is not is vague. The content states plainly: air, water, and soil pure; children safe and nourished; no one hungry, no one alone, nothing wasted, everything alive; every person free, connected, and empowered; every community self-governing, self-sustaining, and able to plant the next; the old debts forgiven, the land breathing, the People home; and the Story carried on, to the rising generations and outward to other worlds. That is specific enough to be tested against, which is the point of a vision.

In the Game each Cell renders it locally: every member is asked how they would like the world to be, and from the answers the Circle crafts a shared picture of what Heaven looks like, feels like, tastes like right where they are. In the System it is the constraint on all design — [[Design To The Goal]] — and the reason the new civilisation is built in twelve spheres at once rather than one at a time.
