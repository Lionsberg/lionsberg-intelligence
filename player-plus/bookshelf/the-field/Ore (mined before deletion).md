# Ore (mined before deletion)

*Before a page goes, it is mined: what still carries meaning is set down with its date, its source page, and where it went or could go.*

The practice is three sentences long and it is one of the most useful things a body can do with its own past. Read the page that is about to leave. Take out the lines that still land — the sentence that says something better than anything that replaced it, the image nobody has used since, the one-line statement of a mission. Write them down together with the date, the page they came from, and where each might now belong. Then let the page go; the version history keeps every byte, and the record of decisions keeps the why ([[The four fates]], [[Weave, do not delete]], [[Provenance]]).

It is load-bearing because most pages that need to go still contain something that does not. The usual choices are both bad: keep the whole page, and the living body fills with things nobody reads; delete it, and the good lines go with it and are never recovered, because nobody ever browses a version history for a sentence they do not know exists ([[Prune]], [[Compost]], [[The Living Library]]).

What makes it work is that the ore is **addressed**. It is not a scrap pile. Each piece is set down beside a note saying where it should go — this line for the opening of that book, this image for the door, this sentence for the page that will be written later. Mined ore that nobody routed is simply a second place where good lines go to be forgotten ([[Curation]], [[Linking (as curation)]], [[Submittals]]).

It also leaves an honest record of the *left behind*: the headers that held nothing, the pointers to things that still stand elsewhere. Saying what was not worth carrying is part of the same honesty as saying what was ([[Transparency]], [[Decision Records]], [[Every Defect Is A Gift]]).

**Stands on:** [[Weave, do not delete]] · [[The four fates]] · [[Provenance]]

**In play:** Home — done once, by whoever is retiring the page
**Open:** Nothing states who reads the ore afterwards, or what happens to a mined line that is never routed anywhere; the first page of ore is also, so far, the only one.
