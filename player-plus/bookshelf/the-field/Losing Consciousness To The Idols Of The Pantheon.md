# Losing Consciousness To The Idols Of The Pantheon

*Under threat, the state of consciousness drops — and what is lost is access: to other futures, other memories, other rules, other selves one could have been in that moment.*

The mechanism is stated as a sequence. A threat or an opportunity presents itself. The state of consciousness drops to a simpler one. The autonomic body takes over executive function. The signals that would have settled it are no longer being sent, so the system rises to its default-on state, and all of that energy is directed in the most primitive available way.

**The loss-of-access framing is the separable and useful part.** What goes missing is not intelligence but *reach*: the stores of memory, the patterns, the rule-sets, the alternative courses of action, the futures one could have envisioned — all still there, all unavailable. That is why the behaviour produced is not merely poor but **simplified**: the person is choosing well from a catastrophically reduced menu ([[The Dual-Axis Energy Model]], [[Green, Yellow, Red — And Its Calibration]]).

It explains the familiar experience of thinking of the right response an hour later. Nothing was learned in that hour. Access was restored.

Two consequences follow. First, the remedy is a state remedy rather than an argument: what restores access is coming back up into a higher state — breath, pause, deliberate attention — before deciding anything ([[Breath]], [[Composure Under Threat]], [[Silence And Stillness As The Ground]]). Second, this is the argument for rehearsal: what a person has practised is available at a lower state, because it does not require reach ([[The Practice Cadence]], [[Form Practice, And Beyond Form]]).

The canon describes the lower drives that take the controls in figurative language — older gods lying in wait to possess whoever drops far enough. The figure carries the felt truth of it, that the thing in charge is not you and does not have your aims, and it is doing mechanistic work it cannot support. Held as an image it is apt; held as an explanation it explains nothing ([[Metaphor]], [[Word Tools]]).
