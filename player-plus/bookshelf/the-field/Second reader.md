# Second reader

*Someone who did not write a page and reads it whole. The writer sees what they meant; the second reader sees what the page says.*

They read it as a stranger would, start to finish, and report where it stalls, where a word arrives unexplained, and where a sentence says something other than what was intended. Then the page is repaired — and the true things the second reader defended are kept.

It is load-bearing because the gap between meaning and wording is invisible from inside. A writer reading their own page supplies the missing half from memory every time. This is the same principle that keeps a look-back honest: whoever is being looked at does not hold the pen, and an assigner is not the auditor of their own assignment.

A Player+ finds a second reader for anything that will be read by someone who was not in the room — a lesson, a proposal, a story that will travel. The reader need not be an expert; not having been there is the whole qualification. Two readers are worth having wherever provenance is the value, because provenance is exactly what a single pair of eyes stops checking.

What a second reader owes back is specific rather than general. The place they stalled, the word that arrived unexplained, the step they could not do from the page alone — each named with the line it belongs to. "This is good" and "this needs work" both leave the writer exactly where they were.

An agent can serve as a second reader for a page it did not draft, and cannot serve for one it did. The same rule, at every scale: the writer may not be the checker of their own work.
