# Requests and offers

*A request is a plain ask of the whole. An offer is what a Player has to give. They travel on the card, every week, up and down and sideways.*

*We need three volunteers on Saturday.* *We have a van and a Tuesday evening.* Two sentence-shapes, said at the table, written on the card, and sent through the door. Together with commitments, they are the only things that cross a membrane between bodies — never a task, because no one in the Game assigns work to anyone else's table.

They are load-bearing because this is how a body allocates itself without a center. Nobody dispatches resources. A request simply is, and anyone who can answer it may; an offer sits in the open until someone needs it. That is enough to move hands, tools, money, and skill across a town, and it preserves the thing an allocation office would destroy: every yes is still a free yes from a sovereign table.

How it is played. Each person names an offer and an ask in their round at [[The Turn]]. The table's own go on lines 10 and 11 of [[The Season Sheet]] and through [[The weekly door]] to [[The Board]] each week. A request that a Cell cannot answer travels to its [[Home Cell]], and what a level cannot answer travels up with the sheet; what is sent up as [[The Offering]] is what resources the answers. Answer what you can and say so; an unanswered request left visible is honest, and a request quietly dropped teaches a body not to ask. Anything that blocks the work is not a request but an [[Issue]], and it gets an owner and a date.
