# The guard

*A defect is not closed when it is fixed. It is closed when something stands where it was, so the same defect cannot return unseen.*

A repair removes today's instance. A guard removes tomorrow's. It is whatever small, durable thing now sits in the path of the fault: a check that fails if the fault comes back, a line added to the checklist the next person reads, a rule written into the form so the wrong entry cannot be made, a sentence put onto the card that will be in someone's hand when the same moment arrives again. The question that finds it is always the same — *what would have caught this?* — and the answer is placed where the work will meet it, not where the lesson was learned.

It is load-bearing because without it a body can fix the same thing a hundred times and record a hundred successes. Repair without a guard is the reason organisations with busy maintenance and honest people still fail the same way every year: the knowledge lived in whoever happened to be there, and left when they did. A guard is how a fix stops depending on memory and becomes a property of the thing itself.

The discipline is small and it is strict. A fix without a guard is not closed. The guard is named in the record beside the repair, so a later reader can see not only what went wrong but what now stands against it. And the guard is chosen for the cause rather than the symptom — a rule that merely forbids the last mistake teaches nothing, while one that removes the conditions the mistake grew in protects things nobody has thought of yet ([[Root Causes]]).

A guard also has a cost, and the cost is honest: every one of them is a small weight the body will carry forever. So they are chosen, not accumulated. A guard that no longer guards anything is retired the way any instrument is retired, on purpose and out loud, rather than left to harden into a ritual nobody remembers the reason for.
