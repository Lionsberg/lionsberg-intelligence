# Consent

*The absence of [[Meaningful Objection]] to a [[Proposal]] — which is to say, the proposal, though not perfect, falls within every member's [[Range Of Tolerance]] as the body advances toward its aim.*

Consent is not enthusiasm and it is not consensus. The working test is one question: *does anyone have a reasoned, paramount objection?* If no one does, the proposal stands. This is why bodies that decide by consent move quickly without anyone being overridden: the standard is workability, not unanimity of preference.

It is load-bearing because it is the only thing that makes binding decisions legitimate among sovereigns. Nobody is governed who has not agreed; agreement can be withdrawn; silence is never a yes. Remove consent and everything else in this Pillar becomes decoration — the [[Field of Agreements]] becomes house rules, the [[Selectively Permeable Membrane]] becomes a wall, [[Authority]] becomes command.

Two disciplines keep it honest. First, **every decision has a review date** — consent is not forever, and a body that never revisits has quietly converted a yes into a permanence nobody granted. Second, the closer a proposal comes to the wise right thing, the more harmony surrounds it; rising dissonance is information about the proposal, not about the dissenter. As a proposal drifts out of tune, it eventually falls outside someone's range of tolerance, and a member who does not consent may require it improved until it is inside everyone's.

The canon also holds a gradient: the greater a decision's span of impact, the more time a body should spend moving past the lower standard of consent toward the higher standard of consensus — which is always available given enough wisdom, time, and patience.
