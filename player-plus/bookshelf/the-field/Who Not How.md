# Who Not How

*The question of the age is not how the world will be changed but who will arise to change it — because transformation does not begin with systems, it begins with people who remember what they are for.*

Design is not the constraint. There is no shortage of good plans, sound analyses, or workable arrangements; there is a shortage of people willing and formed enough to carry one. So a body facing something large gains more by asking *who* than by asking *how* ([[The First Three Percent]], [[Capacity]], [[The Calling of the Ecclesia]]).

It is a reordering rather than a dismissal of method. The how still has to exist and still has to be good — but it follows, because a plan handed to people who cannot carry it produces nothing, while capable people handed a mediocre plan will fix it ([[The Plan]], [[Prototyping]], [[Every Defect Is A Gift]]).

Practically it changes the first move on any piece of work. Not *what is the approach* but *who is already doing something near this, who is ready, and who would have to be formed* ([[Choose a Quest]], [[The First Few]], [[Requests and offers]], [[Sponsorship]]).

It also explains why formation is treated here as strategy rather than as support. If the binding constraint is people, then the highest-leverage investment is the one that makes more of them ([[The LIØNSBERG Academy]], [[Metaphysical Infrastructure]], [[The Three-Year Formation]], [[Pass The Flame]]).

Its failure mode is the obvious one and should be held with it. *Who* can become a way of avoiding the harder work of *how* — waiting for the right person instead of building the thing, or treating a shortage of method as a shortage of character. Both questions are real; this says only which comes first ([[Do the Work]], [[Critical Path]], [[Honesty about time]]).
