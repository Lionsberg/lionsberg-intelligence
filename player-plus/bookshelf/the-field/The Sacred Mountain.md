# The Sacred Mountain

*What the name points at: the climb toward ultimate unity, whose summit reaches into unseen realms and whose foundation anchors creation itself.*

Mountains have carried this meaning in nearly every tradition that has ever existed, which is why the image needs no explaining to anyone: the high place where the human meets what is above it, reached by climbing, and never by being carried. The Sacred Mountain is that referent — the ascent toward union, made by love rather than by force, with a summit that is not a location ([[The Sacred Ascent]], [[Coming Home]], [[Union]]).

Three things are said of it. Its summit stretches into realms nobody has surveyed, so the climb is not finite in the way a project is. Its foundation anchors creation, so it is not an escape from the world but the axis of it. And it is not far: *the Sacred Mountain dwells in the Spirit and Heart of All* — the path to it begins within ([[Immanence]], [[Transcendence]], [[Divine Spark]]).

It is load-bearing because it makes the whole enterprise legible in one image, to anyone, in any language. Not an organisation. Not a programme. A climb — undertaken together, from wherever each person is standing, toward something that was always there ([[The Sovereign Path]], [[Metaphor]], [[Mythos]]).

And it carries the one warning any sacred image needs. A mountain is not owned, not sold, and not anyone's possession; it belongs to nobody and it is climbed by everybody. A body that begins to speak as though it holds the summit has stopped describing the mountain and begun building an idol at the foot of it ([[Do Not Concretize the Absolute]], [[The Golden Calf]], [[The Eternally Nameless]]).
