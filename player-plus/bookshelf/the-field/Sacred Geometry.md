# Sacred Geometry

*Proportion and pattern in made things that answer to the order already present in the world — form built to the same mathematics as growth.*

The observation behind it is plain and checkable: certain proportions recur across galaxies, hurricanes, shells, sunflowers, branching trees, and the human body, and buildings made to those proportions are experienced as harmonious by people who could not name why. Whether that recurrence is evidence of an ordering [[Logos]] or a consequence of how growth and packing work under constraint is a question this page does not settle. Both readings support the same practice.

It is load-bearing as the point where [[Beauty]] becomes a design discipline rather than a taste. [[Architecture]]'s one sentence — form follows function, function follows meaning, and beauty is essential rather than luxury — needs some way of being acted on, and proportion is the most transmissible of them. A builder who cannot afford ornament can still afford ratio. [[Human-Scaling]] and the shaping of [[Sacred spaces]] run on the same instrument, and so does the more ordinary craft of making a page, a card, or a room that people want to be in.

In play it is less exotic than its name. Where does the eye rest; what is the ratio of this to that; how does the light fall; what is the size of a thing relative to a body. A [[Cell]] making anything — a garden bed, a sign, a printed card, a room laid out for [[The Turn]] — is making these decisions whether or not it knows the vocabulary. [[Fibonacci]] gives one well-tested set of answers.

Two cautions, said plainly. The territory attracts claims well past what anyone has established — about frequencies, about ancient measurements, about hidden orders — and this is marked as a gap rather than a body of knowledge, listing sacred geometry, sacred mathematics, and the hidden mathematical order of things among the Patterns needed. And geometry is not virtue; beautifully proportioned buildings have housed terrible things. The ratio helps; it decides nothing.
