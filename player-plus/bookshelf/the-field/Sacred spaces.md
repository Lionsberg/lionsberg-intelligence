# Sacred spaces

*The named rooms a living community needs — the hearth, the council space, the dojo, the sanctuary, the garden, the burial ground.*

A community is not only a set of relationships; it is a set of places where particular things can happen. The canon names them as a family because each one does work the others cannot. The **hearth**, where people are warm together and nothing is required of them. The **council space** and the **roundtable**, where decisions are made among equals with no head of the table. The **healing space** and the **learning space**. The **dojo**, where strength is trained under discipline and witness. The **sanctuary**, where someone in danger may go. The **garden**, which feeds and teaches patience. The **burial ground**, which holds the dead and keeps a people in relation to its own ancestors and its own end.

The claim is that a community missing one of these is missing the capacity, not just the room. Where there is no sanctuary, someone in danger has nowhere; where there is no burial ground, grief has no address; where there is no council space, decisions migrate to wherever power already sits.

Held honestly, this is ore. The canon names the set and has written none of the cards — what each place is for, what forces it must resolve, how it is made at the scale of a village or an apartment. Until those exist the family is a good question rather than a craft.

What a Cell can do now is small and real. Notice which of these functions your body actually has and which it borrows or lacks. Most of them begin as a practice before they are ever a building: the hearth is whoever's kitchen; the council space is the circle of chairs; the sanctuary is a person who will open their door at two in the morning.
