# Speaking Truth In Love

*Saying the true thing in the way it can be received. When warmth and honesty seem to fight, honesty wins and warmth carries it.*

The composed name carries the whole Pattern, which is why neither half will do alone. Truth without love arrives as an attack and is refused, and the refusal is not the hearer's failure — it is the speaker's, because the point of speaking was to be received. Love without truth is not kindness; it is the withholding of what someone needed in order to choose well, dressed as care.

The order is stated and it matters: honesty wins, and warmth carries it. Warmth modulates *how*, never *whether*. A Body whose members soften the true thing until it is no longer the true thing has become comfortable and blind, and the thing unsaid will be paid for later by whoever is standing there when it lands.

It is a composed phrase rather than a noun for a reason ([[Naming Forms]]): the wisdom lives in the qualification. Strip it to *truth-telling* and the Pattern becomes licence for bluntness; strip it to *loving speech* and it becomes evasion.

Its near kin is speaking truth to power, where the risk moves onto the speaker, and which is why the Body builds structures that make it survivable: anyone may stop the line without penalty, all authority is recallable, and cause is sought in the set-up rather than in the person.

The practices around it do most of the work. Listen first, so the truth can be shaped to what can be heard. Speak of people not in the room as if they were present and might read it. Say the one thing first and leave the depth pullable. And let the word improve the silence it breaks.
