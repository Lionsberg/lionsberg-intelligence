# Time

*The unfolding of events in rhythm with the Logos — more than sequence; a vessel of meaning, and the one thing every table must share.*

Time has at least two faces, and a life needs both. [[Chronos]] is the measurable face: seconds, weeks, dates, the sun's position — countable, divisible, and shareable across a planet. [[Kairos]] is the qualitative face: the moment when purpose, possibility, and preparedness converge and action changes the course of things. Neither is dispensable. Chronos without kairos is a schedule nobody lives inside; kairos without chronos is a sequence of good intentions that never meets a Tuesday.

It is load-bearing because coordination without authority runs on shared time. There is no headquarters issuing instructions; what makes ten thousand tables one game is that they keep the same week and the same turnings. Time is also the medium of the only currency a person truly has — their [[TimeEnergy]], the time, energy and attention of an embodied life — which is why time discipline is a moral matter here and not merely a practical one.

In the Game, time appears as a small set of hard rhythms: the day, the week's [[The Turn]], the ninety-day [[Season]], the four [[The turnings]] by the sun. The governing discipline is that the clock is read, never computed — a time is taken from an actual clock at the moment of writing, and an estimate is said in words rather than dressed up as a number. In the System, time is what the critical path is measured in and what honesty about time protects: plan to finish a season ahead of each marker, run the path with no slack to the plan date, and if you are past the marker, say you are behind before you move it. Temporal markers — solstices and equinoxes — are never milestones.

The older order made time a possession and then a prison: counted, commodified, and used to manufacture urgency. The correction is not to abolish measurement but to put it back in service — to count moments as well as minutes, and to let the counting serve the living rather than the other way round.
