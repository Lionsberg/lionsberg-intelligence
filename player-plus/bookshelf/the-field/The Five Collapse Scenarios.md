# The Five Collapse Scenarios

*Five named futures, weighted by reasoned estimate, so that "collapse" stops being a word too vague to act on — and so that whatever actually arrives is recognizable.*

The word *collapse* carries no information. Which collapse? How bad? How fast? Two people using it are usually imagining different worlds, and neither can prepare for a fog. Naming the futures is what makes preparation possible instead of panicked. Nobody needs to predict which one arrives; what is needed is to see all five clearly enough that none of them is a surprise.

**Localized crises** — regions fail while the global system holds: particular countries, particular watersheds, particular cities become unlivable, with refugee flows and humanitarian catastrophe concentrated there. Fine for most; devastating for those inside it. **Widespread social unrest** — institutions hold formally and lose practical legitimacy; civil disorder, contested elections, regional conflicts, intermittent supply, chronic economic stress. Everyone feels it; most survive it. **Global humanitarian crisis** — systemic failures cascade into one another, supply chains break for months at a time, medicine and banking become unreliable, authoritarian answers proliferate. Civilization breaks and does not end. **Near-total collapse** — cascading failure across all major systems, migration at continental scale, states failing and other actors filling the vacuum, mass die-off through famine, disease, and conflict. Surviving pockets begin rebuilding. **An extinction-level event** — the worst case, from some combination of full nuclear exchange, engineered pathogen, runaway machine intelligence, or ecological tipping; most of humanity perishes and the species itself is at risk.

The weights given them are approximately twenty · thirty · twenty-five · fifteen · ten percent — reasoned estimates from one analysis working from the evidence of its moment, spoken from the early 2020s. They are estimates, not measurements, and not predictions; anyone is invited to reweight them from their own reading. What the weights are for is the arithmetic they make visible: on those numbers, the seriously catastrophic outcomes together sit near half, which is the same likelihood as a coin landing on one side. No sober person plans a life on the assumption that a coin will land favourably.

And then the conclusion that matters more than any of the numbers: **across all five, the response is the same.** Build the new, in parallel, while there is still time. In the gentlest future the work is valuable; in the middle futures it is essential; in the worst it is the only continuity there is. The work succeeds in every scenario. It fails only if it is not undertaken — which is why they are told as [[Story]] rather than used as forecast. Their proper use is with another person: read them plainly, neither exaggerated nor softened, let the silence sit, and then ask the one question — *what does this ask of you?* The answer is theirs, and it is where a spectator becomes a player.
