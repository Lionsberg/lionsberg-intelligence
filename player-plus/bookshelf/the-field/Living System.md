# Living System

*Any coherent, self-regulating, dynamic whole made of interrelated living parts — and, in the largest reading, the total system of systems that creates and sustains life.*

The term works at every scale without changing meaning. A cell is one. A body is one. A [[Cell]] of people is one. A forest, a [[Watershed]], a [[Community]], a [[Bioregion]], [[Earth]], and the [[Cosmos]] are each one, and each is a part of the next. In religious language the largest reading is all Creation; in the canon's language, the Universe and each of the All are interrelated living systems. This is the same claim [[Holarchy]] makes about structure, stated in the vocabulary of life.

It is load-bearing because it decides what kind of thing a body of people is, and therefore how it may be treated. A machine is assembled, optimized part by part, and repaired by replacement. A living system is grown, optimized only as a whole, and killed by the removal of what looked redundant. Every structural commitment in the Pattern follows from choosing the second: [[Subsidiarity]] because living systems regulate locally, [[Biodiversity]] and redundancy because resilience is not waste, [[Emergence]] because the behaviour is not in the parts, discrete birth and discrete death because organisms end rather than being maintained forever.

In play the reading is a diagnostic. When something is failing, ask whether it is being treated as a machine — a person managed, a Circle restructured from outside, a place optimized for one output, a commons audited into compliance. The correction is usually to tend conditions rather than to intervene in parts, which is precisely what [[Stewardship]] means: the steward tends conditions; the tended flourish as themselves.

The frame also sets the limits of the analogy. A living system can be killed, and it does not owe anyone its survival. Calling a movement a living system is a design commitment, not a promise that it will live.
