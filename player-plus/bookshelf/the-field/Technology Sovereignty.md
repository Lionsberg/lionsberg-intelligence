# Technology Sovereignty

*[[Sovereignty]] applied to the substrate — whoever holds your data, network, energy, and means of speaking holds a claim on you.*

A body can be sovereign on paper and dependent in fact. If its record lives on someone else's machines, its conversation runs through someone else's wires, its power comes from a source it cannot influence, and its tools can be withdrawn by a decision it has no part in, then its self-governance lasts exactly as long as the goodwill of parties it never agreed with.

So the question is asked of the substrate the way it is asked of governance: who decides, on what authority, and can it be withdrawn? Data sovereignty asks who holds the record and on what terms. Network sovereignty asks whether the body can still reach itself when a service is gone. Energy sovereignty asks what happens when the supply is interrupted. Communication sovereignty asks whether the body can speak without permission.

The discipline is not self-sufficiency, which is neither possible nor desirable — it is *[[InterExistence]] with the dependencies known and chosen*. A body that knows exactly which of its capacities rest on outside parties, and has a floor beneath each, is free in a way a body that has never counted is not.

The floor is the practical answer, and it is old: everything essential degrades to paper and speech. A hand copy on any paper counts exactly as much as a printed one; a person with a notebook is a complete player; the turnings are kept by the sun, which no one owns. Keys stay home, whatever room you are working in, and what runs on someone else's computers is understood to be read by them. Build with whatever serves; never let the thing you cannot replace become the thing you cannot live without.
