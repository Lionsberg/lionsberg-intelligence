# Wiki Is Primary And Standalone

*The canon must be readable on its own: it depends on no companion repository, no tool, and no account to be understood.*

A body of knowledge that can only be read through a particular application has been quietly enclosed, whatever its licence says. So the rule is that the wiki is primary: plain files, human-legible, complete in themselves. A reader with a text editor and no network has the whole of it. Anything built beside it — an agent, a site, a database, a set of scripts — is an accelerator, never a dependency.

That has consequences for how pages are written. A page states its own subject rather than assuming the reader arrived through a particular path. Links enrich and do not carry load-bearing meaning that the page itself omits. Where something is generated from elsewhere, the generated form is a convenience and the page remains the truth.

The same instinct runs all the way down the stack: nothing here needs a screen, and a group with a notebook and a group with a network are playing the same game, with the notebook as the floor the network stands on. A canon that cannot be printed is a canon that can be switched off.

Read together with the library's role, the division is clean. The wiki holds the canon; a site built over it makes the canon pleasant to read; and the second can be rebuilt at any time from the first. It does not work the other way around, and the day it does, something has gone wrong.
