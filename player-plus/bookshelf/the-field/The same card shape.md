# The same card shape

*Upper half common, lower half your own — one card at every level, from a person to a world.*

The upper half arrives from above and is the same on every card in the Game: the Goal, the moment, the milestones, this season's plan for the whole and the stretch of road this player's part touches, the rules and the score. It changes only by [[Proposal]], at a turning. The lower half belongs to the player and is filled at the Turn: who we are and our roles, where we are, how we are, our Quest, what I will do this week, what I did last week and the gap, our measures, our proposals, when we read again.

It is load-bearing because it is the cheapest possible interface. Two halves and a fixed order mean a card from a valley you have never heard of can be read at a glance, and a season can be rolled up without anyone agreeing on software, language or culture. Read downward, a card opens onto the cards beneath it; read upward, onto [[The plan of the whole]]. That is how the whole is woven with nobody in charge of the weaving.

It is the first of the [[Minimum standards of interoperability]] because the others depend on it: an address is meaningless without a line to address, and a number means the same thing everywhere only if it sits in the same place on every card. A hand copy on any paper counts exactly as much as a printed one, and the edition is printed on the back so two cards can be told apart.
