# Source Narratives

*The older text behind the one you are holding — and the discipline of reading an inherited text as something that travelled a long way to reach you.*

Any text that has come down across millennia has a history: earlier forms, reconstructions, translations, and the decisions of whoever made each of those. Calling what lies behind it the **source narrative** is a way of holding the difference between the thing and its transmission, without deciding in advance that the transmission was faithless ([[Scripture]], [[Lineage]], [[Provenance]]).

The method that goes with it is ordinary scholarship and is worth stating because it is so rarely applied outside the academy. Ask **when** the text as we have it was assembled. Ask **by whom**, and what they were trying to secure. Ask which grammatical rules were derived **after** the text they are used to read. And hold the limit honestly: even the agreement of an entire scholarly establishment over centuries does not produce certainty about an original meaning ([[We Do Not Know]], [[Navigating Towards Truth]], [[The Five Verification Filters and the Spectrum of Knowing]], [[Epistemology]]).

Two companion terms travel with it. **The old authorities** — the custodial class whose keeping became, on the corpus's reading, an editing ([[The Old Authorities]], [[Institutional Idolatry]]). And **new eyes and new ears** — the posture in which a text is approached again, which is neither credulity nor dismissal but re-encounter, with respect ([[Shoshin]], [[Reverence]], [[Unlearning]], [[Discernment]]).

What makes the discipline sound rather than corrosive is the respect in it. The point is not to discredit an inherited text but to read it more nearly as it is, which is generally more interesting than the received version and is what the traditions' own best scholars have always done ([[ReHarvesting the Great Traditions]], [[Welcoming The Stranger]], [[The Translation Journey]]).

Where the corpus moves from this method to conclusions about particular texts and particular custodians, those are its claims and not this page's, and they are contested ([[The Elohim Are Plural]], [[The Great UnVeiling]]).
