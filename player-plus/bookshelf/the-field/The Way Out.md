# The Way Out

*When something is wrong, and when someone leaves. The Game has no fence, and it has no cliff either.*

Say it at the table. That is the first rule and most of the time it is the last: a Cell that can hear *this is not working* from any member, in the open, at the weekly gathering, will mend most things itself. If the Cell is the problem, say it to your home — a body that knows your Cell, is not inside it, and can sit with both sides; it does not judge, it helps the two tables talk, and if talk fails it helps someone leave in order. If a home is the problem, its Cells say so to one another and may leave together and become each other's home. A supercell its Cells have left has ended, and that is how one ends: nobody has to fight it, they only have to go, in order, and say so on their sheets.

It is load-bearing because it is what makes the yes at the door real. [[Consent]] that cannot be withdrawn is not consent; an exit that costs a person their record, their friendships, and their name is not an exit. The Game's answer is that leaving is ordinary, recorded, and honourable.

Leaving in order is three things: say so at the table, or to a host if the table is too hard; finish or hand over what you said you would do; and let your lines stay in the record. Your name on [[The Circle Sheet]] gets *left, in order* and a date. Nothing is erased. You may come back, and many do. What belonged to the table stays with the table: [[The Offering]] was given freely and is not owed back. A person who leaves takes their gifts and their word, and leaves the commons whole.

And the floor beneath all of it: some seasons are hard, and a Cell may hold someone through illness, grief, or a winter with no work and count that as its Quest — and it is. Line 13 of [[The Season Sheet]] exists so a home can see *grieving* or *thin* before it sees any number, and come. When a member is in danger, in body or in mind, the Cell acts first and records later. No card is more important than the person at the table.
