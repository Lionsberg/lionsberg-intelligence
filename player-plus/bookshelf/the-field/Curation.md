# Curation

*The act that makes a knowledge commons actually work: solve · document · push up · **curate** · pull down · localize · feed back.*

Six of those seven steps can be described mechanically. The fourth cannot. Curation is a person going back over scattered material — formal pages, informal notes, meeting records, chat threads, half-finished drafts — and pulling together what belongs together, then saying, in their own voice, what they noticed about it and why it matters. It is human pattern-matching, not search ([[Pattern Recognition]], [[Sensemaking]], [[Knowledge as commons]]).

The difference from search is the whole of it. A search finds the exact phrase. A curator remembers a conversation from three months ago that used a different word entirely, notices that two groups have been circling the same problem without knowing it, and joins them. Nothing automatic does that, because the join is a judgment about meaning ([[Linking (as curation)]], [[The Incipient Link]], [[Semantic Capture]]).

It is load-bearing because a commons without curation silts up. Material accumulates, each piece perfectly good, and the body slowly loses the ability to find what it already knows — which is indistinguishable, from the inside, from not knowing it. Curation is the maintenance that keeps a store of knowledge usable, and it is almost always the first work to be dropped because nothing visibly breaks when it stops ([[The Living Library]], [[Drift]], [[Prune]]).

It is also cheap and pleasant, which is the encouraging part. One person, one afternoon, one page that gathers what was scattered and says what they saw in it — easy to do, and it ties together things in ways other people can then build on ([[Submittals]], [[Curriculum]], [[Second reader]]).

Its discipline is voice and provenance. A curator's reading is *theirs* and is presented as such; the underlying works keep their authors; credit travels with the work; nothing is absorbed so thoroughly that its source disappears ([[Provenance]], [[Attribution]], [[Author's Voice Is Sovereign]]).
