# The forging criterion

*An instrument is well-forged when using it teaches the wise right embodiment of its Pattern and its Play.*

One sentence, and it is the standard every tool in the kit and every commission in [[The Forge]] is meant to be judged against.

It starts from how the Way is actually learned. **People do not learn Patterns by reading them.** They learn by doing the thing with a well-made instrument in hand — the way music is learned by playing rather than by theory — until the Pattern lives in the body and the instrument becomes a companion rather than a crutch. If that is true, then the teaching cannot be left to the page that explains the tool; the teaching has to be *in* the tool, in the order of its fields, in what it asks first, in what it refuses to let you skip.

So the criterion is a test with teeth. It is not *is this tool useful*, and it is not *is it faithful to the Pattern*. It is: **does using it, without instruction, form the person using it?** A worksheet whose first question is *why are you here* teaches something a worksheet whose first question is a name does not. A commitment ledger that asks what was promised before it asks what was done teaches the discipline it exists to serve. A gathering agenda that opens in silence and closes in gratitude puts a soul in the beat rather than a note about souls in the margin.

Two consequences follow. A tool that produces the right artefact while teaching the wrong habit has failed, however efficient it is — this is how instruments quietly install the old world inside bodies that meant to leave it. And a tool ceases to be needed, at some point, by the person it has formed; the criterion contains its own ending.

The criterion is the instrument-level form of what [[The Architecture Of A Way]] says of a Way as a whole: the embodied form teaches. It is stated here as the design standard for everything still to be made.
