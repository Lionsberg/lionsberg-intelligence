# Like Gold Refined Seven Times

*The image behind the improvement ethos: the refining is not damage to the gold — it is what makes it gold.*

The phrase comes from the Psalms, where it describes purity: silver refined in a furnace, seven times. Seven, in that idiom, means completeness rather than a count. The picture is of a substance put through heat repeatedly, each pass carrying off impurities that the previous pass could not reach, until what remains is only the thing itself.

As a working image it does something a principle cannot. [[Continuous Improvement]] states the discipline; this states what the discipline feels like from inside, and why the discomfort is not a sign that something has gone wrong. Each pass through the fire is a real loss of something — a draft rewritten, a plan abandoned, a self-image that did not survive contact with a gap honestly examined. The image says: that was the dross, and the losing of it is the process working. A body or a person that has been through many passes is not damaged goods. It is refined, and it is recognizably itself in a way it could not have been at the start.

It carries into character as much as into work. Trials, challenges, and honest correction purify what a person is — thought, word, action, faith — toward integrity and coherence, and that is a different kind of gain than the accumulation of skill. It also carries into a canon: a page that has been revised by everyone who used it is worth more than a page written well once, and [[Progressive canonization]] is the same idea applied to text.

The caution worth keeping: refining is a process, not a verdict. Nothing here is ever declared finished, seven times or seventy; the fire is what is happening, not a phase to be got through. And heat applied to a person without their consent or without care is not refining — [[Zero Blame Environment]] is what keeps the image from being used to justify harm.
