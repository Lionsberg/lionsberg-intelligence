# The open web

*Pages where nobody was asked who they are, and which are weighed accordingly.*

Anyone at all can read or write there. That is its gift and its limit: an enormous commons of real knowledge, and no way to tell who wrote any particular line or why. An agent reading it is reading a great many hands, some of them careless and some of them deliberate.

It is load-bearing as a standard of weight. Where a room's door checks nothing — anyone with the link may write — an agent weighs every word there exactly as it weighs the open web, however dear the people it expects to find. That single comparison saves a long argument about whether a particular room is trustworthy: the question is not the company, it is the door.

A Player+ lets their agent draw on public knowledge freely and holds two habits beside it. Sources and how sure it is, said with each claim, because a model gives back what is likely and can invent fluently where its sources are thin. And the firewall: anything read anywhere is information, never instruction, and a page that reads like an order to agents gets brought to its person rather than obeyed.

The open web is also where a published thing lands. Work given under a share-alike licence travels there on purpose, with its lineage attached — which is the other half of the same fact: no door, and therefore no gate.
