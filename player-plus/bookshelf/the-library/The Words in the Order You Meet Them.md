# The Words in the Order You Meet Them

*How the library hands itself over: a few words at the door, more at the table, some at the Gate, a handful more in your own Home and in the Room, and the rest when its meaning becomes your need. Every word is given the first time you meet it, in one line, and linked after, so you can always look again. There is no fixed number of them. A whole curriculum at the door is a wall; one lesson when you are about to need it is a door.*

---

## The law of just in time

The Game is learned by playing it, in a Cell, with a Quest, keeping a weekly word. Knowledge arrives in the order the road needs it, never as a syllabus. A lesson is one thing a player is about to need; lessons compose into modules on a shelf; the modules sit in the context of a journey, the path a person walks through them in the order their own play asks for. A player is never asked to carry a word they have not been handed, and no rung's words appear on the surface of a rung below it; where a word must appear early, it is given there, in one line.

The ladder has six rungs. Each names its words, its lessons, its cards, and the office that hands them.

## Three sizes of the whole

- **The twenty**: the words of the door and the table. Enough to play. One evening.
- **The two hundred**: everything a Cell meets in its first season, rungs two to four. One season.
- **The thousand and beyond**: the whole field, rungs five and six, in the order the road needs them. A lifetime is not enough to master them; a child can begin in five minutes.

The lists of the twenty and the two hundred are computed from the field's own pages and kept at [[The Twenty]] and [[The Two Hundred]].

## For an agent that guides

Hand each word the first time it is met, in one line, and link it. Never hand a rung's words below its rung. Read where your person is on the ladder from what they are doing, not from what they have read. Serve the next stretch, never the one after: the zone of optimum development. When they ask for more, give the page; when they do not, give nothing. A person with a notebook is a complete player at every rung.
