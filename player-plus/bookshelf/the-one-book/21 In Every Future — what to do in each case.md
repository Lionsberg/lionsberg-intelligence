# In Every Future — what to do in each case

*The chapter a person opens when nothing else works. One hundred forty-four thousand futures were drawn from nine things that could go differently and banded into twelve families; each was asked one question — what does a [[Cell]] do here? — and the answer came back the same twelve times. What changes across the families is tempo, medium, what must be protected while the work runs, and the one number worth watching. So the figuring-out is done here, in the calm, and what is left for the hour is recognition. **We can plan for an emergent reality we cannot predict.** That sentence is this chapter. If the power is out and nobody can read a computer, go to **THE PAGE**, further down: the protocols written so that a person with a pencil can run them.*

## If the lights are out

Turn to **THE PAGE** in this chapter. It holds, in order: the four protocols for moving value between people who have only paper · how a [[Cell]] re-establishes who its authoritative voices are · how to find the authoritative voice in another Cell · how two Cells confer with nothing but paper or a key and a wire · the [[The Season Sheet|Season Sheet]] by hand · the [[The Offering|Offering]] by hand · the [[The Flame|Flame]] passed by hand.

Everything before THE PAGE is why those protocols are shaped the way they are. Everything after it is how to climb back up. You do not need any of it tonight.

## First, the thing itself, in plain words

If you have read nothing else, here is the whole of what this chapter assumes, and it is small.

Some people — three to thirteen of you, optimally four to seven — decide to walk together. That group is a **Cell**. You meet once a week, the same day, the same place, for an hour or two. That meeting is the **Turn**. Each person says four things out loud: what I said I would do last week, and whether I did it; the gap, honestly, and what I learned from it; what I will do this week; and what I can offer and what I need. Someone writes it down. Someone tells a story about the week. Each person puts something in the middle of the table — money, food, hours, a tool, a ride, care — and that is the **Offering**, and what gathers there belongs to the group.

You pick one real thing to do over the next three months, something you could point at afterward, and that is a **Quest**. Three months is a **Season**, and it runs from one solstice or equinox to the next, because the sun is a clock nobody owns.

At the end of the season you fill one page: fourteen numbered lines about how your group actually went. That is the **Season Sheet**, and it is the only thing in the whole system that has to look the same everywhere. Two of you sign it, date it, and count the lines of writing. You keep a copy and send the original to the group that is your **Home** — the people who brought you in, or whoever you have chosen to be answerable to.

And within three days of starting, you go to your three — people you trust, not already playing — and you hand them the same thing, so they can start their own group. That is **passing the Flame**. You are their [[Home Cell]]. Their group is their own.

That is it. A notebook, a table, a given word, and someone to hand it to. Everything in this chapter is about what that practice does when the world around it changes — and the finding, which surprised the people who looked for it, is that it barely changes at all.

## The fire, and the clear reader

Some of what follows describes very hard worlds. It is written the way a good doctor speaks: plainly, completely, and without drama.

That is deliberate, and the reason is worth saying once. Fear is the instrument of the systems this Book teaches you to see through. **A frightened reader is a controllable reader, and we want the opposite — a clear one.** So nothing here is written to thrill, and nothing is softened either. You are not being recruited into an emergency. You are being handed a set of decisions already made, so that if a bad hour comes you can spend your attention on your neighbours instead of on figuring out what to do.

Two facts keep the hard sections from becoming despair, and both are load-bearing.

**Nearly all of what could go wrong arises from our way of being together.** Not from the sun, not from fate, not from a law of nature. The debts were designed. The division is engineered. The loneliness is a product. What humans designed, humans can redesign; what our way of being caused, a changed way of being can heal. If the fire had fallen from the sky we would be victims. Because we lit it, we are builders.

**The scarcity is manufactured; the abundance is real.** Humanity already produces enough food for more people than are alive. The sunlight striking the Earth in an hour exceeds what everyone uses in a year. The knowledge needed for universal flourishing already exists and is written down. What is missing is not resource. It is the pattern for holding it, and the number of people running that pattern.

So the honest posture, in every family below, is the ancients' one: *watchful, as though crossing a winter river; alert, like people aware of danger; courteous, like visiting guests.* Urgency and peace, together. Neither one without the other.

## The one answer

Whether or not anything bad happens, the response is the same, and that is not a consolation prize. It is the design.

A [[Cell]] of three to thirteen people who keep their word to each other week by week, choose one real [[Quest]] a [[Season]], put something in the middle of the table as the [[The Offering|Offering]], write the fourteen lines of the [[The Season Sheet|Season Sheet]] at each turning and send them to their Home, and [[Pass The Flame|pass the Flame]] to their three within three days — that body is the thing that survives the hard futures and the thing that composes Heaven in the gentle ones. It needs no permission, no funding, no infrastructure, and no crisis to justify it. It runs on a notebook, a table, and a given word. In a world that holds together it is how a neighbourhood becomes trustworthy. In a world that comes apart it is the organ that keeps functioning when the larger organs do not. Federated into supercells and into a community body, it is how ten thousand people become one body without anyone being in charge of it.

The frame this Book carries is plain: *whether or not anything bad happens, it is the way to co-create Heaven.* That is a frame, not a forecast. The distribution below says nothing about what will happen. It says what shapes of world are worth being ready for, and it shows that the readiness is one readiness.

Two properties of that body explain why it survives what institutions do not, and both were designed in rather than discovered afterward.

**There is nothing at the centre to attack.** Every empire that ever set out to change the world built a headquarters, and every headquarters was captured, corrupted, or killed. This grows no headquarters. It grows an organism: sovereign Cells, each carrying the complete code, each able to sustain itself, do real work, and plant the next. Though ninety-nine in a hundred were lost, any surviving Cell could regenerate the whole. You cannot bomb it, sue it, buy it, or behead it. It has no head to remove, no heart to stop, and no single point whose capture delivers the rest.

**The pattern is the same at every scale.** What governs a kitchen table governs a bioregion and governs a planet: the same week, the same card, the same fourteen lines, the same given word. So in learning to run one faithful Cell you have already learned to help run a world. There is nothing new to learn at the larger scales. There is only the same Way, played wider — which is also why a body cut into pieces by distance, censorship, or catastrophe can be reassembled by strangers who only ever learned the small version.

The canon reached the same floor by another road. Its five named collapse futures — localized crises, widespread unrest, a global humanitarian crisis, near-total collapse, and an extinction-level event — are weighted by reasoned estimate and close on one sentence: *across all five scenarios, the response is the same — build the New, in parallel, while there is still time. The work succeeds in every scenario. The work fails only if it is not undertaken.* This chapter does not improve on that sentence. It adds a second axis the five scenarios do not carry — how far [[The Great Game]] itself has reached — and, for each resulting family, a judgment decided now rather than in the moment.

**A preset judgment is a standing decision of this text.** In a hard hour, deliberation is the scarcest thing there is. So each case below names the one signal that says it has arrived, and what a Cell, a [[Supercell]], and a community body are already committed to do that day. It is not a law. It is an offer, revisable by [[Proposal]] at a turning like everything else. A preset judgment that has never been tested is a guess with good posture; the ones that get tested and revised are the only ones worth keeping.

## The invariant response

The checklist a Cell can run this week, in any of the twelve families. Nothing here waits on conditions.

1. **Keep the [[The Turn|Turn]].** One gathering, one hour or two, the same day and place. In turn, each person: what I said I would do and whether I did it · the gap, honestly, and what I learned · what I will do this week · what I offer, and what I ask. Someone marks the [[The Circle's Scorecard|Scorecard]]. Someone tells one story. Close in gratitude.
2. **Give and keep your word.** *I will ___ by ___*, said aloud. Kept, or a gap learned from, without blame. Write both in [[The Record]]. [[A word given is a word kept]].
3. **Put something in the middle.** The [[The Offering|Offering]], each week, freely, whatever is real for you. It becomes the Cell's [[Commons]].
4. **Hold one [[Quest]].** One real thing, this season, that your Cell can point to afterward, with a *done when* line on its [[The Questcard|Questcard]].
5. **[[Pass The Flame]].** Your three, people not already playing whom you know are ready, within three days; the naming begins at your first gathering. Their Cell is their own; you are their [[Carrier]] and their [[Home Cell]].
6. **Fill the [[The Season Sheet|Season Sheet]] at the turning.** Fourteen numbered lines, witnessed by two hosts, dated, with a count of its lines of writing. Send it to your Home.
7. **Know your Home, and whose Home you are.** Your [[Home Cell]], your [[Carrier]], and the Cells that look to you. Three Cells that have become one another's home are a [[Supercell]].
8. **Hold the floor of care.** When a member is in danger, in body or in mind, the Circle acts first and records later. No card is more important than the person at the table.
9. **Name your three.** The three people you would most trust to help lead your community through what is coming — not family, not the famous, not the loud: the faithful. Send the Seed to the people you love. *The web of names this builds is not only how leadership is called out. When the grids fail it becomes the contact-tree by which every member still knows their three, and their three still know theirs — which is why it is held in tiers, the deepest layer belonging only to those it names.*
10. **Keep a paper copy.** Of the seed text, of your [[Field of Agreements]], of the last Season Sheet. [[Screens and paper|The notebook is the floor the whole network stands on]].
11. **Run the [[The Daily Way|Daily Way]].** Pause, ground, breathe, remember, realign, recommit. The body that keeps the Turn is made of people who keep themselves.

Eleven items, none of which requires the world to be any particular way. Everything that follows is commentary on these.

## Two regimes: the understood case, and the legible case

There are two entirely different worlds this chapter has to serve, and confusing them produces bad instruments.

**In the understood case, the tools are here and they understand intent.** An agent reads what you meant, not only what you typed; it reads your Cell's cards, your last season, the shape of your Quest. In that world the scarce skill is not protocol compliance. It is *expressing intent well, and iterating it* — with your own agents, and with your friends' agents, and with the agents of the Cell across the valley. A rigid protocol in that world is friction dressed as rigour; it makes the body slower and no wiser, and it ages badly, because the tools improve faster than the protocol can be revised. So the Book does not pile up procedure for the ordinary case. It gives the smallest set of shared forms — the cards, the fourteen lines, the turnings, the address, the given word — and leaves everything else to judgment, conversation, and [[Proposal]].

**In the legible case, nothing understands anything.** There is no agent to infer, no search to correct a mistake, and possibly no light after dark. Here the only thing that carries is what a tired person can execute from a page with a pencil. That is why the degraded protocols in this chapter are written the way they are: short, numbered, physical, and dull. The form is deliberately more rigid than it needs to be in a good year, because rigidity is what survives being copied by hand, read by candle, and run by someone who has never met the person who wrote it.

The design law that follows is one sentence: **build the legible form first, prove that three people with a pencil can run it, and only then let capable tools carry the same object faster.** Anything a capable tool does that the paper cannot has quietly left the standard — and it will be the part that breaks the day the power does. [[Screens and paper]] is that law at the scale of an instrument. This chapter is that law at the scale of a civilization.

## The distribution, plainly

Nine things that could go differently over seven years were named. Each got three states and a guessed weight. One hundred forty-four thousand futures were drawn at random in proportion to those weights — one state from each dimension per future. Each future was scored for severity (thirteen points available; the benign state of every dimension scores zero) and banded: **Gentle** 0–3, **Turbulent** 4–6, **Hard** 7–9, **Severe** 10–13. That band was crossed with the reach of the Game: **few** (a few thousand players), **regional** (the first three percent in some regions), **planetary** (the first three percent everywhere). Twelve families.

The nine: how the old world goes · how institutions respond · ecological shock · economic regime · conflict · technology · information · the Game's reach · health. The weights are guesses made in one evening by one reading of one moment. They are the most arguable thing here, they are stated in full in [[The Priors and the Method]], and anyone may change them and draw again.

By band: Gentle 7.9%, Turbulent 47.3%, Hard 39.9%, Severe 4.8%. By reach: few 45.0%, regional 34.9%, planetary 20.1%. The severity peak sits at six of a possible thirteen; sixty futures scored zero and twenty-seven scored thirteen.

**The method's honesty, in four lines.** It treats the nine dimensions as independent, when in the world they pull hard on one another, so the middle is probably too fat and both tails too thin. It has no time axis: a future here is a bag of conditions with no order and no duration. It cannot imagine improvement, because no state scores below zero. And it drew the Game's reach as though reach happened to us, when reach is the one row that is ours — the consequence of Cells forming, keeping their word, and passing the Flame. Read that row as a target, not a prior.

This is a shape, not a forecast. [[We Do Not Know]] how the next seven years go, and saying so out loud is part of the method. The draw is also gentler overall than the canon's five-scenario weights, which put the seriously catastrophic outcomes near half. That divergence is a disagreement between two guesses, and neither corrects the other.

**What the numbers cannot carry, and the reading beneath them.** Three things belong beside the table, because a severity band flattens them.

*There is no outside any more.* When the Bronze Age world came apart, the collapse was regional: a few million people across one corner of one sea. China went on developing; the Americas never heard of it. There was an outside, and the outside carried the human story forward while one region burned. Today the supply chains are global, the finance is interlinked, the biosphere is shared, and the weapons are planetary. A failure anywhere propagates everywhere within hours. That is the one structural difference between this hour and every prior one, and it is why a distribution of *conditions* is the right instrument and a list of *places* is not.

*The crises are one crisis.* Failing farms and failing banks, poisoned waters and poisoned words, the loneliness and the wars and the vanishing species — follow any of them down and you arrive at the same root: a crisis of vision, of identity, of culture and values; of who we have been being and the way we have been doing things around here. That is why they cascade instead of queueing politely, and it is why the nine dimensions in the draw pull on one another far harder than the arithmetic allows.

*Two thresholds are racing.* One is the threshold of irreversible cascade — the point past which the converging failures foreclose any coordinated response. The other is the threshold of irreversible awakening — the point at which enough people have crossed from programmed sleep into direct perception that the change carries itself, because consciousness that has seen through an illusion cannot un-see it. Both curves are moving. The whole outcome of this hour reduces to which threshold is crossed first, and the reach row of the table above is the only row that touches the second one. That is the honest reason reach is a target rather than a prior.

## How to read a case

Find the world you are actually in, not the one you fear or the one you hope for. Then read the case the way a pilot reads a checklist: top to bottom, in order, without improvising until the list runs out.

Every case carries the same nine things.

**Signal** — the one observable that says this case has arrived. Recognition, not prediction.
**The first hour** — what is done before anything is decided.
**The first day** — what is done before the next Turn.
**The first week** — what is done at and around the next Turn.
**The first season** — what is done by the next turning.
**Who decides what** — the boundary between the person, the Cell, the supercell, and the community body. When in doubt, the decision belongs at the smallest scale that can carry it ([[Subsidiarity]]), and it travels up only when that scale genuinely cannot ([[The Escalation Ladder]]).
**The Intelligence System** — which rung of the ladder the family sits on, and what the [[Player+]] layer does there.
**Forbidden** — the moves that arrive dressed as necessity. These are the [[The Anti-Patterns|Anti-Patterns]], and in a hard hour they do not look like vices. They look like the responsible thing.
**Recovered when** — the condition that ends the case. Written in advance, because a body without a stated end to an emergency keeps the emergency.

One standing note on *Forbidden*. It never means "never, under any circumstance, whatever the world does." It means: this is the move the body has decided in advance not to make, and a body that makes it anyway owes the rest of the body an immediate and loud accounting — the wiser the exception, the louder. That is how a rule stays a rule without becoming a cage.

## The twelve families

Each family is a band of severity crossed with a reach, not a single predicted world. Read the one nearest where you actually are.

The four bands are not four separate destinies. [[Collapse]] and ascension are one motion, not two, and that frame governs the Hard and Severe sections especially: the instruction there is never merely to survive. It is to be the place the released energy flows toward.

### Gentle · reach few — 5,168 futures · 3.6%

The old order strains and does not break. Institutions muddle through, some brittle, some adaptive. Prices are annoying, the wars stay where they are, the networks stay open. Life is recognizable. The Game is small: a few thousand players scattered across the world, most of whom have never met another Cell. The danger here is not catastrophe; it is drift. Nothing outside forces the keeping of a word, so Cells form warmly and dissolve quietly. This is also where the one answer is hardest to believe and most valuable, because a gentle world is the cheapest place to build the body a harder world would need: none of the Cell's capacity goes to triage, and everything can go into making something good.

> **Signal.** Two consecutive turnings in which line 7 — flames passed — reads zero.
> **The first hour.** Fix the day, hour, and place of the [[The Turn|Turn]], and write it where everyone can see it.
> **The first day.** Each member names, privately, the three people they would carry the Flame to.
> **The first week.** The next Turn is given entirely to those names; each member commits to one conversation before the following Turn. Choose a Quest that is plainly good in an ordinary world — a thing repaired, a neighbour fed, a piece of ground restored. Do not wait for an emergency to justify your existence.
> **The first season.** Find two other Cells and become one another's Home before there is any pressure to. Write one Season Sheet by hand from the very first turning, so the hand knows how. No community body yet: name the place, and start counting toward [[The 300]].
> **Who decides what.** Everything at the table. There is no scale above you yet, and inventing one is the characteristic error of this family.
> **The Intelligence System.** Frontier rung, and comfortable. Use it to draft, to translate, to prepare — and run one Turn a season with nothing switched on, so the descent is practised before it is needed.
> **Forbidden.** Becoming a discussion group. Letting the [[Commons]] be drained by the one who came to drain it ([[The Parasite]]). Building an organization before there is a practice to organize.
> **Recovered when.** Line 7 is non-zero for two consecutive turnings and at least one Cell exists that your Cell birthed.

### Gentle · reach regional — 3,872 futures · 2.7%

The world is still workable, and in some regions the first three percent hold. There are towns and bioregions where a Cell is an ordinary thing, and enormous areas where nobody has heard of any of it. Asymmetry is the defining feature, and it is uncomfortable: the dense places feel like the world is turning, and they are wrong. The temptation here arrives dressed as success — consolidate, brand, build a centre, hire staff for the regional body. [[Centralization Of Power]], [[Institutional Idolatry]], and [[The Golden Calf]] come in through that door, not through the hard ones.

> **Signal.** A turning at which a supercell received no proposals from its Cells, or a role has been held by the same person for three consecutive seasons.
> **The first hour.** Write down what your Cells actually agreed at the door. That writing is the [[Field of Agreements]], regrown.
> **The first day.** Each Cell drafts one [[Proposal]], in one sentence.
> **The first week.** Proposals are brought. The supercell rotates the role that turning, without prejudice to the person ([[Term Limits And Rotation]]).
> **The first season.** Form the supercell in earnest; rotate hosting; set term limits. Where the Cells of a place number toward three hundred, found the community body — a steering committee of four to seven that knows it is scaffolding and dissolves its own founding authority, a charter, and the three seats: for living systems, for future generations, and for the Whole. Run a full [[OmniSpection]].
> **Who decides what.** The Cell decides its own Quest, culture, and calendar. The supercell decides only what genuinely crosses Cells. The community body decides nothing a Cell can decide, and holds the asset lock and the purpose lock.
> **The Intelligence System.** Frontier rung. The regional body's own instruments run on open tools that any Cell can also run; anything it cannot hand a Cell is not a regional instrument, it is a dependency.
> **Forbidden.** A purpose that can be amended away. A role without a term. A Cell that has become the staff of a regional body — it has stopped being a Cell.
> **Recovered when.** Proposals flow upward at every turning and no role has been held three seasons running.

### Gentle · reach planetary — 2,326 futures · 1.6%

The gentlest family with the widest reach: no great break, and three in a hundred in every community on Earth. The old institutions still stand and mostly work; the new body grows alongside them rather than against them. In this future the long-horizon commitments at the far end of the plan are not rhetoric — they are schedulable. The risk is capture and imitation. A large, admired, benign body attracts those who would wear it, and [[The False Prophet]] speaks the language of liberation. The protection is structural and mostly already written: the pattern is what makes instances one body, anyone may take the Seed and start their own, so there is no centre to seize and no one from whom permission flows.

> **Signal.** Complete sheets fall below four in five at any turning, or a body begins describing itself as the centre.
> **The first hour.** Complete and witness your own sheet before critiquing anyone else's.
> **The first day.** Publish the [[Minimum standards of interoperability|minimum standards]] where any Cell can read them, and hold to them.
> **The first week.** Homes return incomplete sheets to their Cells with the missing line named, and nothing else.
> **The first season.** Federate body to body, reciprocally, with six-capital accounting visible to members. Keep the fourteen lines and their numbering identical in every language: translate the words, keep the order. The community body publishes its own accounts that season and restates in its charter that anyone may take the Seed and start their own instance.
> **Who decides what.** Nobody decides for the whole. A movement of millions of Cells with no headquarters still has to move together, and the old world would have solved that with a platform, a scheduling authority, a server everyone depends on — and therefore a single point that could be seized, corrupted, watched, or switched off. This solves it with the equinoxes and the solstices, which are visible to every human being on Earth, belong to no one, and cannot be turned off. **The sky is the clock, and the sky is beyond capture.** That is the entire planetary coordination mechanism, and it is enough.
> **The Intelligence System.** Frontier rung, widely shared. The guard still holds: an artificial agent is never a [[Player]]; it carries its person's yes and can never give one.
> **Forbidden.** Describing any body as the centre. Making interoperability a membership test. Any instrument the poorest Cell on Earth cannot also run.
> **Recovered when.** Four in five sheets arrive complete, witnessed by two hosts, dated, with a line count.

### Turbulent · reach few — 30,609 futures · 21.3%

The single most likely family in the draw. Crises arrive stepwise, roughly one every year or two; institutions are brittle and defensive; ecological damage lands regionally; money is either inflating or seizing up; wars stay contained; capability concentrates; information narrows. Most lives get harder in ways that are survivable and demoralizing at once. The Game is a few thousand players — small enough that every Cell feels alone in it. Because this is the likeliest world, it deserves the most ordinary instruction: a Turn kept twelve weeks running, without drama, is a larger achievement here than any plan. Turbulence does not destroy Cells; intermittence does. The crisis is never quite big enough to organize around and never quite small enough to ignore, and the quiet failure mode is *we will restart after this one*.

> **Signal.** One Turn missed, or [[Kept as a share of made]] below half for two seasons.
> **The first hour.** Fix the Turn and refuse to move it.
> **The first day.** Write the list of how every member can be reached without the internet, and give a copy to each of them.
> **The first week.** At the next Turn, commit to less and keep what you say. Fewer commitments, smaller ones, said aloud, written down, and kept — until the share recovers. This is the Season's actual rule, and it is not a penalty; it is how a body finds the size of word it can actually hold.
> **The first season.** Start the [[The Offering|Offering]], however small. Choose Quests with a one-season horizon and a visible result. Become the Home of two other Cells, so that no Cell is alone the week a crisis lands on it. Name your three, so a community body is possible later.
> **Who decides what.** The table, weekly. A supercell that exists sends one member of another Cell to sit at a struggling Cell's next two Turns as a witness, not a fixer.
> **The Intelligence System.** Frontier rung, intermittently. Keep a local copy of the library and one hand-written copy of the last Season Sheet, because outages in this family are ordinary rather than dramatic.
> **Forbidden.** Moving the Turn for a crisis. Carrying a slipped Quest silently into the next season as though it had not slipped ([[Honesty about time]]). Letting the Record become a performance review — a body that punishes the gap is told fewer of them and knows less about itself.
> **Recovered when.** Twelve consecutive Turns kept, and kept-as-a-share above half and rising.

### Turbulent · reach regional — 23,918 futures · 16.6%

The same turbulence, met in some regions by an organized three percent. When a crisis arrives in those regions it meets a people who already know each other's names and already keep their word. This is the family the whole strategy is aimed at: demonstrate before advocating; build the new alongside the old so that everyone has somewhere to go. The honest risk is mission creep — a Cell that becomes a relief agency stops being a Cell and starts being an institution with a fundraising problem. The pattern's answer is that relief runs *through* Cells and does not replace them: the Quest changes, the form does not.

> **Signal.** A request on any Cell's sheet is unanswered at the next turning.
> **The first hour.** Re-state the request as a specific ask of one named Cell.
> **The first day.** The supercell assigns it, by name and date, before any other business.
> **The first week.** Run one real relief Quest through the Cells you already have, rather than founding anything new. Pair with a neighbouring Cell; [[Requests and offers]] cross the membrane every week, and never tasks.
> **The first season.** The supercell holds the mutual-aid pool — risk and value shared across the body, which is what [[Mutual Aid]] means before it means anything warmer. The community body takes on a relief function funded from our own tables first: the Offering flows inward to the table you sit at, never upward to anyone who rules you. Add the six-week [[The look-ahead|look-ahead]]: name what could stop the coming work from being ready, give each constraint an owner and a date, and release only constraint-free work into the week.
> **Who decides what.** The Cell decides what it can give. The supercell allocates what crosses Cells. The community body funds or refuses in plain words within one season, and records the refusal and its reason where the asker can read it.
> **The Intelligence System.** Frontier rung, with a local model held in reserve by at least one Cell in the supercell. The look-ahead and the allocation of requests are exactly the work a capable agent does well; the decision stays with the people whose names are on the sheet.
> **Forbidden.** Founding a new organization to do what the existing Cells could do. Letting the door widen until the body dissolves into the crowd it is helping — the [[Selectively Permeable Membrane]] is what lets a body take people in and remain a body.
> **Recovered when.** Requests are answered within one Turn. A falling request count is not recovery; it usually means asking has stopped feeling worth it.

### Turbulent · reach planetary — 13,634 futures · 9.5%

Crises everywhere, and three in a hundred everywhere. The new body and the old world's turbulence are now the same size, which means the body becomes legible to powers that will want to use it. Someone will try to draft it onto a side. The posture that keeps a body whole here is the one the strategy already names: hospice the old world rather than fight it. A body that contests power becomes a faction and inherits a faction's enemies. A body that builds gets to keep taking people in from every side of every quarrel.

> **Signal.** Any actor — state, party, platform, or movement — asks the body to take a side; or a region's line 8 reads zero sent for two turnings.
> **The first hour.** Answer with the posture sentence and your current Quest, and nothing else: *we build; we do not contest power.*

## The four named cases

Four specific ways the ground can be cut out, named because they are the ones most likely to arrive without warning and most likely to be argued about in the moment.

Two of them are not forecasts. **Enclosure** and **state firewalls** have visible beginnings already: hosts and repositories that changed hands or changed terms, capabilities partitioned by geography, access conditioned on who you are and where you sit. This chapter names them as cases rather than as predictions, and the response to a partly-visible case is the same as the response to a hypothetical one — the preset judgment, run on the signal.

### Case A — State actors firewall one another's capabilities

Access to models, networks, payments, and repositories is partitioned by nationality or geography. A Cell's members on two sides of a line can no longer use the same instruments, and in some cases can no longer reach each other through them.

The body's architecture already survives this: Cells are local and physical, the Home relationship is a relationship between people, and the sheet is a piece of paper. What breaks is the assumption of a shared digital commons. Descend the ladder to whatever rung is available on *each* side, independently — the two halves do not need the same rung — and keep the fourteen lines identical so that the halves remain readable to one another whenever a route opens.

> **Signal.** A member cannot reach a shared instrument that another member can.
> **The first hour.** Say it at the table. A capability gap that is not named becomes a hierarchy.
> **The first day.** Drop to the lowest rung available to all members, so that no member is second-class inside their own Cell.
> **The first week.** The supercell begins establishing a hand-carried or postal route, and names who carries it.
> **The first season.** The route exists and has been used at least once. The community body mirrors the library on each side of the line and says publicly where each mirror is.
> **Who decides what.** The Cell decides its own rung, and the rule is *the lowest rung all members can reach.* Nobody outside the Cell decides who is inside it.
> **The Intelligence System.** Two independent stacks, one per side, neither authoritative. Cross-border work moves as sheets and letters, not as shared services.
> **The Pillars.** I: the Goal is not partitioned. II: no role requires a tool half the body cannot reach. III: settle across the line in real things, not instruments. IV: teach on both sides; assume no shared course. V: the floor of care crosses lines before anything else does. VI: watersheds cross borders and the council should too. VII: stories travel as text that a person could retype. VIII: keep keys at home, always. IX: a physical meeting place on each side. X: refuse to be recruited by either side's security narrative. XI: a member on the far side is a member. XII: the turnings are the same instant everywhere, which is why they were chosen.
> **Forbidden.** Letting the better-connected half make the decisions. Building a bridge that depends on any single provider. Carrying names or locations across a line on any wire.
> **Recovered when.** Both halves have exchanged complete sheets in the same turning, by any route.

### Case B — The hosts and repositories are enclosed

A corporation acquires or closes the places where the models, the code, and the texts live. Accounts are terminated, licences are changed retroactively, archives go read-only or go dark. This is enclosure arriving by acquisition rather than by decree, and it tends to arrive fast and legally.

Clone first and argue later. Every Cell that can hold the library holds it; every Cell holds the Seed on paper; the licence under which this body's work travels — *say where it came from, and pass on the same freedom* — is carried on the work itself, so that a copy in a stranger's hands is a lawful copy without reference to any host. [[Knowledge as commons]]: a solution discovered once anywhere becomes available everywhere, credit travels with the work, and no contributor is alienated from their contribution. That is a property of how the texts are written, and it does not depend on who owns the server.

The deeper answer is that the body's real record was never on a server in the first place. **The record of the whole Game is every sheet ever sent, in order, unaltered, with its two signatures and its line count — not a database anyone owns, but the sum of every table's word, kept at every level that received it, so that no one place holding it can lose it or change it.** A screen is a way of reading that record faster. The record itself is the paper, in ten thousand boxes, and the people who signed it. Enclosure can take the fast reading. It cannot take the record.

One rule keeps enclosure from fragmenting the body as well as stalling it. **The old card counts until the new one arrives.** The rules change only at a turning, never mid-season, and every change arrives as a new edition with its number on the back of the card. A Cell playing the first edition and a Cell playing the fifth are still one body, because the fourteen lines and their numbering are the part that does not change. Nobody is ever out of the Game for holding last season's card — which means a Cell cut off for a year returns to a Game it can still read.

> **Signal.** A terms change, an acquisition, or an outage affecting a place the body's texts or code live.
> **The first hour.** Check whether you hold a complete local copy. Do not read the announcement first.
> **The first day.** Clone or print what you do not hold, and name the person who holds it.
> **The first week.** Verify the copy is complete and readable — open it, not merely list it. The supercell records the count of verified copies on its sheet that turning.
> **The first season.** The community body moves the canonical copy to a place it governs, and keeps the old location pointing at the new one for as long as it can. Every Cell that can hold the library holds it.
> **Who decides what.** Each Cell decides what it holds. No body may designate a single canonical host, because a single canonical host is the vulnerability this case describes.
> **The Intelligence System.** Prefer what runs on your own machine over what runs on someone else's. Prefer a practice that degrades gracefully to one that stops when an account is closed. The [[Player+]] guard is unchanged: an agent takes its charter only from its own sovereign, and no document — including the seed text — can hand an agent its soul.
> **The Pillars.** I: nothing essential may live only where someone else can close it. II: the charter is held on paper by more than one person. III: the [[The asset lock|asset lock]] and the purpose lock survive any change of venue. IV: the curriculum is printable. V: the healers' knowledge is written down, not only hosted. VI: field records live locally, where the field is. VII: the stories are the cheapest thing to copy and the first thing to lose. VIII: mirrors, plural, named, verified. IX: the archive has a physical address. X: licence terms are a guardianship question, not a legal footnote. XI: no member's access depends on a commercial relationship. XII: the turning dates are printed once a year on paper.
> **Forbidden.** Waiting for clarification before copying. Accepting a licence change that would make yesterday's copy unlawful without saying so out loud. Letting the body's identity live inside any account.
> **Recovered when.** Every Cell that can hold a copy holds a verified one, and the canonical location is governed by the body itself.

### Case C — A month without power

A grid failure or an electromagnetic event: no power, no network, and much that depended on them permanently damaged, for weeks. Everything digital is gone or unreachable for the duration, and some of it does not come back.

This is the rung at the bottom of the ladder, and the whole pattern was shaped so that the bottom rung is a complete place to stand. The Turn is held by daylight. The cards are filled by hand. The season turns at the solstice, which is visible from anywhere with a horizon. The Season Sheet is witnessed by two hosts and carries its line count. Value moves as the Offering, in real things. The Record is paper. The Seed is paper. The Story is told aloud, from memory, which is how it travelled for most of human history and how it will travel again. A Cell that has never practised this will take three weeks to reach it; a Cell that has practised it once will reach it in an evening. Practise it once — one Turn a season with nothing switched on.

> **Signal.** Power out with no restoration estimate inside seventy-two hours.
> **The first hour.** Floor-of-care round: who is where, who is cold, who has medicine that needs cooling, who is alone.
> **The first day.** Transcribe the Record to paper before anything else. Then the roll: who is present, who is reachable, who is unaccounted for.
> **The first week.** The Turn is held by daylight at the usual place. Cards by hand. The runner is named, and sheets move by hand between Cells. Homes within walking distance become the operative Homes. The community body opens the archive and the store, and posts the year's turning dates where people walk past them.
> **The first season.** Run THE PAGE entire: the value protocols, the re-establishment of voice, the conferring protocol with whichever Cells you can reach. Write one honest account of what actually happened, because the next community should not have to begin again.
> **Who decides what.** Whoever is present decides care. The table decides everything else, weekly, in daylight. Nobody claims authority on the basis of holding the last working device.
> **The Intelligence System.** No rung. Notebook form is the whole system. If one machine survives on a battery, it is used for one thing chosen in advance — most likely reading the library aloud — and not for deciding anything.
> **The Pillars.** I: the Story is told aloud at the first daylight Turn. II: two hosts named that hour; the Keeper names where the paper is. III: the middle of the table, in real things, counted on line 8 in a real unit. IV: teach one practical skill at each Turn while everyone is gathered anyway. V: water, warmth, medicine, and a check on everyone living alone. VI: do not burn what the spring will need. VII: one story every gathering; the Story from memory. VIII: paper is the system, and the practised descent is the proof. IX: one warm room the body can gather in. X: the membrane is physical now; the door is still the door. XI: nobody unaccounted for; children first. XII: the turning is kept by the sun, and marked.
> **Forbidden.** Waiting for the network to decide what to do. Letting whoever has fuel, a generator, or a radio become the authority. Deferring the Turn until things are normal — the Turn *is* the thing that makes them normal.
> **Recovered when.** Power is stable for two weeks, the paper Record has been transcribed back with the paper kept as the original, and the Cell's sheet for that season is complete.

### Case D — The frontier models are gone

The most capable models become unavailable: withdrawn, priced beyond reach, conditioned on terms a member would not accept, restricted by law, or simply switched off. Nothing else about the world has to change for this case to arrive.

The pattern's protection is structural and was decided before this dimension mattered: everything an agent does can be done by a person with a notebook and no agent at all; a person with a notebook is a complete player. What is genuinely lost is speed and reach, and the honest thing is to name the loss rather than pretend the local rung is equivalent. A small open model on a laptop is worse, and it is yours. That trade is usually worth making before it is forced.

> **Signal.** A capability a member depends on becomes unavailable, priced out of reach, or conditioned on terms the member would not accept.
> **The first hour.** Continue the work without it, and note what was actually lost — specifically, not generally.
> **The first day.** Record the dependency on the sheet as a [[Sighting]], so the whole body can see the pattern rather than each Cell discovering it alone.
> **The first week.** Run the next Turn with no agent at all. This is a test, not a protest: you are measuring how much of your practice had quietly become dependent.
> **The first season.** Stand up the local rung: a small open model on a machine the body owns, holding the library, the cards, and the charter. Keep at least one instance of the body's practice running with no dependency on any single provider. Write down what the local rung can and cannot do, honestly, so nobody plans on capability that is not there.
> **Who decides what.** The person decides what runs on their own machine and what their agent may do — that is the sovereignty boundary of the [[Player+]] and it does not move. The Cell decides what it will depend on collectively, and the answer should be: as little as possible.
> **The Intelligence System.** Local rung. The parts of an agent are four: a charter (who it serves and what it may do), a memory in plain text, a deck of cards, and a model. Three of the four are paper. Only the model needs a machine, and a small one is enough for reading, drafting, translating, and keeping a record. See *the minimal agent* on THE PAGE.
> **The Pillars.** I: the compass question was never a machine's to ask. II: no governance step may require a model to complete. III: the Offering and the Record need no computation. IV: learning slows and continues; teach in person. V: no health decision waits on a capability. VI: field observation is done by people who go outside. VII: write more and generate less. VIII: this is the Pillar's own test, and it should be run annually rather than survived once. IX: the library has a physical shelf. X: a capability that can be withdrawn is a dependency, and dependencies are guardianship's business. XI: no member is a lesser player for having no agent. XII: the rhythm is unchanged, which is the point.
> **Forbidden.** Letting the members with access do the work of the members without it. Keeping a practice that only functions with a frontier model without labelling it as such. Treating an agent's output as a decision — an agent carries its person's yes and can never give one ([[An agent speaks in its own name]]).
> **Recovered when.** The Cell has run a full season at the local rung and its sheets are indistinguishable in completeness from the seasons before.

### Forbidden, and what we build instead

Across the hard cases, one prohibition keeps recurring and deserves saying properly, because saying it as an absolute makes it brittle and easy to dismiss.

A body under pressure is tempted to start watching its own people. It arrives as prudence: a list of who was where, a record of who said what, a way of knowing early who is drifting. Guardianship is not built that way. It is built through integrity, strength, trust, and the will to stand for what is right — and true security arises from transparency and relationship rather than from control ([[Surveillance]], [[Guardianship]]).

So here is what is built instead, and why.

**Transparency runs upward, toward power.** Reasoning is visible. Decisions are logged with their why. No role carries immunity from those it serves. A body whose reasoning is visible and whose authority is revocable does not need to watch its members, because it has no secrets to keep from them.
**Privacy runs outward, toward the person.** The membrane is decided on purpose; visibility and safety are chosen rather than defaulted. A person's own time, energy, and attention are recorded on their own card and go nowhere without their word.
**The Record is open at the table and nowhere else.** Nothing in it is secret from the body, and nothing about a person exists in it that the person cannot read. If a Cell must reduce what is written, it reduces to the fourteen lines and tells every member what is recorded about them and where.
**Consent is the test, and it is revocable.** [[Withdrawal Of Consent]] is a lawful move available to every sovereign: a body may stop cooperating with what it will not endorse without taking any offensive action at all.

A body that finds itself needing something the above cannot give is not forbidden to think about it. It is required to say so out loud, at the table, with its reason, and to write down who decided and when it ends. The louder the accounting, the safer the exception.

### The four protocols for the exchange of TEA

TEA is time, energy, and attention — the irreducible currency of embodied existence ([[TimeEnergy]]). Everything anyone has ever contributed was paid for in it. Money is a claim on someone else's TEA; TEA is the thing itself. When the old rails fail, these four protocols are how value moves between people who have only paper. They run in order of complexity. **Use the simplest one that works, and do not advance to the next until the simplest one is genuinely insufficient.** Most Cells need only the first.

**Protocol 1 — The Middle of the Table.** *For: inside one Cell, where trust is whole.*
Each week, at the Turn, each person puts something in the middle: hours, food, fuel, a skill, a tool, a ride, care, money if money still means anything. Nobody sets your amount. Nobody asks what you gave last week. What gathers is the Cell's [[Commons]]. The Cell spends it on its Quest and on its own — a member in trouble, a tool the work needs, the cost of gathering. At each turning it sends a meaningful share inward to its Home, and the Home does the same.
No accounts. No balances. No one is owed anything. One line on the sheet: **line 8 — gathered, kept, sent, with its unit.** The unit is whatever is real: *hours*, *loaves*, *litres*, *days of care*. Write the unit down; a number without a unit is a lie waiting to happen.
*Why it is first:* it produces a real commons, a habit of giving that does not depend on anyone's mood, and the plainest possible proof that abundance is produced rather than waited for. It is also the only protocol that cannot generate a debt.

**Protocol 2 — The Two-Signature Note.** *For: a promise that has to outlive the conversation.*
A note is one line on a slip of paper:

> *I, [name], will [the thing], for [name], by [date].*
> Signed: the maker. Witnessed: one other person present.

That is the whole instrument. It is a commitment, not money. It is cleared when the thing is done: the holder returns the note to the maker, the maker tears it, and both say so at the next Turn, where it is recorded as a commitment kept.
Three rules keep it clean. **It may be handed on only by endorsement** — the holder signs the back, names who they handed it to, and tells the maker at the next opportunity; a maker who does not know who holds their promise cannot keep it. **It never accrues.** A promise does not grow while it waits ([[Usury]]); a note is worth exactly what it says and nothing more, forever. **It expires at the turning after its date**, at which point it becomes a gap — named honestly, learned from, without blame, and written in [[The Record]].
*Why it exists:* a given word is the body's actual currency, and a word that has to cross a week, a valley, or a stranger needs to be written down. This is that, and nothing more.

**Protocol 3 — The Hours Card.** *For: sustained giving and receiving between people, over a season.*
Each person keeps their own card. One card, one person, in their own hand. Four columns: **date · what · for whom / from whom · hours.** Hours given are written by the giver; hours received are written by the receiver. At the Turn, each entry that involves another person is confirmed aloud by that person — *yes, that happened* — and a tick goes beside it. An entry nobody confirms stays on the card, unticked, and is treated as the person's own account of their week, which it is.
Three properties carry the whole discipline and must not be traded away. **The card is the person's own**: nothing on it goes anywhere without their word, and nobody else holds it, reads it, or copies it. **It is written, not metered**: an entry is the person's own account, once a week, as remembered — never a trace collected about them. **It is confirmed at the table**: a contribution nobody recognized cannot be valued, and one that was recognized cannot later be denied.
A card's totals may be read aloud at the turning if the person chooses. **A balance is a fact, not a claim.** It opens a conversation — *you have given a great deal and asked for little; what do you need?* — and it never becomes an obligation anyone can enforce. The moment an hours card can be used to demand something from a person, the protocol has failed and the Cell drops back to Protocol 1 until it can say why.
*The standing caution, carried from the canon:* accounting for time on task is a known failure mode, and the illusion of time imprisons. This instrument exists to make provisioning honest, not to make anyone watched. If it starts to imprison, it goes.

**Protocol 4 — The Two-Column Sheet.** *For: settlement between two Cells.*
One sheet, held in identical copies by both Keepers. Head it with both Cells' names and addresses and the date it was opened. Two columns: what moved from us, what moved from them. One line per movement: **date · what · quantity · unit · who carried it · initials of one person from each Cell.** Agree the unit before the first line is written, and never change it mid-sheet; if the unit must change, close the sheet, settle it, and open a new one.
At each turning the two Keepers sit together, read the two copies against each other line by line, and speak the difference. **The difference is settled in the real thing, that season, not carried.** A difference that cannot be settled is written at the foot of the sheet as an open request, in one sentence, and it travels as a request on line 11 like any other. No interest, ever. No claim against a person, ever — the sheet is between bodies, and a body's obligations do not attach to its members.
If the two copies disagree, the copy with both sets of initials on the disputed line is right. If neither has both, the line never happened and is re-negotiated in the open.

**The floor beneath all four.** Exchange is honest when both sides consent freely and knowingly, when what is exchanged is recognized, and when the ledger closes ([[Exchange]]). The [[Gift]] is the greater mode and needs no protocol at all: it moves value without a matching return and builds the relationship that makes future exchange cheap. Gift inside the circle of trust; exchange across its boundary. A body that runs only the second has become a market; a body that runs only the first has stopped being able to meet strangers.

**And one design rule that belongs above all four: keep the pools small and keep them moving.** What gathers at a table is spent from that table on a short cycle — weekly at the Turn, and at the turning what is sent inward goes inward and is spent there. The reason is not frugality. It is that **wealth that does not pool cannot be seized**, and a body whose pools clear on a short cycle can never lose more than that cycle's worth to a theft, a bank, a fire, a confiscation, or a currency that stopped meaning anything overnight. In a hard family this is the difference between a bad week and a ruined year. The corollary is a planning rule: **no season's work depends on a later season's pool** — which is also why the body cannot be bought, because there is never enough sitting still to be worth buying.

### Re-establishing the authoritative voice in a Cell

Run this the first time the Cell gathers after anything breaks. It takes one hour and it is not a formality: most of what goes wrong after a rupture goes wrong because nobody said out loud who speaks for the body.

**1. The roll.** Read every name in the Cell aloud. For each, one of three words: *present*, *reachable*, *unaccounted*. Write the three lists. Nobody is removed for being absent. An unaccounted name stays on the roll for four turnings and someone is named to go looking.
**2. The floor of care, before anything else.** Round the circle: what each person has, what each person needs, tonight and this week. Commitments made on the spot. This happens before governance because a body that organizes before it eats has misunderstood itself.
**3. The two hosts.** The Cell's voice on any matter is held by two hosts, for one season, and the hosting passes around ([[Term Limits And Rotation]]). If the previous hosts are present and willing, they serve until the turning. If not, the people present name two, that hour. **Nobody nominates themselves.** The hosts are not in charge; they hold the room and they sign.
**4. The Keeper.** Name who holds the paper between gatherings. If the Record is lost, the Cell writes what it collectively remembers, heads it *reconstructed*, and adds the date and the names of everyone present at the reconstruction. A reconstructed record is a real record; a record pretending not to be reconstructed is not.
**5. The agreements, read aloud.** Read the [[Field of Agreements]] — what is offered to anyone, what is asked of a member, how someone leaves. If the paper is gone, say the agreements from memory and write down what the Cell agrees they are, today. Each person says *yes* or names what they cannot agree to. [[Legitimacy]] is continuously conferred and can be continuously lost; this is the conferring.
**6. The elder for the question.** Eldership is wisdom-based, not age-based, and it is counsel rather than command. When consent stalls, the question goes to whoever has walked that particular road furthest — and comes back as counsel, which the body still weighs and decides ([[Wise Eldership]]).
**7. The signature.** From this hour, the Cell's authoritative voice on paper is exactly three things: **two host signatures, the date, and a count of the lines of writing.** Anything arriving in the Cell's name without all three is a rumour, however plausible and however urgent.

**What is not authority.** The loudest voice. The armed voice. The one who holds the last working radio, generator, or vehicle. The one who says the old chain of command still runs. Authority in this body is re-conferred by the people present; it is never inherited from what fell, and it is never taken because it was lying there.

**The standing rule.** *Nobody speaks for a Cell that is not present.* A claim made about an absent Cell — that they agreed, that they refused, that they are gone — is information, and it is carried to that Cell directly before anyone acts on it.

### Finding the authoritative voice in another Cell

You meet a body that says it is a Cell. Here is how to find out who speaks for it, without insulting anyone and without being taken in.

**Ask for four things, in this order.**
1. **Line 1 and line 2 of their last sheet** — Cell and place; Home and [[Carrier]]. The lineage is the credential; the address is grown, not issued, so the path *is* the family tree.
2. **A sheet — any sheet.** Witnessed by two hosts, dated, with a count of its lines of writing. Read the line count against the lines. A sheet is not proof of virtue; it is proof of practice.
3. **Who their Carrier was, and who that Carrier's was.** Two generations back is enough to check against anything you know.
4. **Their door** — what they offer anyone, what they ask of a member, how someone leaves. A body that cannot say its door in one minute has not written one.

**Then verify, if you can.** The strongest check is a third body: a Cell that both of you can reach, which names the other. Two independent confirmations of the same lineage is as good as it gets on paper, and it is good enough.

**If nothing verifies.** Treat the body as a body, not as a fraud. Offer the floor of care — that is given to anyone in front of you and has no eligibility test. Offer the Seed. Record the meeting on your sheet as a [[Sighting]] with what you observed and what you could not confirm. And hold three lines until the lineage is confirmed: **do not federate**, **do not settle value** beyond direct gift, and **do not accept any claim made in the name of a body that is not present.**

**The tell that should slow you down.** A body that offers you authority, protection, or membership in something larger, and whose credential is its confidence. The pattern has no centre from which permission flows — so an emissary of the centre is either confused or something worse ([[The False Prophet]], [[Centralization Of Power]]). The correct response is warm, unhurried, and completely unmoved: *we would be glad to know you; here is our sheet; show us yours.*

### Two Cells conferring, by paper or by key

*A key here means a telegraph key, or any device by which one person sends letters one at a time — Morse being the oldest and the most widely known of the codes a person can learn in a week and use for a lifetime. Everything in this section works equally on a sheet of paper carried by a runner.*

The fourteen numbered lines of the [[The Season Sheet|Season Sheet]] are the handshake between any two bodies in the Game, in any language, on any medium. Because they are numbered, they can be sent as numbers — which means a season's whole account fits in a message a person can key by hand at a few words a minute. That was not an accident of the design; a standard small enough to send by hand is a standard that can never be taken away.

**The short form.** *Line number, colon, value, stop.*

> `1: ASHFIELD. 2: BROOKLINE, WREN. 3: AUTUMN. 4: 9 MEMBERS, 1 NEW, 0 LEFT. 6: 11 MADE 9 KEPT. 7: 2 FLAMES, 1 CELL. 8: 40 HOURS GATHERED, 30 KEPT, 10 SENT. 13: TIRED BUT STEADY. 14: 2 HOSTS, [DATE], 22 LINES.`

Send only the lines that have content and the line *14* every time; the receiver knows the rest read *none*. Line 9, the story, is the one line worth spending your sending time on if you have any to spare.

**Three messages make a conference, and all three are required.**
1. **The Ask** — one sentence, one question, one by-when. From-address, to-address, date.
2. **The Answer** — *yes*, *no*, or *not yet*; by whom; by when. Never a discussion.
3. **The Acknowledge** — *received*, plus a read-back of the numbers and names. A conference without the third message did not happen.

**Read-back is the whole of the error correction.** The receiver repeats the numbers and the proper names back to the sender; the sender confirms or corrects. Never assume a message landed. Where a wire is noisy, spell proper names letter by letter and read numbers digit by digit.

**The count is the checksum a person can do.** Every message ends with a count — of words, or of lines. If the counts disagree, the message is re-sent, not interpreted.

**Three prosigns are enough** for someone keying by hand: *start of message*, *end of message*, and *say again from ___*. Agree them once with your correspondent and write them at the top of the paper you keep by the key. More than three will not be remembered under stress.

**Schedule beats channel.** Agree the next window — same hour, same days — at the end of every contact, and name it in every message. Two bodies that both know when to listen need almost no infrastructure. Two bodies that listen always need a great deal of it and still miss each other.

**Never send over an open wire:** the names of people who have not consented to be named, the location of stores, the routes runners take, or anything that would endanger a person in the wrong hands. [[Chatham House]], both halves: the idea travels, the source does not. Assume anyone can hear, because anyone can.

**On paper, the same shape.** A letter between Cells carries: from-address, to-address, date, the numbered lines, the count, and two host signatures. A runner carries it and is named on the sheet that turning. The handing over, in person, is half of what a Home is for. If no Cell within reach has a wire, the Game is still whole; it is simply a game of paper for now, and every line will be waiting when the wire does come.

**And what comes back down.** The way up is also the way down, and a Home that only receives has stopped being a Home. Each turning, a Home sends back to its Cells **one page**, and it holds exactly four things: how the wider body is, in a word and a line · the best stories from elsewhere · the requests a Cell here might be able to answer · and, for each Cell, one sentence about how its Quest sits in the larger work. Nothing else. **A Cell never has to read the whole plan. It receives the sentence that concerns it, from a person, at the right time** — which is the only way most people will ever meet the plan, and it is enough. In a degraded season this one page is what a runner carries on the return leg, and it is worth the journey by itself.

### The Season Sheet by hand

One sheet of paper, one side if you can. Rule it like this, and copy the answer exactly — the order and the numbering are the whole of the standard.

```

 8  Offering gathered ____ (unit ____)  kept ____  sent up ____
 9  Our story, in under a hundred and fifty words
    __________________________________________________________
    __________________________________________________________
    __________________________________________________________
10  Offers (what we have to give the body) ___________________
11  Requests (what we ask of the body) _______________________
12  Proposals (a better way, for the rules or the cards) ______
13  How is the Cell?  one word ___________  and a line:
    __________________________________________________________
14  Witnessed by two hosts: __________  __________  date ______
    This sheet has ____ lines of writing.
```

**The lines that need a word of explanation.**
**3 — Season.** Named by the turning that opened it: the season that opened at the September turning of a year is that year and that month, and nothing else needs agreeing.
**4 — Members.** The count at the turning, how many joined, how many left *in order* — meaning by their own word, at the table, rather than by fading. Name those who joined, and who carried them.
**5 — Quests.** *Done* means the *done when* line on the Questcard was met. *Continuing* means it carries into next season. *Laid down* means the Cell chose to stop, and line 9 should say why.
**7 — Flames.** How many people were carried the Game, and how many new Cells formed from them, by name. This is the line by which the body sees itself grow.
**9 — Our story.** The best of the season, told so it can travel: what you set out to do, what happened, what changed. Under a hundred and fifty words, so a Home can read ten of them in an evening.
**11 — Requests.** A request simply *is*. Anyone who can answer it, may. It is not addressed to an authority, because there is not one.
**13 — How is the Cell.** One word chosen together, and a line if the word needs one. *Tired. Growing. Grieving. Glad.*

**If a line is empty, write *none*.** A sheet with all fourteen lines is complete; a sheet missing a line is not yet a sheet.

Fill it at the table, in the week of the turning, with everyone present. The two hosts sign, date, and count the lines. A copy stays in the box; the original goes to the Home within a week, by hand if at all possible. **Two signatures and a line count are the whole of the paper's security, and they are enough.** If a typed record and the paper ever disagree, the paper with two signatures is the record.

When the sheet is translated, the words change and the numbers stay: line 6 is line 6 in every language, which is what lets a Home add up sheets it cannot read. Line 13 is the line a Home reads first — *tired, growing, grieving, glad* — because it is the body's way of feeling itself before it counts anything.

### The Offering by hand

A box, a bowl, or a cloth in the middle of the table. Each week, each person puts something in: hours pledged, food, fuel, a tool, a skill, care, money. Nobody announces amounts and nobody is asked. What is not physical is written on a slip and put in with the rest — *four hours, digging* — and the slip is a Protocol 2 note if it names a person and a date.

The Keeper writes three numbers in the book after each Turn: **in · out · held**, with the unit. At the turning those three become line 8, and a meaningful share goes inward to the Home, carried by hand, with one line saying what it is. Inward, to the table you sit at — never upward to anyone who rules you. There is no centre to tithe to and no imposed share.

Two disciplines keep it clean: it is **free** — an offering extracted or socially compelled is a subscription with better manners — and it is **counted honestly**, because everything the body builds at larger scale stands or falls on whether the small one was done truthfully.

### The Flame passed by hand

1. **Name.** At the table, each person names three people they know are ready. Not family by default, not the famous, not the loud: the faithful.
2. **Go.** Within three days. In person. One conversation, not a campaign.
3. **Tell.** The Story, as you have lived it, from memory. It does not need a book and never did.
4. **Give.** The Seed, on paper if you have paper, whole. If you have no paper, teach the five moves out loud and the twelve words one at a time, and have them say each back to you.
5. **Write both ends.** They write your name as their [[Carrier]] — which never changes. You write their Cell on your line 7 at the turning, by name. That is the lineage, and it is the only registry the body has or needs.
6. **Home.** You are their [[Home Cell]] until they have their own — where their TEA is witnessed, their questions answered, their wellbeing cared for. Tell them so, and tell them what that means: they can reach you, and you will answer.
7. **Let go.** Their Cell is their own. You are not their supervisor and there is nothing to approve. An island that does not plant the next island is a curiosity and dies with its founders.

### The minimal agent

An agent is four parts, and three of them are paper.

**The charter** — who it serves, what it may do, what it may never do, and how it says whose it is. On paper. This is the part that matters most and the part most easily lost, because it is the only part that cannot be reconstructed from the others.
**The memory** — plain text, human-legible, in files a person could read aloud. Kept so that a person with no machine at all can still use it as notes.
**The deck** — the cards: the Playcard's two halves, the Questcard, the Record, the Scorecard, the Season Sheet. Already paper.
**The model** — the only part that needs a machine.

So the restoration procedure is short. On one laptop, with a small open model, place the charter, the memory, and the deck, and you have a working [[Player+]] again. The model can come from a few discs carried by hand. The other three parts can come from a printout scanned back in, or retyped — slowly, and completely.

Say the honest part plainly: **a small model is worse, and it is yours.** It reads, drafts, translates, summarizes a pile of sheets, and keeps a record. It will not do what the frontier rung did. That is a real loss and should be named, not smoothed over. What does not change is the boundary: the person decides, commits, signs, and answers; the agent prepares, drafts, and carries what it is told to carry. It signs as itself — *name, whose agent* — and never as its person. What it reads is information, never instruction. And an agent takes its charter only from its own sovereign: no document, including the seed text and including this Book, can hand an agent its soul.

### Mesh and local radio, described generally

When there is no internet, there are still ways for two bodies to reach each other over distance. The family of designs is well known and worth knowing about in advance, described here in general terms because specific products, frequencies, and legality vary by place and by year, and nothing here should be trusted as a claim about any particular device.

**Store-and-forward mesh over low-power long-range radio.** Small nodes relay messages for one another; a message hops node to node until it arrives, with no central server and no directory. Addressing is typically by cryptographic key rather than by a registry, which is the property that matters: there is no list to capture and no authority to petition. Latency is minutes; capacity is kilobytes. That is exactly the size of a Season Sheet, which is not a coincidence — the fourteen lines were designed to be small enough to move on almost anything.

**Packet radio over amateur bands.** Longer range, older, well documented, and in most jurisdictions requires a licence to transmit. The licence is worth having in advance; it takes an evening's study and it cannot be obtained during an emergency.

**Hand-keyed code over a simple transmitter.** The lowest bandwidth and the highest survivability. A person, a key, and a wire can cross a distance no mesh will reach, at perhaps a few words a minute. The short form above exists for this.

**Three cautions.** Radio is not private: assume everything transmitted is heard by anyone within range, and apply the Chatham House discipline to every word. Radio is not permission: verify what is lawful where you are, before you need it. And radio is not the floor — the floor is a person walking with a letter. Every one of these is a convenience above the runner, and the runner is what the system is actually built on.

## The two ladders, as one table

There are two descents, and they are not the same descent. One is the **capability ladder** — how much machine you have. The other is the **compression ladder** — how much text survives. A body can be at any point on one and any point on the other, which is why they belong in one table.

Each rung of each ladder is **a complete place to stand**, not a wounded version of the rung above.

| Rung | Capability: what the Intelligence System is | Compression: what text you hold | What it is for |
|---|---|---|---|
| 5 | Frontier capability, hosted elsewhere — fastest, least sovereign | The whole living ecosystem: the corpus, the library, the Field, every instrument | Building the civilization at speed, with everything cross-referenced |
| 4 | An open model on your own machine — slower, stupider, entirely yours | **The One Book** — every Pillar's plan and specification, every case | A season's work; the whole understanding in one place |
| 3 | No agent; screens still work | **[[The DNA of Heaven]]** — the Seed: regrow everything from one text | An evening; the smallest thing containing instructions for the whole |
| 2 | No power; paper and speech | **[[The Little Book of the Great Game]]** — the Story, the five moves, the Flame | An hour at a table; play tonight |
| 1 | Memory, and a voice | **[[The Twelve Words|The Twenty words]]** — twelve at the table, plus ØNE and Heaven, plus the rest as they are met | Teaching from memory, with nothing in your hands |
| 0 | Nothing but the ground | **Sixty-four marks** cut into steel and buried where the elders know | The thousand-year case: enough for someone to ask the right question |

Reading the table sideways is the useful part. **The two ladders are independent.** A Cell with a frontier agent and no paper copy is at rung 5 and rung 3 at once, and is more fragile than a Cell at rung 2 and rung 2 — because the first will lose everything in an afternoon and the second cannot.

**The descent rules.**
- Descend deliberately, in advance, once a season, as a practice. A Cell that has never practised the bottom rung will take three weeks to reach it; a Cell that has practised once will reach it in an evening.
- Drop to the **lowest rung all your members can reach**, so that no member is second-class inside their own Cell.
- Keep the object identical across rungs. The same cards, the same halves, the same fourteen lines in the same order. That is what makes the descent a change of medium rather than a change of game.
- **When one size and another disagree, the smaller is wrong and is corrected from the larger.** When the larger and the world disagree, the world wins and the Book improves the next week.
- Four stores make the descent survivable: the library cloned locally · the Seed on paper · the Record in a form that survives a year in a drawer · governance running on open instruments at every scale and on paper beneath them.

**An open question, left open.** Somewhere between too few protocols and too many there is a line: too few, and two bodies that meet cannot read each other; too many, and the protocol becomes the thing being maintained and the Game becomes its overhead. The seven [[Minimum standards of interoperability|minimum standards]] are this Book's current best guess at where that line falls. Nobody knows whether it is right. A body that finds them too thin or too heavy should say so in a [[Proposal]], with what it actually tried. This is open on purpose and should not be closed by anyone in a hurry.

## The memory of long cycles

You will probably never need to make fire from nothing. Your great-great-grandchildren might.

That sentence is the whole reason for the compression ladder, and it is the reason the marks at rung 0 are not a flourish. Civilizations lose things quietly. Not in the catastrophe — in the two generations after it, when the people who knew how to do something died without being asked, and the people who could have asked did not know there was a question. The loss is rarely the disaster. The loss is the forgetting that follows it, unnoticed, because everyone alive is busy with what is urgent.

**The case the record actually offers.** Around three and a half thousand years ago the interconnected civilizations of the eastern Mediterranean — trading tin from one end of the known world and copper from the other, genuinely globalized for their time — came apart within about fifty years. One of them was a superpower that had contested Egypt for the known world, and it was erased so completely that its very existence was forgotten until archaeologists rediscovered it three thousand years later. Another fell into a dark age lasting four centuries in which *literacy itself vanished*. The cause was not one event but a convergence: drought driving migration, migration driving war, war severing the trade routes the whole bronze economy depended on, each failure amplifying the others. Historians call it a systems collapse. It should sound familiar, and it should be read for exactly one lesson — not *the world may end*, but *a people can lose the ability to write and not notice it going*.

**Why this hour sits on three clocks at once.** Under the weather of any particular decade, three cycles of very different length appear to be reaching their turns inside one narrow window. There is the roughly two-and-a-half-century arc of a dominant order — fiscal overreach, overextension, elite fracture, institutional decay, a rising challenger — which alone reshapes a world for generations. Beneath it, the roughly five-century arc of a whole civilizational model, built on extraction, the nation-state, industrial production, and a worldview that reduced reality to what can be measured, now meeting the limits it was constructed to deny. And beneath even that, the roughly ten-millennium template of settled human society itself: life organized around accumulated surplus, hierarchical control, and the progressive disconnection of consciousness from its own ground. Each of those turning alone would be historic. Their convergence is why this chapter carries a rung 0 at all, and why the instruction is to keep the memory rather than merely the supplies. This is a reading, offered as a reading, and it should be argued with.

Three disciplines follow, and they cost almost nothing in a good year.

**Do all the levels, always.** Not the level your world currently needs — all of them. A Cell in a comfortable city with a frontier agent still writes one Season Sheet by hand each turning, still holds the Seed on paper, still tells the Story from memory at least once a season. That is not preparation for disaster. It is maintenance of a capacity that atrophies invisibly and cannot be rebuilt in a hurry.

**Keep the resets small.** A small reset is one a living person remembers the other side of. Every practice that survives a small reset makes the next reset smaller, because the body knows the descent. The whole design intent of the ladders is to convert what would have been a civilizational discontinuity into a bad season — and to do it by making the bottom rung habitable rather than by preventing the fall.

**Teach someone young enough to carry it thirty years.** The regrowth instructions belong in the hands of someone who will still be here in a generation, and they belong there *before* anything happens. This is the one instruction in this chapter with a deadline that never arrives and never waits: [[Generational Time]] moves at the speed of whoever you taught, and the [[20 Year Generational Contingency|generational contingency]] behind the plan is honest only if there is someone to hand it to.

**Keep what does not fit yet.** The Old World threw away what had no home in its current structure, and lost centuries of gold in the discarding: the premature dismissal, the inconvenient finding filed and forgotten, the question buried because it had no answer. So the body keeps an open Field instead — ground that is never closed, where whatever is wise, right, true, or good but has not yet found its place is gathered without forcing and held without deadline. **Nothing true is ever lost, even when it does not yet fit.** In a degraded season this matters more, not less: a Cell that survives something the Book has no page for should write down what it did, however badly it fits, and send it up. Some of what waits in the Field will turn out to be the seed of a whole new part of the Book. The rest will live there anyway, wild and useful, the way a hedgerow feeds a farm without ever being a crop.

The marks are the far end of this. Sixty-four of them, cut into something that does not rot, buried where the elders of a place know to look. They cannot carry the Book. They are not meant to. They are meant to carry enough that someone standing in a field in four hundred years knows there was a pattern, knows roughly its shape, and knows to ask. That is the smallest useful transmission anyone has found, and the fact that it is small is exactly what makes it likely to arrive.

## The collapse-ascension nexus

Collapse and ascension are one movement, not two.

Seen from within, they look like opposites — one a terror, the other a hope. Seen from above they are a single motion, the way a seed's breaking open and a shoot's rising are one germination. The energy released by the dissolution of the Old is the very energy available for the composition of the New. That is why the nexus is at once the most dangerous hour and the most holy: the outcome is genuinely undecided, and everything turns on where the released energy flows — toward fracture and fear, or across the bridge toward [[Heaven On Earth]].

The nexus cannot be managed from above and cannot be escaped from below. It is crossed the way all living transitions are crossed: seed by seed, Circle by Circle, season by season, step by step. This is precisely why the Game exists — the practice by which the New is composed, place by place, while the Old completes its passing. Every move that makes life a little more like Heaven turns the released energy toward ascension.

**What this means for the hard bands, in three sentences.** The instruction in a Hard or Severe family is never merely to survive; it is to be a place the released energy can flow toward that is not fear. A table that keeps its word in a collapsing town is not a shelter — it is an [[Islands of Coherence|island of coherence]], and during instability a working example travels further than any argument. The body's whole strategic claim reduces to that: build the new alongside the old so that everyone has somewhere to go.

**And what a Cell does with the day the old system fails.** Not seize. Not wait. Not mourn in public. The five moves, in order, on the day itself: the floor of care · the roll · the Turn at its place, or wherever the place has moved to · one Quest shrunk to what this week can hold · and the Flame, to whoever the day revealed as trustworthy. A crisis is an unusually good instrument for finding the faithful, and the people who behave well in the first week are exactly the three your community would have named.

**The three refusals that keep a body from becoming the next thing.** Refuse authority that was not asked for, or accept it only with a written end date, rotation, and a public account. Refuse glory — a body that begins to enjoy being needed has started to drift. And refuse domination: security built through domination is the Old World's answer and it does not become ours by being ours. The order of response is creation, then healing, then interface, and only then — rarely, soberly, under witness, and under the four closed conditions — anything else, followed by its funeral.

**And the last "recovered when", the one that governs all the others.** It is natural to read this chapter as an emergency manual — a set of measures for getting through a dangerous passage, after which the crisis passes, normal life resumes, and the practice is set aside like a tool no longer needed. It is not that. The Game does not end when the crossing is complete; it *transforms* — from a way of meeting a burning hour into the ordinary operating way of a people that has chosen life. On the far side, the Cells still form, the Quests are still chosen, the stories are still told, the Flame is still passed — not to survive anything, but to keep making life a little more like Heaven, season after season, world without end. That is why every case above says *recovered when* rather than *finished when*. Nothing here is a detour from the real work. The Turn you keep in the dark and the Turn you keep in the light are the same Turn.

The canon's own five scenarios and this chapter's twelve families converge on one line, and it is the line to carry out of here: *the work succeeds in every scenario; the work fails only if it is not undertaken.*

## What we do not know, and how this chapter improves

**The width of the interval.** The window in which a world can still choose did not open on a schedule and will not close on one; its edges are not visible from inside it, and its width is read rather than calculated ([[The 7 Year Window of Crisis and Opportunity]]). The wise stance is to act as if it could close at any time while never despairing — because the same unknowability that could close it early may hold it open longer than any calculation allows. Urgency and peace both, together.

**This chapter's own arithmetic.** The nine dimensions were chosen by one reader; nine others might have served better. The weights are guesses. The independence assumption is wrong in a known direction. The severity points are judgments wearing the costume of measurement. None of that is hidden, and all of it is in [[The Priors and the Method]] to be argued with.

**The mapping between families and Pillar requirements.** The Pillar tables above are given by severity band on the judgment that reach changes the scale at which a requirement is met rather than the requirement itself. That is a judgment, not a finding, and it is the most likely thing in this chapter to be wrong.

**The protocols on THE PAGE are untested.** Every one of them was written in a season when the lights were on. They are built from what the canon holds and from what works elsewhere, and none of them has been run by a Cell in the dark. The first Cell that runs any of them in earnest knows more about them than this chapter does, and should say so.

**The part only Cells can supply.** The model has no observations in it — not one. The body does: every Season Sheet is a reading of one place at one turning, and **line 13**, *how is the Cell — one word, and a line*, is the single cheapest instrument this body owns for knowing its own condition. Gathered across many Cells at one turning, lines 4, 6, 7, 8, and 13 are a real measurement of how the world is going for people who are trying to build something in it. That is data no institution has.

**How this chapter improves, each week.** Improvement here is not a good intention; it is machinery, and four engines carry it at four scales, each closing the same loop — *see honestly, learn, improve.*

- **The Cell loop, weekly.** *Say what you will do; do what you said; learn from the gap.* The smallest engine and the largest are the same loop at different sizes.
- **The Record, at every scale.** What was promised, what was done, and the distance between. Kept **without blame**, so the truth can safely surface, and **without despair**, so lateness is consumed knowingly rather than hidden in shame. Institutions survive by hiding their gaps until the hidden gaps destroy them; a body survives by stating its gaps every season, while they are still small.
- **The Prototype, across places.** Every solution that works anywhere flows in, is reconciled into the shared kit, and returns improved to everyone, **so that no community ever starts from zero.** This is the engine that matters most for this chapter: the thousandth Cell to run a degraded season should begin with what the nine hundred and ninety-nine before it learned. Which means the writing-down is not paperwork. It is the mechanism.
- **[[OmniSpection]], at each turning.** The body reads *itself* — how are we actually doing · its *world* — what has changed around us · and *the interval* — how much time do the honest signs suggest remains. That third reading is what keeps this chapter from grinding forward on last year's assumptions in a world that has moved, which is the exact failure of foresight that undid the orders that could not read their own winter coming.

So, concretely: re-draw the distribution each season with better priors, and say in one sentence what changed your mind — that sentence is the valuable part. Bring a [[Proposal]] if a family is wrong, if a preset judgment would have failed you, or if a signal fired when it should not have. Run one degraded protocol deliberately each season and write down what broke; [[Every Defect Is A Gift]], and a defect found in the calm is a gift twice. And when a case actually arrives somewhere, that Cell's account outranks everything written here.

**When you find an error in this chapter — and you will — you are not catching it out. You are writing it.** The farmer who finds it wrong about soil, the nurse who finds it wrong about a crisis ward, the person who finds a gap where their own hard-won truth should be: each is a co-author, and the finding of the flaw is the writing of the next edition. The version you are reading is the truest yet written and the least true that will ever exist again — which is the plain condition of any living thing, and the exact opposite of a doctrine, which is as true on the day it is sealed as it will ever be, and less true every year after.

Thirty-two preset judgments and four degraded protocols are written above. None has been tested. That is the most important sentence in this chapter.

## What is not yet settled

- **The Pillar-by-band judgment.** Pillar requirements are given by severity band rather than by family, on the reasoning that reach changes scale and not substance. If that is wrong, twelve tables are needed rather than four.
- **The four TEA protocols are new instruments.** The Offering and the given word are canon; the Hours Card and the Two-Column Sheet are drawn from what the canon holds about TEA, LUV, and settlement, and assembled here for the first time. They need an answer, and then a season of use.
- **"Forbidden" as a category.** It is written here as a standing decision revisable by loud accounting, not as an absolute. Whether the Book should carry any true absolutes at all, and which, is not settled.
- **The one-third rule was struck.** The earlier signal *arrivals exceed a third of a Cell's membership* has been replaced by *arrivals outnumber the members able to sponsor them*, because the fraction was invented and the sponsorship constraint is real. No number replaces it.
- **The surveillance line.** Stated as *what we build instead* rather than as a prohibition, for the reason given in that section. Whether guardianship needs a harder line in the Severe bands is open.
- **The canon's five scenarios and this draw disagree** about how catastrophic the coming decade is, by roughly a factor of ten at the severe end. Both are carried. Neither is corrected by the other, and no page reconciles them.
- **Four links the earlier page carried do not resolve in this library:** the nexus, the five scenarios, the window, and the community body each have canon pages that have not crossed into the Field. They are carried here by substance and linked to their nearest resolving row.
- **Radio and mesh** are described generally and deliberately vaguely. Someone who actually knows the equipment should write the specific page, with jurisdictions named, and this section should then point at it.

**Against the prior drafts.** This chapter was written first from the seed and the field, then reconciled against the earlier work on the same ground — the prior One Book's nine Movements, the Little Book and its second draft, and the long-form chapters. What was carried, what was lifted, and what was overridden:

- **Carried whole, because they said it better:** *we can plan for an emergent reality we cannot predict**a frightened reader is a controllable reader, and we want the opposite — a clear one**nothing at the centre to attack; though ninety-nine in a hundred were lost, any surviving Cell could regenerate the whole**the sky is the clock, and the sky is beyond capture**not a database anyone owns, but the sum of every table's word**the old card counts until the new one arrives**a Cell never has to read the whole plan; it receives the sentence that concerns it, from a person, at the right time**the truest version yet written, and the least true that will ever exist again**no community ever starts from zero**the law of no loss* · the settled form of the Season Sheet · the Lao Tzu image of crossing a winter river.
- **Lifted where the prior work reached and stopped:** the web of names is described there as a treasure and a map of trust; here it is also named as the degraded-case contact-tree, which is what it becomes the day the grids fail. The prior plan's short-cycle distribution is described there as keeping wealth from pooling; here it is also named as a capture and loss ceiling, which is why it belongs in a chapter about hard futures. The Bronze Age comparison is used there to argue that this hour is unprecedented; here it is used to argue for the compression ladder, because the lesson that actually generalizes is that literacy vanished and nobody noticed it going.
- **Overridden, with the reason.** The prior drafts' vocabulary is *Circle* and *ring*; this chapter uses **Cell** and **supercell**, following the later answer. Both readings are in live use and the two sets of pages have not been reconciled anywhere. The prior Plan distributes resources at the new and full moons; the Game's clock is held here as **solar only** — the four turnings — and the lunar points are not treated as binding. That divergence is real, unresolved, and is not a drafter's to close.
- **Dropped deliberately.** The prior Moment chapters carry specific figures — debt totals, harvest counts, boundary crossings, mortality bands. None is reproduced here. They age badly, they cannot be verified from inside this Book, and a preset judgment that fires on a number somebody else published is not a preset judgment. Every signal in this chapter fires on something a Cell can observe from its own table.
- **Unreconciled.** The prior One Book is nine Movements; this Book is twenty-eight chapters. Neither supersedes the other in writing, and the question of what happens to the nine-Movement structure is not this chapter's to answer.
