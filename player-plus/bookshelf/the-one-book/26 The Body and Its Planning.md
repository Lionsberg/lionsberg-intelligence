# The Body and Its Planning

*What the body is made of, organ by organ, from a Player at a kitchen table to the planet; how it governs, sustains and replicates itself as a team of teams; where it stands in the land, in commons and hubs; how it plans at every scale without a master plan; and how it knows, every day, where it actually stands. This chapter gives depth to Part IV of [30 The Strategy and Plan](28%20The%20Strategy%20and%20Plan.md). Governance has its own chapter ([09 Pillar II](09%20Pillar%20II%20%E2%80%94%20Sacred%20Governance%20and%20Stewardship.md)), and so do agents ([25 Players, Agents, and the Intelligence System](25%20Players%2C%20Agents%2C%20and%20the%20Intelligence%20System.md)) and the chart of the first three percent ([24 The Plan](29%20The%20Plan%20%E2%80%94%20the%20first%20three%20percent.md)). This chapter links to them rather than repeating them.*

---

## Start here

**The body grows from the bottom up.** A [Player](../the-field/Player.md) is a person who has chosen to play. Players sit together in [Cells](../the-field/Cell.md) of three to thirteen, optimally four to seven. Every Cell has a [Home Cell](../the-field/Home%20Cell.md) that hears its season. Three or more Cells that have become one another's home form a [supercell](../the-field/Supercell.md), and supercells form supercells. Where a place's Cells come close to its [three hundred](../the-field/The%20300.md), a [community](../the-field/Community.md) of about ten thousand people forms. Communities join into [bioregions](../the-field/Bioregion.md), which are read by their [watersheds](../the-field/Watershed.md), and so on up to [the planet](../the-field/The%20Planet.md). Other bodies cut across the places. [Guilds](../the-field/Guild.md) hold a craft. [Guide Cells](../the-field/Guide%20Cells.md) hold a field's understanding and answer questions in it, and the first of them, the **RFG Cell**, receives every [Request For Guidance](../the-field/Request%20For%20Guidance.md). Each person may have [agents](../the-field/Agent.md) working for them, which add reach but no authority. And [the Record](../the-field/The%20Record.md) is how the whole body knows itself.

**It governs, sustains and replicates itself.** Each Cell carries the whole code, pays its own way from its own table, does real work, and plants the next Cell. The Cells stack their Quests into one larger work, and nobody assigns it. That makes the body a *team of teams* with no one in charge.

**It stands in the land.** Every Cell needs places to gather, learn, heal, make, grow, store and celebrate. Each place meets those needs through commons it holds together, and the commons grow into hubs inside the libraries, schools and halls that already stand. The places call their hubs forth themselves.

**It plans at every scale.** The plan has one *shape*, and each scale writes that shape for itself: a person for a life, a Cell for a season, a community for its ten thousand, a bioregion for its watershed. The plans are woven together, and no scale overrules another.

**It knows where it stands, daily.** In the first three percent the body reads four numbers every day: **the ring it stands on, the days per ring, the Players counted, and the commitments kept.** Every season it writes a few numbers and a sentence in its Record. The entry has no adornment and no blame, and it never quietly moves the end date.

The rest of this chapter says the same things with their reasons.

## I. Why a body needs an anatomy

### Taking flesh

A pattern can be true everywhere and always, and it still has to take flesh in one place, one season, one set of people. It grows by meeting what that place and season ask of it ([Instantiation](../the-field/Instantiation.md)). Anything that takes flesh needs organs, and the organs of a living body of co-creation come in an order, each one calling for the next. Knowing the order lets a body see which organ it is missing.

**Something to live in.** Whatever is coming into being needs a structure to shelter it while it grows. The provisional structures of the preparatory years have all been dissolved on purpose. The structure now is alive: the Cell, the body the Cells make together, and the assembly of those called out to do the work ([The Ecclesia](../the-field/The%20Ecclesia.md)).

**Many, not one.** A body is not one enormous cell. This one grows as a federation of federations: united, yet spread out as far as the work allows. Every part keeps its sovereignty while all of them co-operate as ØNE ([Holofractal Omnifederation](../the-field/Holofractal%20Omnifederation.md), [Federation](../the-field/Federation.md), [Sovereignty At Every Scale](../the-field/Sovereignty%20At%20Every%20Scale.md)).

**A boundary that breathes.** Every living whole keeps a boundary: a person, a Cell, a commons, a community, and the body entire. The boundary shelters the order inside it. It lets nourishment in and gifts out, and every crossing happens by [mutual consent](../the-field/Mutual%20Consent.md) ([Selectively Permeable Membrane](../the-field/Selectively%20Permeable%20Membrane.md)).

**A welcome, and a way to tell harm from help.** A growing body takes in newcomers, so it greets them in an orderly way, each by their own consent. Whatever would harm the body also arrives at the door sooner or later, so the body keeps a living discernment and improves it alongside the welcome. Take away the welcome and the boundary hardens into a wall. Take away the discernment and it opens into a wound ([Guard the Membrane](../the-field/Guard%20the%20Membrane.md), [Structural Immunity](../the-field/Structural%20Immunity.md)).

**A way of deciding together.** A body at odds with itself does not last. Its parts need a shared way of deciding, a shared culture and shared agreements. Each body writes those agreements in its own [Field of Agreements](../the-field/Field%20of%20Agreements.md), and the called-out carry them at every scale. The full account of how deciding works is in [09 Pillar II](09%20Pillar%20II%20%E2%80%94%20Sacred%20Governance%20and%20Stewardship.md).

**Learning for everyone, for life.** Everyone in the body has to learn how to flourish as part of a whole. That holds whether they crossed the boundary as adults or were born inside it. So one of the deepest organs is learning, open equally to every person for their whole life. Each of us is here to become the fullness of who we are, and helping every part of Creation grow toward its potential comes first ([11 Pillar IV](11%20Pillar%20IV%20%E2%80%94%20Sacred%20Learning%20and%20Mastery.md)).

**Grown from the ground up.** The preparatory years taught this lesson more plainly than any other: *people who have not yet been changed cannot build a world that changes them.* The change starts inside each person, as healing, growth and turning ([32 The Turning](05%20The%20Turning%20%E2%80%94%20consciousness%2C%20healing%20and%20wholeness%20at%20every%20scale.md)). From there it moves outward, one step at a time:

1. People cross the boundary and become co-creators.
2. Co-creators form Cells.
3. Cells choose a Quest each season.
4. Each Cell tells its [stories](../the-field/Story.md), so the body can see itself.
5. Each Cell sends the first tenth of what it gathers inward, to the [commons](../the-field/Commons.md) of the table it sits at, and spends the rest of its strength on its own place.

Three units, repeated at every scale, build the whole from below: **people · Cells · Quests.**

### One pattern, many hands

The many parts can only work as one if something unites them. Here, what unites them is one [Goal](../the-field/The%20Goal.md) and one pattern, not a head. Anyone may take the seed and start their own instance, and the shared pattern is what makes all the instances one body. Why the system carries a name at all is answered in [24 The Work and the System](24%20The%20Work%20and%20the%20System.md).

### An organism

Every empire that set out to change the world built a headquarters. Every headquarters was captured, corrupted or killed. This body grows as an organism instead. Its Cells are sovereign, and each one carries the complete code. Each can feed itself, do real work and plant the next Cell, and together they make one body of Cells ([ØNE Body](../the-field/%C3%98NE%20Body.md)). **If ninety-nine Cells in a hundred were lost, any one survivor could grow the whole again.** The body is already growing, everywhere at once.

It serves more than the people alive now. It is built as [co-operation across generations](../the-field/Multi-Generational%20Co-Operation.md). It honours the ancestors whose work it stands on. And it passes the Flame to generations who will change everything in it except its Spirit, its wisdom, its culture and its values.

## II. The organs, from the bottom up

### Players

A [Player](../the-field/Player.md) is a person who has chosen to play. For the first three percent a Player is counted by five marks. Each mark is something done, not something felt. A Player has:

1. **read the Story**;
2. **taken [the Pledge](../the-field/The%20Pledge.md)**, giving their word where others could hear it, and been written on [the Roll](../the-field/The%20Roll.md) with who brought them and who witnessed;
3. **formed a Cell**;
4. **chosen a Quest**;
5. **passed the Flame for the first time**, by bringing in their three: people who were not already playing.

The fifth mark is what makes the body grow on its own. For the first three percent, the Pledge includes the promise to bring those three within three days, and the Roll shows whether each promise was kept.

A Player is always a person. A person together with the agents that work for them is one [Player+](../the-field/Player%2B.md), and the plus adds reach but no authority. An agent is never a Player. It never votes, never speaks in a Turn, and never says yes for anyone. Everything an agent does, a person with a notebook can do, only slower, and a person with a notebook is a complete Player ([25 Players, Agents, and the Intelligence System](25%20Players%2C%20Agents%2C%20and%20the%20Intelligence%20System.md)).

What each Player carries is small and complete:

- the Story, and this Book;
- the Cell's weekly practice, which any kitchen table can run;
- a place in their Cell and in its commons;
- the people who named them and carried them in, who stay beside them as living sponsors;
- an agent, if they choose one. It is always optional.

### Cells

A [Cell](../the-field/Cell.md) is the smallest complete unit of the body, and it carries the whole. It starts as a [Circle](../the-field/Circle.md) of three to thirteen people, optimally four to seven. It becomes a Cell when it takes up the whole code. It has:

- written a Field of Agreements;
- begun to keep itself going;
- chosen a [Quest](../the-field/Quest.md);
- begun to share its stories;
- begun to pass [the Flame](../the-field/The%20Flame.md).

What a Cell needs to run is in [04 Cells](../the-great-game/04%20Cells.md), and the weekly [Turn](../the-field/The%20Turn.md) is in [30 The Strategy and Plan](28%20The%20Strategy%20and%20Plan.md).

**Why this size.** Small enough for trust and closeness. Large enough to hold real differences and real capability. It is the size of the group that has sat around every fire humanity has ever lit. Past thirteen the fire thins, so a Cell that grows past thirteen divides the way a living cell does, and both halves go on.

**What belongs to a Cell alone.** Its commons, its agreements, and its legal form, if it has one. Most Cells need no legal form for a long time ([09 Pillar II](09%20Pillar%20II%20%E2%80%94%20Sacred%20Governance%20and%20Stewardship.md) sets out the tiers). Each season a Cell chooses a Quest from the work of the [twelve Pillars](28%20The%20Strategy%20and%20Plan.md). It keeps the rhythm of the turnings, and it may keep an agent of its own if it wants one.

**Every Cell's standing Quest, from its first day, is to seed a new Cell.** A Cell grows up by the Cells it helps bring into being, not by its own size. Every Cell holds the whole pattern, so it needs no centre in order to live, and nothing that happens to any centre can take away what each Cell carries.

**How many.** At the first three percent, two hundred and fifty million people, the body would hold something like fifty million Cells, at about five people each. That figure comes from the arithmetic. It is not a measurement ([24 The Plan](29%20The%20Plan%20%E2%80%94%20the%20first%20three%20percent.md)).

### Home Cells and the Carrier

Every Cell has a [Home Cell](../the-field/Home%20Cell.md). This is the nearest body outside the Cell. It hears the Cell's season, keeps a copy of its sheets, and comes when something is wrong. A new Cell's home is the Cell of the person who brought it the Flame, its [Carrier](../the-field/Carrier.md). The Carrier never changes. The home can. A Cell may move to another home with that body's consent, and the move is written down with its date, so the address changes and the history stays. (*Home* on its own means something different: your own place inside your Home Cell. See [Home](../the-field/Home.md).)

A home gives no orders and owns nothing of the Cell's. It receives only what the Cell chooses to send. What it has is a relationship and one evening each season.

At each turning, a home does this:

1. **It receives one sheet from each of its Cells**, by hand where that is possible. Handing the sheet over is half of what a home is for.
2. **It reads the sheets in a set order:** the one-word line on how each Cell is, then the stories, then offers laid beside requests, then the proposals gathered onto one page, then the numbers.
3. **It notices, kindly.** Two Cells might be questing for the same corner lot. A Cell might have passed no Flame in two seasons. Noticing is never a verdict. When a sheet says *grieving* or *thin*, the home goes to that Cell.
4. **It fills in the same lines about itself** and sends them on to its own home ([The Way Up](../the-field/The%20Way%20Up.md)).
5. **It sends one page back down** to each Cell: how the body is, the best stories from elsewhere, and a sentence on where this Cell's Quest sits in the larger work ([The Return](../the-field/The%20Return.md)).

That is the whole of how the body holds together. A Cell knows its home and a home knows its Cells, all the way up and all the way down.

### Supercells

Cells come to know one another by place or by a shared Quest. When three or more of them have become one another's home, they make a [supercell](../the-field/Supercell.md). A supercell has no office and no officer, and its only resources are what its Cells choose to send inward. Every Cell in it stays sovereign and may leave ([The Way Out](../the-field/The%20Way%20Out.md)).

The same step repeats with no limit. Supercells become one another's home, and inside a Cell, its subcells do the same. So one card, one evening and one page can carry a neighbourhood, a town or a planet without a new institution anywhere along the way ([Cell of Cells](../the-field/Cell%20of%20Cells.md)).

Here is how that adds up:

- A thousand Cells make about a hundred supercells.
- A hundred supercells make about ten.
- Ten make one.

That is four evenings, each reading about a dozen pages. In one turning, ten thousand people can know how they are, in any language, because line six means the same thing on every sheet ([Minimum standards of interoperability](../the-field/Minimum%20standards%20of%20interoperability.md)).

What moves **inward and up** is the Season Sheet, a share of [the Offering](../the-field/The%20Offering.md), and proposals. What moves **down** is help, guidance, and the plan of the whole. A Cell that joins a supercell finds its own table just as it was, and that is the test of the design. The federation is at its healthiest when a supercell proposes something a Cell does not want, the Cell says no, and it is still a member ([09 Pillar II](09%20Pillar%20II%20%E2%80%94%20Sacred%20Governance%20and%20Stewardship.md), *How a Home Cell and a supercell relate*).

Constituting a supercell takes nothing more than the evening itself, held three times running. Cells that have come to know one another gather, bring their sheets, and name aloud that they have become one another's home; the third time they do it, they are one. **Who holds the hour between them** is never an office and never a person: two Cells host each turning, chosen in turn from among those present, and their whole charge is logistics — the room, the time, the order of reading. The pair changes at the next turning and carries nothing forward as standing.

The joint sheet is read exactly as a home reads its Cells, at whatever size now holds it: the one-word line first, so the room knows how its Cells *are* before it knows a number; then the stories; then offers laid beside requests; then every proposal gathered onto one page; then the sums — commitments kept, flames passed, the Offering gathered. The supercell then fills the same fourteen lines about itself, Cells counted instead of people, signs with two hands from two Cells, and sends it on to its own home. What comes back down is the same page any home returns — nothing more.

A supercell may ask its Cells for exactly what travels upward and nothing else: the sheet, a share of the Offering, a proposal a Cell wants decided at the larger size. It may never ask a Cell to meet without its own table present, to send what it did not offer, or to reshape its table for the supercell's convenience. The day it does, the answer is the ordinary one: a Cell says no and stays a member, or says no and leaves. Leaving a supercell is exactly leaving a Cell — said at the table, written *left, in order* with its date on the sheet, nothing erased, the Offering already given never asked back. A Cell loses nothing by leaving, because the supercell holds no authority that did not come from its Cells in the first place, which is precisely why nothing is lost when one is cut off.

### Neighbourhoods, communities, and the three hundred

Places nest by the same law as Cells. The ladder of scale runs outward from the person, and each rung is roughly ten times the one before ([Levels Of Scale](../the-field/Levels%20Of%20Scale.md)):

| rung | about how many people |
|---|---|
| the person | one |
| the Circle | three to thirteen |
| the [Neighbourhood](../the-field/The%20Neighborhood.md) | fifteen to forty |
| the Village | fifty to a hundred and fifty |
| the Microlocality | about a thousand |
| the [Community](../the-field/Community.md) | about ten thousand |
| the Mediolocality | about a hundred thousand |
| the Macrolocality | about a million |

Beyond those come the Ecoregion, the Bioregion, the Georegion and the Planet, and on outward to ØNE.

At some rungs the way a body holds together changes, not only its size:

- **Around thirteen**, a close Circle has to divide.
- **Around a hundred and fifty**, everyone can no longer know everyone, and some structure becomes necessary.
- **Around ten thousand**, a place is held steady by its three percent.

The ladder is a tool to use, never to worship. If the wise and right thing in a given place and season goes against it, do the wise and right thing.

**The neighbourhood** is not chosen. You are in it because you live there, and that is exactly its strength. At this size:

- help between neighbours is immediate;
- shared ground is cared for by the people who use it;
- the first people to arrive in trouble are the ones who live nearest;
- a child grows up with fifteen to forty adults who know their name.

A Cell is chosen for a purpose and may be spread across a city. A neighbourhood is simply there, and it holds everyone in it, including all the people who have never heard of the Game. That is why a Cell's Quest usually lands in a neighbourhood first. It is also where a life made a little more like Heaven first becomes visible.

**The community** is about ten thousand people, held steady by its three hundred. It is the smallest whole that can carry everything a civilization does: teach its young, heal its sick, care for its watershed and feed itself. In the Game, a community is what a web of Cells becomes once enough of them are one another's home. The sheets meet, the supercells form, and the turning of the season becomes a gathering rather than a meeting. The three hundred are roughly fifty Cells. In practice a community of ten thousand is several hundred neighbourhoods, each holding a few Cells.

A community body is founded in this way:

- **A steering group of four to seven starts it.** The group knows it is scaffolding, and it sets down the date it will hand back its founding authority, beside its first step.
- **Its charter has nine sections.**
- **Every member has one voice.**
- **Three more seats are held in trust by named members:** one for the living systems of the place, one for the generations to come, and one for the Whole ([Seats at the Table](../the-field/Seats%20at%20the%20Table.md)).
- **Every member holds two stakes from the first day:** a stake and a voice in the Whole, as well as in the local body ([The Two-Stake Principle](../the-field/The%20Two-Stake%20Principle.md)).

The charter, the founding steps and the seats are set out in full in [09 Pillar II](09%20Pillar%20II%20%E2%80%94%20Sacred%20Governance%20and%20Stewardship.md).

**Communities join with one another.** They form communities of communities, then bioregions read by their watersheds, then georegions, then the planet. No level swallows the one below it. Each body keeps what it was born with, and the whole holds together because the pattern is shared ([Subsidiarity](../the-field/Subsidiarity.md), [Total Nested Holarchy Of Sovereignties](../the-field/Total%20Nested%20Holarchy%20Of%20Sovereignties.md)).

### Bodies that cross places: guilds and Guide Cells

Place cannot carry everything. Four kinds of body cut across the ladder of places, and they join with one another across places and generations:

- the [guild](../the-field/Guild.md);
- the [tribe](../the-field/Tribe.md);
- the species;
- the [voluntary association](../the-field/Voluntary%20Association.md).

Each one nests by the same law as a body of place.

**A guild** gathers the people who hold a craft: builders, healers, growers, instrument-makers, the keepers of a tradition's music. A community of ten thousand never has enough people in any one craft to move that craft forward. A guild spread across twenty communities does. A guild keeps its craft's standard, its apprenticeship and its [commons](../the-field/Commons.md), so that mastery passes through a whole people instead of being reinvented in every town. A guild can fail in only one way: when its standard becomes a gate kept for the benefit of those already inside. The guards against that are rotation, openness, and a door that opens both ways.

**The healers' guild** gathers the healing crafts by the same law — medicine, nursing, midwifery, herbalism, bodywork, mental health, dentistry, rehabilitation, recovery, hospice, the movement and breath disciplines, and whatever traditional practice a place actually holds. Beyond the standard and the apprenticeship every guild owes its own, it holds three things a healing craft specifically needs: a stated boundary of what it does not do, a commons of solved problems, and the care of its own practitioners, who carry a weight most crafts do not. At its smallest, the guild is a list — names, what each person can actually do, who witnessed them doing it when it counted, how to reach them, and what they will not take on — read aloud whenever someone new joins. Where a network reaches, the same list lives as a shared directory a Cell can search by need and by place, cross-checked against the same witnessed marks; where it does not, the list is a page in the box, copied by hand whenever it changes, and it is no less the guild for being paper. If everything else fails, the list is the guild. Its members hold it and nobody else; roles rotate, because a role held forever quietly becomes a throne; and its standing with a community is the standing of a craft freely trusted, revocable by that community and by each person in it, never a licence granted from above. It fails in one direction only — its standard becoming a gate held for those already inside, the licensure cartel arriving again in new language — and the guard is built into the guild's own accounting: it keeps, openly, a record of who it turned away and why, reviewed at the same rhythm as its charter. Its guidebook, its apprenticeship, and the mark of a belt are carried in full in [12 Pillar V](12%20Pillar%20V%20%E2%80%94%20Sacred%20Health%20and%20Wellness.md).

**The artists' guild** holds the making crafts: a standard written by the people who do the work, apprenticeship at the elbow, a review a maker asks for and is never ambushed with, a commons of methods, and a commons of works held share-alike under [CC BY-SA 4.0](../the-field/CC%20BY-SA%204.0.md). It gates craft, never making — every person is an artist of the real, and the guild has no authority over who may sing, paint, carve, or tell, only over what its own members hold in common and teach one another. At its smallest, the guild is five lines on the back of a thing made — made by, made where and when, grown from, made for, shared under — a minute's work that hands the next maker a map back to everything that taught them. Where a network reaches, those five lines travel with the work into a shared commons anyone may copy, adapt, and build on, provided they carry the same five lines forward; where it does not, the commons is a folder of works in somebody's house, and a song carried in memory needs no medium at all. Its members hold the standard and revise it themselves; nobody outside the guild edits it. What would show it wrong: a payer beginning to shape what is made, the oldest failure a maker's guild guards against; or the guild's own judgment of what is worth reviewing hardening into a committee deciding what may be said, the same failure wearing the guild's own clothes. Its standard, its apprenticeship, and the full account of how a work is held are carried in [14 Pillar VII](14%20Pillar%20VII%20%E2%80%94%20Sacred%20Culture%2C%20Art%2C%20and%20Story.md).

**The guild of place** builds, mends, and tends the made world. It keeps the pattern cards of place, receives the reports of what was actually built from them, and holds what a single locality cannot: the heavier tools, the rarer skills, and the memory of what was tried on this ground before. Without it, every community rediscovers the same wrong roof detail and the same corner nobody can hear in, and the [Kit of Parts](../the-field/Kit%20of%20Parts.md) never receives a correction — it hardens into a monument to whoever wrote it first rather than a living standard that improves with every place that uses it. At its smallest, the guild is the walk: before anything is designed for a piece of ground, walk it at three hours of the day and once in bad weather, and write down what the land already does — where the water goes, where the wind comes from, where people already cut the corner. From the walk comes one card per room actually built, in three parts: the pattern it answers, its smallest workable form, and its scale ladder from a single room to a whole hall. Where a network reaches, instantiation reports from every place feed back into one kept version of the Kit, so a correction found in one town reaches the next before it needs it; where it does not, the card is copied by hand and carried table to table, which is how it always travelled and travels still. It is held by whoever builds, together with whoever already lives there, and it forms as those people find one another across places rather than by any founding. What would show it wrong: reports arriving and the Kit never changing, which means nobody is listening; or the Kit changing with no reports behind it, which means somebody is designing from a desk. Its fuller form lives in [16 Pillar IX](16%20Pillar%20IX%20%E2%80%94%20Sacred%20Architecture%20and%20Design.md).

Every guild's agreements, whichever craft it holds, cover the same nine fields, reviewed at least once a year and not perfunctorily: what it vows, as this guild holds it; how the work itself is done; how it trains, and what gates each threshold; who it lets in, and who screens them; what a member leaving is given back and what the guild keeps; how it stands with the other guilds; how it stands with the sovereigns it serves — the Cells, the communities, the councils that call on it; the lineage it carries; and how it catches a member's drift, and its own. What a guild needs to found itself is less than it seems: some people who hold a craft, an agreement on these nine fields written on one page, and a door that opens as freely outward as inward. The full nine fields, and the guilds named by craft, are carried in [18 Pillar XI](18%20Pillar%20XI%20%E2%80%94%20Sacred%20Society%20and%20Justice.md).

What the roster does not need is a map settled in advance. The archetypal guilds and the trade domains named elsewhere in this body's account of itself do not divide evenly by the twelve Pillars, and forcing a fixed table before any guild has actually formed produces turf, not clarity. The working rule instead: a guild's field is the work in front of it. When a request could reasonably be answered by more than one guild, both may come; the table holding the request decides which craft the work actually needs, and writes that decision, with its reason, into the record the request already carries — settled by the work at hand, never argued as a boundary beforehand. A guild that finds itself answering the same kind of request again and again has, in practice, taken that ground, and says so plainly. A guild that stops being asked has learned something about itself, exactly as a Guide Cell does. The map draws itself from use, and it stays truer for having been drawn that way. Never a fixed map: like everything in this body, the roster is built to evolve.

**A Guide Cell** is a Cell first. It has three to thirteen people, carries the whole code, pursues a Quest of its own, and passes the Flame by raising the next people who will know what it knows. What makes it a Guide Cell is what it has become for the body. It holds the [current best understanding](../the-field/Current%20Best%20Understanding.md) of one field, and it answers questions in that field. Its people might be healers, builders, or those who have done this before ([Guide Cells](../the-field/Guide%20Cells.md), [Cell Types](../the-field/Cell%20Types.md)).

Three bodies are easy to confuse:

- a guild shares practice among many groups doing the same work;
- a Guide Cell answers the whole body in one field;
- a [Wisdom Council](../the-field/Wisdom%20Council.md) walks beside one sovereign body's governance.

A Cell may offer itself as a Guide Cell for a field. It becomes one when the questions it answers show that it is one. Its only standing is the quality of its guidance, checked against what happened, in a record anyone can read. A Guide Cell that stops being asked has learned something about itself.

### The ladder of guidance, and the RFG Cell

Knowledge is spread unevenly, and it always will be. There are two honest ways to live with that. One is to put authority where the knowledge sits, which builds a hierarchy. The other is to make the knowledge easy to ask for, which builds a commons. This body chooses the second, one question at a time.

Guidance climbs a ladder, one rung at a time ([Asking for Guidance — the Ladder and the Request For Guidance](../getting-started/Asking%20for%20Guidance%20%E2%80%94%20the%20Ladder%20and%20the%20Request%20For%20Guidance.md)):

1. **A person asks their own agent first.** Most questions end here.
2. **What the agent cannot answer goes to the person's Cells**, the few people who know them and their work.
3. **What a Cell cannot answer, the Cell sends to the wider body as a [Request For Guidance](../the-field/Request%20For%20Guidance.md).** The Cell asks together, as a Cell.
4. **The RFG Cell receives the Request.** It is the first of the body's Guide Cells. It brings in the Guide Cells whose fields the Request touches. In time there will be many Guide Cells, working together.

A Request names four things:

- the question;
- what the Cell already knows;
- what the answer will decide;
- when the answer is needed.

The answer carries three:

- the guidance;
- how confident the guide is;
- the reasoning.

Both are kept in the open record. The next person with the same question finds the answer already there, so a question answered once, anywhere, is answered everywhere.

Two rules keep this safe, and they are all the governance it needs. **A Request binds no one to answer, and guidance binds no one to follow it.** The Cell that asked still decides. Take away either rule and asking for guidance turns into asking for permission.

Guidance also flows the other way: the whole body may ask the called-out assembly for its guidance.

Raising an *issue* is a separate motion. An issue is something that blocks the work. It gets an owner and a date. It climbs [the Escalation Ladder](../the-field/The%20Escalation%20Ladder.md) to a stewardship body only when the body whose work it is cannot resolve it. It climbs rarely, and it carries an issue, never a person.

Beside both ladders is the one move that goes straight to the front: anyone may say [stop](../the-field/Stop.md), and the stop takes effect at once.

### Agents

A Player may have agents that prepare, draft, remember and carry for them. The person decides, commits, signs and answers. The agents of the whole body, together with the library, the Board, the Roll and the Record, are a nervous system for the body, and it has no brain ([25 Players, Agents, and the Intelligence System](25%20Players%2C%20Agents%2C%20and%20the%20Intelligence%20System.md)).

In this chapter, agents do three things:

- **They carry the plan.** They re-pull it, compute the float, and draft the sheet for a person to check.
- **They keep the Record.** That means one line per commitment, and every defect on the day it happens.
- **They climb the ladder of guidance for their person.** At each rung they draft what is needed, and they send nothing without the Cell's word ([24 The Plan](29%20The%20Plan%20%E2%80%94%20the%20first%20three%20percent.md), *How an agent guides the plan*).

### The Record

[The Record](../the-field/The%20Record.md) is how the body knows itself. It is one discipline at two sizes: a Cell's weekly record of its word, and the whole body's reading at each turning. Part VI of this chapter is given to it.

### The boundary of the whole

Only three things cross the boundary between bodies: [requests, offers](../the-field/Requests%20and%20offers.md) and commitments. A task never crosses, because in the Game nobody assigns work to another table.

This is how the body shares out its work with no centre. Nobody hands anything out. A request is simply there, and anyone who can answer it may. An offer waits in the open until someone needs it. Every yes stays a free yes, given by a sovereign table.

The same boundary guards what the body knows about itself. The web of names that grows from the Calling ([03 The Calling](03%20The%20Calling.md)) is kept in three layers:

- **The public layer:** counts, Cells, Quests and stories.
- **The shared layer:** what Cells share with one another, by consent.
- **The private layer:** the web of people itself, kept locally by the people it names, who can export it or delete it.

The more dangerous a place is, the less it shows. Under every digital layer runs a paper contact-tree: each person knows their three, and their three know them. Each person, Cell and place chooses for itself what it shares, and with which levels of the whole.

## III. A self-governing, self-sustaining, self-replicating team of teams

### Three capacities

The first three percent organize themselves into teams, and each team has three capacities:

- **It governs itself.** Each Cell decides its own work by consent, as close to the work as the decision can be made well, under agreements it wrote itself ([09 Pillar II](09%20Pillar%20II%20%E2%80%94%20Sacred%20Governance%20and%20Stewardship.md)).
- **It sustains itself.** Time, energy, care and resources move among the people who have agreed to walk together. Each Cell puts something real in the middle of its own table, and what gathers there is its commons ([27 Resourcing and the New Economy](27%20Resourcing%20and%20the%20New%20Economy.md), [Voluntary Tithing](../the-field/Voluntary%20Tithing.md)).
- **It replicates itself.** Each Player passes the Flame, and each Cell plants the next ([Self-Sustenance and Self-Replication](../the-field/Self-Sustenance%20and%20Self-Replication.md), [Pass The Flame](../the-field/Pass%20The%20Flame.md)).

A group with all three can be left on its own and still be there next season, doing real work, with a younger Cell beside it. That is what it means to be a living part.

### One team of teams

The teams **stack up and align** into one team of teams, and no one assigns the alignment. Each Cell chooses its Quest against two things: the plan of the whole, and the Vision of its own place. The Quests add up to [throughput](../the-field/Throughput.md) of the Goal, which is what [value](../the-field/Value.md) means here ([Stacking Up And Aligning](../the-field/Stacking%20Up%20And%20Aligning.md), [Design To The Goal](../the-field/Design%20To%20The%20Goal.md)). At the first three percent, that is something like fifty million teams working as one body.

Seven principles keep the team of teams whole:

1. **Each decision is made as close to the work as it can be made well.** Moving a decision upward is what has to be justified ([Subsidiarity](../the-field/Subsidiarity.md)).
2. **Every team is equipped.** The body's shared work is to put the solutions, tools, resources and support in each team's hands that let it do its best work: [the kit](../the-field/The%20Kit.md), the library, the [kit of parts](../the-field/Kit%20of%20Parts.md), and the ladder of guidance.
3. **Each team keeps and cares for what its work produces.** The fruit of a Cell's Quest belongs to that Cell and to the place it served.
4. **Teams join into larger working bodies when the work calls for it,** by consent and at arm's length. Each part stays whole, and what the parts build above themselves is a tool, never a master ([Federation](../the-field/Federation.md)).
5. **The first tenth goes inward.** About a tenth of what a table gathers goes to the commons of that table, and a share of it travels inward to the table's home at each turning. The same pattern repeats one level up ([Fractal Commonsing](../the-field/Fractal%20Commonsing.md)).
6. **Everyone holds two stakes:** one in their own body and one in the larger Whole. Holding only one fails in a way anyone can predict ([The Two-Stake Principle](../the-field/The%20Two-Stake%20Principle.md)).
7. **The pattern does the coordinating.** Two Cells that have never met can work together because they share the same Goal, the same fourteen lines, and the same way of deciding.

### The centre equips; the body acts

**The centre may equip the body, but it never runs it.** Anything that needs a centre's hand to keep running is a flaw in the design.

The test is framed as a loss. Suppose every tool of the centre disappeared tonight. The body should carry on with its Book in hand, its web of trust known, and its Cells sovereign. That is the design, and it is the test for everything built.

**Roles, not persons.** No single life is part of how this body works. Roles hold its stewardship:

- keepers;
- hosts;
- carriers;
- the RFG Cell;
- the steering groups that hand back their own authority;
- the called-out assembly.

Roles rotate, because a role held forever slowly turns into a throne.

At the start, very few hands hold four things: the canon, the planning, the path of the treasury, and the tools. That is said plainly, and a trigger is set on it. When the first 144 Co-Creators stand, at about Cycle 10, they name five to ten respected representatives to begin the shared planning of the whole. From then on, stewardship of all four begins to spread into the called-out assembly. Until that is complete, every key and every duty is held by more than one person ([The de-concentration of stewardship](../the-field/The%20de-concentration%20of%20stewardship.md)).

## IV. The commons and the hubs

### One town

Picture one community of ten thousand people. Each season it works out its own priorities, its challenges, its needs and its opportunities. Its fifty or so Cells are trained, equipped and connected. They line up their Quests for the season so that together they meet the needs, solve the problems and reach the goals in front of them.

Now picture a hundred thousand such places. The whole planetary design is there to be seen in that one town.

### What every table needs, held together

Every place's Cells have the same basic needs. They need places to gather, learn, heal, make, grow, store and celebrate, and the living skill to run those places. Most of this is beyond any one Cell or family, and building it fifty times over in one town would make no sense. So each place holds the basics of a good life as a commons. Every member of the community can use it equally, in whatever form is wise and right for that place ([Commons](../the-field/Commons.md), [Fractal Commonsing](../the-field/Fractal%20Commonsing.md), [Commons-Tending](../the-field/Commons-Tending.md)).

A commons appears wherever sovereign people freely join together. That happens at every scale: a Cell, a neighbourhood, a community, a bioregion, a world. At each scale:

- the people who sit at that table make the commons, run it and care for it;
- their own gifts pay for it;
- its own agreements govern it;
- its own boundary protects it.

What it provides is concrete: energy and clean water, workshops and food forests, places to learn and to heal, and the tools of making and repair. The faster value moves within and between these commons, the faster everyone rises together.

A commons also holds knowledge: humanity's shared inheritance of wisdom, solutions and technologies. Everyone has equal access to it, except where a technology's danger calls for gates. The rules of that commons of knowledge and tools are set out in [24 The Work and the System](24%20The%20Work%20and%20the%20System.md) ([Knowledge as commons](../the-field/Knowledge%20as%20commons.md)).

### Every holon has a hub

A household gathers around its own table. A Cell gathers around its own commons, small enough to fit in a kitchen. A neighbourhood, a community, a bioregion, and in time the whole planet gather the same way, only larger. Nothing about the pattern changes as it grows, only its scale and its reach: what a household holds in a cupboard, a planet holds in a core hub ([Holon](../the-field/Holon.md), [Holofractal Scaling](../the-field/Holofractal%20Scaling.md)). This is why the tiered network below is not one design choice among several. It is the household's own table, read at every size up to the instance of the whole, and the commons and hubs described in the rest of this section are that one principle, built out.

### Islands, then hubs, then a grid

The body appears in the land in three stages, and keeping them in order keeps the work in order ([Islands, Hubs, Grid](../the-field/Islands%2C%20Hubs%2C%20Grid.md)):

- **Islands.** Coherence shows up first, in a Cell, a street, or a village that works while the land around it frays ([Islands of Coherence](../the-field/Islands%20of%20Coherence.md)).
- **Hubs.** An island becomes a hub when it is equipped to serve beyond itself, with storage, healing, repair, tools, training, and a place people can come to ([The Hub](../the-field/The%20Hub.md)).
- **A grid.** Hubs link into a planetary network with no centre, where any node can be lost and the rest stays lit ([Anti-Fragility](../the-field/Anti-Fragility.md)).

A hub built where there is no island is a building with nobody in it. A grid announced before there are hubs is only a drawing. Coherence comes first, then capacity, then connection. Each stage is useful on its own, so the path can be walked from wherever a body actually stands.

### The hub

A hub is the pattern standing in the land: somewhere a person can walk to, see, and join. Wherever possible it lives in buildings that already stand: libraries, schools, community halls, monasteries, barns, empty shops. Anything new is built with care for its place ([16 Pillar IX](16%20Pillar%20IX%20%E2%80%94%20Sacred%20Architecture%20and%20Design.md)).

A community's centre needs seven rooms, and each room is a capacity ([The Hub's Seven Spaces](../the-field/The%20Hub%27s%20Seven%20Spaces.md)):

- **a hall** big enough for the whole body to gather;
- **a council room**, kept apart from the hall, because deciding and practising are different acts;
- **healing rooms**;
- **an archive**, which holds the body's record and its lineage;
- **a shrine**, the room of stillness;
- **living quarters**, for people in intensive seasons and for visitors;
- **a kitchen and commons**, where the body eats together and the wider community joins it.

A centre with no archive forgets itself within a generation. A centre with no kitchen is a facility, not a home. **A community with a working centre has a body; a community without one has members.** Most of it starts small. The first version of any of these rooms is a corner, a shelf or a table, and someone who looks after it.

Resilience is built in from the start. Every place works toward meeting its own core needs for as long as necessary, while staying connected to everywhere else. Two plans carry this:

- [the Joseph Plan](../the-field/The%20Joseph%20Plan.md): storing against lean years and building up what a place can do for itself;
- [the Noah Plan](../the-field/The%20Noah%20Plan.md): building the vessel that carries what must survive the storms.

In a hard season, a place's Cells, its hubs and its paper contact-tree become the backbone that feeds people, keeps the peace and shows the way forward ([21 In Every Future](21%20In%20Every%20Future%20%E2%80%94%20what%20to%20do%20in%20each%20case.md)).

### The network of hubs

When the same pattern is built out at every scale, the hubs form a linked network that nests like everything else in the body, from the smallest room to the planetary whole. Every hub carries the same pattern, shaped to its scale and its place, and each is sovereign at its own scale and connected outward by consent. A One Room lives inside every hub, sized to what that hub coordinates: a neighbourhood's is a shelf and a table, and a georegional hub's holds the plan for its whole georegion. Each nests inside the next, so a person can read the state of their own scale without waiting on any other (§V, *The One Room*).

The cascade runs, tier by tier, each figure a design estimate made before the first hub of that tier stood, not a measurement:

- **Micro hubs**, inside neighbourhoods and villages — roughly two million of them.
- **Community hubs**, at the scale of ten thousand, where the three hundred work — roughly a million of them.
- **Local hubs**, anchoring localities of about a hundred thousand people — roughly a hundred thousand of them.
- **City hubs**, at the scale of a million — roughly a thousand of them.
- **Ecoregional hubs**, one to a handful of ecoregions — roughly seven hundred fifty to a thousand of them.
- **Bioregional hubs**, read by their watersheds — a hundred and fifty to two hundred of them.
- **Georegional hubs**, which hold the heaviest shared capabilities: advanced manufacturing, large-scale energy, and works that reach beyond a single bioregion — twelve to twenty-four of them.
- **One planetary core hub**, the whole read as one.

The tiers overlay rather than partition the people they serve. Added straight up, the community and local tiers alone would each reach billions, because each tier describes a layer of shared capacity rather than an exclusive slice of the map, and a person's neighbourhood, locality and city are all real at once ([16 Pillar IX](16%20Pillar%20IX%20%E2%80%94%20Sacred%20Architecture%20and%20Design.md)).

Together the tiers would serve every one of the twelve Pillars for every family and community on Earth. **Retrofits come first, everywhere they can.** Libraries, schools, halls, monasteries, empty shops and other buildings that already stand are the first form of nearly every hub; new construction follows only where no existing fabric can be adapted, and only as far as the resources pledged at that stage reach. What the whole network costs, and how it is resourced stage by stage, is in [27 Resourcing and the New Economy](27%20Resourcing%20and%20the%20New%20Economy.md).

The reasoning behind the size is simple. Suppose one working, joined-up system that serves every part of a society can serve about a hundred thousand people. That is the lesson drawn from [Mondragon](../the-field/Mondragon.md). Then serving all of humanity means building about a hundred thousand such systems. Each one connects its people to meaningful work, to meaningful relationships, and to a web of services, support and safety nets. The network links them as a forest links its trees underground, so that help can flow across the planet in answer to each body's offers and requests.

How the network is paid for depends on the stage. In the seed stage, the body's own people carry everything. The great harvest that could build the whole network becomes possible only once the body's presence is undeniable: the turning, by consent, of what humanity now spends on the tools of war toward life. No stage's plan relies on money from a later stage ([27 Resourcing and the New Economy](27%20Resourcing%20and%20the%20New%20Economy.md), [30 The Bets and the Stages](30%20The%20Bets%20and%20the%20Stages.md)).

### A hub comes when a place asks for it

A hub follows an invitation. A place asks for its hub itself, as part of organizing itself and pursuing its own Quests. The request travels as a proposal and is met from what the body has learned together. Nobody sends a hub down from above.

Buildings that already stand lead the way:

- **A library becomes the community's knowledge hub**, a place where people are matched with the solutions and ideas they need.
- **A school becomes a hub of lifelong, project-based learning** for every age.

Hubs are designed to meet or beat the best building codes in force, so a place can get its permits quickly. The shared standards and codes behind that are in [24 The Work and the System](24%20The%20Work%20and%20the%20System.md).

### The whole body learns together

Above the commons of each place stands the commons of the whole. It works in four steps:

1. It gathers the best wisdom, solutions, technologies, templates and patterns from every hub and every place.
2. It brings the best of them together into [the Prototype](../the-field/Prototype.md).
3. It offers all of it back as a [kit of parts](../the-field/Kit%20of%20Parts.md), in pieces that work together.
4. Any place that takes up a part and adapts it joins the whole network's learning.

Every new place begins where all the earlier ones arrived, so no community ever starts from nothing. The cycle runs: solve it, write it down, send it up, choose the best, bring it down, fit it to the place, and report back ([Polycentric Implementation of Shared Solutions](../the-field/Polycentric%20Implementation%20of%20Shared%20Solutions.md)).

A solution moving from one place to another is first set in the place and time it came from, then adapted to the land and culture it is going to. Ancestral and indigenous solutions are honoured, tested with the best of modern science, and given their proper setting.

Sovereignty holds all the way down. Each person, Cell and place chooses for itself what it shares, and with which levels of the whole.

### How the work gathers around the hubs

The hub network is where the work of the twelve Pillars reaches each place. Some core functions of a society are ones that failing institutions can no longer carry: water, shelter, health, learning, safety and care for the land, among others. For each of them, the called-out form ventures to carry the function ([Ecclesia Led Joint Ventures and Soft Franchises](../the-field/Ecclesia%20Led%20Joint%20Ventures%20and%20Soft%20Franchises.md)).

Each venture keeps raising the quality of its function and lowering its cost, place by place. It works through the hubs:

- A venture's best solution **travels with the training, coaching and mentoring needed to run it**. The people of each place take it up, adapt it and run it themselves.
- **Matchmakers**, human and machine, connect each Cell with the right tool at the moment it is needed ([Just in time](../the-field/Just%20in%20time.md)).
- **Peer coaching and mentoring** is open to every person on the Quest.

Who carries each Pillar, and how the ventures and guilds divide the work, is in [24 The Work and the System](24%20The%20Work%20and%20the%20System.md).

### Offers and requests at every scale

Local, bioregional and planetary exchange all run the same way, through [requests and offers](../the-field/Requests%20and%20offers.md) ([24 The Work and the System](24%20The%20Work%20and%20the%20System.md)). In the body's anatomy, each travels a set path:

1. It is said aloud at the table.
2. It is written on line 10 or 11 of the Season Sheet.
3. It goes through [the weekly door](../the-field/The%20weekly%20door.md) to [the Board](../the-field/The%20Board.md).
4. It is carried inward with the sheets. A request a Cell cannot answer goes to its Home Cell, and whatever one level cannot answer goes up with the sheet.

A request left visible and unanswered is honest. A request quietly dropped teaches a body not to ask.

## V. Planning at every scale

### Made from within, woven as one

The vision, values, strategy and plans of any place have to grow **from inside** it. No centre can plan a neighbourhood it has never walked. Yet in an age of crises that feed one another, every place's plans also have to fit together. If the old order comes apart, partly or completely, the plans of every family, neighbourhood, community and bioregion should still hold as one cloth.

So there is no master plan that the rest of the body carries out. There is one *shape* of plan. The people of each scale write it for their own scale, and the plans are woven together, never overruled ([The plan of the whole](../the-field/The%20plan%20of%20the%20whole.md), [Total Nested Holarchy Of Sovereignties](../the-field/Total%20Nested%20Holarchy%20Of%20Sovereignties.md)).

### What we answer for

Planning starts by taking back responsibility for what each of us has been given to look after, from the smallest to the largest ([Domain](../the-field/Domain.md)):

- the core functions of life and society;
- our selves;
- our families;
- our groups and teams;
- our organizations;
- our communities;
- our localities;
- our bioregions;
- our tribes;
- our nations;
- our world;
- and, in time, our long journey beyond this life and this star.

Each of these can be planned for, and the people inside each one are the ones who plan it.

### The one shape, lived at each scale

At every scale the shape has the same seven parts:

1. **The Goal**, restated in that place's own words.
2. **The markers.** These are dates the sky sets.
3. **The [milestones](../the-field/Milestone.md).** These are dates the work sets, and each one belongs to someone who has given their word for it.
4. **The pull**, planning backward from the furthest milestone to this week ([Pull planning](../the-field/Pull%20planning.md)).
5. **The [critical path](../the-field/Critical%20Path.md)**, which has no slack on it, with the [float](../the-field/Float.md) shown everywhere else.
6. **The leading indicators.**
7. **The contingency**, shown as it is used up.

The five pages that fit a plan at any scale, and the weekly and seasonal re-pull, are in [24 The Plan](29%20The%20Plan%20%E2%80%94%20the%20first%20three%20percent.md). The five pages are Intention, Vision, Values, Strategy and Timeline. Here is how the shape is lived at each scale:

- **A person** writes it for a life. The lower half of every [Playcard](../the-field/The%20Playcard.md) belongs to the person who holds it. Beneath it runs the daily practice ([06 The Daily Way](06%20The%20Daily%20Way%20%E2%80%94%20the%20baseline%20practice.md)), because rhythm, not intensity, is what forms a person.
- **A Cell** writes it for a season, on the [Questcard](../the-field/The%20Questcard.md) and the Playcard. Its plan is its Quest, and its critical path runs through the weekly Turn.
- **A Home Cell** holds the plans of the Cells it hears, and **a supercell** holds the plans of Cells that have become one another's home. The weaving first becomes visible here. It is where someone notices two Cells questing for the same corner lot and says so kindly.
- **A neighbourhood** plans for the ground that everyone in it shares, whether they play or not.
- **A community of ten thousand** writes its own vision, values, strategy, plans and Quests. Its three hundred hold it steady, and seats at its table are kept for the living world, for the unborn and for the Whole.
- **A bioregion** plans by its watershed, not by anyone's map. It counts the health of the river beside the health of the people, in one reading.
- **The whole** keeps the plan of the whole. That plan is the upper half of every Playcard, the same on every card in the Game. It changes only by a [Proposal](../the-field/Proposal.md) at a turning.

Local plans become the local Quests that move the whole.

### Seasonal Quests hold the body together

Every person and every team takes on conversations and Quests that make shared goals and values real. At each turning of the sun, every Quest is measured and adjusted. That rhythm lets the whole body keep refocusing, looking back and realigning, without anyone calling a meeting of everyone ([Season](../the-field/Season.md), [The LIØNSBERG Calendar](../the-field/The%20LI%C3%98NSBERG%20Calendar.md)).

A Quest is one real thing the Cell will do this season and can point to afterward. It is real, it is near, and it is the Cell's own ([05 Quests](../the-great-game/05%20Quests.md)). The body's shared Quest toward the Goal is what unites it and lets it work together.

Quests are not a collection to admire. They stack up and align to produce throughput of the Goal. A Quest can be excellent and still not stack with the rest. That does not make it wrong. It is simply not on this season's path, and the body should be able to say so without insult.

### A community's own vision, values, strategy, plans and Quests

Every community and bioregion on Earth is invited to draw out its own vision, values, strategy, plans and Quests, aimed at its own problems, needs and hopes. The process has four steps:

1. **Start with the right question.** Each Cell asks every member, *how would you like the world to be?* From the answers it makes the most vivid picture it can of Heaven, right where it sits ([Vision](../the-field/Vision.md)). A community then gathers its Cells' pictures into one of its own. The place chooses its Quests against that local vision, not against anything written in a book.
2. **Build consent from the ground up.** Move with care, from people and families to neighbourhoods, then to communities, then to bioregions, and on toward the Whole. At every step the standard is [consent](../the-field/Consent.md): does anyone have a reasoned, paramount objection? The further a decision reaches, the more time the body takes to move beyond consent toward full agreement.
3. **Connect every place to the commons.** Every neighbourhood, community, bioregion and team is linked to the commons, the Prototype, and the solutions, services and support the body holds. This season that means the library, the kit, the Board and the ladder of guidance: plain text that anyone can read, carried whole on a drive.
4. **Choose the Quests.** The place divides its plan into Quests for the season. Its Cells choose them freely and align with them.

**Hold what grows from below together with a vision across scales.** In every place, agreement grows from the ground up, and it also needs leaders who can see across scales and hold them together. The balance is struck the same way everywhere. The body finds the first three percent of the wise and right leaders in that place, surfaced by the trust of their own people ([03 The Calling](03%20The%20Calling.md)). It connects them, gives them power to act, and trains and equips them to succeed. The neighbourhood may be the wisest place to begin. Leaders there can strengthen the people and families beside them, and reach up to the communities and bioregions above.

### Two motions, and neither wins

Planning runs as two motions that pull against each other. Neither is allowed to win.

**Outward, each place makes its own plan.** Every place draws out its own plan by answering the right questions: the ones that show the spirit and values of a person, a group or a place.

**Inward, the plans are woven together.** A process that spans the whole system weaves the local plans across every level into one coherent whole. That way no place plans without knowing its neighbours, and the Quests do not cancel one another out ([The System Wide Master Planning Group and Process](../the-field/The%20System%20Wide%20Master%20Planning%20Group%20and%20Process.md)).

The weaving is started by a small group, and the group does not appoint itself:

1. When the first 144 Co-Creators stand, at about Cycle 10, they name five to ten respected representatives.
2. The representatives form the first planning group and run the first shared planning.
3. That planning produces a strategy and plan for the coming season that moves the whole body's critical path forward.
4. As the next rings complete, the first [general assembly](../the-field/The%20LI%C3%98NSBERG%20General%20Assembly.md) meets, refines the strategy and ratifies it.
5. From then on, the *how* belongs to the called-out themselves.

What exists today is the trigger, the size of the group, who names it, and what it produces. Much is still to be written: how a local plan is submitted, how two places with conflicting plans are reconciled, what the group may decide, and how long its members serve. Those questions are listed at the end of this chapter.

### Each one teaches one

Every place needs facilitation as it imagines, designs and builds a better future: someone in the room who can hold the process while the people there do the thinking. Hiring does not grow facilitation. It grows the way crafts always have, in two ways ([The facilitator and guild cascade](../the-field/The%20facilitator%20and%20guild%20cascade.md)):

- **The cascade.** A first group of facilitators is trained to teach others, who teach others in turn. Each one teaches one, through the first three percent and through them to every table. The plan's figure for the first group is fifteen thousand, and that figure is a design estimate. Getting started already works this way. Each one teaches one, the friend who brought you walks beside you, and onboarding office hours are open for anyone who gets stuck ([Bringing Your Friends In](../getting-started/Bringing%20Your%20Friends%20In.md)).
- **The guilds.** Every Cell, community and bioregion needs the same range of skills to carry out the body's core functions in its own time and place. So guilds form around the twelve or so core functions. Each guild trains on the order of twelve to fifteen thousand practitioners from the first three percent, and grows and shares its craft's knowledge around the world. That count is also a design estimate.

The measure of the cascade is not how many people were trained. It is how many of those trained have gone on to train others, the same test every Cell is held to. A body that cannot grow its own facilitators has to import them, and a body that imports its facilitators has quietly imported a centre.

### Stories turn the wheel

As places take on their Quests and begin to succeed, the [stories](../the-field/Story.md) they tell keep the wheel of support and engagement turning. It goes like this:

1. A Cell plays the Game.
2. Its people are changed.
3. They tell what happened.
4. The story reaches someone who is waking up.
5. The wheel turns faster.

[Every story is a prophecy](../the-field/Every%20Story%20Is%20A%20Prophesy.md) of greater things to come. Quests also reveal needs that many Cells share. Around those needs, guilds, services and support grow up in the commons to help every Cell meet what it faces.

### The One Room

A body with no centre still needs a place where it can see the state of the whole. Without one, every part plans blind. That place is a room, not an office ([The One Room](../the-field/LI%C3%98NSBERG%20Mission%20Control.md)):

- **On the wall hangs the plan of the whole.** The Goal is at the top. The markers sit beneath it, with any lateness shown, not hidden. The work is broken down, with the twelve Pillars as the first level. This season's critical path is set out in numbered steps. [The Score](../the-field/The%20Score.md) the Game actually keeps is there too.
- **On the table lie the specifications:** the Story, the plan, the Way, and the Prototype, held as a kit of parts.
- **On the rack hangs the toolkit**, every instrument ready for any team on its first day.
- **At the desks are the doors anyone may walk through:** ask the whole for guidance · propose a change to what hangs on the wall, and never edit it quietly · log what blocks you · record a decision and why · learn from the gap.

Every Cell keeps a smaller room of the same shape at its own table.

The One Room coordinates. It does not command. It holds only what a shared function has to hold, and above all the path by which one place's best solution enters the common kit, so that every place inherits what all the others have learned.

Its most unusual feature is a registry. The registry has a line for every part of the whole and says where that part actually stands, including the parts that do not exist yet. A status board that admits its own holes is why the room can be trusted about what it says *is* finished. Like every other function, its stewardship is set to spread out from the Cycle 10 nomination onward.

### Self-governance, learned at every scale

Every place learns the new way of deciding, so that families, neighbourhoods, communities, bioregions, georegions and more can keep working together indefinitely ([09 Pillar II](09%20Pillar%20II%20%E2%80%94%20Sacred%20Governance%20and%20Stewardship.md)).

The body also meets the governments the old world already runs on: some two hundred nation-states, thousands of states and provinces within them, and millions of counties, cities and boards. It does not fight them. It cares for the old as it ends and builds the new beside it. Each place can make its own plan for rising and changing, watching the rings of growth in that place alongside its election cycles.

One reading carried forward from the body's early planning is that the deeper work of renewing a constitution comes within reach once seventy to eighty percent of a place has taken up the new pattern. This is a reading, not a measured law.

### Planning in any future

The plans are written to survive the loss of every screen:

- **The markers belong to the sun.** The turnings fall on the same days everywhere, and nobody owns them.
- **The floor is paper.** A plan at any scale fits on five handwritten pages, and a Season Sheet is one sheet.
- **The shared pattern is what makes the plans one cloth, not contact between bodies.** A body cut off by a firewall, an outage or a war keeps planning at its own scale. It rejoins the weave the day a route opens again ([21 In Every Future](21%20In%20Every%20Future%20%E2%80%94%20what%20to%20do%20in%20each%20case.md)).

## VI. The Record, and the body's daily scoreboard

### Honesty earns the trust

A plan earns trust by being honest, not by the beauty of its vision. The Record is the body's standing gauge. It says where the body actually stands against the milestones it has set. It carries no adornment and no blame, and it never quietly resets the starting line. It cannot lie, because it is only a few numbers and a sentence.

The Record is one discipline, kept at every scale under one name, at two sizes. There is the Cell's weekly record of its word, and there is the whole body's reading at each turning ([The Record](../the-field/The%20Record.md), [The Record as the Movement's variance gauge](../the-field/The%20Record%20as%20the%20Movement%27s%20variance%20gauge.md)). The whole body's reading is simply the sum of the tables, added up from the parts, with no centre doing the counting. How a room's traces become a record, step by step, is in [08 The Record](../the-great-game/08%20The%20Record.md).

### At the table

Each week the Record gains one line for each [commitment](../the-field/Commitment.md): what was said, whether it was kept, the gap, and what was learned. Beside it the Cell keeps three things:

- **[Decision records](../the-field/Decision%20Records.md).** Each says what was decided, why, what else was considered, and what would change the decision. Nothing gets argued over again by accident.
- **A log of defects.** Every miss is written down the day it happens. A line is closed only when its fix sits where future work will meet it ([Every Defect Is A Gift](../the-field/Every%20Defect%20Is%20A%20Gift.md)).
- **Lines that are only ever added.** Each line has a date and a named author, and none is ever rewritten. A correction is a new line, and both lines stay.

A [keeper](../the-field/Keeper.md) holds the Record between gatherings, and nothing in it is hidden from the table. What anyone owes is worked out from the Record, never from memory, because a body that relies on memory for what it owes forgets selectively, and in its own favour.

The [Scorecard](../the-field/The%20Circle%27s%20Scorecard.md) is the Record on paper. It is one sheet a season, with a row for each week: who came, commitments made and kept, one lesson learned, the Offering, the Flames passed, and a tick for the story. A commitment counts as kept when the person who made it says it was kept and one other person at the table agrees. That second voice is the witness, and it is what lets people who were not in the room trust a Cell's numbers.

At the turning the columns are added up, and the Cell works out [kept as a share of made](../the-field/Kept%20as%20a%20share%20of%20made.md). It is the first number any group should know about itself. Groups that work this way tend to move from finishing about half their plans to finishing eight or nine in ten within a few seasons. That figure is an estimate carried over from the production practices this way of working learned from. It is not a measurement of this body.

The numbers are not a ranking. A Cell of four that kept six of its seven commitments has done something that a Cell of twelve keeping twenty of forty has not. Neither is better. Each is simply seen as it is, and a Cell's worth lies in its story.

The Record is not a performance review either. The gap is where learning comes from. A body that punishes gaps hears about fewer of them, and so knows less about itself ([Zero Blame Environment](../the-field/Zero%20Blame%20Environment.md), [The gap](../the-field/The%20gap.md)).

### The Season Sheet: the one page that crosses between bodies

Everything inside a season belongs to the Cell. The [Season Sheet](../the-field/The%20Season%20Sheet.md) is the one thing that has to be the same everywhere, because it is the one thing that leaves the table. It is how the records of ten thousand tables become the record of a town. It has fourteen numbered lines, and the numbers are the same in every language:

1. Cell and place
2. Home Cell and Carrier
3. the season, named by the turning that opened it
4. members: who is new, and who left in order
5. Quests, and where each one stands
6. commitments made, commitments kept, and kept as a share of made
7. Flames passed, and the Cells formed from them, by name
8. the Offering: what was gathered, kept, and sent on, with its unit
9. the story, in under a hundred and fifty words
10. offers
11. requests
12. proposals
13. how the Cell is, in one word and a line
14. signed by two hosts, dated, with a count of its lines of writing

An empty line says *none*. A sheet with all fourteen lines is complete. A sheet missing a line is not yet a sheet.

The sheet is filled in at the table in the week of the turning, with everyone there. A copy stays with the Cell. The original goes to the Home Cell within a week, by hand if at all possible. Two signatures and a line count are all the security it has, and they are enough. **Nothing typed replaces the sheet. If the paper and the screen disagree, the paper with two signatures is the record.**

Line 13 is the one a home reads first — *tired, growing, grieving, glad* — because it is how the body feels itself before it counts anything. Line 12 is how the rules of the whole actually change.

### Across the whole body

At each turning the whole body writes its entry as a few numbers and a sentence: **the season · the ring it stands on · the days per ring · how far it stands from the milestones · a one-line why, and the response.**

The reading looks ahead as well as back. Beside the ring stand the living indicators, because a count of members can flatter the body while the health of its Cells cannot:

- **invitations accepted:** of those who hear the Story and feel it, how many say yes in their own words;
- **Cells formed:** of those who say yes, how many are at a table within a fortnight, with named people, a day, a place and a Quest;
- **Cells that survive their first season;**
- **the share of Players who bring their three**, and **how many days each generation takes;**
- **the marks of a living Cell:** it carries the whole code, keeps itself going, does real work, makes new Cells, and stays connected with the Cells around it ([04 Cells](../the-great-game/04%20Cells.md)).

Each season the body also steps back and looks at itself from above — inward, outward, backward and forward — in an [OmniSpection](../the-field/OmniSpection.md), done in community. Looking back alone is not enough.

### The daily scoreboard

During the Sprint, from 28 September to the Winter Solstice of 2026, the body reads its scoreboard every day instead of every season. The scoreboard has four numbers, and each one comes from records any Player can check ([LIØNSBERG Cycles of Growth and Cycle Time](../the-field/LI%C3%98NSBERG%20Cycles%20of%20Growth%20and%20Cycle%20Time.md)):

| the reading | what it is | where it comes from | what it tells us |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The ring we stand on** | the Fibonacci [Cycle of Growth](../the-field/LI%C3%98NSBERG%20Cycles%20of%20Growth%20and%20Cycle%20Time.md) the body has reached: Cycle 4 is 8 · Cycle 10 is 144 · Cycle 20 is 17,711 · Cycle 38 is 102 million, or 1%**Cycle 40 is 267,914,296, the first three percent** | the count of Players on the Roll | where we are, measured the way living things grow |
| **Days per ring** | the cycle time: how many days the last ring took | the dates on the Roll | the number we actually manage. A body that shortens it compounds. A body that lengthens it is stalling, however many people have said yes |
| **Players counted** | people who meet all five conditions (the **Qualifying Criteria**) | the Roll, read against [the chart](29%20The%20Plan%20%E2%80%94%20the%20first%20three%20percent.md) | whether today's count is where the chart says it should be |
| **Commitments kept** | the share of Players who brought their three, not already playing, within three days | the Roll: who brought whom, when, and whether the commitment was kept | the second most sensitive number in the arithmetic |

Underneath these four readings sit the numbers that build them, tracked every day: **how many invited · how they responded · how many said yes · what they pledged · the percent of commitments kept.** The yes line is the leading signal; the ring is the measure. Counted by the full Qualifying Criteria rather than by a raw headcount, each ring date lands about one generation later than a bare tally would suggest.

Behind the last two sits a closer view of days per ring: **days per generation**. That is the time from a Player's own yes to their three saying yes, and it is the most sensitive number of all. The Sprint's eighty-four days are fixed by the sky, and the length of a generation decides how many generations fit inside them.

From Cycle 4 to Cycle 40 is thirty-six rings. One ring about every two and a third days is enough to stand on Cycle 40 by the solstice. At the target of three in three days, the body moves up a ring about every one and a third days. The chart generation by generation, the sensitivity table, and the arithmetic behind them are in [24 The Plan](29%20The%20Plan%20%E2%80%94%20the%20first%20three%20percent.md) and [30 The Strategy and Plan](28%20The%20Strategy%20and%20Plan.md).

**The commitment, and how it is measured each day.** The commitment is the first three percent, two hundred and fifty million people, connected and empowered by the Winter Solstice of 2026. We do everything in our power to meet it. Each day the four numbers are read against the chart. A gap is named plainly on the day it appears. It is answered by pulling the plan again, never by moving the line. Days per generation and commitments kept are the first two places to look.

The scoreboard needs no screen. A Cell can keep its part in a notebook: who we brought, on what day, and whether they said yes. A town can add up its Cells' notebooks on the evening of the turning.

After the solstice, in the Wise Pace, the rings stay the measure. The reading goes back to the rhythm of the seasons, and the pace is set by how well people are being formed, not by the arithmetic.

### Three disciplines of the Record

1. **Say what you will do. Record what you did. Learn from the gap.** Every entry names how far the body is off and the root cause beneath it, found by asking *why* five times. This is never done to assign blame. It is always done to find what the pattern needs next.
2. **Spend contingency knowingly, never in silence.** Lateness is paid for out of a reserve set aside for a whole generation ([20 Year Generational Contingency](../the-field/20%20Year%20Generational%20Contingency.md)). [The Window](../the-field/The%20Window.md) may close at an hour no one knows, so contingency that has been spent is never treated as money safely in the bank. How wide the Window still is can only be read by constant sense-making, discernment and OmniSpection.
3. **Markers serve the work.** A marker has done its job when it makes reality visible ([Temporal markers apart from milestones](../the-field/Temporal%20markers%20apart%20from%20milestones.md), [Honesty about time](../the-field/Honesty%20about%20time.md)). Lateness is answered with ignition, never despair, and never with a quiet redrawing of the map. Lateness does not destroy a plan. What destroys it is quietly moving the end date to hide the lateness. Six days are lost, six days are added, and the arithmetic of the whole goes wrong while everyone stays comfortable.

### The longest look back

The Record also holds the longest look back: a careful inquiry, drawing on many disciplines, into how the old world failed. Every part of how we got here is examined and brought into the light. That way, what we build next rests on diagnosis, not forgetting ([02 The Moment](02%20The%20Moment.md), [Retrospective](../the-field/Retrospective.md)).

### The first rows

The whole body's Record opened before the Game did, and its first row records a gap. That is the point of it:

| season | ring | days per ring | distance from the milestones | the one-line why, and the response |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Late Summer 2026 | about 2–3 | not yet flowing | growth had not yet caught; the first three percent, two hundred and fifty million by the Winter Solstice 2026, stands as the milestone, measured daily from the equinox | an invitation to *join* gathered people who waited for someone else to act; nobody had been asked by name to do the work. Response: the Calling opens at the Fall Equinox, and every new Player brings their three |
| Formation, 23–27 September 2026 | the aim: Cycle 4, the first eight Players | — | the milestone: the first eight Players up and running, each with an agent, the Pledge taken, and a Cell | the work of the phase: the kit, the Invitation, the first trust ledger, and onboarding office hours |

From 28 September a row is written every day. A gauge that publishes its gaps is worth more than one whose entries are all triumphs.

## VII. In every future

The body was designed not to depend on the things it uses. Picture a Cell of three to thirteen people who, week by week:

- keep their word to one another;
- choose one real Quest a season;
- put something in the middle of the table;
- write the fourteen lines at each turning;
- pass the Flame within days.

That Cell is a complete organ of the body in any future. It needs no hub, no network and no agent to be whole.

A supercell cut off from the wider body loses its help, the shared learning, the mutual aid and the plan of the whole. It loses none of its authority, because that authority never came from above. The web of who knows whom is kept locally, with a paper tree beneath it. And because the Season Sheet is the same everywhere, strangers who hold the same fourteen lines can join a split body back together, whatever wall divided it ([21 In Every Future](21%20In%20Every%20Future%20%E2%80%94%20what%20to%20do%20in%20each%20case.md)).

*Read the Story. Play the Game. Pass the Flame.*

## What is not yet settled

- **How a ring is declared.** Nothing yet says what evidence moves the body from one ring to the next, or who says so. A ring's number counts Co-Creators, while the Sprint counts Players with all five marks, and how those two relate on a given day has not been stated. *Proposal:* read the ring from the Roll as the highest ring whose number has been reached by the count of Players with all five marks. The keeper of the Roll signs the reading, with a second witness.
- **The first threshold.** Earlier chapters place the first Co-Creators at 55 to 144. The current reading is 144, at Cycle 10. This chapter uses 144.
- **The planning group's process.** The trigger, the size and the output are set. Still unwritten: how a local plan is submitted, how conflicting plans from two places are reconciled, what the group may decide for the body, how long its members serve, and how they are recalled.
- **The RFG Cell.** What it does is set: it receives each Request For Guidance and brings in the Guide Cells whose fields the Request touches. Still unwritten: how it is formed, who belongs to it, how its membership turns over, where Requests are sent, and how quickly it answers. Also unwritten: what a body does when a Request goes unanswered, and what happens when two Guide Cells give conflicting guidance.
- **Who belongs to a Guide Cell, and with what legitimacy**, is still unanswered.
- **Guilds and ventures.** Both are organized around the same twelve or so core functions. It is not settled whether they are one body under two names or two bodies that must be kept apart. Neither list of functions has been spelled out. How a venture works is also unwritten: who forms it, who pays for it, what the agreement for a solution that travels contains, and how the venture is held to its purpose.
- **The hub figures.** The number of hubs and the cost of ten to twenty trillion dollars are estimates made before any hub stood. The seven spaces come from one craft's picture of a centre. Nobody has yet worked out what a centre needs when twelve crafts share it.
- **Where a Cell divides.** One sentence elsewhere has a Cell dividing *at* thirteen. The settled reading (2026-09-26) is that a Cell has three to thirteen people and divides once it grows *past* thirteen. This chapter follows the settled reading, and [30 The Strategy and Plan](28%20The%20Strategy%20and%20Plan.md) should be brought into line with it.
- **Where the Neighbourhood sits.** One reading of the ladder of scale places the Neighbourhood between the Circle and the Village. Another leaves it out. This chapter follows the first.
- **The marks of a living Cell.** Cell health was once measured by "five marks of aliveness" that were never listed. This chapter reads them as the five things [04 Cells](../the-great-game/04%20Cells.md) says a Cell does. That reading is a proposal.
- **One Record or two.** The Cell's Record and the whole body's reading share a name and a discipline. This chapter treats them as one law at two sizes. Whether the whole body's reading needs a name of its own is still open.
- **Line 2 of the Season Sheet.** The printed sheet says *Home*. The words now separate *Home*, your own place in your Cell, from *Home Cell*, the body that hears your season. This chapter prints *Home Cell*, and the printed sheet should follow.
- **The threshold for renewing a constitution.** The figure of seventy to eighty percent is a reading from the body's early planning. It has no derivation and has never been measured.
- **The first governing council.** [09 Pillar II](09%20Pillar%20II%20%E2%80%94%20Sacred%20Governance%20and%20Stewardship.md) describes a first council with a named chair. This chapter describes the body by its roles only, so it does not carry that passage. Chapter 09 is the place to say how the first council is formed, by role.
