# Pillar V — Sacred Health and Wellness

*The fifth of twelve spheres, built at the same time as the other eleven. Its work is that the body, the mind, and the life around them are returned to wholeness — so that no one is asked to build Heaven out of a broken instrument.*

**Nothing in this chapter is medical guidance.** It carries a way of holding health, a set of ordinary practices most of which are thousands of years old, and the instruments a body of people needs in order to care for its own. It does not diagnose, it does not treat, and it does not stand between anyone and the skilled help they need. Before every practice named here stands the floor of care: **when a member is in danger, in body or in mind, the Circle acts first and records later, and no card is more important than the person at the table.** Acting first includes calling whoever can actually help, now.

---

## The Pillar in one breath

The sentence is the shortest of the twelve: **healing is a return to wholeness.**

Not the silencing of a symptom. Not the slow management of a decline. A *return* — which says that wholeness was there first, that it is the natural state of a living thing, and that health is less a thing to be manufactured than a condition to be restored by removing what obstructs it and supplying what was missing.

Around that sentence the Pillar states its function: *restoring the temple of the body and the harmony of spirit, mind, and nature.* Four words in that line carry weight. **Temple**, because a body that is a machine may be repaired, regulated, and worked, while a body that is a dwelling of [[Spirit]] may not — and the difference decides everything downstream. **Harmony**, because health is a relationship among parts and not a property of any one of them. **Mind**, because the mind is not an accessory to the body and suffers in its own right. **Nature**, because a person is not sealed off from the water they drink or the soil their food grew in, and no amount of private discipline outruns a poisoned watershed.

Put plainly, for anyone meeting this for the first time: *health here means the whole of you working well together, in a place that is also working well, among people who would notice if you were not.* [[Wellness]] is that whole reading taken across every plane at once — physical, mental, emotional, relational, ecological, spiritual — and it is not the same as being free of diagnosis. A person can carry no disease and not be well. A person can carry a lasting condition and be flourishing. [[Healing]] is the movement back toward wholeness from wherever you actually are. [[Vitality]] is the felt reading of it: warmth, appetite, clarity, the willingness of a body to begin things.

### Why a civilization without this Pillar fails

Every other move in [[The Great Game]] is made by a person, and people wear out.

A body that cannot rest cannot keep a [[Commitment]] — and a word given and kept is the whole of the Game. A mind carrying unmet [[Trauma]] will hear a [[Meaningful Objection]] as an attack, and a [[Cell]] in which objection cannot be heard has lost its only correction mechanism. A [[Circle]] that burns three members to finish a [[Quest]] has traded the thing that lasts for the thing that ends in ninety days. Measure only illness, and you will build institutions that need illness. Measure only output, and you will watch [[Kept as a share of made]] hold steady for two seasons while the people behind the number quietly empty out — and then lose three of them in a month.

There is a harder version of the same claim, and it belongs here because it explains why this Pillar is not a soft one. A frightened, exhausted, dependent people is a governable people. Systems of domination do not merely find wounds; they benefit from them, and the arrangements that keep a population sleepless, medicated, divided, and afraid are not incidental to how power was held in the old ordering. That reading is carried here as the account this Book holds, not as a proven mechanism — see [[Kontrolle]], and hold it the way you hold every strong claim in these pages: loosely, and testable.

The constructive form of it is simpler and is the reason this Pillar exists. **A people who sleep, eat, breathe, rest, grieve, and heal well cannot easily be settled by fear, and they can build.** That is the whole argument.

## Current best understanding

### The twelve claims

The canon states this Pillar as twelve theses. They are carried here whole, in the order they were given, because together they are the Pillar's grammar.

1. **Life is sacred and meant to flourish** — health is the natural state of a being in harmony with the order of things.
2. **The body is a living [[Temple]]** — worthy of reverence and care, and aligned with Spirit.
3. **Healing is a return to wholeness** — not the masking of symptoms, but the restoration of balance and flow across every dimension.
4. **True health is multidimensional** — spiritual, mental, emotional, physical, relational, ecological, energetic, together.
5. **Each being is a sovereign healer** — capable of participating in their own healing rather than receiving it as a passive object.
6. **The [[Earth]] contains what is needed to heal** — and its plants, elements, and rhythms are to be respected, protected, and understood.
7. **Modern medicine is to be rehumanized, not discarded** — integrated with ancient wisdom and returned to the service of the whole.
8. **The healer is a servant, not a controller of outcomes** — a guide and a midwife of wholeness.
9. **Communities regenerate collective healing systems** — circles of care, shared knowledge, mutual support.
10. **Dis-ease reports a deviation from harmony, coherence, and flow** — and is met with understanding rather than fear.
11. **The systems that profit from sickness are transcended** — replaced by systems that cultivate health, agency, and flourishing.
12. **The goal is the regeneration of all life** — a world where health is the default and healing is sacred.

Two of these are more often misread than the rest, so they get a line each.

**Thesis 7 is not a rejection of medicine.** Surgery, antibiotics, obstetric rescue, vaccination, emergency care, the whole slow accumulation of tested knowledge about bodies — these are among the best things the old world made, and a movement that throws them away to prove a point will kill people. What is refused is not the knowledge. It is the arrangement: the severing of the practitioner from the person, the person from their own judgment, and the whole of it from anything except the bill. The claim is *rehumanize and redeem*, and it is meant literally.

**Thesis 5 is not a claim that you can heal yourself of everything.** *Sovereign healer* names a standing, not a competence: you are a participant in your own healing and not an object of it, you may ask, refuse, and choose, and nothing may be done to you without your word. It does not mean you should treat yourself, and it is not a reason to stay away from someone who knows more than you do.

### Four scales, one Pillar

The Pillar is held at four scales at once, and each scale can do what the others cannot.

**The person.** The ordinary daily practice — [[Breath]], [[Nutrition]], [[Sleeping]], [[Rest And Regeneration]], movement, stillness, and the patient work of meeting what was never metabolized. This is the ground of the Pillar and it is *not* rebuilt here: it is the subject of [[06 The Daily Way — the baseline practice]], which states the practice in full, in three permissive tiers, with a seven-minute floor beneath the smallest of them. Everything in this chapter assumes that chapter and does not repeat it.

**The Circle.** Witness, presence, the willingness to hold rather than to seal off, and a [[Zero Blame Environment]] in which the cause of a miss is sought in the set-up rather than in the person. A [[Cell]] cannot treat anyone. What it can do is notice, ask, stay, adjust, and call — and those five verbs are most of what a person in trouble actually needs and most often does not get. The specifications below make this concrete.

**The [[Community]].** Regenerated collective systems of care: circles of care, shared knowledge, mutual support, the crafts held in a [[Guild]] rather than reinvented in every town, and the places a body of people needs — a healing room, a [[Sacred spaces|sanctuary]], a garden, a burial ground.

**The world.** Clean [[Air · Water · Soil · Microbiome]], living [[Ecology]], and just [[Governance]] are health measures and not adjacent concerns. No being is fully well in a sick society. This is why this Pillar cannot be finished alone, and why [[13 Pillar VI — Sacred Ecology and Living Systems]] and this chapter are two readings of one fact.

### The additive half and the subtractive half

Most attempts at wellbeing *add*: a practice, a supplement, a regimen, a discipline. [[Detoxification]] is the other motion, and it is the one people skip. It asks what is currently poisoning the system and takes it out — the chemical intake and the water it arrives in, a diet the body was not built for, sleeplessness, a stream of information engineered to keep a nervous system alarmed, a relationship that consumes, a commitment that should never have been made.

The order matters, because addition into an obstructed system does nothing. A person who adds a morning practice on top of an unremoved poison gets a well-practised poisoning. Healing often begins with a subtraction; vitality returns when the obstruction goes, not when something clever is applied. [[Fasting]] is the clearest worked example of the subtractive move, and the canon uses the word past food — a fast from speech, from the feed, from a habit, from an opinion one is attached to, from a role one hides inside. [[Rest And Regeneration]] is a fast from production.

Two honesties come with the word. Detoxification is a practice and not an identity; a life organized around purity becomes its own disorder. And the word has been thoroughly colonized by commerce — a thing sold as a cleanse is almost always an addition wearing the name of a subtraction.

### Vitality: the earliest reading, and the two ways it goes wrong

[[Vitality]] is read directly, before any measurement, which makes it the first signal available to a person about their own condition and to a Circle about its members'. It is what [[The Turn]] is asking after when a player says how they are, and what line 13 of [[The Season Sheet]] — *how is the Circle? one word, and a line* — is reaching for. Naming it makes a decline sayable before it becomes a departure.

It goes wrong in two directions and both are common.

**Vitality is not merit.** Illness, age, disability, [[Dying]], and the honest limits of a body are not failures of it. A system that reads vitality as virtue recreates the exact cruelty it was built to answer, and it does so while quoting this Pillar. Guard against it explicitly, because it is the most likely way this material will be misused.

**Vitality can be borrowed against.** Stimulants, urgency, and a genuinely worthy [[Quest]] can each buy months of apparent aliveness at the cost of years. The reading is only useful when it is honest, and the hardest person to be honest with about it is yourself. This is one of the strongest arguments for reading it in company.

### What the emotions are for

The canon treats emotion as information rather than as weather to be endured, and it gives a practice.

**Feel it. Allow it. Read it. Name it. Ask what it is asking for. Make the move. Release it.** Seven steps, workable on any emotion at any intensity, and the release at the end is what keeps a reading from turning into a climate. Each named emotion is read the same way: what it feels like in the body · what it is signalling · its gradient, from its faintest form to its most extreme · its gift, what it gives when it is heeded · its distortion, what it becomes when it is not · and the move it is asking for. [[Grief]] read this way is not a malfunction; it is love with nowhere to go, and it asks to be carried rather than fixed. [[Mastery Of Emotions]] is the discipline, and mastery here means neither suppression nor discharge but the capacity to feel fully and still choose.

Run in a Circle, the same practice becomes something no single person can do: many instruments reading one weather, and the reading is truer than any one of them could take alone. This is a real and underused capacity of a Cell and it costs nothing but time and honesty.

### Open questions

This is the least finished of the twelve Pillars, and its seams are worth naming rather than smoothing.

- **The Pillar's own name.** The Pillars hub and the seed call it *Sacred Health and Wellness*; the Pillar page's own thesis title and the Lexicon call it *Sacred Health and Healing*. This Book uses **Wellness** for the Pillar and **Healing** for its central verb, which is what the two names are each reaching for. Not resolved in the canon.
- **[[Nutrition]] or *Eating*.** The Lexicon's term is *Nutrition*; the written practice card stands at *Eating*. One meaning, two homes. This Book uses **Nutrition** for the concept and speaks of eating for the act.
- **[[Breath]] or *The Sovereign's Breath*.** The Lexicon's term is *Breath*; the Pattern's home is *The Sovereign's Breath* with its six named breaths beneath it — the coherent, the continuous, the deep, the breath under load, the four-seven-eight, the breath of transmission. This Book uses **Breath** and treats the six as a craft rather than a technique.
- **[[Rest And Regeneration]] or *[[Sabbath]]*.** The same idea is carried under this Pillar as rest and under [[19 Pillar XII — Sacred Time, Rhythm, and Celebration]] as Sabbath. Both are kept; rest is the practice, Sabbath is the rhythm that protects it.
- **[[Regeneration]]'s two definitions.** Under this Pillar, renewal *into higher-order coherence*; under Pillar VI, restoration of *vitality, function, and evolutionary potential*. Read here as one meaning.
- **[[Integration]] in three Pillars.** Inner wholeness here, technical integration of systems under [[15 Pillar VIII — Sacred Science and Technology]], social reweaving under [[18 Pillar XI — Sacred Society and Justice]]. The same word doing three jobs, and this chapter carries the first.
- **[[Vitality]] rests on [[Energy]]**, which the canon carries in two senses — capacity to effect change, and subtle life-force. Which is meant is not settled, and this chapter leans on the felt reading rather than on either theory.
- **The energetic layer.** The canon holds an articulated account of an energetic anatomy, of clearing, of shielding, and of practices that act on those layers. It is carried in this Book as the practitioners' own account of their craft — coherent, ancient in its lineages, and without an evidentiary page behind it. It is neither endorsed as physiology nor struck. Where its practices are ordinary and harmless — stillness, clearing the day before sleep, attention to what one takes in — they need no theory to be worth doing. Where a claim is made that clearing resolves a medical symptom, the floor of care governs and the person sees someone qualified.

### What is written, and what is missing

Read honestly, this Pillar is nearly complete at the scale of one person and nearly empty at the scale of a people.

**What exists and works:** the daily practice in three tiers with a seven-minute floor · sleep held as a discipline rather than a leftover · eating held as relationship, with the practical shape of it — real food, slowly, with gratitude, stopping before fullness, inside a window, refusing what was engineered to bypass the body's own discernment · fasting in its daily, weekly, seasonal, and initiatory forms · the breaths · martial and movement cultivation · the emotion practice and its library of named bands · [[The Zone Of Optimum Development]], which keeps formation from either overwhelming or boring a person.

**What is named and not built:** the **healers' guidebook**, named repeatedly as the steward of the healing crafts while the parallel Warrior guidebook runs to twenty-two chapters · a **Circle protocol for emotion and for crisis** · any **health record** a person owns · the **rites** of birth, coming of age, elderhood, and death · [[Mental Health]], [[Addiction]], [[Dying]], and [[Giving Birth]], each named as a needed Pattern with no body written · the interpretation of dreams · the healing of trauma, which the canon explicitly hands to practitioners and traditions outside itself.

That last honesty is worth keeping rather than fixing in a hurry. Naming a gap is a form of integrity. Filling it with confident technique would not be. What this chapter does instead is build the *instruments* a community needs in order to hold the gap safely while the craft is rebuilt by people who actually hold it — which is a different and more modest job than writing the craft.

On [[Mental Health]] in particular, two things are held together and the tension between them is the honest state of the concept. Minds suffer, suffering has causes, and a person in distress needs care, company, and often skilled help that no book supplies. And: some states the old ordering files as pathology have been held by wisdom traditions as passage rather than disorder, and treating every such state as malfunction can lock a person out of what was trying to happen in them. The canon records its own uncertainty here in the entry itself. Both halves are carried. And the second may never become a reason to withhold help from someone who is drowning.

On [[Addiction]], the framing decides what a community does. Read as moral failure it produces punishment; read as pure mechanism it produces management; read as an initiatory arc, recovery becomes a passage a person walks and returns from carrying something. The rooms that demonstrated this for a century are among the lineages the [[Twelve Irreducible Elements]] were drawn from — small groups, one voice each, [[Sponsorship]], nobody in charge, the whole thing given away free. A [[Cell]] is already shaped like the thing that works. What it must not do is mistake that resemblance for competence: a Circle is not treatment, withdrawal from some substances is dangerous, and a well-meaning group that takes on a physical dependency it is not equipped for can do real harm. What the Circle reliably provides is the thing relapse most often follows the loss of — a table with your name on it, every week, where you are expected.

## What lies beyond

Set the plan down for a moment and look at the thing itself.

Picture a morning a generation from now. You wake in a dwelling built to belong to its place, warmed by the sun it faces. You drink the water and it is clean — not filtered-clean, not bottled-clean, but clean, from a watershed tended back to health, and **no one owns it**. The rain that falls here is safe to catch, and the child who cups it in her hands is not, this time, being slowly poisoned. Breakfast is food grown in living soil within walking distance of the table it feeds, and the soil under that field is deepening year over year rather than being mined toward its last harvests. The diseases that came from eating poison for profit have receded into history the way we remember plagues that vaccination ended.

The children are not warehoused. They move through a village that is their classroom, apprenticed to the baker and the builder and the healer, mentored toward the particular [[Genius]] each one was born carrying, and by the time they come of age they know how to grow food, resolve a conflict, tend a wound, and hear their own conscience above the noise. The elders — the ones the old world stored away out of sight — are at the centre of things: consulted, celebrated, and accompanied all the way to the threshold of death with honour and presence rather than abandonment.

And when someone falls ill, the village does what villages did before the severing. It gathers. Not to seal the person off, but to hold, to witness, and to remind a soul of its belonging. The healer knows the land, the person, and the unseen; she listens more than she speaks. Hospitals have become sanctuaries. Clinics have become communities. Every home is a place of care. Nobody is bankrupted by a diagnosis, because nothing in the arrangement needed anyone to be sick.

That is the Pillar at its height, and the culture in it has a particular texture worth naming.

**The practitioner is a guide, not an authority.** They hold craft the rest of us do not, and they hold it in service. They say what they do not know. They hand the decision back.

**Healing is invited, never imposed.** Nothing is done to a person without their word, at the level it is done. A practitioner who heals without consent has violated the thing that made them trustworthy, and the body that holds the craft treats that as the serious breach it is.

**Care is ordinary.** The practices are cheap, mostly ancient, and available to anyone still alive: breath, sleep, food, water, movement, silence, fasting, company, touch, being listened to. The expensive and the technical exist and are honoured for what they do, but they sit on top of a floor that costs nothing.

**The first patient is the practitioner.** Whoever carries the healing craft tends their own instrument daily — clearing, restoration, emotional release, settling, reorientation — because the one who only mends and never mends themselves accumulates the work's wounds until they cannot work.

**The crafts hold each other.** No one holds everything. The pairing of those who protect and those who restore is older than any of our institutions, and the discipline inside it is the deferral: *you are one organ; the Body is many.* The failure mode of every good practitioner is trying to handle everything alone.

And the honest limit, which belongs in the vision rather than in a footnote to it. **People still get sick. Bodies still age. Everyone still dies.** Heaven on Earth is not the abolition of finitude, and a Pillar that promised it would be lying in a way that would eventually be discovered by everyone. What changes is not the fact of a limit but everything around it: whether you meet it alone or held, in debt or in company, treated as a failure of will or as a passage every one of us walks. Not everything regenerates. Some losses are final, and naming a loss final is part of the discipline rather than a failure of it. [[Dying]] is not a regeneration failure. Dying well, accompanied, with your lines left in the record and your story told, is one of the highest forms of health a community can produce.

## The plan

Horizons, named by the turnings of the sun. What follows is plan, not prophecy.

### Season One — to the Winter Solstice of 2026

The whole of it is that the floor of care becomes real at tables that actually exist.

- **The floor is said aloud and written down.** At a Cell's first or next [[The Turn|Turn]]: *when a member is in danger, in body or in mind, we act first and record later; no card is more important than the person at the table.* It goes into the Cell's [[Field of Agreements]] as one line, and onto paper.
- **Every member walks the Essential tier of [[The Daily Way]].** Seven minutes is the floor; below it, the Way is not being walked. Chapter 6 has the practice.
- **Line 13 is read as a health reading.** *How is the Circle? one word, and a line.* A home that sees *thin* or *grieving* before it sees any number can come.
- **Each member says how they actually are at the Turn**, in one honest sentence, before the commitments are reported. Not a performance of wellness; a reading, the way sailors take one.
- **Each Cell writes its three names and three numbers.** Who to call if someone is in danger, tonight, in this place — emergency services, one practitioner, one person who will pick up at two in the morning. On paper, in the Cell's record, known by everyone.
- **One Quest in this Pillar, in some Cells**, from the list below.
- **Whoever among us holds a healing craft is asked to say so**, and the names are written down. The first thing a guild is, is a list.

### The first year — to the Winter Solstice of 2027

- **The healers' guidebook is begun by people who hold the craft**, by the same [[Twelve Irreducible Elements]] every other instrument is built by, and what they build is written down so the next community need not begin again.
- **The first rites are walked before they are written.** A Cell that welcomes a child, marks a coming of age, witnesses a marriage, or buries someone, writes down what it actually did and sends it up as a [[Proposal]]. Rites are not designed in a room; they are found by walking them and then refined.
- **The Cell's crisis instrument is drafted, tested, and improved** — the specification below is a first rendering and expects to be corrected by the first ten Cells that use it in earnest.
- **The emotion practice is run in Circle** by Cells that want it, and what is learned about running it safely travels.
- **A person's own health record becomes a thing they hold**, on paper, going nowhere without their word.

### To the Winter Solstice of 2030

- **A healers' guild in every Community that has one**, federated across communities, holding apprenticeship, the witnessed mark of a [[Belt Progression|belt]], the standard of craft, and its own [[Commons]] of solved problems.
- **The places exist.** A healing room, a sanctuary, a garden, a burial ground — named among the places a community builds, with cards written for each.
- **The health of the watershed is counted beside the health of the people**, in one reading rather than two.
- **The rites are written**, at least in first drafts, in several places and traditions at once — which is the only way they could be written truly.

### To the Winter Solstice of 2033

The Covenant and the Jubilee. By then, if this goes as intended, there are people entering adulthood who have never known health as a commodity, who have been present at a birth and at a death, who can tend a wound and sit with someone who is falling apart. That, more than any instrument, is the deliverable of this Pillar.

### The long now

Seven generations. The measure of this Pillar at that horizon is not any statistic but a question: *are the children born into this arrangement healthier than the ones born into ours, in body, mind, and belonging?* If a practice, a rule, or an instrument cannot be defended on that question, it is revisable — including everything written here.

### What one Cell does in its first season

Concrete, tonight, with a notebook.

**Cards to fill.** One [[The Questcard|Questcard]] with a *done when* line. Each member's [[The Playcard|Playcard]] carries their week. The Cell's [[The Circle's Scorecard|Scorecard]] carries commitments made and kept. Line 13 of the [[The Season Sheet|Season Sheet]] carries the Circle's one word at the turning.

**Quests a Cell could choose tonight** — each is real, near, ninety days, and points at something afterwards:

- **Feed the block one good meal a week for a season.** Real food, from as close as you can get it, at one table. Count who came.
- **Find out what is actually in your water**, and tell your neighbours plainly what you found. Then fix one thing.
- **Grow one bed of food** somewhere it has not been grown, and teach two people who have never grown anything.
- **Learn first aid, all of you**, from someone qualified, and put the certificate on the wall of wherever you meet.
- **Build the list.** Every practitioner within reach, what they actually do, who has been to them, what it costs, who will see someone with no money. Keep it on paper. Give it away.
- **Sit with someone who is dying**, if your place has such a person and they will have you. Nothing else. Then write down what you learned.
- **Keep one night a week dark** — no screens after sunset for every member, for a season — and report honestly at each Turn what it did.
- **Fast together**, one day a week, for those for whom it is safe, with the floor of care ahead of it, and find out what a body of people can do together that none of them would sustain alone.
- **Walk.** Every member, every day, outside, for half an hour, for ninety days. It is the least glamorous Quest on this list and it will change more than most of the others.
- **Hold one member through a hard season** — an illness, a grief, a winter with no work — and count that as the Quest. It is one. Line 13 exists so a home can see it and come.

## The specifications

The instruments this Pillar needs, the standards that let one Cell's health practice be legible to another, the measures, the guards, and what *done for now* looks like.

Several of these appear here for the first time, offered as current best understanding, held loosely, and expecting correction from the first people who use them in earnest.

### The healers' guild

**What it is.** A [[Guild]] is a self-organizing body united by a shared craft and an ethic, federating by the same law as every other body — a [[Domain]], a [[Selectively Permeable Membrane]], a [[Field of Agreements]], a [[Commons]]. The healers' guild is that body for the healing crafts: medicine, nursing, midwifery, herbalism, bodywork, mental health, dentistry, rehabilitation, addiction recovery, hospice, the movement and breath disciplines, and the traditional practices of whatever tradition a place actually holds. It exists because place cannot carry a craft alone — a community of ten thousand has too few of any one craft to advance it, and a guild spanning twenty communities does not.

**Who holds it.** Its members, and nobody else. It has no seat above it. Roles rotate, because a role held forever quietly becomes a throne. Its standing with a [[Community]] is the standing of a craft freely trusted, revocable by the community and by each person in it.

**What it owes.**
1. **A guidebook** — the craft written down, non-redundant with the general practice, improving each season, given away free.
2. **Formation** — [[Apprenticeship]] to those who have gone further, the [[The Zone Of Optimum Development|Zone Of Optimum Development]] honoured, lessons delivered just in time rather than as a wall of curriculum at the door.
3. **The witnessed mark.** A [[Belt Progression|belt]] in this craft follows the same unusual rule as everywhere else in this Way: *meeting the requirements never confers it.* A record shows what you can do; a witness shows that you did it, when it counted, in real conditions. Someone must have been there.
4. **A standard of craft**, stated, so one body's work is usable by another — and so an honest practitioner can be trusted by people who have never seen them work.
5. **A commons of solved problems** — what is learned once, anywhere, available everywhere. Solve, document, push it up, curate, pull it down, localize, feed back.
6. **A stated boundary.** What this guild does *not* do, in writing, per craft. The boundary is the most useful page in the guidebook and the one most often missing.
7. **Referral**, treated as competence rather than as failure. *You are one organ; the Body is many.*
8. **Care of its own.** The first patient is the practitioner. A guild that does not tend its members produces burned-out practitioners and calls it devotion.

**Its rules of practice, carried into every craft under it.**
- **Consent, at the level it is given.** No one is healed without their word. Anything done at a level a person did not agree to was not agreed to.
- **Match the layer.** Meet a wound at its own level. A physical injury is not a spiritual lesson, and a grief is not a deficiency.
- **Say what you do not know.** A practitioner's most valuable sentence.
- **Hand the decision back.** The person decides. Always.
- **Never make yourself necessary.** The measure of the craft is people who need you less over time.

**How it goes wrong.** A guild fails in one direction only: when the standard becomes a gate held for the benefit of those already inside. In this craft that failure has a specific and historically well-documented shape — the licensure cartel that restricts supply, prices out the poor, and calls it safety. The protections are the ordinary ones and they are not optional here: rotation, transparency, accountability, a door that opens in both directions, and the guild's own accounting of who it has turned away and why. The second failure is the mirror: a guild that claims capacities it cannot demonstrate, in which case the belt rule is the antidote — witnessed demonstration in real conditions or nothing.

**How it degrades to paper.** A guild is, at its floor, **a list**: names, what each person can actually do, who witnessed them doing it, how to reach them, and what they will not take on. Written by hand, kept at each table, read aloud when a new member joins. Every other function of a guild — the guidebook, the apprenticeship, the standard, the commons — is a thicker version of that list. If everything else fails, the list is the guild.

### The life-threshold rites

**What they are.** The five crossings a life makes whether or not anyone marks them: **birth · coming of age · marriage · dying · death.** An [[Initiation|initiation]] is a threshold crossed once, in front of others, after which both the person and the body treat them differently — and the change is one of *responsibility* before it is one of privilege, which is why a rite that costs nothing confers nothing.

Cultures that lose their rites do not produce people who never cross thresholds. They produce people who cross them alone, unmarked, at the wrong time, with nobody to say what the other side asks — and who then improvise substitutes, often badly. [[Rites of Passage]] is named in the canon as structural work, not as ceremony for its own sake.

**Who holds them.** The people who are actually there: the [[Cell]], the [[Family]], the [[Kinship|kin]], the [[Community]]. Not a class of officiants. Where a tradition already holds a rite, the tradition holds it, and this Book adds nothing to it and takes nothing away.

**The common shape.** Every one of the five has the same four parts, and a Cell with nothing else can do all four tonight.

1. **Announce.** The threshold is named out loud at the table, before or as it happens, rather than discovered afterwards.
2. **Witness.** People are present. They say what they saw and what they now expect. Two is enough.
3. **Change the standing.** Something about how the body treats this person actually changes, and is said: what is now theirs to carry, what is now no longer asked of them.
4. **Record.** It goes into the Cell's record and onto the Season Sheet. The person's [[Story]] is told once, at the table, and travels if they permit it.

**The five, in their minimum form.**

- **Birth.** [[Giving Birth]] is a passage, not only a medical event with a good outcome — the arrival of a new member, the remaking of a mother, and for everyone present a direct encounter with what life is. *The Cell's part:* gather rather than seal off. Let the midwifing be done by those with the craft and the accompaniment by those with the relationship. Make room in the rhythm — a Circle whose member has just given birth adjusts its Quest, its commitment load, and its expectations, out loud, rather than letting the person quietly fall behind their own word. Mark the arrival: a naming at the table, the child written into the record. [[Protection Of Children]] begins here and is not negotiable anywhere.
- **Coming of age.** The threshold at which a young person is asked to carry something real, in front of people who will hold them to it, and is treated differently afterwards. *The Cell's part:* give the task, witness the doing, say what has changed. The old forms did this with a journey, a fast, a vigil, or a piece of work that mattered; any of the three is available to a Cell with no tradition of its own. What must not happen is the modern substitute — a certificate and no change of standing.
- **Marriage.** Two people give their word to each other in front of a body that will hold them to it, and the body's own obligations change with it. The canon barely names this one, and this rendering is thin on purpose: the traditions hold it far better than this Book does, and where a couple has one, it governs. *The Cell's part:* witness, celebrate, and afterwards be the thing that makes the word keepable — the people who notice, ask, and help, rather than the people who find out two years later.
- **Dying.** [[Dying]] is accompanied rather than managed. The floor of care reaches its furthest point here, and at the end it means presence rather than rescue. Do not isolate the dying; the village gathered. Let the community be present to what is happening rather than protected from it. [[The Way Out]] gives the leaving of a Circle its ordered form, and dying is the version of it nobody chooses. *The Cell's part:* be there, in shifts if need be; take the practical load off the household; ask what they want and do that; and let them finish or hand over what they said they would do, if they wish to, because being useless is its own suffering.
- **Death.** The naming of a death at the table, and the telling of that person's story, is the smallest complete rite available to a Circle that has no other — and it is a complete rite, not a placeholder. Their lines stay in the record; nothing is erased. Their name on the [[The Circle Sheet|Circle Sheet]] gets its date. [[Remembrance]] holds what must not be forgotten. Grief is given time on the card rather than expected to happen in the gaps, and the Circle says on its Season Sheet that this is the season it is in.

**How they degrade to paper.** Entirely. All five are, at their floor, *people in a room, saying true things out loud, and someone writing down that it happened.* No officiant, no building, no power, no network.

### The Cell's care for a member in crisis: the andon for a person

**What it is.** On a good production line there is a cord any worker may pull, and pulling it halts everything. The point is not the stopping; it is that a defect is surfaced at the moment and by the person who saw it, rather than travelling downstream inside the work until it is expensive. [[Whistleblower Protection|The cord]] is already carried up into this body's governance as the word [[Stop]].

This instrument is the same cord for a *person*. A body that will halt its line for a defect and will not halt it for a member has its values in the wrong order.

**Who holds it.** Everyone. No standing is required to pull it, and no explanation is owed before it takes effect.

**The two words, and what each does.**

- **[[Pain (as signal)|Pain]]** — *a request for help that requires a response.* Someone names that something hurts, in the work, in the Cell, or in a life. The naming carries an obligation: it is answered by a person, not filed. A Circle that reliably answers pain hears about trouble early; one that does not hears about it once, and late.
- **[[Stop]]** — halts what is running. Said by anyone, effective at once, lifted only by the one who said it. That asymmetry is what makes the word safe to say; if the room could vote it away, saying it would cost something, and the cost is exactly what the word exists to remove.

**The sequence.** When a member is in crisis:

1. **Someone turns toward them, now.** Not a plan — a person, saying *I heard you*, and staying until it is held by somebody. The response is not necessarily a solution.
2. **Danger check, before anything else.** Is this person in danger in body or in mind, tonight? If the answer is yes or unclear: **act first, record later.** Call the three names and three numbers the Cell wrote down in its first season. Emergency services exist and are used without hesitation or embarrassment. This step outranks every other sentence in this chapter.
3. **Bring in the craft.** Someone who actually holds it. This is care, not failure, and a Cell that treats a referral as a defeat will do harm out of pride. **The Cell does not diagnose and does not treat.** The [[Zero Blame Environment]] does not make a Circle a clinic.
4. **Name the hold, out loud.** The Cell says what it is taking on, what it is not, for how long, and who is doing what. An unnamed hold falls on one person and burns them.
5. **Adjust the work and say so.** The member's commitments are lifted or handed over, in the open, with no gap recorded against them and no apology required. The Cell's [[Quest]] changes if it must. Line 13 says what season the Circle is actually in.
6. **Do not isolate.** The old instinct was right and the modern one is wrong. The village gathered around the one who fell — not to seal them off, but to hold and to witness. Keep the table. Keep the invitation open even when it is declined. A Cell with a member in a hard place keeps a standing, unpressured door.
7. **Look back afterwards, kindly.** When it has passed, the Cell asks *what was the set-up?* — not *whose fault?* Cause is sought in the arrangement, not in the person. What is learned goes into the record and, if it is general, up as a [[Proposal]].

**What it is not, stated plainly because the failure modes are serious.**
- Not treatment, not therapy, not diagnosis, not a substitute for emergency services.
- Not a place a person may be kept. Anyone may leave, at any time, in peace. A body that holds someone "for their own good" has become something else.
- Not a reason to offer a practice instead of an action. A Cell that answers a member's crisis with a breathing exercise has taken a real instrument and used it as a way of not acting.
- Not a place where a person's condition is written into any shared record without their own word.
- Not permanent. Care that never ends becomes [[Manufactured Dependency]], which is one of the named [[The Anti-Patterns|Anti-Patterns]] and is no kinder for being well-meant.

**How it degrades to paper.** One index card, written before it is needed, kept where everyone can find it: *the floor of care in one sentence · three names and three numbers · the seven steps · what we do not do.* That card is the instrument. Everything above is its commentary.

### The body as temple, in daily life

**What it is.** A test, not a decoration. *Would this be done to a temple?* Would this food, this schedule, this silence, this hour, this substance, this room be offered to a dwelling of Spirit? A person can ask it of their own week. A Cell can ask it of the room it meets in. A Community can ask it of the land it sits on.

**Why it settles arguments before they start.** The old ordering reduced the body to a machine to be repaired, regulated, and worked — and once that is granted, extraction from a person and extraction from a watershed follow by the same logic. Naming the body a [[Temple]] denies the premise at its root. *What is revered is protected without being asked; what is owned is spent.* [[Nutrition]], [[Sleeping]], [[Rest And Regeneration]], and [[Healing]] all follow from which of the two the body is taken to be.

**Who holds it.** Each person, for their own body. Nobody holds it for anyone else, and a body of people that begins policing its members' practice has inverted the instrument into the surveillance it was meant to answer.

**The practice itself is chapter 6.** It is not restated here. What belongs here is the ordering beneath it: reverence first, then rhythm, then the specific disciplines — and the specific disciplines calibrated to *the body the person actually has*, in the season it is in, with the work it is called to. A practice that does not bend to a body is not a practice; it is an ideology with a stopwatch.

**How it degrades to paper.** One question, memorized. It needs nothing.

### The temple as a place

**What it is.** A community is not only a set of relationships; it is a set of places where particular things can happen. The canon names them as a family because each does work the others cannot: the **hearth**, where people are warm together and nothing is required of them · the **council space**, where decisions are made among equals with no head of the table · the **healing room** · the **learning space** · the **dojo**, where strength is trained under discipline and witness · the **sanctuary**, where someone in danger may go · the **garden**, which feeds and teaches patience · the **burial ground**, which holds the dead and keeps a people in relation to its own ancestors and its own end.

The claim is that a community missing one of these is missing the *capacity*, not just the room. Where there is no sanctuary, someone in danger has nowhere. Where there is no burial ground, grief has no address. Where there is no healing room, care happens in corridors.

**Who holds it.** The community, as [[Commons]]. Not owned by a practitioner, not rented back to the people who built it.

**Its mobile form.** [[The Roving Ark Temple Dojo]] is the travelling version of the same body — shelter, stillness, and training in one, going to where the people are. It suits a crossing rather than a settlement: the tent before the temple, the circuit rider, the school that arrives. It is a name with a thin body and is flagged as such; held lightly it is a useful shape, held heavily it is a phrase in search of a practice.

**How it degrades to paper — and to nothing at all.** Every one of these begins as a practice long before it is a building. The hearth is whoever's kitchen. The council space is a circle of chairs. The healing room is a quiet room with a door that closes and a chair that is comfortable. The sanctuary is a person who will open their door at two in the morning. The garden is a bed. The burial ground is the one thing on the list that eventually needs ground — and the practice it carries, which is remembering the dead by name in company, needs none.

### Degradation to paper: the practices need nothing

This is the specification that makes the Pillar durable, and it is short because it is true.

**Breath needs nothing.** No equipment, no teacher to begin, no cost, usable by anyone still alive.
**Sleep needs darkness**, which is what the world does by itself when the power is out.
**Food needs soil, water, sun, and knowledge** — and the knowledge is the part worth writing down.
**Fasting needs a decision and a clock**, and works without the clock.
**Rest needs permission**, which is why naming it a phase of the season rather than a lapse is the whole instrument.
**Walking needs a road.**
**Grief needs company.**
**Witness needs two people and no technology at all.**

Nothing in the daily practice of this Pillar requires electricity, a network, a currency, a frontier model, an institution, or a licence. The instruments that *do* need infrastructure — the guild's guidebook, the record, the healing room — each degrade to a hand-written list, and the list is the thing. **Keep a paper copy** of the floor of care, the three names and three numbers, and the practitioner list. [[Screens and paper|The notebook is the floor the whole network stands on.]]

### The health record as the person's own

**What it is.** The record of a person's own body and mind — history, conditions, medications, allergies, what has been tried and what worked, a practitioner's findings in the practitioner's own words, and, where the person chooses, their own wishes for a crisis in which they cannot speak for themselves. It belongs to the person whose body it describes, it is held by them, and it moves nowhere without their word — **per instance, not once and forever.** A yes given last season to one practitioner is not a yes given today to the Cell.

**Why it stands apart from the shared record.** The Cell's record is the table's: what was said, decided, and promised, kept in the Cell's own box in the Cell's own hand, open to anyone at the table to read and correct. A person's health record is a different object entirely, and the difference is the whole of this instrument. **Nothing in the shared record says anything about a member's health that the member did not put there.** Care requires memory, and memory about a body is the most intimate record a body of people keeps; pooled — for efficiency, for research, for safety — it becomes a file about a person that the person cannot read, correct, or take away. Whoever holds a person's record holds the person. The answer is that the person holds it.

**Who holds it.** The person. Nobody else, ever — not the Cell, not a guild, not a practitioner, not an agent. A parent holds a young child's on the child's behalf and hands it over as the child comes of age; nobody else steps into that place for any reason.

**What it contains**, in the person's own words, at whatever depth they choose:
- What is true of their body and mind now — conditions, medications, allergies, what has helped and what has not.
- What a practitioner has told them, carried in the practitioner's own words where possible, so the reading does not thin with each retelling.
- Their own wishes for a crisis they cannot speak through — who to call, what they do and do not want done, and to whom this one page may be shown and when.
- Nothing about anyone else. A record kept about a person by someone other than that person is not this instrument; it is the thing this instrument exists to prevent.

**How it crosses to the shared record, and only this way.** The bridge is the person's own word, given per instance. Telling the Cell "I am living with this, please know it" makes that one sentence shared; it does not open the rest of the page, and it stays shared no longer than the person wishes it to. A practitioner reads what is shown, asks — [[Consent]], at the level it is given — before writing anything down, and writes nothing back into a shared place without asking again. The floor of care narrows none of this except in the one hour it exists for: when a member is in danger, in body or in mind, the Circle acts first on what it can see and what it is told, and the record is written afterward, never instead of the acting.

**Its emergency page.** One page at the front, separate from the rest, that a person may choose to let their Cell hold or keep entirely to themselves, revisited whenever they wish. Its minimum shape is what a stranger arriving to help would need if the person could not say it themselves: the three names and three numbers the Cell already keeps for its own crisis instrument, any condition that changes what help looks like, and what they do and do not want done. Kept where it can be found, not buried in a drawer.

**By hand.** A notebook, in the person's own hand or dictated to whoever they trust to write it, going nowhere without a spoken yes. This is the whole instrument at its floor and needs nothing more.

**By machine, where a body keeps digital records at all.** The rule carries into the code rather than loosening there: the record lives in the person's own store, never pooled into a Cell's or a guild's database by default; nothing copies out of it without a fresh, per-instance consent; the person can export the whole of it and delete the whole of it at will; and a shared record's health fields hold only what the person put there themselves, in their own words, dated.

**How it degrades to paper.** Completely, and it was never anything else at its floor. A notebook, a pencil, a known place to keep it, and a person who knows it is theirs.

**What it guards against.** The pooled file nobody can correct. The practitioner who assumes instead of asking. A crisis met by strangers who know nothing because nothing was ever told — which the emergency page answers without opening the rest of the record. And a Cell, a guild, or any body that quietly begins keeping health notes on members because it is convenient, which is exactly how every prior enclosure of this kind of record began: for efficiency, for research, for safety, and then for control. **A member's condition is never written into any shared record without their own word.**

### The minimum standards of interoperability, in this Pillar

What has to be the same between Cells so that a person moving between them, or a home helping one of its Cells, finds what they expect.

1. **The floor of care is worded the same everywhere.** *When a member is in danger, in body or in mind, the Circle acts first and records later; no card is more important than the person at the table.* Translated freely; never softened.
2. **[[Pain (as signal)|Pain]] and [[Stop]] mean the same thing at every table** — a request that requires a response, and a halt that only its speaker lifts.
3. **Line 13 of the [[The Season Sheet|Season Sheet]] is a wellness reading**: one word for how the Circle is, and one line. Same line number in every language.
4. **A member's health is never written into a shared record without their own word.** Their own card is their own. What leaves the table leaves by their consent, per instance, and the private and the shared are different objects.
5. **A referral travels in one form**: what the craft is, what the practitioner does and does not take on, and who witnessed them doing it when it counted.
6. **A rite is recorded the same way**: what threshold, who witnessed, what changed, dated.
7. **Nothing personal goes on any shared play-by-play.** [[Elision]] is available for one's own words; nobody edits another's.

### The measures

The honest ones are cheap and nearly all of them are qualitative.

- **Line 13** — the Circle's one word, each turning, over several turnings. A word that does not change for a year is itself a reading.
- **Each member's own reading at the Turn**, in a sentence.
- **[[Kept as a share of made]], read against exhaustion.** The number is only meaningful when you know what it cost. A rising reliability number alongside a falling vitality reading is a body eating itself, and it is the single most useful cross-reading in this chapter.
- **Pain named, and answered within a day.** Whether it was answered, not how well.
- **What the Cell does when someone misses.** Measured once, the first time it happens, and it sets the culture permanently.

**Where value flows.** Care is precisely the work that money never counted — the night sat up with, the meal brought, the hand held, the decades of raising and tending on which every economy silently rests. The argument for a unit rooted in contribution rather than in payment is therefore strongest in this Pillar. [[LUV]] is that unit in intent: calculated from the record of what was actually given and kept, never issued by decision, never retracted once recorded, circulating locally and readable across the federation. The state of it should be said plainly: **the principle is held; the ledger is not built.** No Cell needs it to play a season. What a Cell needs is [[The Offering]], a [[The Record|record]] of what was kept, and [[Voluntary Tithing]] at the turning — and care counted honestly in that record, which most Cells will have to consciously decide to do, because the habit of not counting it is very old.

And one thing not to do while waiting for the ledger: **do not price care.** The moment a Circle attaches a rate to sitting with someone, it has rebuilt in miniature the thing this Pillar exists to transcend. Count it. Honour it. Do not sell it to each other.

### The guards

**The anti-patterns of this Pillar**, each named so it can be caught early.

- **The wellness industry.** [[Extraction Of Wealth]] wearing health's name — the product, the status, the aesthetic, the private optimization project undertaken while the commons burns. Wellness is what a community owes its members and what its members owe one another. It is not for sale.
- **The system that profits from sickness**, in any of its forms, including the small ones a good community can build by accident.
- **[[Manufactured Dependency]].** The practitioner, the practice, or the Circle that makes itself necessary. The test is whether people need you less over time.
- **Purity as identity.** The clean-living self that has become another way of managing the self, and that judges. A life organized around purity is its own disorder.
- **Vitality as merit.** The most likely misuse of this chapter. Illness, age, disability, and dying are not failures. A body of people that reads them as failures has produced a cruelty and wrapped it in this Pillar's language.
- **The Circle playing clinic.** A well-meaning group taking on what it is not equipped for. Real harm follows, and it follows fastest where people care most.
- **The practice used to avoid the action.** The breath offered instead of the call. The ceremony instead of the ambulance.
- **The guild as gate.** Standards held for the benefit of insiders; the craft restricted so that its price stays high.
- **The framing used to withhold help.** Any reading that dignifies a state — passage, initiation, dark night — turned into a reason not to help someone who is drowning.
- **The hero who never rests.** A body of people that cannot stop has decided in practice that its work matters more than the people doing it, which contradicts the floor the whole Game stands on.
- **[[The Parasite]] at the door of care.** The one who arrives to be held forever and never to hold. Naming it is not unkindness; a commons that cannot name it will be drained and will then help no one.

**The structural immunities** — the protections written into the form, so they do not depend on whoever holds authority that day being in a good mood.

- **[[Stop]]**, available to anyone, lifted only by its speaker.
- **The floor of care written down before it is needed**, and read aloud when a new member joins.
- **Consent for any care, at the level it is given**, revocable at any time.
- **The door that opens both ways** — [[The Way Out]] applies to care as much as to membership.
- **Rotation and transparency in the guild**, and an accounting of who was turned away.
- **The witnessed mark**, which cannot be granted by completing anything.
- **The private and the shared held as different objects**, always, with the person's own word the only bridge between them.
- **[[Every Defect Is A Gift]]** — every miss in care surfaced the day it happens, cause sought in the set-up, fixed where the next person will meet it.

### What "done for now" looks like

Checkable, and modest on purpose.

- Every Cell can state the floor of care from memory, and has it on paper.
- Every Cell has three names and three numbers, current.
- Every member walks the Essential tier of the daily practice, or has said out loud that they are not.
- Line 13 has been filled honestly at two consecutive turnings, including when the honest word was a bad one.
- One member's crisis has been met by the instrument above, and the look-back has been done and written.
- The Cell knows every practitioner within reach and what each does and does not do.
- A threshold — any of the five — has been marked at the table and recorded.
- Whoever in the body holds a healing craft has said so, and is on a list somebody keeps.
- Nothing in the Cell's shared record says anything about a member's health that the member did not put there.

When those nine are true, this Pillar is *done for now* at the scale of one Cell — and the guild, the guidebook, and the rites are the next season's work.

## In every future

Open [[21 In Every Future — what to do in each case]] for the full distribution and the preset judgments. This Pillar's behaviour across them is unusually simple, and the simplicity is the design rather than a consolation.

**The practices need nothing, so nothing that fails takes them away.** If the frontier models are gone, the daily practice is untouched — it was never in them. If the power is out, sleep improves, screens stop, the dark returns, and the only real losses are refrigeration, clean water at the tap, and the machines of acute medicine, which is exactly why the paper list of who can do what, and the tending of the watershed, were built early. If a state firewalls the network, breath crosses no border and neither does a Circle that already meets in a room. If enclosure comes — if the supply chains, the records, or the crafts themselves are captured — what is enclosed is the infrastructure, never the practice; a guild that exists as a list of people who can do things cannot be enclosed, only dispersed, and dispersal is a thing lists survive. In the gentle futures, the danger is not catastrophe but drift: nothing outside forces the keeping of a practice, and a gentle world is therefore the cheapest place to build the body a harder world would need, because none of the Cell's capacity is going to triage.

**The preset judgment for this Pillar, decided here, in the calm.** *Signal:* two consecutive turnings in which line 13 reads thin, or any turning in which a member's crisis was met with a plan instead of a person. *Cell:* the next Turn is given entirely to the members and not to the work — how each one actually is, what they need, what is lifted — and one commitment each to rest before the following Turn; the Quest waits. *Supercell:* a home that sees a thin word on a sheet comes, in person, before the next turning, and brings something. *Community body:* the practitioner list is refreshed and republished, and the healing room, wherever it is, is opened. In a hard hour, deliberation is the scarcest resource; the deliberation is done here so that what is left for the hour is recognition.

## What is not yet settled

**The what waits on an answer**, one line each.

1. **The Pillar's name.** *Sacred Health and Wellness* (the hub, the seed, this Book) or *Sacred Health and Healing* (the Pillar page's own thesis title, the Lexicon) — one, and the other becomes an alias.
2. **[[Nutrition]] or *Eating*** as the canonical home of the concept.
3. **[[Breath]] or *The Sovereign's Breath*** as the canonical home, with the six breaths beneath whichever is chosen.
4. **[[Rest And Regeneration]] or [[Sabbath]]** as the canonical home of the rest concept, and which Pillar it sits in.
5. **Whether the healers' guild as specified here is adopted**, amended, or refused — and if adopted, who is asked to write the guidebook.
6. **Whether the five life-threshold rites are commissioned now** or deliberately left to be walked first and written after. This Book recommends the second, with the walking recorded from the first day.
7. **Whether marriage belongs in this Pillar at all**, or under [[18 Pillar XI — Sacred Society and Justice]] or [[19 Pillar XII — Sacred Time, Rhythm, and Celebration]]. It is placed here because the canon groups it with the life thresholds; the placement is weak.
8. **Whether the andon-for-a-person instrument is adopted as specified**, and in particular whether **[[Stop]] carries into a person's crisis** or whether this Pillar needs a distinct word.
9. **The status of the energetic layer** — carried as the practitioners' account (this chapter's choice), elevated, or bounded. The chapter needs an answer more than it needs a position.
10. **Whether a health record exists at all**, and if so, that it is the person's own and moves nowhere without their word — which this chapter has assumed and which no page states.
11. **Whether care work is counted in the record** from the first season, before any ledger exists.
12. **The word for what a practitioner is** — the canon uses *healer*, which in several living traditions is a claimed and protected term. A guild that adopts it casually will cause offence it did not intend.

**The contradictions found.** The Pillar has two names in the canon, and the page carrying the name *Wellness* leads with theses titled *Healing*. Three concepts have two homes each (Nutrition/Eating, Breath/The Sovereign's Breath, Rest/Sabbath). [[Regeneration]] is defined twice, differently, under two Pillars. [[Integration]] is used in three Pillars with three shadings and has not been cohered. [[Vitality]] is defined in terms of [[Energy]], which the canon carries in two incompatible senses. [[Detoxification]] carries a physical and an energetic sense that no page reconciles. [[Fasting]] is listed twice — once as a drafted discipline, once as a practice still to be patterned. The canon's entry for madness and breakdown records, in its own text, *not sure how to address this yet* — and that honest line is better than most of what would replace it.

**What this chapter is least sure of.** The healers' guild and the andon-for-a-person are first articulations and are the least tested things in this Book's Pillar chapters; they are built from the shape of other instruments rather than from anyone's experience of running them, and the first ten Cells that use them in earnest will know more than this page does. The rites are rendered at a minimum shape that may be too thin to do the work rites actually do. And the whole chapter walks a line it may not have walked well: holding the canon's real critique of a medicalized, commodified, severed system *without* becoming the kind of text that talks someone out of care they need. Where that line is walked badly here, the floor of care governs, and this page is wrong.
