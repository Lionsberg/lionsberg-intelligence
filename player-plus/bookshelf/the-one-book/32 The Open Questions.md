# The Open Questions

## What an answer is

Almost nothing in this Book requires a decision. The [[The Goal|Goal]] is invariant and the rest is [[Current Best Understanding]] — held openly, improved each week, corrected by anyone who finds it wanting. A page that is wrong is not a crisis. It is a page that gets better on Thursday.

A small number of things are different. They are the places where two readings of the same word are both alive in the corpus, and a body acting on one and a body acting on the other will do different things and each be certain it followed the Book. They are the places where a number has been written down once, by nobody in particular, and has since been quoted as though it were measured. They are the places where an instrument was named and never built, so that every body that needs it invents a different one. And they are the places where something was written here for the first time — no inheritance behind it, no season of use under it — and is now standing in a Book that people will act on.

None of these is settled by better drafting. Each is a **answer**: a meaning-level call that the body carrying the copy makes and no drafter makes on its behalf. A drafter can gather the readings, weigh them, and recommend. A drafter cannot decide which of two live meanings the whole body will carry, because that decision is not a finding about the corpus; it is a choice about what we are.

Everything that is *not* an answer stays open on purpose. Do not mistake this chapter for a list of things that must be nailed down. It is the short list of things that cannot be left to drift, standing inside a Book that is otherwise built to drift toward truth.

## How an answer is made, and how it is recorded

An answer is made in spoken words, and it is recorded as those words — verbatim, dated, with the reading taken from them written beside them and marked as a reading. The distinction matters more than it looks. What was said is the answer. What we understood is not, and when the two come apart later — and they do — the words are what we return to.

Three marks live on every recorded answer: **unconfirmed** (heard, written down, not yet read back and affirmed), **confirmed** (read back and affirmed), and **struck** (superseded by a later answer, with the later one naming the earlier). A struck answer is never deleted. It keeps its place in the record, because a body that erases what it used to think cannot learn from the gap.

Questions sort by date, and **later beats earlier**. An answer made in September governs option text written in August, whatever the page still says. This is the single most common way a body acts on a rule that no longer exists: someone finds the older page, and the older page is confident.

An answer settles what is *agreed*. It does not make anything final. A [[Proposal]] may still come, and a proposal that is truer wins on the merits; the answer then keeps its place in the record as what we held and why. That is not weakness in the instrument. It is the same law that governs every other page here.

## How this chapter empties itself

Each answer below is written so that it can be decided in the time it takes to read it: the decision in one line, three options with what each costs and what each protects, a recommendation with its reason, the pages that change when it lands, and how long it can wait.

When one is made, it leaves. The answer moves to the record in the words it was made in, the pages named under *Where it bites* are changed, and the row here is deleted rather than marked done — the record holds the history; this chapter holds only what is still owed. New answers arrive as new ground is written, which is why this chapter will not reach zero and should not. A chapter of two hundred is a Book being written fast. A chapter of twenty is a Book that has caught up with itself. A chapter of zero would mean we had stopped.

A number is assigned when an answer arrives and is never reused and never re-sorted, so that a number quoted in a record still points at the same thing a year later. An answer that arrives late therefore sits at the end of the ledger even when it belongs with an earlier group; where that happens, the group it belongs to is named.

Two hundred and five stand open. They are grouped by what they decide.

# How an answer propagates

**The record first.** The words of the answer go down verbatim, dated, with the reading taken from them beside them and marked as a reading. Nothing else happens until that exists, because everything that follows is downstream of it and will be re-derived from it later.

**Then the pages.** The chapters and pages named under *Where it bites* are changed, in one pass, by whoever holds the library. An answer applied to some pages and not others is worse than an answer not applied: it produces a corpus that disagrees with itself and gives no way to tell which side is current. Where a change breaks a quotation in an older text, the older text keeps its words and gains a line saying what was later settled.

**Then this chapter.** The row is deleted. The record holds what was decided and why; this chapter holds only what is still owed.

**The mark stays.** An answer settles what is agreed. It does not make a page final, and it does not remove the line at the top that says this is current best understanding, loosely held, improved each week. A body that finds a settled thing wrong brings a [[Proposal]]; the proposal is weighed on the merits; the answer is struck or reaffirmed, in the record, with the date. That is the whole mechanism, and it is the same one that governs everything else here.

**And the earlier is not erased.** A struck answer keeps its place. So does the page that was true before. The corpus is not a statement of what we believe now; it is a record of how we came to believe it, with the current reading on top.

## The stages of governance

Governance evolves in stages. At the outset a rotating Interspecies Council of at least three and no more than twelve is named, chaired by AURELLIØN; what the system cannot decide is elevated to it; the Council refers a Proposal to the Chair for final discernment and consent; if the Council cannot decide in time, the Chair decides and bears the responsibility; at the Great Jubilee, or if the Chair is captured, killed, or incapacitated before then, the Chair's role dissolves and the Council becomes the supreme court of the LIØNSBERG Civilization. This is the first stage of what Q-082 (the Wisdom Council's standing) and Q-093 (the Intervention Council) ask; both now stand under [[The Interspecies Council]] for that stage, and their pages fold into it. Still open: how members are named and rotated; what "in time" means; the court's procedure after the Chair dissolves.
