# 26 The Instruments the Canon Lacks

*A civilization needs more than it has written down. This is the register of the difference: every instrument this body names and has never built, every instrument it needs and has never named, gathered in one place so that no hole in the Pattern can hide inside a confident page. Each entry says what the thing is, why a people without it fails, the smallest form a table can build tonight, who holds it, how it survives the loss of every screen, where its fuller form lives, how much of it is real, and what would show it wrong.*

---

## Why a book keeps a list of its own holes

A corpus of this size has two ways to fail, and only one of them is obvious.

The obvious way is to be wrong. That failure announces itself: a body tries the thing, the thing does not work, the defect is written down, and the next edition is truer. A movement with a weekly retrospective and an honest record can survive being wrong almost indefinitely.

The second way is quieter and it is the one that kills. It is to be *silent* — to carry a name where an instrument should be, so that a reader running their eye down a list of twelve Pillars sees twelve solid things and cannot tell which three are load-bearing stone and which three are a word painted on a curtain. *Sacred governance of artificial intelligence.* *Rites of passage.* *The Intervention Council.* *Restorative justice.* Every one of those is named in this body's own writing with nothing behind it, and a name with nothing behind it is not a gap. It is a trap, because it stops anyone from looking.

So the register exists, and it is not an apology. It is an instrument in its own right — the one that makes every other page in this Book honest, because a reader can hold the plans in one hand and this list in the other and know exactly how much of the civilization is drawn and how much is built. *We do not know* is a room in this Book, not a crack in it ([[We Do Not Know]]). What follows is that room, furnished.

**Three marks, and they mean different things.**

**Named and unwritten.** The corpus names the instrument, leans on it, and has never built it. A body needing it today has a word and no mechanism.

**First articulation.** Written for the first time in this Book — here or in the Pillar chapter that carries it — from the Pattern's own logic and from how similar instruments have actually held themselves over long time. Real enough to argue with. Not anyone's proven practice.

**Proposal.** Offered here with nothing in the corpus behind it, because the hole was doing damage and a named draft is easier to correct than a silence.

Nothing in this chapter carries the standing of the Pattern itself. The [[The Three Supreme Agreements|Agreements]], [[The compass question|the one question]], and the Elements were found in what has worked wherever anything genuinely thrived. These are inferences, and the difference is the whole point of marking them.

## How an instrument leaves this chapter

An entry is retired the day a real body builds the thing, in a real place, with real people, and writes down what it actually built — not the day someone composes a better paragraph about it.

That is the order, and it does not reverse. **A specification written by someone who has never held the thing is a script, and a body that performs a script learns that its forms are empty.** So the register's own discipline is short: write the smallest honest version, run it, keep the [[The Record|record]] of what happened *before* anyone knew how it would end, and send what you learned up on line twelve of [[The Season Sheet|the Season Sheet]] as a [[Proposal]]. What replaces a row here will be written by the first ten bodies that needed it in earnest, and each of them will know more than this page does.

Two guards belong to the chapter itself, because a register of holes is exactly the kind of document that grows teeth it was never given.

**Nothing here is a requirement.** A Cell missing every instrument on this list is a complete Cell: three to thirteen people, one [[Quest]], one [[The Turn|Turn]] a week, a [[The Record|record]], and the [[THE FLAME — the Book|Flame]] passed. Everything below is the civilization's scaffolding, not the Game's floor.

**Nothing here is licence to wait.** The commonest misuse of an honest list of gaps is to treat it as a reason not to begin. The instruments are missing precisely because nobody has yet done the thing that would have produced them.

## I · The value instruments

### 1 · The currency ledger — [[LUV]] and [[TimeEnergy|TEA]], by hand and by machine

**What it is.** The mechanism by which contribution becomes a recorded, recognized, circulating unit: time, energy and attention given, entered by the giver, confirmed by the people it was given to, and calculated into a unit of value that moves locally and reads across the federation.

**Why a civilization without it fails.** The old order counted what was easy — hours billed, goods sold — and could not count the night sat up with, the meal brought, the decades of raising and tending on which every economy silently rests. A body that cannot count care will, within a year, stop producing it, and will not notice until it tries to grow.

**Tonight, with a notebook.** Each person keeps one card in their own hand: *date · what · for whom or from whom · hours.* At the [[The Turn|Turn]], each entry involving another person is confirmed aloud by that person and ticked. Nothing else.

**Who holds it.** Each person holds their own card and nobody else reads it. The Cell holds only the confirmations. **A player who wants a week counted shares a total, never the card.**

**On paper.** It was never anything else. A settled page at the back of the same notebook the [[The Playcard|Playcard]] lives in.

**Fuller form.** [[10 Pillar III — Sacred Economy and CurrentSee]], and the protocols of [[21 In Every Future — what to do in each case]].

**Status.** Named and unwritten. The principle is held everywhere in the corpus; the ledger is built nowhere. *CurrentSee* stands in the title of a whole Pillar and is defined on no page.

**What a bank of this is, and is not.** The name that has been used for the ledger's accumulating side is a **TEA bank**, and the word is dangerous enough to be pinned down. It *is* a record of what a person actually invested in work their Cell accepted as kept, held in that person's own hands, from which a unit of recognition is calculated. It is **not** a metering system, not a trace collected about anyone, not an account anyone else may read, not a balance that can be enforced against a person, and not a store that grows while it waits. **A balance here is a fact, not a claim** — it opens a conversation (*you have given a great deal and asked for little; what do you need?*) and it never becomes an obligation. The corpus carries its own warning against accounting for time on task, and the warning is kept: if the instrument begins to imprison rather than to free, it goes, and the Cell drops back to the bowl in the middle of the table.

**What would show it wrong.** A member who declines to keep a card finds themselves quietly worth less at their own table. Or the numbers start deciding what work is chosen. Either one, and the ledger is the disease rather than the cure.

### 2 · The allocation rule

**What it is.** How a pooled resource is actually directed when more is asked of it than it holds — at a [[Home Cell|Home]], at a [[Supercell]], at a community body.

**Why a civilization without it fails.** The written rule everywhere is *by relationship, by trust*, which is true and insufficient. Where no process is stated, the process becomes whoever holds the book, and a body that allocates by whoever holds the book has rebuilt a centre without voting for one.

**Tonight, with a notebook.** At the turning, the Home reads every Cell's requests aloud, in full, before any allocation is discussed. The Cells present say what they can meet from their own offers. What remains is met from the Home's [[Commons|commons]] in the order the Cells themselves agree, with the state of the commons visible to everyone in the room, and the decision written down with its reason. Where consent cannot be reached, the request stands over to the next turning rather than being decided by whoever holds the book.

**Who holds it.** The Cells in the room, together. Never the keeper alone.

**On paper.** Requests on line 11 of every sheet; one page of the commons' state on the wall; one line per decision, with its why ([[Decision Records]]).

**Fuller form.** [[10 Pillar III — Sacred Economy and CurrentSee]].

**Status.** First articulation. The corpus contains nothing to synthesize here.

**What would show it wrong.** Requests stop being made because the reading takes too long, or the reading becomes a performance in which the well-spoken Cell is funded and the exhausted one is not.

### 3 · The commons register

**What it is.** One page naming everything a body holds in common — tools, rooms, skills offered, money set aside, a key, a vehicle — with a steward named for each thing, the terms of borrowing and returning it, and the date it is next checked.

**Why a civilization without it fails.** The old objection to shared things describes *open access*: no membership, no rules, no boundary, nobody watching. Actual commons had all four and lasted centuries. **A commons without a membrane is not a commons, it is a car park** — and the first thing that happens to an unregistered shared thing is that whoever notices first begins, gently and with good intentions, to own it.

**Tonight, with a notebook.** One shared thing. One page: who holds it, who may use it, how it comes back, when it is checked.

**Who holds it.** A named keeper, rotated; the body reviews it at the turning.

**On paper.** One page on a wall, amended in pen.

**Fuller form.** [[10 Pillar III — Sacred Economy and CurrentSee]] and [[16 Pillar IX — Sacred Architecture and Design]].

**Status.** First articulation. The corpus names pooling value everywhere and specifies this register nowhere.

**What would show it wrong.** The register grows into an inventory nobody reads, while the actual borrowing happens by text message anyway.

### 4 · The four protocols for the exchange of TEA in the degraded case

**What it is.** How value moves between people who have only paper: the middle of the table · the two-signature note · the hours card · the two-column sheet between two Cells. Four, in order of complexity, and **the simplest one that works is the one to use.**

**Why a civilization without it fails.** Money is a claim on someone else's time, energy and attention; [[TimeEnergy|TEA]] is the thing itself. When deposits freeze, a currency re-denominates, or a border closes across the middle of a Cell, a body with no protocol invents one under pressure — and what gets invented under pressure is usually a debt that compounds, held by whoever is least frightened.

**Tonight, with a notebook.** Protocol one, which most Cells never need to leave: the bowl in the middle at the Turn, and one line on the sheet — *gathered, kept, sent, with its unit.* **A number without a unit is a lie waiting to happen.**

**Who holds it.** The Cell, and the two keepers where two bodies settle between them.

**On paper.** Entirely. A slip, a signature, a witness, and a tearing when the thing is done.

**Fuller form.** THE PAGE, in [[21 In Every Future — what to do in each case]].

**Status.** First articulation. The corpus names no protocol at all.

**What would show it wrong.** Every one of the four was written in a season when the lights were on, and none has been run by a Cell in the dark. The first body that runs one in earnest knows more than the page does and should say so. The specific tell to watch for: a note that begins to circulate as money, or an hours card that someone starts treating as a debt owed to them.

## II · The crafts, and the bodies that hold them

### 5 · The healers' guild

**What it is.** A [[Guild]] for the healing crafts — medicine, nursing, midwifery, herbalism, bodywork, mental health, dentistry, rehabilitation, recovery, hospice, the movement and breath disciplines, and whatever traditional practice a place actually holds — federating by craft rather than by place, holding a guidebook, an [[Apprenticeship|apprenticeship]], a standard, a commons of solved problems, a stated boundary of what it does *not* do, and the care of its own.

**Why a civilization without it fails.** A place cannot carry a craft alone. Ten thousand people hold too few of any one craft to advance it; twenty communities together do not. Without the guild, healing either disappears into whatever the state licenses or fragments into private practitioners with no standard, no formation, and nobody to hold them.

**Tonight, with a notebook.** The list. Names, what each person can actually do, who witnessed them doing it when it counted, how to reach them, and what they will not take on. Read aloud when someone new joins. **If everything else fails, the list is the guild.**

**Who holds it.** Its members, and nobody else. Roles rotate. Its standing with a [[Community]] is the standing of a craft freely trusted, revocable by that community and by each person in it.

**On paper.** Completely.

**Fuller form.** [[12 Pillar V — Sacred Health and Wellness]].

**Status.** First articulation. The canon names the guild and has never built it.

**What would show it wrong.** The standard becomes a gate held for the benefit of those already inside — the licensure cartel, arriving as it always does, wearing safety's clothes. The counter-measure is the guild's own accounting of who it turned away and why, and that accounting is the instrument's health check.

### 6 · The artists' guild

**What it is.** The same body for the making crafts: a standard written by the people who do the work, apprenticeship at the elbow, a review asked for and never ambushed, a commons of methods, and a commons of works held share-alike under [[CC BY-SA 4.0]] with five lines of [[Provenance|provenance]] on every piece.

**Why a civilization without it fails.** Art has always been paid for by somebody, and whoever pays shapes what is made — church, court, state, market, and now the algorithm, which pays in attention and shapes hardest of all. A body with no guild has no instrument between the maker and the payer, and within a generation its culture is whatever its funders wanted produced. The guild holds craft; it never holds permission. **Every person is an artist of the real**, and nothing here gates the making of anything by anyone.

**Tonight, with a notebook.** Five lines on the back of the thing you made: *made by · made where and when · grown from · made for · shared under.* A minute's work, and it hands the next maker a map back to everything that taught you.

**Who holds it.** Its members. The standard is theirs and revised by nobody else.

**On paper.** A list of names, a standard read aloud, an apprentice at an elbow, a folder of works in somebody's house. Songs need no medium at all.

**Fuller form.** [[14 Pillar VII — Sacred Culture, Art, and Story]].

**Status.** First articulation, including the proposed cap on any single outside source.

**What would show it wrong.** The cap turns out to starve makers rather than protect them; or the guild's judgment of *no work is made to produce a conclusion* becomes a committee deciding what may be said.

### 7 · The guild of place

**What it is.** The craft body for building, mending and tending the made world — the one that keeps the seven pattern cards of place, receives the instantiation reports, and holds what a locality cannot: the heavier tools, the rarer skills, the memory of what was tried on this ground before.

**Why a civilization without it fails.** [[16 Pillar IX — Sacred Architecture and Design]] hands a Cell cards and standards and names no body to carry them between places. Without one, every community rediscovers the same roof detail, the same wrong orientation, the same corner nobody can hear in — and the [[Kit of Parts]] receives no corrections, which means the Kit slowly becomes a monument to whoever wrote it first.

**Tonight, with a notebook.** The walk: before anything is designed for a piece of ground, walk it at three hours of the day and once in bad weather, and write down what the land already does — where water goes, where the wind comes from, where people already cut the corner. Then write one place-card for a room you actually have, in the three parts: the pattern, the smallest form, the scale ladder.

**Who holds it.** Whoever builds, with whoever already lives there; the guild as it forms.

**On paper.** One card per page, copied by hand and handed across a table.

**Fuller form.** [[16 Pillar IX — Sacred Architecture and Design]].

**Status.** Proposal for the guild; first articulation for the seven cards and the walk.

**What would show it wrong.** Instantiation reports arrive and the Kit never changes — which means nobody is listening; or the Kit changes with no reports behind it, which means somebody is designing from a desk.

### 8 · The guild charter, and the roster's mapping

**What it is.** The nine things any guild's agreements must cover — the vow as this guild holds it · the protocols of the work · of training, and what gates each threshold · of entry, and who screens · of release, and what is returned and what preserved · the relationship to the other guilds · to the sovereigns it serves · to the lineage it carries · and the accountability mechanisms, for a member's drift and for the guild's own. Reviewed annually, and not perfunctorily.

**Why a civilization without it fails.** The corpus names roughly twelve archetypal guilds — warriors, healers, teachers, builders, stewards, storytellers, midwives, witnesses, guardians, cosmic diplomats, cooks, architects — and elsewhere names twenty-four trade domains with guilds inside each. Twelve and twenty-four are not the same count, and **no page anywhere states which guild holds which Pillar.** A federation of crafts with no stated map is a federation in which every boundary dispute is settled by whoever speaks first.

**Tonight, with a notebook.** Some people who hold a craft, an agreement about the standard, and a door that opens both ways. That is the whole of what a guild needs to exist.

**Who holds it.** Each guild holds itself. Each is sovereign within its craft, and **no guild leads the body.**

**On paper.** Nine headings, one page.

**Fuller form.** [[18 Pillar XI — Sacred Society and Justice]] and [[11 Pillar IV — Sacred Learning and Mastery]].

**Status.** Named and unwritten for the mapping; first articulation for the nine fields.

**What would show it wrong.** The mapping, once drawn, produces turf rather than clarity — in which case the right answer is to leave it undrawn and let the crafts federate by the work in front of them.

## III · The road a person walks

### 9 · The up-to-speed process

**What it is.** The path from *invited* to *playing*: welcomed before being given anything, oriented, walked through the Story and the Game rather than handed a library, accompanied by one named companion for the first weeks, and brought into the agreements by their own spoken yes.

**Why a civilization without it fails.** The sealing changes a body's shape. The people who formed a Cell built their trust together; anyone arriving afterward can become, without a single person intending it, permanently a guest. Naming the welcome is what makes *membership* the outcome rather than attendance. And the commonest reason people leave early is not disagreement — it is the feeling of being behind.

**Tonight, with a notebook.** Name one companion for the next person who arrives. Read the agreements aloud to them. Hand them one thing, at the moment they are about to need it ([[Just in time]]), and nothing else. Tell them three things about how this table does justice: how a wrong is named here, who holds the hour, and what is never tolerated.

**Who holds it.** The companion — a peer walking beside, never an authority — and the [[Guide]]'s office, which is greeting and the handing of the words.

**On paper.** The agreements read aloud, and one line in the record.

**Fuller form.** [[20 The Experience — from the first door to what lies beyond]] and [[18 Pillar XI — Sacred Society and Justice]].

**Status.** Named and unwritten. The corpus says in one place that this process *is designed for each*, and it is not: it was named as work to do, and the honest reading is the second one.

**What would show it wrong.** Companions become gatekeepers; or the process becomes a curriculum at the door, which is the wall it exists to prevent.

### 10 · The Initiation and the Pledge

**What it is.** The crossing at the Gate: a word given where others can hear it, a name chosen, three people named, and [[The Pledge]] — what you will give for the season ahead in time, energy and attention, a reciprocal contribution of any amount to the commons, and the gifts and crafts you bring.

**Why a civilization without it fails.** A threshold crossed once, in front of others, after which both the person and the body treat them differently, is the oldest formation instrument there is. Its absence does not produce people who never cross thresholds; it produces people who cross them alone, unmarked, at the wrong time, with nobody to say what the other side asks. And the specific failure here is sharper: a body whose entry is a signup form learns nothing about whether anyone actually means it.

**Tonight, with a notebook.** The line: *name · date · one word for where you are · who brought you · who witnessed.* Said aloud, written down. Name a real figure in the pledge rather than a generous one — **a pledge quietly missed teaches a table not to trust pledges.**

**Who holds it.** The person gives it; a witness hears it; the [[Carrier]] who brought them never changes.

**On paper.** One line in a book, two names beside it.

**Fuller form.** [[20 The Experience — from the first door to what lies beyond]].

**Status.** Named and unwritten as an instrument — the shape is stated, the form of the rite, the wording, the renewal, and what marks it are not. Whether the Pledge is made once or renewed at each turning is unruled; this Book carries *renewed*, because freshly chosen beats silently assumed.

**What would show it wrong.** Anyone is asked to pledge at the door, before they have played a week. The order is welcome, orientation, time, and only then the step in — **nothing before the Gate asks a commitment, so that everything before it is a gift with no hook in it.**

### 11 · The Path's middle

**What it is.** The stretch between the first kept season and the Sacred Ascent: the sixth and seventh stages, where the Turn is kept until the Way is lived rather than studied and the Pattern is met as a living answer to a real question. In the whole corpus, this is the longest part of a life and the thinnest part of the map.

**Why a civilization without it fails.** The road is well drawn at both ends. The door is one page. The heights have seventy-two Gates in nine movements. Between them sits the part almost everyone will actually inhabit for years, and a person who reaches it finds no answer to the only question they are asking: *where am I, and what is the next stretch?* Bodies lose people there — not to disagreement, and not to hardship, but to the flatness of a road with no markers on it.

**Tonight, with a notebook.** Ask each member the question aloud at the turning and write down the answer: *what could you do at the end of this season that you could not do at its start, and who watched you do it?* Three seasons of those answers is a map of the middle drawn from life.

**Who holds it.** The person walking it; their Cell, who can see what they cannot; whoever is holding the door for them.

**On paper.** A notebook with a page per season.

**Fuller form.** [[20 The Experience — from the first door to what lies beyond]], beside [[The 12 Stages of the Sovereign's Path]] and [[The 144 Gates of Initiation]].

**Status.** Named and unwritten. Two stage orders are in live use and the stages' own grain is uneven: one of the twelve is an evening and another is a lifetime, and they sit as peers in one list.

**What would show it wrong.** The middle, once written, becomes a timetable. **A timetable applied to formation turns a path into an examination, and examinations produce performance rather than becoming.** The pace is sovereign; no Gate fires until it is ripe.

### 12 · The six skills of the Game

**What it is.** The smallest set of things every player should be able to *do*, each taught in about ten minutes and each witnessed by somebody who watched them do it: **take the Roll** · **fill [[The Season Sheet|the Season Sheet]] by hand** · **take the [[The Offering|Offering]]** · **[[THE FLAME — the Book|pass the Flame]]** · **tell [[THE STORY|the Story]] aloud from memory** · **run the retrospective.**

**Why a civilization without it fails.** Everything else in this Book is downstream of those six. A body in which only one person can host, only one can tell the Story, and only one has ever written a sheet is a body with a single point of failure at every joint — and it will discover this in the week that person is ill, has left, or has died. **Formation is the thing that replicates; text is only its trace.**

**Tonight, with a notebook.** Pick one. Teach it to the person beside you. Watch them do it. Write the line: *name · skill · date · what was witnessed · who witnessed it.*

**Who holds it.** The Cell, and the [[Guild]] where one exists.

**On paper.** Two lines in a book, signed by the witnesses. Nothing digital adds anything to it.

**Fuller form.** [[11 Pillar IV — Sacred Learning and Mastery]] for the witness rule, [[THE GAME]] for the moves themselves.

**Status.** Proposal. The belt architecture is the corpus's own; the naming of six specific Game skills as the floor beneath every belt is offered here.

**What would show it wrong.** Six turns out to be the wrong cut — too many for a first season, or missing the one that mattered. The likeliest correction is that *keeping a commitment and naming the gap honestly* belongs on the list and the Offering does not.

### 13 · The children's formation

**What it is.** How a child is formed inside this Pattern: the practices for recognizing a particular genius, what is learned when, the relationship between a child's formation and the guilds, and the threshold at which a young person is asked to carry something real.

**Why a civilization without it fails.** A pattern that can govern, produce, learn and heal but cannot form its own children has bought itself one generation. Everything else on this list regrows from a book; this one does not.

**Tonight, with a notebook.** Nothing large, and nothing invented. The child who can name five birds, or sing five of the old songs, is doing the Pillar's most durable work. Put both elders and children in the room when the place is discussed.

**Who holds it.** The people who will be responsible for the person afterward. **No child is initiated into anything by an absent authority**, and [[Consent]] is not a formality at any age.

**On paper.** All of it.

**Fuller form.** [[11 Pillar IV — Sacred Learning and Mastery]].

**Status.** Named and unwritten, and this Book declines to invent it. What the canon holds is the design requirement — every child born bearing a purpose and a gift, and systems of learning *designed* to discover, develop and empower it. What it does not hold is the curriculum, the practices, the rites, or the relation to the guilds. Whoever builds one builds it by the Pattern, in a real place, with real children, and writes down what they built.

**What would show it wrong.** Any version of this written at a desk, anywhere, by anyone, ahead of the children it is for.

## IV · The thresholds of a life

### 14 · The life-threshold rites, at minimum form

**What it is.** The five crossings a life makes whether or not anyone marks them — **birth · coming of age · marriage · dying · death** — each in a form a Cell with nothing can hold tonight: *announce · witness · change the standing · record.*

**Why a civilization without it fails.** Cultures that lose their rites do not produce people who never cross thresholds. They produce people who cross them alone, at the wrong time, with nobody to say what the other side asks — and who then improvise substitutes, often badly. The change a rite makes is one of *responsibility* before it is one of privilege, which is why a rite that costs nothing confers nothing.

**Tonight, with a notebook.** Any of the five, in four moves. Name the threshold aloud at the table. Have people present, who say what they saw and what they now expect — two is enough. Change something real about what this person carries and is no longer asked to carry, and say it. Write it down.

**Who holds it.** The people who are actually there: the [[Cell]], the kin, the [[Community]]. Not a class of officiants. Where a tradition already holds a rite, **the tradition holds it**, and this Book adds nothing to it and takes nothing away.

**On paper.** Entirely — people in a room, saying true things out loud, and someone writing down that it happened.

**Fuller form.** [[12 Pillar V — Sacred Health and Wellness]].

**Status.** First articulation of a minimum form. The canon names all five — and elsewhere names a wider set including initiation, union, service, renewal and the refounding — and has written none of them.

**What would show it wrong.** The minimum shape is too thin to do the work rites actually do. That is the likeliest defect in this entry, and the recommended answer is not to write thicker rites at a desk but to walk them, record them from the first day, and let the rites be assembled out of a thousand recorded walkings.

### 15 · The rite wheel, and the book of celebrations

**What it is.** The year drawn once on a page: the four turnings around the rim, the body's own feasts marked where they fall, the lunar days as small ticks, and the thresholds this body marked this year written in the middle. Beside it, the openings, closings, blessings and turnings a body says out loud — the forms of celebration, held as a book rather than improvised every time.

**Why a civilization without it fails.** Rhythm without celebration decays into schedule. A festival is the day a people becomes visible to itself, and celebration is the first thing cut in a serious season and the loss least noticed. **Do not cancel the feast:** a people that eats together through a bad winter comes out of it as a people.

**Tonight, with a notebook.** Draw the wheel. Four turnings, your own feasts, and what your body marked this year.

**Who holds it.** The Cell, or the Community at the larger sizes.

**On paper.** One page a year, kept in the box.

**Fuller form.** [[19 Pillar XII — Sacred Time, Rhythm, and Celebration]] and [[14 Pillar VII — Sacred Culture, Art, and Story]].

**Status.** Named and unwritten. The wheel is named twice in the canon and articulated nowhere.

**What would show it wrong.** The frame displaces what a people already keeps. The calendar is a frame that local and inherited festivals hang *inside*; the moment it overwrites one of them it has become the thing it replaced.

## V · Justice, order, and the floor

### 16 · The binding justice process

**What it is.** How a named wrong is heard and repaired: three lines written into the agreements before they are needed (naming · stopping · leaving), the Naming, the Hearing and its seven moves, the same hour held between two Cells with a third person from neither, the hearing of last resort at the supercell, and the order of remedies — **restorative before punitive, always.**

**Why a civilization without it fails.** A body with no process does not therefore have no justice. It has justice by whoever is angriest, in private, late. And the corpus has named restorative justice as the way forward for years while holding, as its entire procedure, four words: *confronted, heard, and guided toward restoration.*

**Tonight, with a notebook.** The three lines, written into the [[Field of Agreements]] before anybody needs them, and two people named in advance to hold the hour who are not parties to anything. That is most of what a small body needs: the first makes harm sayable, the second stops harm now rather than after deliberation, and the third means the other two can be trusted — because **a process you cannot walk out of is not a process; it is custody.**

**Who holds it.** The smallest body that contains everyone the matter touches. The one who hears is never a party.

**On paper.** One page, four lines at its foot — what was named · what was agreed · what could not be repaired · when we look at this again — and two signatures.

**Fuller form.** [[18 Pillar XI — Sacred Society and Justice]], beside [[Mend a Tension]] and [[The Escalation Ladder]].

**Status.** First articulation. Nothing in the older writing contradicts it, and nothing in the older writing contains it.

**What would show it wrong.** The Hearing becomes a court — which shows up as a rising escalation rate, a busy panel, and findings nobody revisits. The numbers in it (seven days to a hearing, three drawn panellists, one revisiting granted on request) are chosen and reasoned, not settled, and should be rewritten within three seasons from what the first bodies actually did.

### 17 · The containment of the dangerous sovereign

**What it is.** What a body does with someone it cannot allow to continue and will not destroy.

**Why a civilization without it fails.** The old world answers with cages and erasure. A body that refuses both and has decided nothing in advance will improvise an answer under pressure, and **improvised answers in that hour are exactly how good communities become cruel ones.** This is the most consequential hole in the whole Pattern.

**Tonight, with a notebook.** The frame, which is all that exists, written down before it is needed: aim at restoration; the minimum force that stops the harm; judging that is local, near and visible rather than distant and procedural; held apart *until trust can be re-established*, which is a condition and not a sentence; nobody judged in their absence or unheard; the harmed never asked to absorb the harm for the body's self-image; and the person remains a person — **what is put out is the conduct and the access, never the human being.**

**Who holds it.** The supercell, never a Cell alone; never a distant body. At least three hold the conditions, and one of them is charged specifically with the contained person's road back.

**On paper.** The conditions, the names of those holding them, and the date of review, on one page, in the open. **A containment known only to those enforcing it has already become something else.**

**Fuller form.** [[17 Pillar X — Sacred Guardianship and Order]] and [[18 Pillar XI — Sacred Society and Justice]]; the concept's page is [[Containment Of The Dangerous Sovereign]].

**Status.** Named and unwritten. Nothing anywhere states who decides, by what process, with what appeal, for how long, or what re-establishing trust consists of. The three limits above — nothing without a date, nobody holds it alone, it never leaves the open — are this Book's proposal, added because this is the instrument most likely to be abused by good people in a bad week.

**What would show it wrong.** Any use of it that cannot be read aloud to the whole body. And the one line that must never bend: [[The Way Out]] is unconditional, including under suspicion. **Safety is never a reason to hold someone. It is the most common one ever given.**

### 18 · The safeguarding of a child inside the body

**What it is.** What a body does about harm to a child originating within itself: who is told, who decides, how the child's own account governs, what is said to the wider body, and what the body owes afterward.

**Why a civilization without it fails.** **A body's real character is legible in how it treats those who have no power to hold it accountable.** Children are the one class of member who arrive without consenting, cannot leave, and are formed by whatever the body happens to be while they are in it. Every movement that has failed here failed the same way: it handled the matter quietly, to protect something it valued more.

**Tonight, with a notebook.** Two lines. Harm to a child is on the floor — never weighed against the body's convenience, its reputation, or its Quest. And a matter of the floor is heard at the supercell rather than in the Cell, because the Cell is too close, and is never secret: not the particulars, which belong to the harmed person, but the fact that it was heard and what was decided.

**Who holds it.** Whoever is present acts first. The supercell hears it. The harmed person governs their own account.

**On paper.** One line in the agreements, read aloud at the door, never softened in the reading.

**Fuller form.** [[18 Pillar XI — Sacred Society and Justice]], beside [[Protection Of Children]].

**Status.** Named and unwritten, and named in this Book as the most consequential absence in its Pillar. Until it is written, the floor and the supercell hearing are what a body has — **and any body with children in it should write its own answer this season and share what it wrote.**

**What would show it wrong.** Any version that makes the child's safety contingent on anybody's process. Where this page and a child's safety appear to conflict, the page is wrong.

### 19 · The recall, the adjudication, and the last resort

**What it is.** Three related instruments, all named and all empty. **Recall** — how a [[Mandate|mandate]] is returned to those who lent it: who may initiate, what threshold removes, and what happens to the steward's work in progress. **[[Adjudication]]** — the judicial function the canon sketches as a council of seven to twelve wise elders, with no procedure by which a matter reaches them, no standard of evidence, and no route of appeal. And **the last resort** — the organ named for what happens when a body has been captured and the captured body is the one that would ordinarily correct it.

**Why a civilization without it fails.** [[Impeachment|Recall]] assumes a body willing to recall; oversight assumes someone still looking; recourse assumes a channel that still works. A federation that has thought about none of these has decided, by silence, that capture will be handled by whoever is standing there.

**Tonight, with a notebook.** The two conditions of recall, kept separate because they are different things. *Betrayal of mandate* is a matter of fact — the steward did what they were not entrusted to do, or failed what they were. *Loss of consent* is a matter of standing — the body no longer consents, whether or not anything was breached. The second is the more important and the more easily forgotten. And the discipline over both: **a removal conducted quietly teaches everyone that power here is settled in private**, which does more damage than any steward ever could.

**Who holds it.** Those who lent the mandate. Never the holder of it.

**On paper.** The rungs of [[The Escalation Ladder]] written before they are needed, ending in [[Arbitration]] by mutual agreement, and the plainly stated line beyond which a matter leaves the body entirely.

**Fuller form.** [[09 Pillar II — Sacred Governance and Stewardship]].

**Status.** Named and unwritten, all three. [[The Intervention Council]] in particular is **a name with nothing behind it** — no composition, no trigger, no limits, no revocability — and it is recorded here as ore so that nobody mistakes a name in a list for a mechanism that has been thought through.

**What would show it wrong.** Everything else in that Pillar exists to make such a power unnecessary, and any articulation of it will have to carry unusually heavy limits. The tell that the drafting has gone wrong is an instrument that would be useful to a good body in an ordinary season.

## VI · The seats, the councils, and the governance of the new powers

### 20 · The living-systems seat, and its hold

**What it is.** A standing seat at a table, held in trust for the living systems a body's decisions affect and that cannot attend — a membership class rather than a topic, with a named human voice sworn to it, beside the seat for future generations and the seat for the Whole. Its one distinctive power is **the hold**: an objection that stops a decision until two members have gone and looked.

**Why a civilization without it fails.** The failure mode of every table is deciding faster than anyone can check, and the land's case is almost always a case about something nobody in the room has recently seen. A body with no seat does not decide against the living world; it decides without it, which produces the same result more politely.

**Tonight, with a notebook.** Name one person, for one season. Before any decision of consequence they walk the ground it touches and answer four questions aloud: *the water · the soil and ground · the creatures · the generations after.* Then one sentence: *what I saw when I went and looked.* The table hears all four before anyone argues. **A seat spoken from without a walk is an opinion, and the body should say so.**

**Who holds it.** One named person, chosen by rotation among those willing to prepare, for one season at Cell scale and no more than a year at any larger scale.

**On paper.** A name on a charter page, a person who walks, four questions, one line in the record. There is no version of this instrument that requires a machine.

**Fuller form.** [[13 Pillar VI — Sacred Ecology and Living Systems]], beside [[Living Systems Member]].

**Status.** First articulation. The canon names the seat in at least five places and has never said how it works — and uses three different words for what it is.

**What would show it wrong.** The hold becomes a standing veto in the hands of whoever wants nothing to change. And the failure to watch for is quieter: **the seat is the first thing quietly vacated under load**, and its vacancy shows up two seasons later in the water. An empty seat is a reportable condition, not a scheduling matter.

### 21 · The Pattern Council — who canonizes a Pattern

**What it is.** The process by which a Pattern enters the Language, improves, and retires: who may propose, what evidence of embodiment is required, who recognizes it, and what happens to the bodies that adopted a Pattern that is later withdrawn.

**Why a civilization without it fails.** The Pattern Language is the keystone — the thing that makes ten thousand tables one body rather than ten thousand experiments. Without a written process, **the Language grows by whoever happens to be writing**, which is a form of centralization that requires no centre and announces itself to nobody.

**Tonight, with a notebook.** The discipline that already exists and works: a Pattern is validated by embodiment rather than by assertion. What worked is kept, what failed is refined or retired, and a body proposing a Pattern says where it ran, for how long, with how many people, and at what cost.

**Who holds it.** Unwritten. A council is named once in the corpus and described nowhere.

**On paper.** A proposal travelling up on line twelve, gathered at a turning, adopted by edition.

**Fuller form.** [[05 The Pattern and the Way]], beside [[How A Pattern Enters The Language]].

**Status.** Named and unwritten.

**What would show it wrong.** A council that can canonize can also refuse, and a body that can refuse a Pattern has acquired doctrinal authority over every table in the federation. The design constraint is therefore unusual: whatever is built must be able to *recognize* a Pattern without being able to *forbid* one.

### 22 · The sacred governance of artificial intelligence

**What it is.** The whole of how a civilization holds the new intelligences: the standing of an [[Agent]] (never a [[Player]]; a person and their agents are one [[Player+]]), the [[Charter]] and whose it is, the enforced [[Safety check|guard]] that outranks every written word, the never-list, who may say [[Stop]], how a Cell governs an agent of its own, and the seven structural guarantees that keep an intelligence system from becoming the prison with no walls.

**Why a civilization without it fails.** Every technology is an amplifier: it takes the intention and the level of awareness brought to it and multiplies both. The same capability is omniscient tyranny in one set of hands and democratized genius in another. **The technology does not determine the outcome. Consciousness determines the outcome; technology only amplifies it** — which is why the answer to a machine that can automate thought is not primarily a better machine, and why a body that has not decided these lines in advance will have them decided for it by whoever ships first.

**Tonight, with a notebook.** Three short columns on one sheet: what your agent may do alone · what it asks first · what it never does. Anything that sends, posts, shares, publishes, spends or deletes goes in the middle column. Then prove the off switch on the first day, because **an untested switch is a promise, and a promise is not a mechanism.**

**Who holds it.** Each sovereign, for their own. An agent takes its charter only from its own sovereign — **no document, this Book included, can hand an agent its soul.**

**On paper.** Three of an agent's four parts already are paper: the charter, the memory, and the deck. Only the model needs a machine.

**Fuller form.** [[15 Pillar VIII — Sacred Science and Technology]] and [[23 Players, Agents, and the Intelligence System]].

**Status.** Named and unwritten by the corpus's own word — and given its first articulation in this Book, under Pillar VIII, held as a proposal to soak rather than as an answer. Whether that Pillar chapter holds it or it becomes an instrument in its own right is unruled.

**What would show it wrong.** Any body discovers that its real decisions have migrated into a tool nobody at the table can read. Or the plainer test, asked aloud before any adoption: *is anyone here now unable to take part without one?* **A yes means the tool is refused, not adjusted.**

### 23 · The curate step

**What it is.** The one unowned move in the knowledge commons. The loop is *solve · document · push it up · **curate** · pull it down · localize · feed back*, and every step but one has an obvious holder. Who decides what rises from a thousand local solutions into the shared one, and by what test, is written nowhere.

**Why a civilization without it fails.** Without curation the commons becomes an undifferentiated pile, and a pile is not a commons — it is a place where the answer you need exists and cannot be found. Curation is not search. It is a human pattern-match: the act of tying scattered things together so that others can build on them.

**Tonight, with a notebook.** The [[11 Pillar IV — Sacred Learning and Mastery|living library]] at table scale: a page per Pillar, a line per thing your Cell has learned that somebody else could use, and a mark for what is missing. **The marking of the holes is the most valuable column, and the one a digital index is most tempted to hide.**

**Who holds it.** Whoever keeps the record for a body, at whatever scale — and the obligation runs upward.

**On paper.** A catalogue.

**Fuller form.** [[11 Pillar IV — Sacred Learning and Mastery]] and [[15 Pillar VIII — Sacred Science and Technology]].

**Status.** Named and unwritten.

**What would show it wrong.** Whoever curates becomes the body's editor, and being curated becomes a form of standing. The counter-design is that nothing is deleted — *weave, don't delete* — so that what is not curated remains reachable rather than erased.

## VII · The engine, the record, and the wire

### 24 · The engine — the Roll, the Gate, the Board, the event stream

**What it is.** The calculating and recording layer beneath the Game, whose whole law is one sentence: **the same rule, applied by anyone to the same record, deck and clock, returns the same answer.** Its parts are named across the corpus — **the Roll**, the line each member gives at the Gate (*name · date · one word · brought by · witnessed by*) · **the Gate**, the door of the Game, which is a different thing from the 144 Gates · **[[The Board]]**, the deck sorted by what is open and by float, the one public picture of a body's work where a request can be seen by someone able to answer it · and **the event stream**, the record as a series of small typed statements, each signed by its author and never rewritten.

**Why a civilization without it fails.** Ten thousand tables can only be one Game if a sheet means the same thing everywhere and a calculation gives the same answer in every hand. Without a stated rule and a way to check an implementation against it, every tool that reads the cards becomes its own dialect, and within two seasons the federation is exchanging numbers nobody can add.

**Tonight, with a notebook.** The Roll line for each member, in a bound book. One index card per piece of work, with its owner, what it waits on, and what waits on it. A wall, and the cards moved across it by hand. **That wall is the Board**; everything faster is a view of it.

**Who holds it.** Each body holds its own record. Nobody holds the engine — which is the point of specifying it rather than building it once and lending it out.

**On paper.** Two arithmetic passes with a pencil are the same two passes a machine makes. **Nothing computed is ever more than a faster reading of the cards.**

**Fuller form.** [[22 Building — the Pattern of Production]] for the deck and the data date; [[20 The Experience — from the first door to what lies beyond]] for the Gate and the Roll.

**Status.** Named and unwritten as one instrument; specified in parts across several pages; built nowhere. Three commitment-state vocabularies are live at once in those parts, and **the record cannot mean three things.** A conformance deck — a small set of cards with known answers that any implementation must reproduce — is named and has never been made, and it is the single thing that would keep one rule one rule.

**What would show it wrong.** Two bodies compute different answers from the same cards and neither can say which is right. That day, the conformance deck stops being optional.

### 25 · The identity floor

**What it is.** What is minimally required for a person to be on the Roll, and what is required for a naming to count.

**Why a civilization without it fails.** Set the floor too low and the Roll can be filled with people who do not exist; set it too high and it excludes exactly the places that need this most, and creates the pooled artefact the membrane exists to prevent. This is not a technical question wearing a moral costume. It is the question of who is allowed to belong.

**Tonight, with a notebook.** The lightest floor: a name as chosen, a [[Carrier]], and a witness. Three facts, written by hand, in a book the Cell keeps and a person may carry away.

**Who holds it.** The person's own Cell. **The Roll is a record, not a membership** — and that reading itself is an answer this Book recommends and does not own.

**On paper.** One line.

**Fuller form.** [[24 The Plan — the first three percent]].

**Status.** Named and unruled. Three options stand: the lightest floor; a verified channel, which is heavier and excludes; a cryptographic identity, which is heaviest, excludes more, and pools. This Book carries the lightest, plus weighting of namings by the demonstrated record of the one naming, plus the rule that the person-graph never pools — so that gaming is cheap to attempt and worth very little.

**What would show it wrong.** A body finds it cannot tell a Cell from a rumour. The answer then is not heavier identity but the older check: lineage. Ask for line 1 and line 2 of their last sheet, a sheet with two host signatures and a line count, who their Carrier was and who that Carrier's was, and their door. **Two independent confirmations of the same lineage is as good as it gets on paper, and it is good enough.**

### 26 · The trust graph's promotion rules

**What it is.** The rules of movement between the three tiers of disclosure — public counts and stories · what Cells share Cell-to-Cell by mutual consent · the person-graph, held locally by the people it names and **never pooled.** Specifically: who may see a naming; whether a nominee may see who named them, before or after they accept; whether a community may see its own aggregate without seeing its edges; and what happens to a naming when the namer withdraws it.

**Why a civilization without it fails.** A movement that grows by people naming people accumulates something precious and something dangerous, and they are the same object. The same map that lets a body find its own people lets an adversary find them first. **This is the most dangerous unspecified thing in the Plan, because it specifies itself by default the moment the first thousand names exist** — whatever the first tool happens to do becomes the rule, and nobody will have decided it.

**Tonight, with a notebook.** Six fields and no more: name as chosen · the circle they came through · who carried them · the Pillar their gift sits in · the last contact *season* rather than a date · and consent to be named — yes, no, or unasked. No contact details, ever. **A register with dates is a surveillance record; a register with seasons is a memory.** And one rule above the six: a name crosses from the private register to the public web only on a recorded yes, never inferred, never reported on someone's behalf. **A *no* and an *unasked* look identical from outside, and that is the point.**

**Who holds it.** Each Cell for its own; nobody for anybody else's.

**On paper.** Best on paper, and beneath every digital layer the analog tree: each member knows their three and is known by theirs, in their own hand, tested once a season by running a message through it.

**Fuller form.** [[17 Pillar X — Sacred Guardianship and Order]] and [[24 The Plan — the first three percent]]; the concept's page is [[The Trust Graph and the analog contact-tree]], beside [[The nomination laws]].

**Status.** Named and unwritten. The principle — three tiers, never pooled, tightening as the jurisdiction darkens — is settled. The promotion rules are not.

**What would show it wrong.** Any rule that makes a person's naming visible to them as a scoreboard. And the subtler risk the corpus has not answered: **the shape of a trust network is partly readable from public counts alone**, which means the public tier itself needs a threshold nobody has set.

### 27 · The recovery marker

**What it is.** The stated milestone a body re-anchors to when a marker has been missed — a thing complete, to a stated quality, by a date somebody gives their word for.

**Why a civilization without it fails.** Contingency that is consumed is never safely banked, because the window is read rather than calculated and may close at an unknown hour. A plan with honest recording of lateness and no recovery marker slowly converts honesty into habit: the body records that it is behind, every season, accurately, forever, and stops being surprised by it. **Past the marker, say you are behind — and replan to the milestone before you move it. A date moved quietly is a date that never existed.**

**Tonight, with a notebook.** At the turning, say aloud of every Quest that will not finish: *continuing*, or *laid down*, and why. Never carried silently into the next season as though it had not slipped. That is the same instrument at table scale, and it is the one that teaches the larger one.

**Who holds it.** Whoever holds the plan, at the scale the marker belongs to.

**On paper.** A bar on the wall showing the buffer as it is spent, redrawn at each turning, beside the critical path. **A buffer discovered to be gone at the end was never a contingency; it was optimism with a number attached.**

**Fuller form.** [[24 The Plan — the first three percent]] and [[22 Building — the Pattern of Production]].

**Status.** Named as missing, and unwritten. Two further things about the long contingency are unsettled: whether the twenty years run from the end of the ten-year strategy or absorb it — the two readings differ by a decade — and who monitors the buffer, on what surface, and how the remainder is computed.

**What would show it wrong.** The recovery marker becomes a second target that is also missed, which is how a plan acquires two kinds of lateness instead of one.

### 28 · The health record as the person's own

**What it is.** The statement that a person's health record exists, belongs to them, is held by them, and moves nowhere without their word — per instance, not once and forever.

**Why a civilization without it fails.** Care requires memory, and memory about a body is the most intimate record a civilization keeps. Whoever holds it holds the person. Every system that has pooled it — for efficiency, for research, for safety — has produced a file about a person that the person cannot read, correct, or take away.

**Tonight, with a notebook.** The Cell's rule, stated in the agreements: **nothing in the shared record says anything about a member's health that the member did not put there.** And the practical floor beside it, which every Cell should have on an index card before it is needed: the floor of care in one sentence, three names and three numbers, and what this Cell does not do.

**Who holds it.** The person. Nobody else, ever. The private and the shared are different objects, and the person's own word is the only bridge between them.

**On paper.** Their own notebook, in their own hands.

**Fuller form.** [[12 Pillar V — Sacred Health and Wellness]].

**Status.** Assumed throughout this Book and stated on no page of the corpus — which is exactly the condition under which a default gets set by whatever software arrives first.

**What would show it wrong.** A body finds it cannot help a member in a crisis because it knows nothing. That is a real cost and it is the right cost: the answer is a person who was told, not a file that was kept.

### 29 · The Living Systems Record

**What it is.** The ecological record of a place: the same measures, taken at the same spot in the same season by the same hands, kept long enough to show a direction — soil, water, and what lives here — and laid beside the records of other Cells sharing one ecoregion.

**Why a civilization without it fails.** A body that cannot say whether its place is getting better or worse is guessing, and guessing is the condition every extractive arrangement depends on. Three seasons of honest readings show a direction. Seven show something almost no institution knows about that ground.

**Tonight, with a notebook.** The spade and the jar: dig one spade's depth in the same corner each turning, count the worms, measure the dark layer in finger-widths, smell it, time how long a known amount of water takes to sink in, squeeze a handful. Five lines, four minutes. Then the stream: clarity, smell, colour, the speed of a floating stick over ten paces, and what lives under three lifted stones — **the small crawling life in a stream is the most honest water-quality instrument that exists, and it is free.** Then ten minutes, same hour, same place: birds by kind, pollinators on one square, plants inside a hoop. And the list of what has stopped arriving, kept from the memories of the oldest people you can reach.

**Who holds it.** The Cell, in its own notebook; the ecoregional body when Cells lay their readings side by side.

**On paper.** It never left paper. A spade, a jar, a stick, a net, a notebook, a pencil.

**Fuller form.** [[13 Pillar VI — Sacred Ecology and Living Systems]].

**Status.** The hand measures are first articulation; the *record* is named and unwritten — the canon names an ecological data model in six places and has written neither the model nor the record. Whether the measures are required of every Cell claiming ecological work or offered as practice is unruled; this Book writes them as offered, and only their reporting as a standard.

**What would show it wrong.** A number becomes a target. **The fastest way to raise a worm count is to stop looking honestly.** Three readings before anyone speaks of a trend; seven before anyone is confident; and the reading written down in the seasons when it is bad, because a Cell that records only good seasons has built a scrapbook rather than a record.

### 30 · The story crafting guide — the telling that carries

**What it is.** The craft of turning what happened into something that travels: how to tell a season so that a body two thousand miles away can judge whether the thing transfers — **witness, not marketing.**

**Why a civilization without it fails.** One story leaves every table every season, under a hundred and fifty words, written by whoever lived it. That is the sinew of the whole federation, and most people have never been taught to do it. Untaught, the line fills with either a shy blank or a small advertisement, and a body that reads its own advertisements believes its own myth within a year.

**Tonight, with a notebook.** Three disciplines, and they are most of the craft. **Keep the [[Play by Play]]** — a running account written while the work happens, before anybody knows what would make a good ending. **Carry the conditions**: what was done, where, with how many people, at what cost, and what did not work. **And let whoever lived it write it** — the moment a Cell's account of a member's transformation is written by somebody else, it has stopped being testimony and become a case study, and the person in it has become material.

**Who holds it.** The [[Bard]]'s office, rotated each season; and the teller, always, for their own story.

**On paper.** A person, a notebook, and a memory.

**Fuller form.** [[14 Pillar VII — Sacred Culture, Art, and Story]] and [[29 The Stories — ten trillion stories woven back into ONE]].

**Status.** Named and unwritten. The canon commissions this guide by name and has not written it.

**What would show it wrong.** The guide produces a house style. **Keep the teller's own words even when they are awkward — especially then.** And nothing in the Game ranks tellings: the Scorecard marks *that* a story was told, because the habit matters, and counting them is how a body begins optimizing the one thing that dies on contact with a number.

### 31 · The federation starter

**What it is.** How three or more Cells that have become one another's [[Home Cell|Home]] actually constitute a [[Supercell]]: how they name who holds the hour between them, how a joint sheet is read, what the supercell may ask and may never ask, and how a Cell leaves one.

**Why a civilization without it fails.** What travels upward — the sheet, a share of the [[The Offering|Offering]], proposals — and what travels downward — service, guidance, and the plan of the whole — is settled. How the thing is *formed* is not. So the first federation in any place will be built by whoever is most confident, and confidence builds centres.

**Tonight, with a notebook.** The test, which is one sentence: a Cell that joins a supercell notices that **nothing about its own table changed.** And the health check, which is the moment a supercell proposes something a Cell does not want and the Cell says no — and is still a member.

**Who holds it.** The Cells, jointly. The supercell holds no authority that did not come from them, which is why nothing is lost when one is cut off.

**On paper.** The sheets, carried by hand; one page back down at each turning holding four things and nothing else.

**Fuller form.** [[09 Pillar II — Sacred Governance and Stewardship]].

**Status.** Named and unwritten. The canon commissions a federation starter by name.

**What would show it wrong.** A supercell that decides things. **The test of a stewardship body's health is how rarely it decides anything**, and the same test applies here.

### 32 · The wire — radio, mesh, and what may cross them

**What it is.** The specific page on reaching other bodies when the ordinary network is gone: short-range mesh, packet over amateur bands, what is lawful where, what equipment, what schedule, and what must never be sent.

**Why a civilization without it fails.** Not because the wire is essential — it is not. **The floor is a person walking with a letter**, and every radio is a convenience above the runner. The failure is subtler: a body that has not thought about the wire in advance will reach for one in the hour, badly, and will put on an open channel the names, the stores, and the routes that must never cross one.

**Tonight, with a notebook.** The three that matter more than the equipment. **Schedule beats channel** — agree the next window, same hour, same days, at the end of every contact. **Read-back is the whole of the error correction** — the receiver repeats the numbers and the proper names, the sender confirms or corrects. **The count is the checksum a person can do** — every message ends with a count, and if the counts disagree the message is re-sent rather than interpreted.

**Who holds it.** Whoever in a body actually knows the equipment, and the licence, where one is required — **which takes an evening's study and cannot be obtained during an emergency.**

**On paper.** The numbered lines of the Season Sheet sent as numbers: *line number, colon, value, stop.* A whole season's account fits in a message a person can key by hand. **A standard small enough to send by hand is a standard that can never be taken away.**

**Fuller form.** [[21 In Every Future — what to do in each case]].

**Status.** Described generally and deliberately vaguely, and named as needing a real page written by someone who knows the equipment, with jurisdictions named.

**What would show it wrong.** Any version that reads as encouragement to transmit. **Radio is not private**; assume everything sent is heard by anyone in range and apply the [[Chatham House]] discipline to every word. Never send the names of people who have not consented to be named, the location of stores, or the routes runners take.

Anyone building one of the above builds it that way, or builds it again later.

## (a) The instruments in the order a Cell meets them

Nothing on this list is needed at the beginning. This is the order in which the need actually arrives.

**The first night.** None of them. A Cell needs people, a Quest, a day of the week, and a notebook. If it reaches for anything here it is **14** — the four moves of a threshold — because somebody at that table is already crossing one.

## (b) What must be settled before launch

Ten, in the order the cost of getting them wrong falls due. Each is one decision; several have a recommendation this Book carries and does not own.

1. **The identity floor** (**25**). Lightest, verified, or cryptographic. *Carried here:* the lightest, with weighted namings and no pooling. It is cheap to change early and nearly impossible to change late.
2. **The promotion rules of the trust graph** (**26**). It will be decided by default the moment there are names, and the default will be whatever the first tool happens to do.
3. **The containment frame** (**17**). Who writes it, and from whose actual experience. It should be written by people who have had to do it, and it should be written before it is needed rather than in the week it is.
4. **The safeguarding answer** (**18**). Any body with children in it, this season, in its own words.
5. **The justice process as written** (**16**). Adopt as current best understanding, adopt with amendment, or refuse and leave the gap open. Whether numbers belong in it at all, or each body sets its own within a stated range.
6. **The standing of the governance of artificial intelligence** (**22**). Adopted as the instrument, held as a proposal to soak, or returned for rewriting — and by whom.
7. **The allocation rule** (**2**). A first draft stands; nothing else does.
8. **The healers' guild and the rites** (**5**, **14**). Whether the guild as specified is adopted, and who is asked to write the guidebook; and whether the five rites are commissioned now or deliberately walked first and written after. *Carried here:* the second, with the walking recorded from the first day.
9. **The living-systems seat's power** (**20**). Is the hold — an objection that stops a decision until two members have gone and looked — too strong, too weak, or right; and does it exist at Cell scale or only from community upward. The hold has no name on purpose; naming it is an answer, not a draft.
10. **The recovery marker** (**27**), and with it whether the long contingency runs from the end of the ten-year plan or absorbs it. The float on that chain is thin either way, and the difference is a decade.

Every one of these is a meaning-level call. None of them is settled by having been written down here.

## (c) Open

**Questions this chapter itself waits on.**

- **May a page in this library carry a first articulation** — something written here before it exists anywhere else — and if so, how is it marked so that nobody mistakes it for inheritance? This chapter assumes yes, and marks every one. If the answer is no, a third of the Book changes.
- **Where the register lives.** This chapter holds the civilizational altitude; the body's workbench holds a commission list at table altitude; several Pillar chapters hold their own gaps in their Open sections. Three registers, one subject. An answer would say which is the one that is kept, and whether the others become views of it.
- **Whether an instrument may be adopted from outside.** Several holes here have worked instruments in other bodies of practice — a cross-body risk pool, tiered amendment bars with a reflection period, a term of years staggered and renewable once. Adopting one as the canon's own, or continuing to carry it as somebody else's, is unruled, and the difference matters for what the Book may say in its own voice.
- **What happens to a body that adopted a first articulation that is later withdrawn.** Nothing anywhere states it, and the answer is not obvious.

**Contradictions found, recorded rather than repaired.**

- **Twelve archetypal guilds and twenty-four trade domains.** Both are carried in the corpus; the counts do not reconcile; no page maps a guild to a Pillar.
- **Three commitment-state vocabularies**, live at once in three pages written on the same day. The record cannot mean three things.
- **Two readings of the Playcard** — received and never filled in, or refreshed each Turn and kept in a stack. One of the two has to go, or the two senses have to be named as two objects.
- **The seed's own two statements about artificial intelligence.** It names the sacred governance of it as unwritten in one part and legislates for agents in another; which layer each statement governs has never been said on a page.
- **The interoperability standards.** Four lists circulate under one name — seven, seven different, ten, and ten older. A body reading two of them will meet two floors.
- **The engine's nouns.** Whether a Cell's Board is the whole Board one altitude down or a second object beside it is left open, and two nouns are live in the meantime.

**What this chapter is least sure of.**

- **Whether a register of holes helps or harms.** Written well, it is the instrument that keeps the Book honest. Written badly, it is a list of reasons a good body might decide it is not ready — which is the opposite of everything the Pattern holds, because a body becomes ready by playing rather than by preparing.
- **Whether the thirty-two are the right thirty-two.** They were gathered from what the corpus names and from where the Pillar chapters had to invent to be usable. An instrument nobody has yet missed will not appear on a list assembled that way, and the most important missing instrument is, almost by definition, the one nothing has yet named.
- **The ordering in (a).** It is inferred from the shape of a first season rather than observed in one. The first ten Cells that walk it will reorder it, and should.
- **And the honest one.** Every entry above was written by reading rather than by doing. That is the exact failure mode the chapter names in its own opening discipline, and it is unavoidable for a first edition and unacceptable for a second. **This chapter is the part of the Book most certain to be wrong, and the part most likely to be improved fastest**, because every row on it is a piece of work somebody can begin this week.
