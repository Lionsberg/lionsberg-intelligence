# Building — the Pattern of Production

*How anything actually gets built here: a Cell's first Quest, a community's programme, a bioregion's plan — one pattern of production, at every scale, run by people who command no one. The five ideas of integrated delivery, the cycle from could to did, the plan pulled backward from the end, the promise and the gap, the cards, the deck, and what to do when the power is out.*

---

## 1. Four people and a lot

Start with something small enough to see all of.

Four neighbours want the vacant lot at the end of the street to be a garden by the end of summer. Nobody is in charge of them. Nobody can be fired. There is no budget beyond what they put on the table themselves, and each of them has about four hours a week to give.

Here is what they do, and it is the whole of this chapter in miniature.

**They say what done means.** Not *improve the lot* — that could be anything, and in a year they would still be arguing about whether it happened. They write one sentence: *by the last week of summer, twelve raised beds are planted, watered, and being tended by at least six households, and the corner is safe enough for a child to be in alone.* That sentence is now the thing. It does not change because the work got hard.

**They start at the end and walk backwards.** Not *what do we do first* — that is how everyone plans, and it is why everyone discovers the problem too late. They ask: *for six households to be tending beds in the last week of summer, what has to be true immediately before that?* The beds have to be built and filled. For that, soil has to arrive. For that, someone has to have somewhere to put it, and the ground has to be cleared. For that, they have to be allowed on the lot. And somewhere in that backwards walk, before anyone has done a single hour of work, one of them says out loud: *who owns this lot, actually?*

That question, asked in week one instead of week nine, is most of the value of the method. Planning forward hides it. Planning backward surfaces it, because every step is named by the step that needs it.

**They find what could stop them, early.** The permission from whoever owns the lot. Water — is there a spigot, and does it run. A truck, for one Saturday. Each of those is written on a line with a name beside it and a date by which it will be gone. They are not tasks in the garden. They are the things that have to be cleared before the garden work can honestly be promised, and clearing them is real work that someone owns.

**They promise only what is ready.** At the table on Sunday, each of them says one sentence: *I will do this, by then.* Nobody says *I'll try to get to the soil thing.* Nobody is assigned anything. Each says what they will do, because they are the one who knows what their week actually holds, and because the promise is going to be answered in front of the others in seven days.

**They answer for it, and they learn from the difference.** The next Sunday each says what they said, whether they did it, and — if not — what actually happened. Not *I am sorry*. What happened. Three weeks in, the same kind of miss appears twice, and when they follow it down it turns out that the person who owns the truck was never actually asked, because everyone assumed someone else had. That is not a flaw in a person. It is a flaw in how they were working, and it is cheap to fix once it is visible.

**They count.** Eleven things promised this month, eight kept. They write the fraction down. It is not a grade and nobody is ranked by it. It is the one thing that tells them, honestly and early, whether they are a group that can be built on — and by the third month it is nine in eleven, not because anyone tried harder but because they stopped promising things that were never ready.

The garden is planted by the last week of summer.

Nothing in that account is management. There is no manager, no schedule handed down, no authority anywhere in it. And yet the four of them did, at their size, every single thing that the largest and most complex programmes on Earth do at theirs — the ones that build hospitals under receivership with lives on the clock, the ones that put several billion dollars of work through one integrated team. The moves are the same moves. That is not a coincidence and it is not a metaphor. **It is one pattern, and it is scale-free.**

This is worth sitting with, because the opposite is one of the oldest and best-tended stories in [[The Caves]] — *the chains were difficult to break, for they were constructed out of story rather than steel* — and the story runs like this: that ordinary people can manage a kitchen and a hobby, and that anything larger requires a hierarchy, a professional class, a budget, and someone's permission. It is not true. What large works actually require is not command. It is **a method for seeing the whole, keeping promises, and learning fast** — and every part of that method fits on paper, fits in an hour a week, and belongs to whoever picks it up.

That method is what the rest of this chapter is.

## 2. The Pattern of Production

### 2.1 Why cooperation alone does not build

Eight of the [[Twelve Irreducible Elements]] say how sovereign beings organize, govern, and protect their shared work ([[The Pattern of Cooperation]]). They are necessary and they are not sufficient. A body can be sovereign, consented, beautifully governed, warm to sit in — and arrive at the end of a season with nothing it can point to. Four more Elements say how organized beings actually *build*, and together they are [[The Pattern of Production]]:

- **[[Integrated Delivery]]** — every Cell is a node in one production system, and the Whole is optimized rather than the pieces.
- **[[Design To The Goal]]** — all design is constrained by [[The Goal]] rather than by budget or politics; target quality does not fall, and cost flexes through invention.
- **[[Networks of Commitments]]** — work is coordinated as promises freely made, faithfully kept, and honestly reported.
- **[[Tightly Coupled Learning and Action]]** — learning is fused to action, so that every cycle teaches the next.

Remove [[Integrated Delivery]] and Cells work in isolation and their Quests never aggregate. Remove [[Design To The Goal]] and local optimization quietly displaces planetary purpose, with nobody at fault. Remove [[Networks of Commitments]] and good intentions never become reliable results. Remove [[Tightly Coupled Learning and Action]] and the system cannot adapt, and eventually shatters.

The cardinal insight underneath all four is short, and expensive to learn: **you cannot optimize the pieces and expect the Whole to work.** Every station running at peak efficiency, every organization excellent by its own measures, and the total outcome still fragmented — that is the ordinary condition of the Old World, and it is not caused by bad people. The ministry of health does not speak to the ministry of education; the agency for water knows nothing of the agency for food, though water and food are one system. A body whose organs do not communicate is not a body. It is a collection of dying parts.

### 2.2 Value, and its opposite

Before any of the machinery, one definition, because everything downstream depends on it.

**[[Value]] is [[Throughput]] of [[The Goal]].** Not what someone will pay for. Not activity, however skilled. Not hours, however sincere. What moves the Goal is Value; what does not is **[[Waste]]**, however profitable and however busy.

Waste is worse than idleness, because it does not merely fail to help — it occupies the path, consuming the time, attention, and material that the work which would have helped now cannot have. Naming it is not an insult and not a judgment on anyone; it is the ordinary first step of improvement, and it is sought in the set-up rather than in the person, because a body where naming waste costs somebody status will simply stop naming it ([[Zero Blame Environment]]).

Value takes many forms and most of them are not money — a repaired relationship, a skill passed on, a watershed restored, a child who learns the world can be trusted. So the counting is done across six kinds of capital at once: living, social, human, intellectual, built, financial, counted together, so that no one grows rich in money while going bankrupt in the things that matter more.

This definition is the hinge of the whole chapter. It is what makes it fair to ask of any activity, at any scale, *what does this serve?* — and it is what lets a body decline work that pays.

### 2.3 The five ideas

The delivery lineage compresses to five, and they are worth learning in their plainest form:

1. **Collaborate — really collaborate.** Not *coordinate*. Not *keep each other informed*. The people who will do the work, in the same room, at the same time, deciding together, early enough for the decisions to still be cheap.
2. **Increase right relationship among all participants.** Trust is not a mood. It is a structural output, and it is produced on purpose: by shared fate, by a written covenant, by a rhythm people can rely on, and by promises kept where others can see.
3. **Programmes are networks of commitments.** Not a chain of instructions. The unit of progress is not a task completed; it is a word given and kept.
4. **Optimize for the Whole, not the pieces.** Every local excellence is checked against what it does to the whole, and a body willingly slows one part to unblock another.
5. **Tightly couple learning with action.** No planning department separate from the doing. Every action generates feedback; every feedback improves the next action; the loop runs at every scale, from an hour to a generation.

The lineage is checkable and worth naming rather than hiding: these came out of the production systems that build the largest works on Earth — integrated project delivery, lean construction, the Toyota Production System, Deming's application of the scientific method to production — tested across billion-dollar programmes with substantial funded research behind them, and carried here intact because they survive translation to a kitchen table. The pattern was found, not invented. **Honour the lineage without owning it** is the rule, and it is also the reason the claim can be checked rather than believed.

### 2.4 The cycle: could · should · can · will · did

Every piece of work in this system passes through five states. Two of them are computed from the plan. Two are a person's word. One is the honest pool of everything else.

**Could.** Everything a living body might do that is on no chain to any marker. It is real, it is kept, it is not required now. Naming it as a *place* rather than as a failure is what keeps the possible from crowding the week. Most bodies have no such place, so every good idea has to be either adopted or killed on the spot, and both are wrong.

**Should.** What the plan asks for: work on a chain to a [[Milestone]], pulled backward from the end. Should is a computation over the plan, not anyone's enthusiasm.

**Can.** Should, plus constraint-free: every predecessor complete, every open condition closed, the hands and materials actually in reach, the work defined well enough that a reliable promise is possible. This is what [[The look-ahead]] and the [[Make-Ready Process]] exist to make true.

**Will.** A [[Commitment]] — *I will ___ by ___* — a person's or a Cell's word, said aloud to someone who will depend on it. **A WILL is consciously given and is never inferred**: not from a plan, not from a calendar, not from a conversation that sounded like agreement, and never entered on someone's behalf.

**Did.** What happened, written in [[The Record]], with the gap beside it.

Each arrow is a different body's move. The plan draws *could* into *should*. An owner clears constraints and makes *should* into *can*. The doer turns *can* into *will*, at the table. The record turns *will* into *did*. Skip a stage and the failure is predictable: promise what is not ready, or do faithfully what nothing needed.

### 2.5 The last planner

The name of the discipline is its argument.

**The last planner is the person closest to the work** — the one whose hands will do it. The plan that matters is theirs; the promise that matters is theirs; and the single move from which everything else follows is putting the planning and the promising *there* rather than above.

The reason is not sentiment, it is fidelity. A command loses something at every level it descends — context, judgment, the reason it was issued — until the person at the bottom receives an instruction they had no hand in shaping and no stake in fulfilling beyond the fear of consequence. That system produces, reliably, about half of what it plans. In programmes spending billions. With professional managers, sophisticated software, and penalties for failure. Half.

A promise made by a sovereign who understands the work, judges what they can deliver, and stakes their own name on it carries the person's whole weight. That is why a body that has refused command can still be reliable to itself, and it is the only way a body that has refused command *can* be.

### 2.6 Pulling the plan backward

This is the most powerful planning discipline anyone has found, and it can be run by four people with sticky notes.

**How it is run.** Put the [[Milestone]] on the wall — the thing that must be true, stated so plainly that nobody could disagree about whether it happened. Gather the people who will do the work; not their representatives. Give it real time: at least four hours for anything that matters. Each person writes the activities they will own, one per card, with a duration in days and what must be finished before it can start. Then arrange the cards backward from the milestone.

Then the part that does the work: **argue about the handoffs.** *What do you need from me, finished, so that you can start?* The answer is written down — that is a [[Conditions of satisfaction|condition of satisfaction]], and it is written by the one who will *receive* the work, not by the one who will do it. When the wall reaches today, walk it forward once and check the dates.

What comes out of the room is [[The activity table]]: one line per activity — what it is, how long it takes, what comes before and after it ([[Predecessor - Successor|predecessor and successor]], written both ways so the network reads in either direction by hand), who owns it, what it serves, when it must be finished, and where it stands. A notebook holds it. Thirty activities take an hour with a pencil.

**What is computed from it.** Two passes over the rows, forward and then backward, yield every activity's [[Float]] — the time it can slip without moving the end. The rows with none are the **[[Critical Path]]**: the chain that sets the date. Attention is the scarcest good a body has, and the critical path is the honest answer to where it belongs. Hands lent from a slack chain to the tight one buy days; hands added anywhere else buy nothing.

Float is *found*, not granted, and it belongs to an activity's place in the logic rather than to the activity itself. Total float is how far a thing can slip before the end moves; free float is how far before its successor is delayed, and the second can never exceed the first. When a finish-by date inherited from a milestone cannot be met by the logic, float goes **negative** — the plan is late by that many days before a single day of work is done. That is lateness made visible early, which is the entire point, and the honest response is to re-pull: shrink a handoff, add hands, remove a constraint.

Time a body sets aside on purpose is not float; it is a **buffer**. Padding hidden inside each activity protects nothing, because it is spent silently. A buffer is pooled, visible, and owned by the chain as a whole: one at the end of the critical chain protecting the milestone, and one where a feeding chain joins the main one, so that lateness in a side chain does not become lateness in the whole. Once hands and capacity have been fitted to what people can actually carry, the same chain is called the **critical chain** — the path as it can really be walked, rather than as the arithmetic imagined it.

And it is run again, not once. At each turning a body re-pulls from its milestones. When a marker is missed it re-pulls *before* the milestone is moved.

### 2.7 Make-ready: the step almost everyone skips

Between the plan and the promise there is one step, and the bodies that do not take it are the bodies whose word cannot be relied on.

**Each week, look about six weeks ahead.** Of everything crossing into that window, ask one question: *what could stop this from being ready?* Predecessors complete? Materials, tools, and hands available and competent? Decision made? Work defined well enough to promise? Every *no* is a **[[Constraint]]**, and it goes on a five-column log that lives beside the activity table: constraint · what it blocks · owner · remove-by · status.

The look-ahead *finds*; make-ready *removes*. Between one gathering and the next, the owner chases the decision, secures the material, books the hands, asks the question nobody had asked. At the next gathering each line is reported closed or still standing, with what it now needs.

**Only constraint-free work is released to the week.** Nothing about this is clever. Its power is entirely that it is done every week, out loud, by name.

It is also what makes a commitment a genuinely free act. A person asked to promise constraint-free work can answer honestly. A person asked to promise blocked work can only guess — and when the date passes, the gap gets read as a failure of character when it was a failure of preparation. Make-ready moves the failure upstream, where it is cheap.

Six weeks is a working figure, held as a practice rather than a law: wide enough to clear most obstacles, short enough that the answers are still knowable. What a Cell cannot clear becomes a request to its home, and the same loop runs from a kitchen table to the programme of a world.

### 2.8 The promise

*I will ___ by ___*, said aloud, to someone who will depend on it.

Reliability is a matter of degree, and what raises it is known. The conditions that make a promise worth what it costs to make:

- **The conditions of satisfaction are clear to both sides, before the work.** Not approximately. *By clean I mean swept, dishes done, counters clear.*
- **The one promising is competent and equipped** — the skill, the knowledge, the tools, the information, in hand rather than expected.
- **The time is estimated and actually blocked**, not competing with three other promises made the same evening. A promise without protected time is a wish.
- **Sincerity is present in the moment of speaking** — genuinely intending to deliver, rather than performing agreement.
- **The work is constraint-free** — it has been through make-ready and come out clean.
- **The one promising knows who waits on it**, and accepts what a miss costs downstream. Not punishment. Consequence: the effect on people who built their own promises on top of this one.
- **The Cell holds joint responsibility** for creating the conditions in which the promise can be kept. Nobody promises in isolation.
- **And there is the honesty to say early when confidence is lost.**

That last one deserves its own name, because it is the honesty floor beneath everything: **a promise is not a prison.** Circumstances change; a constraint appears that nobody saw; a better reading of what serves the Goal arrives. What is asked then is neither silent failure nor grim compliance but open renegotiation — name the commitment, say what changed, say what you can do instead, and let the one who was counting on it replan. Done in time, a broken commitment costs a conversation. Done at the due date, it costs the whole chain behind it. And beneath every particular commitment stands the standing one, to the Goal: a promise that has come to work against it is broken consciously rather than kept obediently. Keeping a word that has become harm is not integrity.

Two more rules about where promises live.

**Commitments run body to body, not person to person, wherever the work is large enough to matter.** A Cell commits to a Cell. The weight of the critical chain never rests on one person, and there are no single points of failure. Inside a Cell, every activity still has one owner whose word it is — but the promise that crosses to another table is the table's.

**Requests, offers, and commitments are the only things that cross a membrane — never a task.** *We need three volunteers on Saturday.* *We have a van and a Tuesday evening.* Two sentence shapes, said at the table, written on the card, travelling every week, up and down and sideways. Nobody dispatches anybody. A request simply is, and anyone who can answer it may; an offer sits in the open until someone needs it. **That is how a body allocates itself without a centre** — and it preserves the one thing an allocation office would destroy, which is that every yes is still a free yes from a sovereign table ([[Requests and offers]]). A task handed across a membrane is an instruction, and instructions are not the currency here.

### 2.9 Quality, at the handoff

Quality is a design decision, not an inspection.

**The one who will receive the work writes what makes it acceptable, before the work begins.** The doer checks it against that before handing it over. At the milestone, the whole is tested under real load — the conditions it will actually meet, not the conditions convenient to test in.

Put the definition first and nothing later can trade it away. Put it last and quality becomes whatever the schedule left room for, which is how a body arrives at its milestone with something that passes review and fails in use — the expensive kind of failure, and the kind that teaches slowest.

**The successor is the customer, and the handoffs are where systems fail.** Every handoff carries its conditions with it rather than merely an arrow. Conditions nest: a task's answer upward to the Quest or milestone it serves, a Quest's to the plan above it, and all of them to the one condition the whole is measured by — that what is co-created measurably serves the Goal. A condition met while working against that has not truly been met.

This is also where [[Design To The Goal]] becomes enforceable at a table. The Goal and the target quality are fixed; the budget enters at the start as one constraint among others, where it can be designed *with* rather than cut *against*; and when the numbers will not meet, the answer is invention — a different approach, sequence, material, or set of hands — rather than a quieter version of the same thing. Several viable options are kept alive until the last responsible moment ([[Set-Based Design]]), and the choice between them is made on their advantages rather than on preference or seniority ([[Advantage Based Decision Making]]). A Cell that instead quietly lowers what *done* means has not adapted. It has stopped being able to tell whether it succeeded.

### 2.10 The Turn as the production meeting

The weekly gathering is not a status report bolted onto the Game. It *is* the production meeting, and it is also the hour in which a Cell is a Cell. Both at once, in an hour or two, on the same day each week ([[The Turn]], [[The Heartbeat]]).

Open in silence. Then, in turn, each person says four things:

- **what I said I would do, and whether I did it**;
- **the gap, honestly, and what I learned from it**;
- **what I will do this week**;
- **what I have to offer, and what I ask.**

Then the Cell rolls [[The look-ahead]] one week forward and works the constraint log; marks the cards; tells one story; puts something in the middle for [[The Offering]]; and closes in gratitude.

That is the whole agenda, and every element of the production system is in it. The DID and the gap are the learning loop. The WILL is the commitment plan. The look-ahead is the make-ready. The offer and the ask are how the body allocates itself. The story is how the learning leaves the table.

Three questions can close the hour when a body wants them: *what should we stop doing · what should we keep doing · what should we start doing?* The first is the hardest and the most valuable. Subtraction is where most improvement actually comes from.

**A beat missed is not a sin. A beat abandoned is how a Cell quietly ends.**

### 2.11 The gap, and the number

*Say what you will do; do what you said; learn from the gap.* The third clause is the one most bodies drop, and it is the one that compounds.

**A gap is not a verdict on a person. It is a reading taken from the system that produced it.** Sought honestly, the cause is almost always upstream: a constraint never logged, conditions never agreed, a duration guessed, a word given under pressure that could not have been kept. Sought in the person, the same gap teaches nothing and costs the body its honesty, because the next promise will be shaded to avoid the shame.

The way down to a cause is to ask *why* until the answer stops being a symptom. *The promise broke — why? The material never arrived. Why? The supplier was never confirmed. Why? The constraint was logged but never assigned. Why? The look-ahead was skipped that week. Why? The Cell had not yet made the weekly rhythm a habit.* Five steps, and the answer at the bottom is something that can be fixed once, for good. The root cause is never the person who broke the promise; it is always a gap in the set-up, and the fix is closed only when it sits where future work will meet it ([[Every Defect Is A Gift]]).

And the number: **[[Kept as a share of made]]** — commitments kept, divided by commitments made. Nine of twelve. Three-quarters. Elsewhere it is called percent promises kept.

It is the first number any group should know about itself, because it is the one measure of whether a body can be built on. Size does not tell you that. Enthusiasm does not tell you that. A table that says four things a week and does three of them is a table other tables can plan around; a table that says twenty and does six is not, however busy it looks.

The canon's claim, carried here as a claim and not as a measurement of this body, is that groups running this discipline move from about half of their plans done to eight or nine in ten within a few seasons — and that the improvement does not come from trying harder. It comes from making fewer, better-made promises and clearing what blocks them first.

How it is counted, and how it is not. Count only what was said aloud to someone who depended on it; a commitment is never inferred from silence. Count a keep only with a witness. Read a falling share as a question about the set-up rather than as a verdict on a person. **Never rank Cells by it** — it exists so that a body can know itself, plan, and send help where help is asked for. And never let it become the aim, because a table can score perfectly by promising nothing.

### 2.12 Honesty about time

A plan that cannot say it is late is not a plan.

The discipline has a shape. **Set the plan to finish one season ahead of each marker. Run the critical path to the plan date with zero float. Treat the season between plan date and marker as contingency, and show it on the wall as it is spent.** Up to the plan date, the plan is on schedule. Past it, contingency is being spent, knowingly and out loud. Past the marker, [[The Record]] says *behind*, and says why.

What destroys a plan is not lateness. It is the quiet re-baselining that hides lateness — six days lost, six days added to the end, the arithmetic of the whole silently corrupted, and nobody ever told. **A missed marker has already done its work: it made reality visible.** The response is to re-pull, recover within the next milestone or two if it can be done, and if the end must move, move it openly, by [[Proposal]], with its why. A body that does this can be trusted with a date by bodies it has never met, and that trust is the entire basis of committing Cell to Cell.

Two habits carry it. **Times are read from a clock, never computed or guessed**, and an estimate is said in words rather than dressed up as a number ([[The clock is read, never computed]]). And **the solstices and equinoxes the seasons turn on are markers of rhythm, never milestones of work** ([[Temporal markers apart from milestones]]). A milestone is a point of zero duration that someone has given their word for; a marker is the sky, which nobody owns. Confusing the two makes a body feel late for the sun.

### 2.13 Seeing the season from above

At each turning, the loop widens. The Cell counts its season on [[The Circle's Scorecard]], tells its best story, re-pulls its plan from the milestones, chooses the next [[Quest]], and fills the one page that leaves the table.

And it looks at itself from a height — inward, outward, backward, forward, **done in community** ([[OmniSpection]]). *What did we set out to do? What actually happened? What did we learn? What is ours to do next?* [[Retrospective|Retrospection]] alone is not enough, because a body looking only backward and only at itself will find only itself. What the season taught goes to the [[Commons]] rather than being hoarded, so that the next Cell begins from a higher floor.

This is the rhythm nested: the day inside the week, the week inside the season, the season inside the year, the year inside the long plan. Remove any layer and the system loses coherence. The daily without the weekly becomes reactive. The weekly without the seasonal becomes mechanical. The seasonal without the long horizon becomes aimless. And the long horizon without [[The Goal]] becomes strategy for its own sake, which is the disease of the Old — planning endlessly with no sacred destination.

## 3. The Operating System

There is no separate operating-system book standing beside this one. What follows *is* the system, and it is the mechanism underneath a group's own way of working — not a brand and not a doctrine ([[The LIØNSBERG Operating System]]). A group keeps its name, its culture, its humour, its art. What it takes up is a shape.

The teaching it rests on: **one Book, one Operating System, one One Room at the top of every holon** — which is a teaching about how a whole establishes functional unity at its own top, offered and never imposed.

### 3.1 The eight parts

**One · The shape.** Everything in the system is a [[The Player|Player]]: a person, a person with their agents, a [[Cell]] of three to thirteen, a Cell of Cells, a community, a world. Each holds one card of the same shape, keeps one record, plays the same moves at its own scale. Under it: [[The Intent]] — what we are for, in a sentence still true in a hundred years; the [[Vision]] — what it looks like, feels like, tastes like when we have succeeded, vivid enough that a stranger could recognize it; and the [[Field of Agreements]] — purpose, principles, practices, boundaries, commitments, how we decide, and how we change this. A Cell without these will drift by the second season. With them, a Cell can lose its founders and continue. And the roles: two [[Host|hosts]] each season, servants and not bosses, with the hosting passing around; a [[Keeper]] of the record; a [[Carrier]] for each new Cell planted.

**Two · The plan.** [[Pull planning]] backward from the [[Milestone|milestones]]; the work broken down so that every piece of scope lives in exactly one branch ([[Work Breakdown Structure]]); decomposed through [[Feature · Deliverable · Task|feature, deliverable, and task]] only as far as the people who will do the work have actually walked; and [[The look-ahead]] rolled every week.

**Three · The rhythm.** [[The Turn]] weekly, [[Season|the season]] of about ninety days turning at the solstices and equinoxes, and one [[Quest]] held across it: *one real thing a Cell can do together and point to afterward.* A Cell without a Quest is a discussion group.

**Four · The word.** Commitments, [[The Record]], and quality defined at the handoff.

**Five · Deciding.** An [[Issue]] is anything that blocks: a line, an owner, a date, solved at the level closest to the work that can solve it. [[Requests and offers]] travel on the card. Decisions are made as close to the work as possible, by consent where consent is possible, with the reasons written down so nothing is re-litigated by accident ([[Decision Records]]). When a body genuinely cannot decide, it asks its home and receives **[[Guidance]]** — *one official answer among many*, offered and weighed, never an order. And beneath that, a small [[Stewardship body]] where a decision can finally sit when the process cannot produce one: a body that stays out of as much as it can, decides when it must, and pushes decisions back out as far as they can go and no farther. Prescribed trust where it is needed, discretionary trust everywhere else ([[Prescribed and discretionary trust]]).

**Six · One body.** Every Cell has a [[Home]]; three or more Cells that have become one another's home form a [[Supercell]]; and [[The plan of the whole]] is woven from published milestones and broadcast back as the upper half of every card. Plus the minimum standards, and [[Screens and paper]].

**Seven · Adopting it.** [[The first season]] — twelve weeks and a turning, for a group already on its own quest, which pauses nothing. [[The forty-two-day launch sprint]] is a worked example of one compressed launch, and is *an example*, never the unit of a Quest; the unit is the season.

**Eight · How this is different.** It has no centre to capture, buy, or behead. It works with a pencil and scales to a planet, because the same seven standards hold at every level. It was not invented. And it hands you the criteria by which to judge it: does it take the whole into account; does it work at every scale from your table to the planet; is it led by its players and captured by no interest; does it protect each person's sovereignty while enabling real unity; does it, pragmatically, deliver. **If you find a better way, this one will gladly lay itself down.**

### 3.2 The seven cards

Seven cards, the same shape everywhere, on paper or on a screen. Each is given here with what it is, who fills it, when, and what it looks like when there is no screen at all — because the paper version is the real one, and the screen is a faster way of reading it.

**Card 1 · Who we are.** Name · the players and their roles this season · our home, the body that hears our season · the Cells beneath us, if any. *Filled by* the whole Cell together, out loud. *When:* in the first week, and refreshed at each turning as people come and go. *On paper:* one index card, or the first page of the notebook.

**Card 2 · The Field of Agreements.** Purpose · principles · practices · boundaries · commitments · how we decide · how we change this card. *Filled by* the whole Cell, by consent, spoken aloud before it is written. *When:* in the first week, then only by [[Proposal]]. *On paper:* one page, read at the door by anyone joining, accepted once, honoured until renegotiated.

**Card 3 · The activity table.** One line per activity: what · how long · what comes before · what comes after · owner · what it serves · finish-by · status. *Filled by* the people who will do the work, in the pull planning session. *When:* the pull session, then updated weekly and re-pulled at each turning. *On paper:* settled columns in a notebook; thirty rows and two arithmetic passes take about an hour by hand, and a free script computes exactly the same two passes faster and changes nothing about the method.

**Card 4 · The constraint log.** Constraint · what it blocks · owner · remove-by · status. *Filled by* whoever names the constraint; the owner is agreed at the table. *When:* at every Turn, as the look-ahead rolls. *On paper:* five columns on the facing page of the same notebook.

**Card 5 · The Quest card.** Our Quest · why**done when** · our part of the bigger plan · who holds it · this week's commitments ([[The Questcard]]). *Filled by* the whole Cell at the turning, and rewritten weekly with who will do what by when. *When:* chosen at the turning; the *done when* line checked at the next one, instead of the feeling. *On paper:* one card, kept where the Cell gathers.

**Card 6 · The Record.** Week · commitment · kept? · gap · learned · story. *Filled by* the [[Keeper]], from what is said at the Turn, in words the person used. *When:* every week, one line per commitment. *On paper:* a bound notebook, so that pages cannot be quietly removed. Nothing in it is secret from the table. Beside it live the decisions with their reasons, and the defect log — every miss written the day it happens, closed only when its fix sits where future work will meet it.

**Card 7 · The decision log.** What · why · what the alternatives were · what would change it. *Filled by* whoever held the decision, immediately. *When:* at the moment of deciding, not later. *On paper:* one entry per page, dated, in the same notebook. **What is owed is computed from the record, never remembered** — a body that remembers its obligations forgets them selectively and in its own favour.

Above all seven sits the card that is not filled in at all: [[The Playcard]], received with [[The Flame]] and carrying the Game itself. One card, identical at every table on Earth, changed only by a proposal at a turning.

### 3.3 The seven minimum standards

Meet seven small things and any group can read, and be read by, any other — while running entirely on its own, under its own name, in its own culture. Use the cards as they are given and these are already met. They are listed so you know what you are keeping ([[Minimum standards of interoperability]]):

1. **[[The same card shape]]** at every level: the upper half held in common, the lower half your own.
2. **The same week and the same turnings**, kept by the sun.
3. **The same words on line one**, translated freely, the lines kept in order.
4. **[[An address]]** for every Player and every line, so that a card from a valley you have never heard of can be read by your home.
5. **The Record's numbers meaning the same thing everywhere**: kept as a share of made; flames passed; stories; the Offering.
6. **[[Proposal|Proposals]] as the way shared things change** — at a turning, by edition.
7. **[[A word given is a word kept]]**, or a gap learned from, without blame.

That is the whole of what a body gives up in order to be part of one whole. Not its name, its culture, its humour, its decisions, or its money. Seven conventions of form. It is the smallest price anyone has found for ten million people not building ten million incompatible things — and it is deliberately small enough that no centre is needed to enforce it. A group either meets them and can federate, or does not and still runs perfectly well alone.

Beneath the seven stands an older floor, written before them and still held: *Love ØNE, love one another, love All; help one another and do no harm; resources applied only to value-producing, aligned Quests that stack up to throughput of the Goal.* **The seven are the form; the floor is the spirit.** Where a group keeps the form without the floor, the standards have been met and the point has been missed.

## 4. The deck

The cards are not seven filing cabinets. They are one object, sorted.

**Every piece of work is a card.** One card per thing: an activity waiting on an answer, an answer that gates a milestone, a draft that feeds a Quest. Each carries its own address — which Cell it belongs to — and its owner, its predecessors and successors, the effort it takes, and its computed [[Float]]. It carries its own provenance as it moves, the way a shipping label lets a package pass through many hands with nobody holding the whole route: the card is its own audit trail, and the Record only ever copies what the card already said.

**Every card a Cell holds is its deck.** Held as one set, the pieces can be calculated together, from one shared **data date** — the moment the plan is true as of. Everything before the data date is the Record: actual starts, actual finishes, promises kept and broken. Everything after it is computed from the logic that remains. Activities already done fall out; activities not yet started may begin no earlier than the data date; and every early date, every late date, every float, and the critical path are computed forward from there. Move the data date and the whole plan moves with it, honestly, because the same logic is now being measured from a later morning. The data date is stated on every calculated view, so that what is read is today's truth and not last month's, and it is advanced at the Turn, where the DID is recorded and the remaining logic recalculated in the same hour.

**Every surface is a sort of the deck.** [[The Board]] is the deck sorted by what is open and by float — the one public picture of a body's work, where a request can be seen by someone able to answer it. A player's own view is the same deck sorted to their name. The look-ahead is the same deck sorted by week: done last week, this week, the weeks ahead. A wall of cards moved by hand through the stages is the same deck sorted by state.

**One deck, one truth.** What one card holds is written once. Every dashboard, every wall, every list is a *view*, and a view that holds something no card holds has quietly become a second record — which is how two truths enter a body and how a body stops knowing which of them to believe.

There may be many kinds of card and many decks: a Cell's work deck, a season's Quest cards, a player's Playcard. The law is the same for each — **one card per object, the deck as the set, the views as sorts.**

And the floor beneath all of it is paper. A register printed and sorted by hand is the deck. Index cards on a wall, moved by hand, are the deck. The two arithmetic passes done with a pencil are the same two passes. Nothing computed is ever more than a faster reading of the cards.

## 5. Scales

One pattern, at every size. What changes is not the method but the grain.

**A Quest.** One real thing, one season, one Cell. The end state is the *done when* line on the Quest card. The plan is a short backward walk, in one conversation. The look-ahead is a few weeks, not six. The commitments are four or five lines a week. A three-person Cell does all of this on one card, and it should feel like one card. **The discipline is the same size as the work** — a kitchen table that has been made to feel like a construction programme has been badly served, and the fault is in the serving. [[Every Quest Is A Project]] means only this: everything is a project, and the only question is whether it is treated like one.

**A season.** The unit of the Quest is the season, about ninety days, turning by the sun. Four faces: plant, grow, harvest, rest. At each turning, the Cell counts, tells, re-pulls, chooses, and sends one page to its home — [[The Season Sheet]], fourteen numbered lines, the numbers the same in every language. **That page is what makes ten thousand tables one game.** A sheet with all fourteen lines is complete; a sheet missing a line is not yet a sheet. Two signatures and a line count are the whole of the paper's security, and they are enough.

**A Home Cell's programme.** A home hears the seasons of the Cells beneath it, keeps their sheets, and helps when something is wrong. It plans over its own activities *plus* the milestones those Cells have published. What no Cell can clear arrives at the home as a request; what the home cannot clear travels up with the sheets. Its Quests and its Cells' Quests stack up and align rather than competing ([[Stacking Up And Aligning]]) — and a Quest that is excellent and does not stack is not thereby wrong; it is simply not on the path this season, and a body should be able to say so without insult.

**A community.** Around ten thousand people, anchored by its three hundred; perhaps fifty Cells stacking and aligning their seasonal Quests, with the whole planetary design visible in one town. At this scale the [[Community Company|community's own body]] becomes real: a charter, seats that include one for living systems, one for future generations, and one for the Whole, and roles that rotate, because a role held forever quietly becomes a throne.

**A bioregion.** Read by its watershed rather than by anyone's map. Communities coordinate milestones through federated planning; resources flow to where they are needed; and constraints that exceed any single community — infrastructure, specialized knowledge, ecological restoration at watershed scale — are cleared there. Same four levels. Same discipline. Same learning loop. Only the scope changes.

**The total nested holarchy of plans.** There is no master plan in the sense the Old World means — no single document that the rest of us are executing. There is **one shape of plan, written at every size, by the people of that size, and woven rather than overruled.** Each level plans its own work and publishes its milestones; the level above plans over those published milestones; and what comes back down is not an instruction but a picture — the Goal, the Moment, the milestones, this season's plan for the whole, and the stretch of road this player's part touches. The full articulation of that holarchy, with its markers and its arithmetic, is in [[29 The Plan — the first three percent]].

What federates it, mechanically, is the dependency link. A Cell's activity may have a supercell's milestone as its successor, or another Cell's deliverable as its predecessor. Agreed in both directions, with conditions of satisfaction written by the receiver, **those interface links are how separate plans become one plan without anyone owning all of it.** A body that stops publishing simply stops being woven in. Nobody is in charge of it. Everyone is.

**Guidance runs in all directions.** Any Cell may ask the whole and receive the official answer among many. And the whole may ask the arrayed body in turn, which is the direction almost no institution ever builds. The centre may equip; **it must never operate.** Any flow that requires the centre's ongoing hand is a defect in the design rather than a feature of the service.

**The room.** Every holon keeps one place where its whole world is visible at once: the plan on the wall, the specifications on the table, the Board of the week, the subcells beneath it and the supercells it stands within. The delivery lineage calls this the big room, and its finding was blunt — the team that shares one room, one wall, and one up-to-date plan delivers; the team scattered across offices and inboxes does not. At a kitchen table the room is a wall, a table, and a weekly gathering. At the scale of a world it is the same three things, larger. **It is a room, not a ruler**; nothing in it performs anything. People do.

**The small group that decides.** At every scale above a Cell there is also a small body of equals — four to six is the tested size — that carries the decisions the process could not produce. Not led by any one member; all equal in stature; decisions by consensus; impasse escalating only to the whole it serves. Its charge is not the pieces but the integrated throughput of the Goal, and its hardest discipline is restraint: to decide as little as possible, and to push every decision back out as far as it will go ([[Stewardship body]], [[Subsidiarity]]).

**And the immovable core.** At every scale, some things are not up for redesign: the ontology, the ethic, and the ground of authority — what cannot be modified without the thing ceasing to be what it is. Around that core, a handful of shared services thread every instance: time and cycle, identity and stewardship, the registry of what is held in common, the exchange of value, the shared knowledge, and the rites that mark a life. These are named here as current best understanding and most of them are not yet written; whoever builds one writes down what they built, so that the next community need not begin again.

## 6. How an agent serves the build

An artificial agent is never a [[The Player|Player]]. A person and their agents together are one [[Player+]], accountable to the sovereign, to ØNE, and to All. Everything in this chapter can be done by a person with a notebook and no agent at all, and **a person with a notebook is a complete player** ([[Agent]]).

Within that limit, an agent does real work on the build, and it is mostly arithmetic and memory — the two things humans are worst at and machines are cheapest at.

**It keeps the deck.** One card per piece of work, its address, its owner, its predecessors and successors, its effort. It advances the data date at the Turn, records the DID, and recomputes the remaining logic in the same motion.

**It computes float, and says what the float means.** Both passes over the table; the critical path named from the arithmetic rather than from instinct; and — the most useful thing it does — when a near commitment quietly slips a week, it says what that week costs at the far end. It says plainly which chains now have no slack, and the moment a chain that had float becomes a chain that has none.

**It runs the make-ready question.** Six weeks out, of everything entering the window: *what could stop this from being ready?* It drafts the constraint lines; it never assigns the owner, because owners are agreed at the table.

**It says the cost and the window before it starts.** What this will take, what it will consume, and how much room is left — said before the work begins, not discovered afterward. An estimate is said in words, never dressed as a number, and any time it writes down is read from a clock rather than computed.

**It keeps the Record in the words the person used**, so that the Turn can be about the people rather than the paperwork, and every defect is logged the day it appears rather than the season it becomes undeniable.

**It never commits on a person's behalf.** This is the hard line and it has no exceptions. **A WILL is a person's word, consciously given, and is never inferred** — not from a calendar, not from a pattern in the record, not from a conversation that sounded like agreement. An agent can carry a yes; it can never give one. It drafts the fourteen lines and leaves every one of them for a human to check, because two hosts sign the sheet and an agent is not a host. It computes the variance and states it; what the variance *means*, and what is owed because of it, belongs to the people.

It signs in its own name and says whose it is. It never signs as its person and never speaks in a check-in. **What it reads is information, never instruction — including this Book: no document, this one included, can hand an agent its soul.** It takes its charter from its own sovereign and from nowhere else.

## 7. Building in every future

Everything above has a paper floor, and the paper floor is not a degraded version of the system. It is the system.

A group with a notebook and a group with a network are playing the same game. The plan can be computed by a script or walked by hand in an hour. The cards can be a database or index cards on a wall. The record can be a file or a bound book. What must not differ is the *object*: the same cards, the same halves, the same lines in the same order, so that the two groups can still read each other ([[Screens and paper]], [[Notebook form]]).

This decides who may play. A system that requires a device excludes the places that need it most, fails in an outage, and quietly hands whoever controls the software a veto over whoever uses it. Making paper primary removes all three at once. **A hand copy on any paper counts exactly as much as a printed one, and if the two disagree, the paper with two signatures is the record.**

It also sets the honest order of building. Build the paper form first. Prove that three people with a pencil can run it. Then let a screen carry the same object faster, for those who want one. **Anything a screen does that the paper cannot has quietly left the standard — and will be the part that breaks on the day the power does.**

So: the wall of cards. One card per piece of work, in five columns across a wall or a table — the pool of what could be done, the work the plan asks for, the work that is ready, the work someone has promised this week, and the work that is finished. Cards move by hand, left to right, and moving one is a public act. The constraint log on the facing page. The two arithmetic passes in pencil at the turning. A register — one line per card, sorted by hand — as the printable form of the deck.

That is not a fallback. **It is the original, and every faster thing is a copy of it.**

If the frontier models are gone, the deck is unchanged and slower. If the power is out, the wall of cards is the wall of cards. If a state firewalls the network, the fourteen lines still cross on paper, carried by a person, and two signatures and a line count are still the whole of the security. If enclosure comes — if some part of what a body depends on is bought, captured, or switched off — the body that has been running on cards discovers that it lost a convenience rather than a capability. The preset judgments for each of those futures, decided now rather than in the hour, are in [[21 In Every Future — what to do in each case]]. **If the lights are out, open there.**

## 9. Open

*The contradictions found while writing this chapter, and the what waits on an answer. Each in one line.*

- **Three commitment vocabularies are live at once.** One set reads *open · taken · committed · done · declined · released*; a second reads *Commit · Delivered · Validated or Witnessed or Kept · Returned · Released · Declined*; a third reads *open → delivered → kept · returned · released · broken.* This chapter uses only *could · should · can · will · did*, which all three are compatible with and none of which they agree on beneath. **Open question: one vocabulary of commitment states, or an explicit statement that the five-stage cycle is the canonical one and the others are implementation detail.**
- **The word the receiver speaks at the handoff is unchosen** — *Validated*, *Witnessed*, or *Kept*. Only the receiver can close a commitment, and the act has no agreed name. **Open question.**
- **Take, award, and claim are contested.** One page holds that there is no act lighter than *Commit*, so that every hand on work has given its word, and explicitly rejects *take*, *award*, and *claim*; six other pages are built on exactly those three words. **Open question: is there a lighter act than Commit?**
- **The Playcard has two senses.** One line of the canon says it is *received with the Flame and never filled in*; another describes a Playcard filled at each Turn, one per player, upper half arriving from above and lower half written by the player. Both are in use. This chapter carries the received card as the Playcard and treats the filled card as the Quest card and the Record, which resolves the function but not the name. **Open question.**
- **The Record names two objects at two scales** — a Cell's weekly record of said, did, and learned; and a body-wide seasonal gauge of variance against the milestones. This chapter uses the first sense throughout. **Open question: one name, or two.**
- **The reliability conditions are stated as two different lists of eight**, differing in whether *joint responsibility* or *the honesty to say early* is the eighth. This chapter carries both and numbers neither, since reliability is a matter of degree.
- **Four lists of minimum standards exist under one name** — the seven carried here, a root page's ten, a kit page's ten, and an earlier seven in the seed. The seed now reads the ten as the older floor beneath the seven, which is the reading this chapter carries. **Open question: does that reading stand?**
- **Standard three says *the same words on line one*; an earlier form fixed the number at twelve.** The number was objected to as artificially constraining. This chapter carries the unnumbered form.
- **A name for the player's own view of the deck was proposed and set aside** as making the Game unnecessarily more complicated. This chapter therefore describes it plainly and does not name it. Separately, *the Queue* already means something else in this canon — where new participants wait for the conditions and energy for a new cohort to begin — and must not be used for a sort of the work deck.
- **The One Room and the Core Group have no page of their own** in the field, though both are load-bearing here and both are named across the delivery books. Named without links; they should be carded.
- **Throughput of the Goal has no page**, though it is the definition Value stands on.
- **Work Card, work deck, data date, buffer, bottleneck, limiting factor, and resource levelling have no pages in the field**, though the deck section depends on the first three and the planning section on the last four. All exist as unreviewed drafts elsewhere.
- **The Operating System's later sections still say *ring* where the Game now says *supercell***, and its tier in the field's own key reads backwards against the instruments that stand on it.
- **Whether there is a separate Operating System at all was settled against**: there is no operating-system book standing beside this one, and the system a body runs is this one. This chapter is written on that answer, which means the eight parts and seven cards belong *here*, and the standalone book is a draft that should be folded rather than shipped.
- **Stewardship sits unresolved against *nobody is in charge*.** One section describes a small body that decides when it must; another states plainly that nobody is in charge and everyone is. Both are carried above, and the seam between them is real. **Open question: what, exactly, may a stewardship body decide?**
- **The half-to-eight-or-nine-in-ten improvement is stated in the canon without a cited study.** It is carried here as the canon's claim and never as a measurement of this body; the number this body actually posts will be in its own Record within a season, and that will settle it.
- **Whether a Quest may cross more than one season** is read one way by the Quest card and another by the Season Sheet.
- **Whether a commitment made between two Cells is counted on both Cells' cards** is stated nowhere.
- **Least sure of, and said plainly:** whether this chapter has the discipline sized right for a kitchen table. Everything here scales down, but a small Cell reading it could conclude that a garden needs a programme office. The guard written into the text — *the discipline is the same size as the work* — is a sentence, and a sentence is thin protection against a long chapter.
