# The Stairs

Nobody arrives at "I run a house full of agents" in one step. People come up a set of stairs, and each stair is a real place to stand for a while. It is worth naming them, because the right size of agent depends on which stair you are on — and a kit that hands you the top stair's agent on your first day has handed you a burden, not a gift.

## The steps

1. **Watch.** You see someone else playing with an agent — on a call, over a shoulder, in a recording. Nothing is asked of you.
2. **Read.** You read about it. This book, a post, a conversation. Still nothing asked.
3. **Try a kit, or buy one.** You are ready to try a starter kit — or to pay someone to hand you an agent in a box. This kit is offered here.
4. **An agent on your own machine.** You run it. It is on your computer, reading your files, with your name on it.
5. **It is not going to hurt you.** You have found that out. Now you just chat with it, ask it things, get to know it. This is a good stair; plenty of people stay here a long time and are well served.
6. **Real work.** You want to *do* things with it — and at some point the small house you set up is not what you want any more. You do not want one agent in one home; you want the agent home with several agents in it. *Your house gets larger.*

The steps are the person's, not the software's. A house does not graduate; a person does, and then asks the house to grow.

## Why it matters for the kit

There are two ways to build an agent for someone on stair 3. You can give them one agent that already knows how to run a large house — foreground and background, several agents, the whole thing — with all of it dormant until they are ready. Or you can give them a smaller agent, ready to accept the pieces as skills when the time comes, and able to say "is it time to add this?"

This kit does the second. Skills are how a house grows one step at a time, and how one house passes a step to another without handing over everything. Someone on stair 5 who only ever chats with their agent never has to carry the machinery of stair 6. Someone on stair 6 takes the `foreground-background` skill and the next chapter, and the house is bigger that afternoon.

## Belts, not ages

It is tempting to describe the stairs as ages — the eight-year-old house, the fifteen-year-old, the forty-five — and the metaphor is useful in private. Do not use it on a person. An accomplished adult who is new to agents is not eight. The better frame, borrowed from the dojo, is the belt: a belt describes a *practice*, not a person, and you can hold a black belt in one thing and a white in another without contradiction. Belts offer; they never unlock.

## Where you are

If you are reading this in your own house, you are at stair 4 at least. If you are deciding whether to try, you are at 2 or 3, and the honest advice is: stair 5 is worth reaching for its own sake. Real work can wait until you want it.
