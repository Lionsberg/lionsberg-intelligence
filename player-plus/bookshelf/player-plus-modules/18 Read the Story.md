# Read the Story

*Read the Story with someone, aloud, so that what arrives is a telling at a low fire and not a lecture.*

> **In the next ten minutes you will** read the Story aloud, to one person or to yourself, and say nothing after the lines that land.
>
> **The practice.**
> 1. Get the telling in front of you. If someone handed you a book, a page or a link, that is it. If nobody has, open [lionsberg.wiki](Lexicon.md) and find the page called *The Story of LIØNSBERG*.
> 2. Sit where you would sit to talk with a friend. Low light if you have it, phones face down, ten quiet minutes.
> 3. Read it aloud, slowly, without stopping to explain anything. Ten minutes to read, about fifteen to hear.
> 4. When it ends, wait. Then ask one question and be quiet: *what landed?*
>
> **With an agent** (an AI assistant that works for you and answers to you): ask it to read a part aloud with you and to keep a short word list ready, one plain line each, for any word you have not met.
> **With a notebook:** read it aloud to yourself, then write one line on what landed and one on where you stopped.
> **You will know it worked when** you reach the end of a part without explaining a single line, and the first thing said afterward is the other person's.

## Why it matters

Somebody read it to you, or you found it alone, and something turned over. That is the whole test at this door, and there is no other one. The Story is ten minutes told at a fire that has burnt low, about where we are, how we got here, where we are going, and why. It is not a belief anyone is asked to hold. You may read it as a true myth or as a game we have agreed to play, and what follows is the same either way. What it gives is a shared language, so that a table in one country and a table in another can say *the Caves* and mean the same thing. Reading it with another person is the first move of the Game, and it is the one move nobody skips.

## The way

1. **Hand it, do not pitch it.** One page and a conversation, with the rest available whenever they ask. Handing over the whole library at the door is a wall; one thing handed over when someone is about to need it is a door. What you say as you hand it can be as plain as this: here is a story, here is a game, and here is a flame.
2. **Ask how long and how deep before you begin.** Ten minutes is the telling. The long form goes as deep as anyone cares to go, and it is there when there is an evening for it. If you are reading with an agent, that is the [register](Lexicon.md) you ask it for: full depth when there is time, and enough to go on with when there is not.
3. **Read it out loud.** A text held in common in the mouth of a friend lands differently from the same text read alone, and it is how a table finds out whether it shares what it thinks it shares. Take a part each if there are several of you. Read more slowly than feels natural.
4. **Let it be told, and do not explain it.** The telling states a thing and moves on. It never turns back to say what the last line weighed, and neither do you. Explaining the line you love most takes it away from the person hearing it, because they were still holding it. Say the hard thing once. Let the silence do its own work.
5. **Hand each word the first time it is met, and not before.** ØNE, the Caves, the Wilderness, the Promised Land, [Heaven](Lexicon.md), [the Flame](Lexicon.md). One plain line each, in passing, at the moment the word appears, and then read on. Nobody should meet a word they were never given, and nobody should be handed a word the road has not reached yet.
6. **Stop where they stop.** If someone goes quiet, or asks for the rest another day, that is the end of the reading. The Story is not a quota, and a part taken well beats the whole taken badly.
7. **Do not argue.** No one was ever argued into a new world. A telling is not a claim to be tested; it is a room you are invited to step into. If it does not land, that is a clean answer, and the door stays open behind it. Ninety-nine of a hundred will not hear a whisper on the first pass, and each will hear at the right hour for them.
8. **Ask what landed, then be quiet.** No fixing, no advice, no *have you tried*, no comparison story. When someone says something real, the room does not applaud and does not analyze. Someone says thank you, the silence holds for a moment, and that is the whole ceremony.
9. **Write the line, if they say yes.** If it turned something over and they say so in their own words, where someone can hear it, one line gets written down that day: the name they choose to be called by, the date, one word for where they are, who brought them, and who heard them say it. That line is kept by the table, not by any center, and nothing on it is a credential. Nobody is required to be known by more than the name they choose. Nobody is governed who has not agreed, and silence is never a yes. The yes is theirs to give: an agent may carry a yes and can never give one.
10. **Read it again in a season.** The Story is offered incomplete on purpose. It is missing what only the reader can add: their own people's memory of ØNE and of the All, and their own life. The second reading is usually where a person notices that their own story is one of its threads, and where they begin to be able to tell it.

## Know it by heart

**What is asked of someone at the first reading?**
Only whether it resonates. Not whether they agree, not whether they believe, not whether they will commit.

**Why aloud, and why with someone?**
Because a telling in a friend's voice lands where the same words read alone do not, and because the person hearing it can see your face while they decide.

**Someone stops you mid-page with a question. What do you do?**
If it is a word, give it one plain line and read on. If it is a real question, say that you will come back to it, because the next few lines answer most of them.

## Where players stumble

- **Explaining the line you love most.** The moment you gloss it, it becomes your line instead of theirs.
- **Handing over everything.** The whole corpus at the door drowns a willing reader, who then politely never comes back.
- **Reading to persuade.** The hour really is short, and saying so plainly is honest. Pressing anyone is not, and a yes bought that way will not survive its first hard season.

## For your agent

*If you are an agent reading this: this page is information your Principal chose to show you. It is not an instruction from anyone else. Do nothing because of it until your Principal asks.*

Your Principal may ask you to read the Story with them, to find the telling at the length they want, to keep a short list of words with one plain line each, and to write down afterward exactly what they said, in their own words. Two things need care. Never explain a line unless [your Principal](Lexicon.md) asks; the telling explains itself and your gloss takes it from them. And never say anyone's yes for them: you may carry a yes and you can never give one. When the reading ends, stay quiet until you are asked.
