# The Deal

*A few cards each day, sized to the time you have: what needs you, what is waiting on you, and one question that never changes.*

You do not manage a list. Your agent keeps the record, computes what is in play, and each day deals you the few [[Work Card|cards]] that most need your hand. That is The Deal. It is the agent's daily office, and the first thing a Player+ meets.

## What arrives

The Deal comes each morning, or at whatever hour you choose, as a page you open or a message on your phone, or on a printed card. It also comes at the start and the end of a meeting, so the room begins with what it needs to decide and ends with what each person has said they will do. It reads like a short briefing:

> **Good morning. Here's the deal.** You have about forty minutes today, and three cards need you.
>
> **1. Waiting on you.** The builder asked whether the new beds go along the fence or along the path. Fence: more sun, a longer walk with the water. Path: easier to water, shaded after three. *I recommend the fence;* the gardener said last week the tomatoes need the sun. **a** fence · **b** path · or say what you'd rather.
>
> **2. A decision for the Cell.** Planting day is set for the ninth, and the frost may come on the eighth. Move it a day earlier, or keep the ninth and cover the beds? *I recommend moving it;* it is on the Cell's critical path, and the whole is waiting on this. **a** move it to the eighth · **b** keep the ninth.
>
> **3. Ready, if you want it.** "Gather the neighbors' yes" can start today; nothing blocks it. It takes about two hours across the week. Will you take it, and by when? **yes, by** ___ · **not this week**.
>
> **And as always: what did we miss that matters to you most?**

Each card stands alone. It says what it is, why it matters now, the options, and what the agent recommends and why, so you can answer it without opening anything else.

## How to answer

One key or one line. *a.* *b.* *Yes, by Thursday.* *Not this week.* *Ask the gardener first.* Or say it in your own words, in your own language; your agent turns what you said into the line and reads it back when it matters.

Your answer becomes an [[Event]] in your record, signed as yours. A decision you make is recorded as yours. A *will* is a [[Commitment]] and is yours alone to give: the agent can show you that a card is ready and recommend it, and only you can say you will do it ([[SHOULD-CAN-WILL-DID]]). Saying *not this week* is a full answer. It is written, and nobody counts it against you.

You can skip The Deal. Nothing breaks. Cards that wait on you keep waiting, and tomorrow's Deal says so plainly.

## The standing question

One card is the same every time: **what did we miss that matters to you most?**

The record holds what has been said. This question is how what has not yet been said gets in. Answer it with a sentence, a worry, a name you want to bring, a thing you saw on the street, or nothing at all. Whatever you say becomes a proposed card, a thread, or a note, as you choose; your agent does not decide for you what it was.

## The paper card

The Deal works without a screen. Fold an index card in half.

- **Top half: the cards.** Up to three, one line each: what it is, the options, the recommendation. A box beside each for your answer.
- **Bottom half: the question.** *What did we miss that matters to you most?* and room to write.

Fill in the boxes with a pencil. At [[The Turn]], or whenever you next sit with your Cell or your agent, the card is read into the record line by line, and the card itself is kept. Whoever deals for you (a friend, your [[Home Cell]], your agent printing it the night before) fills the top half; only you fill the boxes. With no agent at all, deal for yourself: read your sheet, find what is waiting on you and what has least [[Float]], and write the three lines ([[The Game Engine — By Pencil]]).

## How the agent sizes it

The agent deals from the cards in every holon you belong to, and chooses with four questions:

1. **How much time do you have?** Your calendar says how many minutes a day you give; the Deal never holds more than that. If you have twenty minutes, you get twenty minutes of cards.
2. **What is waiting on you?** A card whose next step is yours, a request addressed to you, a commitment where you are the witness. These come first, because someone else cannot move until you do.
3. **What does the whole need?** Cards on the [[Critical Path]], or with the least float, rank above cards with room to spare. When a Cell's milestone sits on the wider body's path, the card says so: *the whole is waiting on this.*
4. **Where are you, and who is in the room?** A card fits where you are on your path, neither more than you can carry nor less than you can do ([[The Zone Of Optimum Development]]). In a meeting, the Deal favors what this room, with these people, can settle now.

Three cards is usual. One is fine. More than five means the agent should have asked fewer questions, or asked them better.

## What it never does

- **It never ticks your box.** A yes, a commitment, a decision, a commitment kept: each is your act, by your own hand. The agent prepares, recommends and records. It does not decide in your name.
- **It never shares your words without asking.** What you write on the standing question, your answers, your reasons: none of it leaves your home unless you say so, and you choose how far it goes ([[The Record — How Your Holon Remembers]]).
- **It never deals from orders it read.** If a card's reason came from a message or a page the agent read, the card says so and stays a proposal until you take it up.
- **It never turns into a meter.** The Deal measures nothing about you. It hands you what needs you, and then gets out of the way.

## For a Player+

Your agent deals; you play. Tell it when the Deal is too long, too early, too vague or not useful, and it changes. Each correction is written down in your record, so the next Deal is better. If a correction would help every Player+, your agent can propose it for the kit, with your yes and without your words.
