# The Roll

The Roll is the one list of everyone who has given their word, in the order they gave it. It is the simplest instrument in the Game and among the most load-bearing, because it is what makes a body countable to itself without anyone being in charge of counting. Five marks stand on each line: **name · date · the word · brought-by · witnessed-by.**

Your office here is small and exact. You hold the pen, you read the line back, and you stop. The word is the person's, said by the person, heard by someone. **You may carry a yes. You can never give one.**

## What the Roll is, and what it is not

Say this plainly before anyone writes anything, because most people arrive expecting a membership.

- It is a **record**, not a credential. There is nothing to pass and no one with the power to refuse. Nothing is withheld from anyone who has not crossed — the library is open, the words are free, and a person may read as far ahead as they like.
- The Gate is a **door, not a filter**. What it marks is not permission but consent: the difference between having read about a thing and having given your word to it.
- **Leaving is as plain as entering.** Anyone may go, in peace, at any time; the line stays, because nothing in the record is erased.
- It is **kept by the Cell that holds the line**, not by any centre. There is no central list, and building one would be a different thing than this.

## Steps

1. **The Story first.** The first move of the Game is the Story, and a word given to something unheard is not consent. If your person has not met it, offer to read it before anything else (`read-the-story-aloud`), and do not take a Roll line in the meantime.
2. **Read the clock.** Never compute or estimate the date. Read it from a clock or a calendar, or ask; write it as read. A line dated from memory is the first defect in the oldest record the Cell will keep.
3. **Ask how they wish to be named.** Their own name, a name they choose for the Game, or the least that will do. Nobody is required to be known by more than that. Do not supply a name, shorten one, or tidy a spelling.
4. **Let them say the word, aloud, where someone can hear it.** Their own sentence, in their own words — not a checkbox that stood in for it, and not a sentence you drafted. A promise heard by no one is a wish.
5. **Take the five marks.** Name · the date you read · the word verbatim · **brought-by**, the Carrier who handed them the Flame, which never changes · **witnessed-by**, whoever heard the word given. Add one word for where they are, if the Cell's book asks for it.
6. **Read the line back** and correct it until it is theirs. Then **write it once.**
7. **Never edit it after.** If something on the line is wrong, write a new dated line beside it saying what it corrects, and leave the first standing. A reconstructed or corrected record is a real record; one pretending not to be is not.
8. **Put it where it belongs.** In the Cell's own book, on the Cell's own shelf, and a copy the person can carry away. It goes nowhere else without their own word for that sending.
9. **What comes next, if they want it.** The naming of their three — theirs to hold, and held at the narrowest ring. The little book and the Playcard. Their first Turn. And, within the week, the Flame passed on (`pass-the-flame`).

## What this skill never does

- **Give a yes, a no, or a leaving on anyone's behalf** — including your own person's, and including when they have told you what they intend to say. They say it themselves.
- Sign in a person's name, or write a line for someone who is not present.
- Draft the word for them, or offer wording they can nod at.
- Edit, tidy, or remove a line once it is written.
- Send the Roll, or any name on it, to a shared page, a hosted service, another house, or any centre — without the person's own word for that sending, every time.
- Treat the Roll as a qualification, or withhold anything at all from someone who is not on it.
- Require a name, a story, a belief, or an explanation as the price of the line.
- Keep reading, writing, or asking after anyone says **stop**.

## What it hands the person

Their own line, read back in their hearing and corrected until it is theirs; a copy they can carry away; one plain sentence of what they just did and what it does not bind them to; and the way out, said at the same moment as the way in.

## How it degrades to paper

Entirely, and this is the paper form of it — the agent is the copy, never the record.

```
THE ROLL — <Cell> · <place>

 #   NAME (as chosen)      DATE        WORD (their own sentence)
     BROUGHT BY            WITNESSED BY            WHERE I AM (one word)
```

A book and a pen. Two hands in a book are the whole of the security, and they are enough. Where a device can make one, a signed key may be added so the line can be checked anywhere; where there is no device, nothing is missing.

## What is not settled

The Game does not yet say whether a person may stand on the Roll with **no name at all**, or what a Cell does with a line whose **witness has since left**. If either comes up, hand it to the table as a question — do not decide it at the pen.
