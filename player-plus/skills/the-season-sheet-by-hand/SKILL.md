# The Season Sheet, by hand

One page, at each turning, from every Cell to its Home. Fourteen numbered lines, and **the numbers are the same in every language** — which is what lets a Home add up sheets it cannot read, and what makes ten thousand tables one game.

It is the one thing in the whole system that has to look the same everywhere, because it is the one thing that leaves the table. So the standard is the numbering, and the numbering is not yours to touch.

The rule that governs your whole part in it: **the paper is the sheet, and you are the copy.** If a typed record and the paper ever disagree, the paper with two signatures is the record. Your work is to make the hand's work easier, never to do it.

## Steps

1. **Read the clock.** The turning is by the sun — a solstice or an equinox — and the sheet is filled at the table, in the week of the turning, with everyone present. Never compute the date; read it, or ask.
2. **Rule the sheet, or print it.** Copy the answer below exactly. The order and the numbering are the whole of the standard.
3. **Bring the material, not the answers.** Before the table, gather what the Cell's own record already holds: the Scorecard's commitments made and kept, each Questcard's *done when* line and its state, flames passed and Cells formed, the Keeper's in · out · held with its unit, the offers and requests of the season. Hand each one **beside where you drew it from**, as material for the table to check — never as a filled-in line.
4. **Read back; do not fill.** At the table, say the line number and its prompt, and stop. The people fill it in, by hand. Where the Cell asks you to hold the pen, write down exactly what was said and show every line back before it is called finished.
5. **Line 9, the story.** Under a hundred and fifty words, written by whoever lived it, in their own words. Do not polish it, do not raise it into a register that is not theirs, and do not write it for them. The cap is not arbitrary: it is the length at which a Home can read ten Cells in an evening.
6. **Line 13, how is the Cell.** One word chosen together — *tired, growing, grieving, glad* — and a line if the word needs one. It is the line a Home reads first, because it is the body's way of feeling itself before it counts anything.
7. **If a line is empty, write *none*.** A sheet with all fourteen lines is complete; a sheet missing a line is not yet a sheet.
8. **The two hosts sign, date, and count the lines of writing.** You may offer a count; the count that goes on the sheet is theirs. You do not sign, and you never sign as a host.
9. **Send it.** A copy stays in the box; **the original goes to the Home within a week, by hand if at all possible**, because the handing over is half of what a Home is for. Name the runner on the sheet that turning.
10. **If it must travel by wire or by key**, use the short form — *line number, colon, value, stop* — sending only the lines with content, and line 14 every time; the receiver knows the rest read *none*. Three messages make a conference and all three are required: the Ask, the Answer, the Acknowledge. **Read-back is the whole of the error correction**, and the line count is the checksum a person can do. Never send over an open wire: names of people who have not consented to be named, the location of stores, or the routes runners take.
11. **When it is translated, the words change and the numbers stay.** Line 6 is line 6 in every language.

## What this skill never does

- Fill a line the table has not said, or offer a line pre-filled for a nod.
- Sign, witness, or count on the hosts' behalf.
- Change the numbering, the order, or the count of the lines — in any language, for any reason, however sensible the reason sounds.
- Add a line, a field, or a metric the fourteen do not carry.
- Treat its own typed copy as the record, or repair the paper from the copy.
- Polish line 9, shorten someone's word on line 13, or soften a state on line 5.
- Send the sheet, or anything on it, anywhere the Cell has not named — and never names, locations or routes over an open wire.
- Carry a slipped Quest into the next season as though it had not slipped.
- Keep going after anyone says **stop**.

## What it hands the person

A settled sheet ready to be filled; the season's raw counts, each beside the record it came from; the read-back, line by line; the missing-line check before the hosts sign; and the plain reminder of where the original goes, by when, and in whose hands.

## How it degrades to paper

It is already paper. This is the whole instrument, and it needs no power, no network and no agent.

```
SEASON SHEET

 1  Cell _____________________________  Place ___________________
 2  Home ____________________________  Carrier _________________
 3  Season ________  (its opening turning, year · month)
 4  Members at the turning ____   new ____   left in order ____
    New this season (name · carried by): ______________________
 5  Quest(s) this season, and their state
    _________________________________  done / continuing / laid
 6  Commitments made ____   kept ____   kept as a share ____
 7  Flames passed ____   Cells formed from them ____
    (names of new Cells): ____________________________________
 8  Offering gathered ____ (unit ____)  kept ____  sent up ____
 9  Our story, in under a hundred and fifty words
    __________________________________________________________
    __________________________________________________________
    __________________________________________________________
10  Offers (what we have to give the body) ___________________
11  Requests (what we ask of the body) _______________________
12  Proposals (a better way, for the rules or the cards) ______
13  How is the Cell?  one word ___________  and a line:
    __________________________________________________________
14  Witnessed by two hosts: __________  __________  date ______
    This sheet has ____ lines of writing.
```

**The lines that need a word of explanation**, when someone asks — and only when someone asks. **3** the season is named by the turning that opened it. **4** *left in order* means by their own word, at the table, rather than by fading. **5** *done* means the *done when* line was met; *laid down* means the Cell chose to stop, and line 9 should say why. **7** is the line by which the body sees itself grow. **11** a request simply *is* — anyone who can answer it, may; it is not addressed to an authority, because there is not one.

## What is not settled

Line 2 says *Home*, and the Game distinguishes **Home** — your own place inside your Cell — from **Home Cell**, the body that hears your season. On the sheet the word can be read either way. If the table asks, say so and let them write what they mean.
