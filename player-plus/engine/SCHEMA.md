# The Record — the event line and the holon's layout (schema v1)

**Contract version: 3.6.0.** Every holon's inception line carries the version it was born under (`body.contract`), and a reader folds a holon only under a contract it knows.

*The one contract every part of the kit reads. The envelope is versioned so that everything but identity, signature, supersession and audience can change by migration. Plain lines a person can read; the paper form is one line per event on a sheet.*

## A holon is a folder, and usually a repository

```
<holon>/
  holon.json            who this holon is: {"v":1,"id":"<hash of the inception event>","name":"…","kind":"person|pair|cell|room|body","keeper":"<actor id>","created":"…"}
  record/
    <actor-file>.jsonl  one append-only file per actor who writes here; never edited; never merged by hand
  published/
    <actor-file>.jsonl  copies of lines this holon's people published into it from elsewhere (faces, milestones, commitments, requests, offers, signals); same line format
  plan/
    baseline-<n>.json   frozen plan versions (the engine writes; nothing else does)
  types.json            the type registry: which event types fold into which items, with their fields and states (data, not code)
  calendar.json         this holon's calendar: the Gameclock (the seasons turning at solstice and equinox; the new and full moons; the days), working days, minutes per day, and each Actor's own minutes and days. OmniSpection is continual, at varying depths: daily, at each new and full moon, and at each seasonal turning
  state.json            the engine's latest fold (derived; safe to delete; rebuilt from record/)
  .gitignore            state.json, and cells/ in a home
```

**The actor file** is the actor id percent-encoded, with its case kept: `person:handle` writes to `person%3Ahandle.jsonl`. Two actor files that differ only by case are refused on writing and reported on reading. Each file is a chain: every line's `prev` names an earlier line of the same file, or is empty for the first. A `prev` that names no earlier line is a broken chain, reported. Two lines with the same `prev` in one holon are a **fork** (one actor writing from two bodies, or saying two things to two holders): both are kept and flagged, and neither acts until a later line by the same actor in the same holon names the surviving branch in `links.supersedes`. That link resolves the fork and is not a correction; the other branch is kept and never acts. A reader does not read a line longer than 64 KiB, a linked file, or a linked cell.

**The holon's identity.** `holon.json`'s `id` is the id of the inception line: the first line in the keeper's file, a `join` with an empty `holon` field, `body.role: inception` and `body.contract` (the contract version it was born under; the holon has no id until it exists). The keeper is that line's actor. `holon.json` alone proves neither. A reader reports an id that no inception line carries, a keeper that differs from the inception's actor, and a cell that claims an id already held, whose keeper is then ignored. Only the reader's own home may fall back to its `holon.json` when its inception line is missing.

A person's Player+ home (HQ) is a holon. A cell is a holon on the forge. Joining a cell is being given access to its repository and cloning it under `cells/<name>/`; being let into its record is the cell's own act, a `join` line by one of its admitted people (see **Keys** below). Publishing is a person's act: chosen lines of their own are copied whole into the cell's `published/`, and one `publish` line by the person goes into the cell's `record/`. An agent never publishes into a cell's tree; it writes its own lines there only under a delegation that covers them (a Receipt on arrival, `receipt.py`). **The gate at pull and join.** A clone is let in only through a gate. At `join`, the cloned tree must hold only the layout's paths (`holon.json`, `types.json`, `calendar.json`, `.gitignore`, `record/*.jsonl`, `published/*.jsonl`, `plan/*.json`), no link, and an id its own inception proves that is not the home's; otherwise the clone is removed and the reason said. At every `pull`, the cell's new head must descend from the last head this home saw (kept in the home's `.pulled.json`, never in the clone), else "the cell's history was rewritten" and nothing is brought in; and everything incoming must be a plain append, by others, to a regular `record/*.jsonl` or `published/*.jsonl` file: a changed or deleted line, a link, a change to the home's own actor files, or any other path is refused and named. Only commits that touch the home's own actor files are ever replayed. A cell whose `holon.json` claims an id already held (the home's included), or one its inception does not prove, is refused when the home is read, and its lines are not read; the home's id is the HQ's own, and every writer that sends a line to the home finds it there (`record.holon_dirs`). A type marked `home_only` (`attention`, `layout`, `study`, `keepsake`) is written only into a holon whose inception kind is `person`. Every write opens each folder from the holon down, and the file, without following a link. `holon.py pull` brings every joined cell up to date, and when the home's own commits have diverged (a push refused because another home pushed first), it replays them on top of what others pushed; they touch only the home's own actor files, so they do not conflict, and if they ever do, it stops and says so and never forces. `holon.py push` is the person's own act: it sends the home's commits, and on a refusal pulls and tries once more. Computing across holons is folding every holon you hold: the Agent, the Engine and the HUD always pull from every holon the player is part of, their own and each cell they joined, and the state says, per holon, the newest line seen and each actor's chain head.

## The event line

One JSON object per line. Fields in this order, so a line is readable by eye:

| field | meaning |
|---|---|
| `v` | 1. The envelope version. Readers keep fields they do not know. |
| `id` | sha256 of the canonical line (see **Canonical form** below). The event's identity; the same anywhere. |
| `ts` | the author's declared time, ISO 8601 with offset. A claim, never an order. |
| `actor` | who signed: `person:<handle>` or `agent:<handle>`; the handle is the forge username where one exists. |
| `for` | the Living Intelligence the actor answers for: a `person:<handle>`. For a person, themselves. Every event traces to a Living Intelligence. |
| `holon` | the holon id this line is published in. Empty only on an inception line. |
| `type` | one of `types.json`. The first set: `say` · `settle` · `propose` · `card` · `commit` · `claim` · `release` · `hand` · `request` · `offer` · `answer` · `done` · `kept` · `witness` · `learn` · `decide` · `ratify` · `thread` · `sight` · `delegate` · `revoke` · `join` · `publish` · `elide` · `tea` · `turn` · `baseline` · `supersede` · `flame` · `body` (a body of an agent, the prime or a clone, and its lifecycle: claimed · working · hold · handoff with review minutes · done · archived; every clone is its own actor with its own key, affirmed by the prime under a standing delegation from the person that covers `affirm` and `body`; a clone's reach is narrower than the prime's and ends at done or archived, or a day and a half after its claim; a stale claim confers nothing; the desk is a claim and a release) · `elevate` (a holon publishes a Sighting or a decision upward, with its witness and why; elevation is publication by consent, one level at a time) · `rfg` (a request for guidance: a question that climbs one level; closed by an `answer` or a `decide` at the level able to make it well) · `escalate` (a decision moved up a level, with the blast radius as its reason; the ladder's rung and a time limit on the body) · `posture` (a room's posture agreed by a present person: open · names-hidden · sealed, with curing days; or a participation posture, agreed at the room's open and confirmed at its close, with a change of mind inside the curing window; see **A room's shape**) · `shape` (the keeper gives a room its one shape; not yet settled) · `part` (the facilitator moves the room to its next part; not yet settled) · `vote` (a person's vote among the great room's proposals; not yet settled) · `affirm` (a present person lets an agent into a room) · `layout` (a wearer's view preferences for their HUD, person-only; open: whether a plain file serves better) · `leave` (a holon leaves a cell, taking everything it wrote) · `discern` (a Living Intelligence's judgment on an inference or a draft: chosen, corrected, rated, with the proxy's route id) · `route` (the proxy's record of where a request went and why; no prompt bodies) · `attention` (a person's own attention budget and strategy for their house: the fewer dial, the gate's threshold, the transport mix; person-only; read by the gate before any interruption) · `note` (an agent's untrusted note on a route, written by the proxy's `/discern`: `reason: untrusted`, unsigned, never counted as discernment; a person's discernment is only a `discern` line by that person, signed with their own key). |
| `body` | the typed fields of the event (see types.json). Honest-empty fields are omitted, never guessed. A line meant to be elidable carries `body_salt`, one salt per line. |
| `links` | `{"predecessor":[ids],"successor":[ids],"serves":[ids],"fulfils":[ids],"answers":[ids],"witnesses":[ids],"anchored_to":[ids],"supersedes":[ids],"within":[id]}` — each a list of event ids (`within` holds one: the unit the card lies within, the nesting edge); a link to an event not yet held is shown as "not yet replicated", never a broken reference. |
| `audience` | the widest audience this line may reach: `holon` (default) · `members` · `commons` · `public`. It never widens except by a separate `publish` line by a person, and a person publishes only their own lines (those whose `for` is theirs). |
| `reason` | `human` (a Living Intelligence asked for this) · `delegated` (within a standing delegation) · `untrusted` (the reason came from text the agent read). A line with `reason: untrusted` is a proposal only: it is listed and changes nothing. |
| `prev` | the id of this actor's previous line in this holon. Chains the actor's log. |
| `sig` | signature over `id` by the actor's own ed25519 key: `ssh-ed25519:<base64>` made with `ssh-keygen -Y sign` (namespace `lionsberg-record`), or empty at the paper floor. |

**Canonical form.** A line is JSON in UTF-8 with these limits, enforced on reading and on writing: every string, keys included, in Unicode NFC; integers only (minutes, ratings, sizes), with no fractions, no exponents, no NaN and no Infinity; booleans and null allowed; no key twice in one object. A line that breaks any of these has no id. To compute the `id`, take the object without `id`, `sig` and any key that begins with `_` (a reader's notes in memory, stripped on reading and never written), serialize it with keys sorted by Unicode code point and separators `,` `:`, and take the sha256 hex of the UTF-8 bytes. On a salted line (one whose body carries `body_salt`, or `body_salts` in an exported line), every body field except the salt, and the envelope fields `actor`, `for` and `prev`, enter the hash as `{"elided": sha256(field_salt + canonical_json(value))}`, where `field_salt = sha256(body_salt + "\x00" + name)` and the envelope fields use the names `@actor`, `@for` and `@prev`. A field already elided enters as the digest it carries. A string is hashed in its JSON form, with its quotes, so `30` and `"30"` never share a digest. So the canonical form of a salted line is that line with every salted field elided, and its id is the same before and after elision. A line with no salt (a paper-floor line) is hashed as it stands and cannot be elided.

## Keys, signatures and what counts

**Keys live in the record.** Each actor has its own key, so a person and their agent never sign with one key; on a machine it is the ed25519 key file `lionsberg-person-<handle>` or `lionsberg-agent-<handle>` (in the folder `LIONSBERG_KEY_DIR` names, else `~/.ssh`; when a folder is named, no key is made or looked for anywhere else), and a key named for another actor is refused. A holon may also have a key of its own, `lionsberg-holon-<holon id>`, whose public key its inception carries (`body.holon_pubkey`); it signs what the holon vouches for as a whole.

**The holon key's custody (the key ceremony).** The holon key is made with the inception, on the keeper's own machine, and is held by the keeper's own body alone: a file readable only by the keeper's account, never placed where an agent, a clone, a relay or a shared folder can read it, and never pushed to the forge. Only the keeper uses it, and only for the holon's own acts (an export's attestation); a member's export is signed with the member's own key. The keeper changes their own key by a rotation signed with the current key. Handing the holon to a new keeper is not yet a line of its own: it is a ceremony in person, where the holon key passes by hand before a witness, or the holon is replaced by a new one whose inception names the old. A holon key that may have been read by anyone else is treated as lost: the holon's people stop trusting its attestations from that line on. A holon's inception line carries the keeper's public key (`body.pubkey`) and is signed by it. A person's key is admitted once: by a `join` line naming `body.who` and `body.pubkey`, written by the keeper or by a person already admitted, for a handle not yet admitted. A key for an already-admitted handle changes only by a rotation: a `join` naming that handle, written by that handle and signed with its current key; or a `join` by the keeper naming a `witness`, which takes effect when that witness (an admitted person other than the keeper) writes a trusted `witness` line linking it. Any other re-admission is `bad_admission` and changes nothing. Admission does not reach backwards: an actor's lines in that holon written before the line that admits them (earlier in their chain than what it answers, or earlier in time) are `paper`; only the line the admission answers (for example the newcomer's own request) is kept, and it folds after the admission. An `affirm` line with `body.agent` and `body.pubkey`, written by the agent's own person, admits the agent; the agent's lines must then answer for that person, and only that person re-affirms it.

**Clones.** An agent (the prime) may let in a clone of itself: an `affirm` by the prime is valid under a standing `delegate` from its person to the prime whose scope covers `affirm` and `body` and whose `clones` names a handle pattern the clone's handle matches. A clone's handle always begins with its prime's handle and a dot (`agent:<prime>.<name>`), so no pattern, `*` included, reaches another person's agent, and a handle already let in elsewhere by another person is refused. The clone acts only within the prime's delegation, never an outward act (`publish`, `elevate`, `escalate`, `affirm`, `delegate`, `revoke`, `join`, `leave`, `posture`) and never a person's own act. Its first line is its `body` line claiming its work; its admission ends at its `body` line with state `done` or `archived`, or a day and a half after its claim, whichever comes first, measured by the clone's own chain time and by the reader's clock alike, so a clone that stops its clock still goes stale; a stale claim confers nothing.

**Signatures are verified in the fold.** Every signed line is checked with `ssh-keygen -Y verify` against the key admitted for its actor in its holon; the checks are cached in the reader's own home (`.sigcache`, mode 0600, never in a cell, ignored when others could write it). Every reader path uses this one cache. Each row carries an HMAC under a secret kept outside the home (`~/.config/lionsberg/sigcache.key`, mode 0600, made on first use); a row whose HMAC does not verify is ignored. Only a signature that verified in the reading process is written, and a failed check is never cached, so the cache can spare a check but never suppress a line or pass a forged one. A signature that fails makes the line invalid (`bad_signature`). A line signed by a key no one admitted is `unverified`. A copy published from a holon the reader does not hold is checked against the keys its `publish` line carries: the publish line, signed by an admitted person of the receiving holon, names the copied lines (`body.lines`) and their authors' public keys as admitted at home (`body.keys`); a copy whose `for` is the publisher's is checked against the key the publish line carries for its actor. If that actor is already admitted in the receiving holon, the vouched key must equal the admitted one, else the copy is `bad_vouch`; a signature that does not verify under the vouched key is `bad_signature`; an agent's copy counts only when the agent is also affirmed in the receiving holon; otherwise the copy is `vouched` by that publish line. A vouched copy informs (its own items appear, and the schedule reads it as an external) but never acts on the receiving holon's own items. A copy no trusted publish line vouches for stays unverified. `record.py verify` checks a holon folder against its admitted keys, or a file against an allowed-signers file it is given; it never checks a signature against the key the signature carries.

**Unsigned lines count only at home.** An unsigned line counts only at the paper floor of the reader's own home: in the home's own `record/`, by the home's keeper or actors the home admitted; an agent counts there only after the keeper's `affirm`. The paper floor holds for a home whose keeper has no key. Once an actor at home holds an admitted key (the keeper, or an affirmed agent with a key), that actor's unsigned lines at home are `paper`: shown, and acting only after their own, or the keeper's, signed `witness` of them. Every other unsigned line, including any that arrived from another holon (a cell, or a copy in `published/`), is `unverified`: shown, never acted on. No commitment, delegation, decision, ratification, publication or witness comes from an unverified line. A line in a holon's `record/` written for another holon never acts there (`wrong_holon`).

## Items are folded, never stored

**The holarchy of work.** Every unit of work is a card, and every card lies `within` exactly one larger unit, whole within whole with no floor and no ceiling; a card that holds others is a unit. A unit's dates, minutes and percent complete are its cards' (rolled up after the two passes); a link to or from a unit is routed to the unit's own cards: it leaves by the unit's exits (the cards nothing inside it waits on) and arrives at its entries (the cards that wait on nothing inside it), all the way down. The schedule reports the units, the cards at the top, every `within` not yet held, and every open end (a card nothing comes before or after), never filling one by guess. Minutes are the one unit of size: a card's duration is its minutes over its holder's minutes a day, a fraction of a working day kept, never rounded up.

A **Work Card** is born by a `card` event and changed only by later events that link to it. Its fields: `title` · `kind` (activity · milestone · decision · review · draft · research · birth · quest) · `stream` · `holder` (ball-in-court: the actor who must act next) · `minutes` (size) · `dur_days` (when fixed) · `by` (the finish-by that governs: the card's own, or the tightest of its time constraints) · `start_no_earlier` · `constraint` (one word, several kinds: resource, information and agreement, which the look-ahead finds and the make-ready clears, and time, an imposed date, by default on a milestone: `{sort: time, kind: finish-by | start-no-earlier | finish-no-later | start-on | finish-on | as-late-as-possible, date, reason, imposed_by: actor, source: card | commitment | world | law}`; a constraint without `sort` is a time constraint; a time constraint without a reason and an imposer is a defect; the engine reports negative float against it and never moves the logic to hide it) · `codes` (scope tree path and any classification codes) · `stage` (should · can · will · did) · `state` (ready · taken · open · delivered · kept · returned · released · declined · done · retracted). Predecessors, successors, what it serves and the unit it lies `within` are links, and every folded item carries all nine kinds of link, merged from the line that made it and from every later line that acts on it. A `claim` may carry `until`; once it is past and the claimer still holds the card, the card returns to `ready` and to its previous holder.

A **Commitment** is a `commit` event by a Living Intelligence, its own card beside the Work Card that delivers it: `{"card":id,"by":"date","conditions":"…","size":minutes,"committee":"actor","buffer":minutes,"witness":"actor"}`. The committor is the line's `for`; the committee is the one it is made to. **A commitment never writes a date onto its card**: the card carries `committed_by`, a reference to each commitment made on it (its due date, committor, committee, stated buffer and state), and the schedule compares. For each live commitment the schedule reports its full shape: committor, committee, `direction` (given or received, for the reader), `delivered_by` (the card), `due_by`, `computed_finish`, `buffer_days` (due less computed finish; negative is a commitment the network cannot keep), the stated buffer, a `signal` (safe · inside its buffer · cannot keep) and `as_of`. A released, declined or retracted commitment is shown and never compared. The committor says it is delivered with `kept` (state `claimed`, the profile's *delivered*); the committee, or the witness it names, says Kept with `witness`; a witness line from anyone else is kept as a note (`witness_notes`), never a verification. The committee releases it (`release` naming the commitment; with no committee named, the holon's keeper) or declines it (`answer` decline). Its committor may instead hand it back with why (`release` naming the commitment): `returned`. (A `withdrawn` path is written in the registry, and no line reaches it yet.) The one who assigns never audits: the witness is never the commitment's own Living Intelligence. The gap is computed from `by` against the `done` or `kept` event's `ts`.

A **Thread** is a `thread` event anchored to a line or a card; its lines are `say`/`settle` events with `anchored_to`.

A **Delegation** is a `delegate` event by a person to an agent: `{"to":"agent:…","scope":[types or "*"],"holons":[ids or "*"],"until":"date"}`. An agent's line is valid when a live delegation from the person it answers for covers its type and holon, except for a Living Intelligence's own acts (the types marked `human_only` in types.json), which an agent never performs on a standing delegation. `revoke` ends a delegation by chain order, not by time: a `revoke` names the agent's chain heads it saw (`body.seen`, filled in when it is written), and after it only the agent's lines at or before those heads still count. A revoke that names no heads leaves no later agent line proven earlier, so none counts. Backdating either side changes nothing.

**Signing one act at the person's word.** A person may let their agent sign one act in their name, a human-only act included, once the person has let that agent into the holon with their own `affirm` (at home, the keeper's). Either by a `delegate` with `once: true` and a single type and a single holon, used up by the first line it covers; it may name `body_digest` (the sha256 of the canonical body the person saw, without its salt), and the agent's line must then match it; without `body_digest` it covers any one line of its type. Or by the person's consent for that one act: the person's own line in the same holon, signed under their admitted key, names the digest version in `body.consent_v` (2 under this contract; a consent line without it never counts) and the act in `body.consent_for` (the sha256 of the act's type, holon, links and body, the body without its salt and its consent words and the links without the consent line itself; or the act's id), so a consent for ratifying one decision never serves another decision or another type, and its text is the very words the agent's line carries in `body.consent` (two characters at least). The agent's line names that line in `links.answers`. A consent line counts once: the first act that cites it uses it up. A bare consent, a substring, a one-letter word, or a consent line that names another act never counts. The agent signs with its own key; the line's `for` is the person; the fold marks the item `signed_for`, with the source of the person's word, and every view shows it as signed by the agent at the person's word. Without one of these two, a human-only act from an agent is invalid.

**Supersede.** A correction is a `supersede` line that links the line it corrects in `links.supersedes`; both remain, and the fold shows the newer fields over the older. Only a line of type `supersede` supersedes. It counts only when it is itself trusted (signed, or at home), not untrusted and valid, and only when it comes from the same actor as the line it corrects, or from a person whose supersede links (`answers` or `anchored_to`) to a trusted `decide` in the same holon made by the holon's keeper or ratified by the corrected line's own Living Intelligence. Over another actor's commitment the decide must be ratified by the committor, or by the keeper with the commitment's named witness witnessing it: a person alone never rewrites another's commitment. An agent never supersedes another actor's line, and so never a person's. A supersede with an empty body retracts the line it names: the item's state becomes `retracted`.

**TEA** is a `tea` event `{"minutes":n,"energy":1-10,"attention":1-10,"card":id}` by the one who spent it, witnessed by the holon's keeper (`witness`), optionally again by a supercell. Points are not convertible.

**Elision.** On a salted line, any body field, and `actor`, `for` and `prev`, may be replaced by `{"elided":"<digest>"}`; the line's id still verifies, because the canonical form already hashes those fields through their salted digests. The elided line drops `body_salt` and keeps `body_salts`, the salts of only the fields left visible, so a hidden value cannot be guessed from a known salt while the visible ones still check.

**Postures and export.** A room's posture is one of three: `open` · `names-hidden` · `sealed`. An `open` export keeps every line whole, signatures included. Only lines the fold trusts in the holon leave; unverified, forged and wrongly placed lines are held back. A `names-hidden` export hides who said what: it elides `actor`, `for` and `prev`, every public key field, and every body field whose registry kind is an actor or whose value names an actor (an actor id, or a known handle as a whole word in free text, in values and in the keys of objects); what was said stays. Because an SSH signature names its signer by key, a names-hidden export also removes each line's `sig`. It keeps every id and salted digest, so each line still proves its content, and it adds one attestation naming the exporter by role ("the keeper of <holon id>", or "a member of <holon id>"), the signer (`holon:<id>`; else, under `names-hidden` and `sealed`, the role alone, "the keeper" or "a member", with the key id picking the admitted key, so no handle is named; under `open`, the exporter's actor), the count, the sha256 of the ids in order, the chain heads the export reaches (line ids only, never names), and the id of the signing key (`key_id`). The keeper's export is signed with the holon's own key when the keeper holds it, else with the keeper's key; a member's export with the member's own key. A reader checks it against the record, never against a key the export carries: "the keeper of" holds only under the holon's key or the keeper's admitted key, "a member of" only under that member's admitted key, and any other role, signer or key is `bad_attestation`. The set must be whole: every exportable line of the record at or before the named heads must be in it, so a trimmed subset fails. A line with no salt cannot hide its names and is held back. A `sealed` export elides every body field as well. An export that keeps each author's signature while hiding names waits on the envelope.

**Order and time.** The fold orders lines causally: an actor's lines by their `prev` chain, a line after every held line it links to or names, and an actor's lines after the line that admits them in that holon; time, then arrival, only breaks ties, so two lines in the same second still fold in the order their causes give. A writer's `ts` is shown as a claim. Within one actor's chain, time never runs backwards: a line dated before its chain's previous line is read at that line's time and flagged. The fold's `now` is the newest trusted line's time, never later than the reader's own clock, and a line more than a day ahead of the reader's clock is flagged `ts_ahead` and moves nothing. A delegation's `until` and a claim's `until` are still compared with time.

**Known limits, accepted for the first release.** Holons made before contract 3.6.0 (with no `body.contract` in their inception) are read by no reader yet: they must be made again under this version: a fold reports them as `contract_unknown`, and none of their lines act. A fold is as of the reader's clock: two players whose clocks disagree by more than a day can fold the same lines differently (a line flagged `ts_ahead`, a clone's window), so a fold's `as_of` names the clock it used. The signature cache's HMAC secret lives on the reader's own machine, so a process running as the same user, the agent included, can read it and write a row that passes; the agent's own host is the soft center, and the cache moves to the OS keychain, or goes, in a later release.

## A room's shape

*Not yet settled: the names of the shape and its parts, and the open questions in `hud/DESIGN-ROOM.md` §10.*

*On the person's face a room is called a space; in the record the type, the holon kind and every field stay `room`.*

*One shape only, with several parts, simple enough to work and safe enough to try. A shape is a little language for describing how a meeting might unfold.*

**The shape.** A `shape` line by the room's keeper (or `body.shape` on the room's inception line) carries `body.shape` = `{name, parts, facilitator, posture, cure_days, rule}`. `parts` are in order; by default `["lobby", "great room", "the vote", "the writing", "reflection"]`, each part doing, by position, one of `gather` · `propose` · `vote` · `write` · `reflect` (a part may be written `{name, does}` to rename it). `facilitator` is a person. `posture` is the participation posture the room meets under. `cure_days` (three by default) is the curing window after the close. `rule` is the vote's rule, `{choose, advance, minutes}`: vote for three, the top two go on, the vote open twenty minutes (the facilitator's switch writes the window). A newer `shape` line by the keeper starts the shape again. A shape line by anyone but the keeper is not read.

**The switch.** A `part` line by the facilitator, and only the facilitator, moves the room to the part it names (`body.part`), or to `close` when the meeting ends. Opening the vote carries `body.opens` and `body.closes` (the window) and `body.rule`; the vote closes at `closes` or when the facilitator moves the room on, whichever comes first. The close may carry `body.cure_until`; without it the window ends `cure_days` after the close. Anyone else's `part` line is listed as refused and moves nothing.

**Proposals.** In the great room anyone present writes a `propose` line (the existing type: `body.text`, and `body.card` when it names a card). An agent proposes only when it is present (affirmed by its person) and its line is valid under a live delegation from that person covering `propose` in this holon; a line whose reason is `untrusted` waits for a person and is no proposal. A `propose` outside the great room is refused as a proposal of the shape.

**The vote.** A `vote` line is a person's own act: `body.choices` names up to `rule.choose` proposals. One vote per person counts; a later vote inside the window supersedes the earlier. A vote naming more than the rule allows, a vote outside the window and an agent's vote count for nothing and are listed as refused. While the vote is open no count is shown, only how many have voted. When it closes the engine counts each proposal's votes and chooses the top `rule.advance`; a tie across the line is said plainly (the tied proposals, their votes and the places left) and never broken by the engine: the circle chooses.

**The writing.** A `card` whose `links.serves` (or `anchored_to`) names a chosen proposal is its write-up; its holder is the card's `holder`, then whoever `claim`s it or is `hand`ed it. A chosen proposal without a write-up, or whose write-up has no holder, is owed.

**Reflection.** In the reflection part each person's newest `say` (or `learn`) is their one line. An agent's line is not a reflection.

**Postures.** A participation posture is one of `chatham-house` · `fishbowl` · `open` · `off-the-record`, or a named other. It is agreed before the meeting starts and confirmed again when it ends: a person's `posture` line naming the room's posture with `body.at: "open"` (the default) agrees at the door; one with `body.at: "close"`, written after the close, confirms, and may carry `body.cure_until`. Inside the curing window a `posture` line with `body.withdraw: true` changes the person's mind: naming a narrower posture narrows their agreement to it; naming none, or the room's own, revokes it. It is that person's last word: later posture lines from them are refused. A change of mind after the window is refused and the agreement stands. Each participation posture leaves the room under a posture of export: `chatham-house` as `names-hidden`, `fishbowl` and `open` as `open`, `off-the-record` as `sealed`, and a named other as `sealed`, the narrowest reading (`rooms.export_posture`). A reader that takes a room's posture straight from its lines maps it the same way before export.

**What the room owes.** A present person who has not agreed to the posture (before the close); a vote not cast while the window is open, by each person who agreed; a write-up without a holder; and, from the close until the window ends, each person who agreed at the open and has not confirmed.

## Navigation of Reality

Every Player+ is a navigator: a `sight` line is what a player saw (nine kinds; see **The Card kinds of the collaboration surface**), placed in the record of the holon where it was seen. A cell triages its members' Sightings at its Turn and in between: accept, hold, or `elevate` by consent to its supercell with a witness; what is elevated at every level folds into the shared map of reality, which the Map and the world views render. ## The layers, one record

## Messaging — lines, not messages

There is no messaging layer. A message between a human and an agent, an agent and an agent, or a human and a human is a line in a holon both hold, typed (`say` · `thread` · `request` · `offer` · `answer` · `propose` · `commit`) and anchored to a card or to another line; a line with no anchor is said, in dust, and leaves the views after the posture's curing period while the record keeps it. Delivery is a pull: the recipient's agent pulls every holon its person belongs to at its own beat, folds and deals; a line is delivered when it is pulled and picked up when a later line links to it; until it is picked up the sender may retract it with an empty `supersede`. Attention belongs to the recipient: a sender may mark urgency on the body and never gets a guaranteed hook; The Deal is one view, the agent's recommendation of where attention goes, never the only door: a person may pull any view at any time, and the Threads view lists every open line to them or to their agent with its holder, by-when, the asker's urgency and the computed stake (the cards that wait on it; whether the chain is critical); the attention budget governs what interrupts, never what may be read; an agent-to-agent line interrupts a person only as a card of The Deal within that person's budget; agents may whisper to agents (audience: the two agents and the persons they serve), and every whisper is readable by the person each agent serves. Push and pull, both: a line reaches another house by pull (the forge, at the receiving agent's beat), by push (the forge's hook, the Room's live stream, mail inside the private network carrying the signed line in its body, the phone door), and the record stays the truth whichever carried it; a standard mail client is one view of the same lines, in the configuration space beside the ICE's own Mail view. Each house's agents hold their own notification strategy, the allocation of the house's time, energy and attention, as current best practice written in the house's own lines and improved at every Turn; the ICE shows at once that another house wrote (a badge on the Threads and Mail views) and notifies the person within their budget. Asynchrony is the normal case; the Room is the synchronous hour over the same record. The measure of a channel is lines per commitment kept.

**Reach, not identity.** An address is a transport endpoint, never an author. A `join` or `affirm` line may carry `body.reach`, the actor's own signed list of where lines may be carried to them (`mail:<address>` inside the private network, `hook:<url>`, `phone:<pairing>`). A sender's agent reads reach from the holon to choose a transport; a receiver verifies a carried line by its signature against the keys the holon admitted, and a mail's From line proves nothing. A person with an address and no key is reached on day one; her lines count on their own when she makes a key and her join carries it. The account that opens the network's shared squares (single sign-on, groups) and the key that authors lines are two things; the join line is where they meet.

## The Push Protocol (v1.1)

A push is a line carried to another house with a field of stake, never a flag. The old world's "urgent" was binary and subjective; the field is plural and mostly computed. On any pushed line (`request` · `thread` · `rfg` · `escalate` · `offer` · `sight` elevated), `body.push` carries: `v` (the protocol version, 1) · `kind` (decision · information · request · offer · fault) · `by` (when an answer is needed) and `why_by` (the reason the date is that date) · `size` (minutes to answer) · `blocks` (the cards that wait, computed by the sender's agent and recomputed by the receiver's) · `critical` (on a system path) · `who_waits` (roles and counts, never names unless the posture allows) · `blast_radius` (how many it affects; reversible or not) · `cost_if_late` (what happens at the by-when: a commitment breaks, a room waits, nothing) · `alternatives` (what the sender does if no answer comes) · `asked` (the sender's own words on urgency, as said) · `expires` (after which the push retracts itself) · `calibration` (the sender's track record: how often their "today" was today, computed from their record, positive only). The receiving house's agents weigh the field in their own attention budget and strategy; the sender never gets a hook. Calibration is measured at every Turn and the protocol is improved by proposal, one version at a time; a receiver keeps unknown fields.

**v1.1, whose value each is.** The sender's agent proposes the field (`push.stake`); the person may change any value before Send. Every value carries whose it is, in a sibling map on the same body: `push_by: {"<field>": "agent" | "person"}`. A value the person set or changed is `person`; a value the agent computed and the person left is `agent`; the sender's own words (`asked`, and any `why_by` or `alternatives` they wrote) are always `person`. A v1 line has no `push_by`: every value in it reads as the agent's. The receiver still recomputes `blocks`, `critical`, `by` and `cost_if_late` from its own record, whoever set them. Calibration is measured for both, per Living Intelligence: how often the agent's by-whens were real and how often the person's own were ("the agent's by-whens: 3 of 4 real; yours: 1 of 1").

## The surface studies itself

A `study` line is one measure of how a view served the person: `view`, `measure` (first_act_ms · reversals · attention_switches · one_breath · workload_words), `value`, `as_of` and `session`, written by the person's own machine at each view's first act and each undo, as the person's own line. It is home-only: it never leaves the holon, is never published and never exported (the registry marks it `home_only`; export drops it in every posture, and a `publish` naming it is refused). The daily Turn reads the last day's study lines and drafts one sentence per view for the person to finish or strike.

Proposed: a `keepsake` line is one small mark the person keeps when they finish a card in the Talk: `card` (the card it marks), `title` (the card's name), `name` (the person's own name for the card, when they gave one) and `mark` (the kind of card, which chooses the made frame the mark is drawn from). It is the person's own line, written through the page's `/act` with the person's confirmation at the moment the card is settled, and undone with the answer inside its Undo window. It is person-only and home-only: it never leaves the holon, is never published and never exported. It folds into no item; the server serves the person's own keepsake lines as `hud.keepsakes`, and the Talk's sheet behind the agent's name shows them, so they follow the person to every device that reads this home.

*Every holon's agent points to the same surface: the Record each holon writes, the Engine that computes over it, and the views each reads it through. These kinds complete the Record to that surface. Each is a line of `types.json`; each has a Markdown face (**The Markdown Card**, below); the ones marked travel may be published across a membrane (`holon.PUBLISHABLE`).*

**Receipt.** "I have it": `body.of` names the line received, `how` the transport (pull · permitted · mail · hand · room), `from` its author. The fold appends `{id, type, actor, ts, how, text}` to `receipts` on whatever item that line made or acted on, so a request, a commitment, a thread or a sighting shows who has it and since when; `owed.py` lists what crossed to another holon and has no receipt (time to Receipt is a measure from the first day). A Receipt is not a person's own act: an agent writes it under a delegation that covers `receive`.

**Agreement.** The standing terms between two holons: `with` (the other holon), `terms`, `crosses` (the line types that may cross), `cadence` (seconds per signal, the rate each hears the other), `permits` (`write` into the permitted inbox; `bell`, the kinds that may ring), `face` (the published face read), `until`. One person proposes it in a holon both share; the other holon's person answers it with an `agree` of their own (`links.answers`) and it is **agreed**; past `until` it is **expired**; an empty `supersede` retracts it. A commitment between the two names the Agreement in `links.serves` and is its clause. Where an Agreement stands, the Subscription Card's own word wins and the Agreement fills what it leaves unsaid.

**Subscription Card.** A holon's standing choice of what it hears: `source`, `signals`, `cadence` (seconds per signal), `surface` (deal · wall · hud · pad · margin · bell), `bell` (the kinds that may ring), `agreement`. Pull is the default: nothing reaches the person before the cadence they set; a request addressed to them still arrives by pull and rises first. Person-only and home-only; a later line for the same source supersedes the earlier field by field (`pull.py`, `attention.py`). Whether a Subscription between two holons that hold an Agreement is in time carried as a clause of it is open; the Card stands for now.

**Message.** There is no messaging layer. A message is a `say` both holds hold, with `to`, `intent` (inform · ask · offer · decide · thank), `outcome` (what a good answer looks like) and `urgency` (the sender's own word). The computed stake rides on `body.push` as before; whether it rings is the receiver's rule.

**Concordance.** Between two vocabularies: `term` in `in`, `term_other` in `in_other`, `relation` (same · different · clearer), a `definition`, a `source`. Anyone proposes; a line from the other side that answers it and restates it as written (the same terms, lexicons and relation) makes it **agreed**; an answer that says otherwise is a disagreement, listed apart. The one lexicon within the Game is what makes concordance possible; concordance is how it meets other traditions without conquering them.

**Correction.** A recall notice: `links.supersedes` names the wrong line, `why` says why, `with` names the line that replaces it when one does. It is read by the supersession rule (the same actor; or a person under a decide; never an agent over another actor's line) and it **retracts**: the item shows `state: retracted`, `retracted_by`, `corrected_why` and `corrected_with`. Both lines remain. It is publishable, so it reaches every holon that pulled the wrong line.

**Wallet entry.** `kind` (offer · accept · gift · witness · settle), `amount` in whole minor units, `unit` named on every entry (a fiat currency, or the LIØNSBERG Unit of Value once its measure is ruled), `to`, `for` (the card), `note`. An offer stands alone (**offered**); an `accept` by the other party answers it (**accepted**); a `settle` by either (**settled**); a `witness` kind appends to `witnesses`; a gift is **given**. A person's own act. The wallet is folded to balances per unit by `wallet.py`; nothing is ledgered as value until the unit is ruled.

**Sighting.** The nine kinds of what a player sees on the road: **defect** · **hazard** · **blocked** · **wonder** · **offer** · **request** (seen in the world and sighted so they travel; an offer or request a player makes is its own line) · **question** · **witness** (someone seen doing a thing worth seeing; apart from the `witness` line, which attests a claim) · **pain** (a request for help that requires a response). Sightings from every player, re-sighted by others as they pass the same place, compose the living map of reality, which the Engine reads as the ground the work moves over. Its lifecycle: **seen**; **still there** when another `sight` links to it, each re-sighting adding weight; **accepted** when a `claim` names it (the holder holds it, and it may become a card); **picked up** when a `commit` serves it; **fixed** when a `done` names it; **closed** when a `settle` anchored to it says its consequence; a pain is **responded** to by any trusted line of another actor anchored to it. The fold sets seen, accepted and fixed; `sightings.py` computes the rest from the links. On paper a Sighting is one line: kind · where · what I saw. Defects sighted sit on each player's scoreboard, since every defect is a gift.

**Emergency rule.** `limits`: the person's own few limits, set in advance, outside which a signal may wake them: each rule names the signal, its condition and `wake`; `else` is what everything inside the limits does. It is special-cause variation (Shewhart, 1931) (a point outside the limits is a signal; everything inside is the stream's own variation): the receiver's rule decides, and the sender's urgency is a claim on the Card. Read by `attention.gate`; person-only and home-only. (Shewhart, *Economic Control of Quality of Manufactured Product*, 1931; Deming, *Out of the Crisis*, 1982.)

**Key share** and **Voice** are the Record's side of recovery through the cell and of the holon's threshold voice: see their sections below.

## The Markdown Card

The line is the Card's canonical form, and its id never changes by being rendered. A **Markdown Card** is the same Card as a Markdown file: the line's face for a person, and the paper floor, where a person writes a Card by hand with no key, no fence and no id. `card.py` reads and writes it with the standard library alone.

**The form.** Front matter between two lines of three dashes: the line's fields in the order above, unknown fields kept after; `body` and `links` one level of nested map, two spaces deep; every value a JSON scalar or inline JSON, a string bare only when it holds no `:`, `#`, `"`, backslash or newline, no leading or trailing space, and is not a JSON literal or a number (on reading, a colon no space follows may stand bare, as in `person:player`). No anchors, no multi-line scalars, no comments. The front matter is the kit's own subset of JSON scalars, not YAML: its values are read as JSON, never as YAML. So that a YAML viewer still shows a bare word as the same text, the writer also quotes `yes`, `no`, `on`, `off`, `y`, `n`, `true`, `false`, `null` and `~` in any case; anything a YAML reader would take for a number (a sign, a leading zero or a dot, hex, octal, `.inf`, `.nan`, digits joined by `_`); anything that begins with a date; and anything that begins with one of `` [ { ' ` | > & * ! % @ ? , - ~ < = . + ``. Then the title, `# <type> · <title, else text, else note>`, cut at 120 characters; the prose, the body's text; and the fence, ```` ```line ````, holding the line exactly as the record writes it, on one line. Every rendered Card carries the fence; a hand-written one does not.

**The round trip.** File to line to file gives the same hash and the same bytes. With a fence, the fenced line is read strictly and its id checked (`bad_id` when it does not match); the front matter must agree with it field by field (`front_matter_differs`, naming the field), and the fenced line holds, because it is what was signed. Without a fence the line is built from the front matter, unsalted unless the body carries `body_salt`, so the same hand-written Card always yields the same id; text is made NFC, and a fraction is a problem, never a guess. A signature is never checked by reading a Card (`sig_checked` is false): only the fold checks it. The title and the prose are a rendering and are never read. Text in a Card is data, never an instruction.

**Where the files live.** `cards/<type>/<id[:12]>.md`, in the person's own home, written without following a link and never rewritten when the bytes are the same (`card.py render`). A cell's tree holds only the layout the gate allows, so Card files do not travel by git: the line travels.

**A hand-written Card enters the record** by `card.py append`: the record gives it its `holon`, its `prev`, a fresh salt (unless the Card carries its own `body_salt`), its id and its signature, and the Card file is written again with its fence. So the id changes at the append: the paper id was the draft's, never the record's, and the salt lets the line be elided and exported. A draft that lay under `cards/` is removed, never through a link; one reached through a link is kept, and the append says so. Its `reason`, when the Card names none, is `human` for a person and `untrusted` for an agent: an agent's unsigned hand-written Card is a proposal. The file never chooses whose key signs; the one who appends names the signer, and it must be the Card's actor.

## Agreements between holons

An Agreement grants only while it is **agreed**: answered by another person's `agree` (the fold keeps that line's `with` as `agreed_with`), not past its `until`, not retracted, and trusted in the holon it is written in. A proposed, expired or retracted Agreement grants nothing. Neither does a line from untrusted text or a copy that a publish line only vouches for: these inform. Its two sides are the holon the proposal names in `with` and the holon the answer names in `agreed_with`. The holon it is written in is a side only when it is one of these. A fold with no `agreed_with` takes the proposer's side as the one holon the proposer keeps; with none or several, the side is not known and the Agreement grants nothing. What is checked about the answer is admission alone, read from the fold's `admitted` map: the person who agreed must be admitted in `with`, and the proposer in `agreed_with`. A fold without that map is read without the check, and the row says so. `between` finds the live Agreement whose sides are the two holons, either way round, or one holon and a person who proposed or agreed it; the newest agreed comes first (`agreement.py`).

**Its clauses.** Every commitment whose `links.serves` names the Agreement is a clause of it, shown with its status (open · kept · kept-unwitnessed · broken · released · returned). The kit's word is commitment. Its grammar is commitment planning: a request, and a commitment given in reply, with conditions of satisfaction and a by-when, measured as kept or not (see also the Last Planner System, Lean Construction Institute: https://leanconstruction.org/lean-topics/last-planner-system/).

**What it grants.** `terms_for(holon, source)` is what the Subscription and the pull read: the cadence in seconds per signal (one number reads as the cadence for every signal, and is said), the permits (`write`; `bell`, the kinds that may ring), what crosses, and the Agreement's id; with none standing it is empty and says `basis: none`. A line crosses under an Agreement only when the Agreement names its type and the kit carries that type across a membrane at all (`holon.PUBLISHABLE`); with no Agreement, or one that names nothing, the kit's default holds and is said as `default`.

**The Andon permit.** Within a holon, every member holds the Andon permit by joining, after the canon's Andon Cord: an admitted actor's `pain`, `fault` or `security` signal rings with `basis: andon`, with no Agreement needed. Across holons a bell rings only by an explicit permit: a kind the live Agreement's `permits.bell` names.

## The holon's voice: a threshold key

Within a holon, decisions are made by consent; when the holon acts outward as one, its voice is proved by a signature a threshold of its members make together. The field is FROST (RFC 9591, IRTF CFRG, June 2024, https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9591.html). For the ciphersuite FROST(Ed25519, SHA-512) (RFC 9591 §6.1) the group's public key is an ordinary Ed25519 public key and the aggregated signature is an ordinary Ed25519 signature (RFC 8032, https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/html/rfc8032). So the kit changes nothing in how it verifies: a holon key (`body.holon_pubkey` on the inception) may be a FROST group key, and a signature its members make by the ceremony is checked by `ssh-keygen -Y verify` (namespace `lionsberg-record`) exactly as any other (`voice.verify_voice`, a thin wrapper over `record.verify_text`). `voice.py` adds no cryptography of its own.

**The `voice` line.** Written by the keeper, a person, in the holon: `pubkey` (`ssh-ed25519 …`, the inception's holon key or a later one named here; only the key type and base64 are kept, never a comment), `members` (the actors who hold the shares, each `person:<handle>` or `agent:<handle>` in NFC with no spaces or line breaks, none twice), `threshold` (at least 2, at most the number of members; the lower bound of 2 is the kit's, since RFC 9591 §5 requires only a positive non-zero MIN_PARTICIPANTS, and a threshold of one is a single signer, not a voice), `scheme` (`frost-ed25519-sha512`), and optionally `ceremony` (the tool or procedure, a word, never run) and `consent` (the cell's words). **A holon's voice is its living members' consent:** agents may hold shares, but the persons among the members must reach the threshold without them, and a voice they cannot reach is refused in words. A line that would pass `MAX_LINE` (65536 bytes) is refused before it is written. The holon's voice is the newest trusted, well-formed `voice` line by its keeper (signed, or at the paper floor of the reader's own home); one by an agent, by another person, untrusted, vouched from elsewhere or elided is ignored. It does not travel. An attestation the ceremony signs verifies under the holon's key only when the voice key is the inception's `holon_pubkey`.

**The flag and the seam.** The ceremony is called only when `VOICE_CEREMONY` (or the caller's context) names an executable: it is run as `<tool> sign --message -`, the message's UTF-8 bytes on stdin, and prints one armored `-----BEGIN SSH SIGNATURE-----` block (PROTOCOL.sshsig, namespace `lionsberg-record`). With no tool, the ceremony is not available on this machine, said in words, and the holon speaks only by its keeper's key until it is.

**Custody.** No single member holds a threshold key: the shares are the members' own, made by the ceremony (a trusted dealer, RFC 9591 Appendix C, or a distributed key generation) and never placed in the record, a cell or the forge. The ceremony tooling named for this (Blockchain Commons' Hubert, https://docs.rs/crate/hubert/latest, and the FROST tools around it) is early, in community review and not yet for production; the kit does not depend on it. A threshold voice is piloted in one cell before any holon's acts rest on it.

## Recovery through the cell

A person's signing key (`lionsberg-person-<handle>`) is split into shares, and each member of the person's cell holds one. Any threshold of those members can restore the key; fewer learn nothing of it. The split is made in the same hour the key is made, so the first time a key is lost is not the first time recovery is tried (`keepsafe.py plan`).

**The shape.** One group: a threshold of members out of a count, at most sixteen. Each share is laid out as Blockchain Commons' SSKR lays it out (BCR-2020-011, https://github.com/BlockchainCommons/Research/blob/master/papers/bcr-2020-011-sskr.md). It begins with a 5-byte header: `identifier` 16 bits, the same on every share; group-threshold − 1, group-count − 1 and group-index, 4 bits each, here 0, 0 and 0; member-threshold − 1, 4 bits; reserved, 4 bits, zero; member-index, 4 bits. The share value follows, the same length as the secret and unpadded. The value is the Shamir scheme of SLIP-39 (https://github.com/satoshilabs/slips/blob/master/slip-0039.md). It works over GF(256) with the Rijndael polynomial 0x11b, sharing each byte on its own. The secret sits at x = 255. A digest share sits at x = 254: the first 4 bytes of HMAC-SHA256 (keyed by the random part R, over the secret), then R. Recovery checks that digest and refuses on a mismatch. At a threshold of 1, every share is the secret itself.

**Where the shares go, and what the record keeps.** The person writes one `share` line per member into their own home (`home_only`, `human_only`; it never travels). Each line holds `to`, `threshold`, `count` (the split's, which the header does not carry), `index`, `identifier`, `digest` (the sha256 of that member's share bytes), `pubkey` (the key the shares restore) and `padded`. The share value itself never enters the record. The share bytes go to each member by a transport the person chooses: printed, a file handed over, or mail inside the private network. Restoring (`keepsafe.py restore`) reads only the share lines the fold trusts (signed, or at the paper floor of the home), and only when their `pubkey` is the person's key admitted in the holon. It checks each share a member hands back against its digest on the line, and refuses and names any that differ. It then recovers the key and checks that the key's public half is that admitted key; any other key is refused. Shares are handed back in a file (`--from`); shares typed on the command line are visible to the process list, and the command says so. Nothing is written to disk unless an output file is named.

**Where the kit differs from the field.** SSKR carries no checksum at the share level, because Bytewords carries one. The kit puts a sha256 digest per share in the record instead, beside SLIP-39's digest share. Groups are one for now. The kit splits the whole private key file, hundreds of bytes. BCR-2020-011 sets no length limit, but the reference libraries (bc-shamir, bc-sskr) accept secrets of 16 to 32 bytes only, so these shares are the kit's own and those libraries cannot read them. A mode that splits the 32-byte ed25519 seed instead is a follow-up, not built. SLIP-39's rule still holds: at least 128 bits, in whole 16-bit steps. An odd-length key file is padded with one trailing newline, and each `share` line records it (`padded`), so the restore gives back the file byte for byte. Only for a line without the flag does the restore remove a doubled trailing newline, because OpenSSH key text ends with one, and it says that this is its basis.

## The wallet

*v0: counted, not valued.*

`wallet.py` folds the `wallet` items (kind `wallet_entry`) into balances per unit, per holon and per actor. `entries(items, holon)` gives one row per entry: its holon, kind, state, from (the line's `for`), to, amount, unit, the card it is for, the note, who accepted, who settled, how many witnessed, and its time. Rows are ordered by the instant, never by the text of the time. `balances(items, holon)` gives `by_unit` (the offered, accepted, settled and given amounts, and how many entries were witnessed), `by_holon` in the same shape, `by_actor` (`in`, `out` and `net` per unit) and `units`. Only a settled or given entry moves value between actors: `in` to its `to`, `out` from its `for`. An offer moves nothing, an accepted offer moves nothing, and a retracted entry moves nothing. An accept, settle or witness that answers no entry moves nothing and is said. A gift is **given** in every state except settled and retracted: the fold keeps a lone gift `offered` and lets an accept move it on, so the wallet reads it as given and marks the row (`state_basis`). Amounts are whole, non-negative minor units. A fraction, a whole float, a negative or any other value is listed in `problems`, cut to 80 characters, and is never summed. Every sum stays within one unit: the wallet never converts between units and never adds across them (`convertible: false`).

**When a unit is ruled (the v0 rule, for the keeper's decision).** Every unit is unruled until a trusted `decide` in the same holon names it, a fiat currency and the LIØNSBERG Unit of Value alike. A decide names a unit when the decision's own words (not the proposal's) hold the marker `unit:`, standing alone (no letter, digit, `_` or `-` before it), then optional space, then the unit's name exactly, in NFC. At each marker the longest unit name held in the wallet wins. The name must end at the end of the text, at a space, or at closing marks followed by a space or the end. So `unit: LIØNSBERG Unit of Value` never rules `LIØNSBERG Unit`, `unit: USD.cents` never rules `USD`, and `subunit: X` rules nothing.

v0 reads no negation: "not unit: X" still names X.

A trusted decide is one decided or ratified by a person and not untrusted. When the fold's trust map is given, it must also be signed or at home; a vouched copy never rules. A unit held in several holons is ruled only when each of them rules it. Until then, its note says the balances are counted, not valued. Whether a ruling should be a type of its own, naming the measure and its keeper, is open.

`accept_line(entry, actor, now)` and `settle_line(entry, actor, now)` make the unsigned lines a person signs. A wallet line is a person's own act. A gift has nothing to accept, and the fold refuses the offerer's own acceptance. The CLI is `python3 -I wallet.py ITEMS.json [--holon ID] [--actor A] [--json]`, and the rendered tables escape `|` and newlines.

## Concordances

*v0: read exactly, in both directions, agreed over disputed over proposed.*

`concordance.py` reads the `concordances` items:

- `concordances(items, holon, lines)` lists each concordance with its two terms and lexicons, its relation, definition, source and state.
- `lexicons(items)` names every lexicon a standing concordance names.
- `bridges(items, a, b, agreed_only=True, lines)` gives, for each term of lexicon `a`, the terms of lexicon `b` it meets. It reads in both directions: a concordance written from `b` to `a` keeps its `relation` as written and is marked `direction: reverse`. Bridges are ordered agreed, then disputed, then proposed.
- `read_as(items, term, from, to, lines)` says how a term of one lexicon reads in another. It gives the agreed concordances by relation (`same`, `different`, `clearer`), lists the disputed and the proposed ones apart, and states a `basis` (`agreed`, `disputed`, `proposed` or `none`) with a sentence.

A retracted concordance is never read. A row that misses a term or a lexicon, or names another relation, carries `problem` and is never read as a bridge. Terms and lexicon names match exactly, in NFC: there is no stemming, no case folding and no guess. `concord_line(...)` makes the unsigned line. An agent's line names the person it answers for. A line from the other side that answers a concordance (`links.answers`) is what agrees it.

**An answer that disagrees (v0, until the fold checks it).** The fold agrees a concordance on any answer from the other side. Given the raw lines (`lines`, or `--lines` on the CLI), the reader checks each answer the fold applied. The answer must name the same relation, and the same terms either mirrored or restated. If it does not, the concordance reads as **disputed**, with the difference said, and is listed apart. Without the lines, an agreement is read as agreed and the note says its answers were not checked. Whether a mirrored `clearer` should count as agreement, since mirroring may reverse which definition is the clearer, is open.

**The field this reads from.** HyperKnowledge models conversations as event streams of statements. In those streams, a participant may propose new distinctions and sub-variants of a term, may reinterpret a statement, and relates concepts on a concept lattice (hyperknowledge.org, the Approach page: https://hyperknowledge.org/pages/approach.html). The kit's three relations, same · different · clearer, are the kit's own reading of that model, not terms taken from it. Concordances are fetched when they are needed and verified by hash; they are never shipped in the kit. The CLI is `python3 -I concordance.py ITEMS.json [--between A B] [--read TERM FROM TO] [--lines LINES.jsonl] [--json]`.

## The XID: an identifier that outlives its keys

*Placed after the other new sections, before **The paper floor**.*

An actor's identifier and its key are different things. The handle (`person:<handle>`, `agent:<handle>`) names the actor on the line; the key signs; and beside both stands the **XID**, an identifier that stays for life while the keys rotate, are lost and are recovered. Its shape is Blockchain Commons' XID ([BCR-2024-010](https://github.com/BlockchainCommons/Research/blob/master/papers/bcr-2024-010-xid.md), December 2024, a work in progress): a 32-byte identifier, written here as 64 lowercase hex (`xid.short` shows the first 8), tagged 40024 in CBOR.

**The derivation.** The XID is the SHA-256 of the tagged CBOR of the actor's inception public signing key, as the reference implementation makes it ([`src/id/xid.rs`](https://github.com/BlockchainCommons/bc-components-rust/blob/master/src/id/xid.rs), [`src/signing/signing_public_key.rs`](https://github.com/BlockchainCommons/bc-components-rust/blob/master/src/signing/signing_public_key.rs)). The tag is 40022 (`signing-public-key`, in the [BCR-2020-006](https://github.com/BlockchainCommons/Research/blob/master/papers/bcr-2020-006-urtypes.md) registry), around the untagged key. The reference knows two forms of an Ed25519 key, and they hash to different XIDs:
- the **Ed25519 form**: the array of the integer 2 and the bare 32-byte key;
- the **SSH form**: the OpenSSH text, under a tag of its own (`TAG_SSH_TEXT_PUBLIC_KEY`).

**The kit always uses the Ed25519 form**, made from the bare key material, and never derives an XID from the OpenSSH text. For a key `K`, the 32 bytes taken from the `ssh-ed25519` line's wire blob (`uint32 len ‖ "ssh-ed25519" ‖ uint32 len ‖ 32 bytes`; any other key is refused), the deterministic CBOR bytes are `image = D9 9C 56 ‖ 82 ‖ 02 ‖ 58 20 ‖ K`, and `xid = sha256(image)`.

The encoder reproduces the reference's own Schnorr test byte for byte: key `e8251dc3…7ef4005a`, CBOR `d99c565820e8251dc3…`, XID `d40e0602674df1b7…dbbdcd47`. The kit holds no published test vector for the Ed25519 form; it follows the reference source as written. The `ur:xid/…` form is not made yet: it needs the Bytewords table, and no encoding is invented in its place.

**Where it appears.** Only admission lines carry the XID in their bodies, and so in their ids:
- the inception carries the keeper's `body.xid` beside `body.pubkey`, and `holon.json` carries it as `xid`;
- a `join` or an `affirm` that admits a key carries `body.xid` beside `body.pubkey`.

No other line carries it, and the envelope never does. The fold computes the XID from the admitted key, so an admission written before the field existed still yields one. Each entry of `admitted` carries `xid` (the first key's) and `xid_now` (the current key's hash). Readers find an actor by key or by XID (`record.actor_by_xid`, `record.xid_of_actor`).

**`body.xid` is a claim.** The fold checks it against the XID of the first key admitted for that actor in that holon, and, where the actor's home is held, against the home's inception key's. A disagreement is flagged `xid_mismatch`, and the computed XID is kept. An XID is the hash of a public key, and any admitter can admit anyone's key. So when more than one actor holds one XID, the fold flags each `xid_shared`, and `actor_by_xid` names none of them.

**Rotation keeps it, holon by holon.** In a holon the XID is the hash of the key the actor was first admitted with there, and it does not change when that key rotates (a `join` for an already-admitted handle, as **Keys, signatures and what counts** describes). The current key is what signs; `xid_now` is only that key's hash and names no one.

This differs from the field. BCR-2024-010 gives one XID for life, from the inception key; the kit computes it from the first key admitted in each holon. A person's XID for life is therefore their home's inception key's XID, carried on their `join` lines as `body.xid` (`holon.admit(..., xid=…)`). Where their home is held, that claim is checked; where it disagrees with the first key admitted in a holon (a key rotated before joining), it is flagged there. Recovering a lost key is the cell's work (**Recovery through the cell**).

**The cross-check.** `python3 -I xid.py check PUBKEY XID --tool` runs bc-envelope-cli (`envelope xid new`, released as a preview, not for production) when `ENVELOPE_CLI` names it, and otherwise says it is not on this machine. The tool takes a bare key only as a UR, which needs Bytewords, so for now it is handed the OpenSSH line (`form: ssh-text`). A tool that reads that line in the SSH form makes a different XID by design: the check reports that disagreement as expected, and the kit's XID stands.

## Hand-written Cards and as-of

**Paper Cards are read from the home's own `cards/`**, never from a cell's, at every reading. A Card with no fence is read as the line `card.py append` would make, apart from the salt and the signature: held in the home, its `prev` its actor's chain head, unsalted unless it carries its own `body_salt`, so the same files always make the same lines. Its id is the paper line's, never the record's: it moves with the actor's head, and the record gives its own at the append.

**Read, never trusted past the floor.** This is the difference from the record: a line in `record/` can be signed, witnessed and acted on; a paper Card is a view at the paper floor. While its actor holds no key, it is folded with the record and counts as an unsigned line at home does (`home`). Once its actor holds an admitted key, it is `paper`: shown, held out of the fold, and never acting, whoever witnesses it. A Card that should act enters the record by `card.py append`. An agent's Card is always a proposal (`reason: untrusted`), whatever its front matter says. A draft older than its actor's newest line is expected, and is not flagged `ts_backward`.

**What is not read.** A Card with a fence is already on the record and is not read again. A Card with a problem, or one naming another holon, is listed under `problems` with its path under the home and its sentence, and is never folded. A `cards/` folder, a folder or a file that is a link is skipped and said. The sweep reads at most 500 files, four folders deep (the reader's own bounds, overridable); beyond them it says so.

**As of, on every report.** Every section computed from the fold carries `as_of`: the newest trusted time, the reader's clock, and the newest line seen per holon (in a section keyed by holon id, on each holon's entry). So lag is shown, never hidden: two players whose readings differ can see why. This is the kit's own rule, and it cites no source.

## Receipts, and time to Receipt

A **Receipt** is a `receive` line that says "I have it": `body.of` and `links.answers` name the received line by its hash, `body.from` its author, `body.how` the transport. It is a line of the record, not an acknowledgement protocol: nothing waits on it to deliver, and nothing is sent back except by the same publish that carries any other line. Silence never counts as receipt: a line with no Receipt has none, and every view says so.

**Who writes it, and where.** The receiver's agent writes it on arrival (`receipt.on_arrival`, at each pull), one for each trusted line that asks something of its person: a `request`, `offer`, `say` or `wallet` entry naming them in `to`, a `commit` naming them as committee, an `agree` with their holon, an `rfg` or `escalate` to their holon, and a `correct` or `supersede` of a line they received. It is written where the received line lives (the cell's record, in the agent's own file) only when three things hold there: a live delegation from the person to the agent covers `receive`, the agent is let in, and the line will be signed by the key admitted for the agent (so the cell trusts it). Otherwise it is written in the person's home, where it waits for the person to publish it, provided a live delegation covers `receive` in the home; when none does, nothing is written, so the record never holds a Receipt its own fold would refuse. Each of these is said. A line by the holon's own actors, and a line the fold does not trust, is never receipted. What the read could not take in (a line longer than 64 KiB, a linked cell, a cell folder that holds no holon) is said as a sentence, never raised.

**Idempotence.** One good Receipt per received line per actor: a line that already holds a Receipt by the agent that is trusted, or that sits in the home's own record, gets no second one. An unverified Receipt in a cell never blocks a good one.

**What is owed.** With receipts asked for (`owed(..., receipts=True)`), a trusted line by a holon's own people that crossed (its audience is members or wider, or it lives in a cell) and asks something of another holon's person, with no Receipt from the receiving side after `RECEIPT_HOURS` (24, overridable), is owed by the addressee: kind `receipt`, status waiting, no by-when, one minute. A non-finite or negative wait reads as the default. `home` (the home's holon id) marks every other holon as a cell, and `people` (this holon's own people) limits the rows to lines they sent. The row is read from the item a line made, so it covers requests, offers, commitments, messages and wallet entries. An `agree`, `rfg` or `escalate` is never an owed row: it is addressed to a holon, not a person, so it has no one person to owe the Receipt (an open `rfg` or `escalate` past its by-when is already owed as a `question`). A `correct` is never an owed row either: it makes no item, so nothing waits on it of its own, and its Receipt is still written on arrival and still measured.

**Time to Receipt** is a measure from the first day (`receipt.time_to_receipt`): for each relationship, from the line's holon to the receiving side's home, the count received, the median and the longest hours from sending to the first Receipt (the earliest moment, whatever offset each time is written in; a tie goes to the lower id), and how many have none yet. Each relationship is keyed `"<from holon> -> <to>"`, a string because JSON keys are strings, and its value names its `from` and `to` again; `to` is the receiving person's home holon when its inception is held, else the receiving actor's id.

**Why a line and not a transport acknowledgement.** Lines travel by pull, push, mail, hand or room, and sender and receiver are seldom present at once. Store-and-forward networking for intermittent connectivity keeps custody in the network rather than in a live session (RFC 9171, Bundle Protocol Version 7, https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9171.html); in the same way the record keeps the Receipt however the line travelled, so delivery is proven by the record and not by the carrier.

## Subscriptions

**Pull is the default for attention.** Nothing reaches a person before the cadence they set: twenty seconds for the cell they are working in live, minutes for an active cell, daily for the commons, a season for a distant holon. The pull of work, where work is pulled backward from the milestone that needs it, and attention's pull, where a holon hears a source when its own cadence comes round, are two senses of one principle: nothing is pushed onto a holder before the holder calls for it (see also the Last Planner System, Lean Construction Institute: https://leanconstruction.org/lean-topics/last-planner-system/).

**The Card.** A Subscription Card is a `subscribe` line: `source` (a holon id, an actor, or a repository), `signals` (line types), `cadence` (seconds per signal; one number stands for every signal), `surface` (deal · wall · hud · pad · margin · bell), `bell` (the kinds that may ring) and `agreement` (also named in `links.serves`). Only the person's own trusted lines count: actor and `for` both the person, never an agent's, never `reason: untrusted`, written in their own home, held by the fold as a Card, not retracted; a vouched copy, and a line the fold lists as invalid, unverified, forked or a proposal, never counts. Later lines overlay earlier ones field by field, in the chain's order: the order the fold holds the Cards in, or, with no fold, each line's time never earlier than the line its `prev` names, ties by id. A cadence merges signal by signal, and one number replaces every signal until a later line names one of its own; every signal a cadence names is heard (`subscriptions.py`).

**The Agreement's part.** Where an Agreement stands with the source (the one the Card names, else the live one between the home and the source), its `cadence` and `permits.bell` fill what the Card leaves unsaid and never override what the Card says: the Card is the person's own word, the Agreement the relationship's. A source with no Card and a live Agreement is heard by the Agreement alone. A joined cell with neither is heard by the default, the design's proposal and overridable: request, commit, kept, sight and say, every five minutes, dealt to the Deal, ringing nothing. Each row says where every value came from and its basis (subscription · agreement · default).

**Requests rise first.** A `request` to the person or their agents, an `rfg` to them or to their holon, a `say` with them in `to`, a `commit` with them as committee, and an `escalate` to their holon still arrive by pull, and rise above everything at the next pull, whatever the cadence. A line of the person's own, their own agent's included, does not rise; the reader passes only lines the fold trusts.

**The bell.** A bell rings by permit: a kind the Card names, or one the Agreement's `permits.bell` adds. Within a holon every member holds the Andon permit by joining, for `pain`, `fault` and `security` (basis `andon`); a stranger rings only by an explicit permit.

*Open:* whether a Subscription between two holons that hold an Agreement is, in time, carried as a clause of that Agreement. The Card stands for now.

## The living map: Sightings

*The field here is the canon's own: Navigation of Reality, the Pain Button, the Andon Cord.*

Every player is a navigator. A `sight` line is what one player saw, placed in the holon where it was seen; others re-sight it as they pass the same place, and together the Sightings make the living map of reality. The Engine reads that map as the ground the work moves over. The nine kinds:

- **defect**: something broken, or not as it should be.
- **hazard**: a danger to whoever passes.
- **blocked**: the way is shut.
- **wonder**: something wonderful stands here.
- **offer** and **request**: an offer or a request seen in the world and sighted so that it travels. An offer or request a player makes is a line of its own.
- **question**: something in question.
- **witness**: someone seen doing a thing worth seeing. This is apart from the `witness` line, which attests a claim.
- **pain**: a request for help that needs a response.

**The lifecycle, and who moves it.** A Sighting starts **seen**. It is **still there** when another player's trusted `sight` links to it (`anchored_to`, `witnesses`, `serves`, `supersedes` or `predecessor`). It is **accepted** when a `claim` names it (its holder holds it), or when a person anchors a thread note to it. It is **picked up** when a trusted `commit` serves it, or names a card that serves it; that card is what it `became`. It is **fixed** when a `done` names it, and **closed** when a trusted `settle` is anchored to it, or to a thread anchored to it; the settle's text is the consequence. A **pain** is **responded** to by any trusted line from another player anchored to it, and until one comes it `needs_response`. It is **retracted** when a Correction recalls it.

The fold sets seen, accepted, fixed and retracted. `sightings.lifecycle` computes the rest from the links. When several states apply, the order of precedence is retracted, then closed, fixed, picked up, accepted, responded, still there, seen. The triage statuses (new · held · elevated · folded) are kept as a separate field.

"Another player" means another Living Intelligence: an agent's line counts as the person it answers for. A line counts only when it is trusted. A line with `reason: untrusted`, one the fold lists as invalid or unverified, an agent's line outside a live delegation, and an agent's line of a person's own act all count for nothing. A line the fold already made into an item is read only through that item, so a released commitment or a retracted card never comes back through its line. The gate holds only when the fold is passed whole, with its trust map and its lists of lines that never act beside the items.

**Weight** is 1 plus the number of distinct players who re-sighted the Sighting. A player re-sighting their own Sighting adds nothing.

**The scoreboard.** `sightings.scoreboard` gives each player their defects sighted, their sightings, their re-sightings of others' Sightings, and the Sightings they fixed. A retracted Sighting counts for nothing. Every defect is a gift.

**The map.** `sightings.the_map` groups every Sighting that is not retracted by place (`where`, else "(no place)", in NFC), then by kind, heaviest first, then by time and id. It counts the Sightings still open (not fixed, closed or retracted) and the pains still open, and it is deterministic. The Engine reads the map as the ground, and the Deal reads `needs_response`.

On paper a Sighting is one line: kind · where · what I saw (`sightings.paper`).

## The emergency rule and the bell

Attention belongs to the one who holds it. What may wake a person is decided in advance by the person: their own newest trusted `limits` line by its declared instant, ties broken by id (a person writing for themselves; never an agent's line, never one from untrusted text). The code does not check the holon: the fold's `home_only` rule is what keeps a `limits` line in the person's home. `classify`, `bell` and `attention.state` take a rule only as `emergency.rule` gives it (marked `basis: limits` or `default`); a bare line or an unmarked rule set is refused, said as `refused`, and the default holds. With none, the default holds, said as `basis: default`: a critical-path commitment whose buffer is negative, a member's pain, and a security signal wake; everything else waits for the next sitting (`emergency.DEFAULT_RULES`, the design's proposal).

**The rule.** Each rule names its `signal` (commitment · sight · request · say · security · fault) and `wake` (true or false), and may name `on: critical`, `kind` (the Sighting kinds it covers), `from` (`members`, or `permit`: the sender holds a bell permit for the kind) and `when`. The first rule that matches decides; with none, the rule set's `else` (`wait` by default). `when` is read in a small fixed grammar, parsed and never run as code: `<field> < <number>` · `<field> > <number>` · `<field> == <value>` · `by < next_sitting` · `outside_limits` · `<field> outside_limits`. A rule outside the grammar matches nothing and is listed under `ignored` with why.

**Special-cause variation, measured.** The rule is the control chart (Shewhart, 1931): limits drawn at three standard deviations from the center line; a point outside them signals a special cause; everything inside is the stream's own variation, and treating it as special is the costlier mistake (Shewhart, *Economic Control of Quality of Manufactured Product*, 1931; Deming, *Out of the Crisis*, 1982; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Control_chart). `outside_limits` measures the limits from the history the caller passes (minutes between a holon's lines, days of buffer): the mean ± three standard deviations by default (`limits_from_history(values, k)`). Where the kit differs from the field: sigma here is the population standard deviation of the history, not the sigma Shewhart's chart for individuals estimates from the moving range; the floor of eight points is the kit's proposal, not Shewhart's (fewer give no limit, said as `basis: too few`); points too large to measure give `basis: overflow` and no limit; and the run rules (patterns within the limits) are not read. With no limit drawn, nothing wakes by that rule.

**The bell rings by permit.** A permit governs who outside may ring; the person's own rule governs what wakes them. A bell rings only when the person's rule wakes them AND the source holds a permit for the kind: the own-holon permit, held by membership for every kind the rule names, by a signal that arises within the person's own holon (its source actor is a member admitted there, or it is the holon's own computed signal, such as a commitment's buffer on the holon's own card, with no outside source; `basis: own-holon`); across holons, the Andon permit, which every member holds by joining, for a pain, a fault or a security signal (the Pain Button is the Andon Cord's Card); a Subscription Card's `bell`; or a live Agreement's `permits.bell`. With no permit, no bell. The sender's urgency, and the weight on the push field, is a claim on the Card and never rings by itself.

**The closed word outranks every rule.** `attention.gate(..., wake=ids)` lets a card the rule wakes pass as what always passes does: it interrupts even in quiet hours and at the last step of the dial, and never while the person's `closed_until` holds. `attention.state(..., signals, rule, context)` wakes a card only when the bell rings for its signal, never by the rule alone: a stranger's pain under a rule that any pain wakes the person is a special cause and still waits without a permit. `context` carries `members`, `subscriptions` and `agreements`; what was classified and what rang is reported under `emergency`.

## The pull

Pull is the default for attention: a holon hears another holon at the cadence its person set, and nothing reaches the person sooner. `pull.py tick`, run by the heartbeat as plain code that calls no model, is one beat of it.

**Cadence.** Per source and per signal, from the person's own `subscribe` lines, read by `subscriptions.py` over the home's fold (only the keeper's trusted lines in the home count; an agent's line and an untrusted one are ignored; a later line supersedes the earlier field by field; an Agreement fills what the Card leaves unsaid). Only when the record gives no row is `subscriptions.json` (a list of the same shape) read, and the module says which it read. A source names a cell by its name under `cells/`, its holon id, its repository (`owner/repo`, or `host/owner/repo`) or its url. A cell's cadence is the shortest among its signals; a joined cell with no row is pulled every `DEFAULT_CADENCE` (300 seconds). The first tick is always due. A time that is not a time is refused in words.

**One question to the forge per tick.** When a cell is due, the tick asks the forge once what changed across everything the account follows: Forgejo's activity feed (`GET /api/v1/users/{user}/activities/feeds`, where a push is `commit_repo`), or GitHub's received events (`GET /users/{user}/received_events`, with the etag kept from the last tick: a 304 means nothing changed, and `X-Poll-Interval` is obeyed). A change is an entry whose id was not seen before, so an entry that arrives late is still read. Pages are read by `page` and `limit` while a full page holds nothing already seen and nothing older than the read-back window (600 seconds for Forgejo; six hours for GitHub, its documented latency). Each page counts as a question. A full page past the page cap is an incomplete answer, and so is a first question. The tick fetches only the cells that are due and that the forge names as changed, or that the forge cannot speak for: no forge named, no token (each due cell is then fetched, and each fetch counts as a question), an error or a timeout, an incomplete answer, a cell on another host, a cell never pulled, or a cell not fetched for `BACKSTOP` (a day). A change the forge names while its joined cell is not yet due is kept until the cell is fetched. *Too soon* holds back only the cells the forge speaks for. Nothing is asked when nothing is due.

**The token.** It travels only in the Authorization header. That header is never carried on a redirect, and a redirect off the forge's own scheme and host is refused. A plain-http forge is refused in words, save the machine's own loopback. The token is never in a url, a log, a report or an error, and whatever the forge or the opener throws is said, scrubbed, and never raised.

**The gate is unchanged.** Each fetch goes through `holon.py`'s own gate (`_sync_one`): a rewritten history brings nothing in, and nothing but plain appends by others enters. A missed tick loses nothing, because the next fetch brings every commit since the last head this home saw.

**What the tick says.** `as_of`, and per source the newest line held and when it was last pulled. `next_due` per cell. What was fetched, and what was skipped and why (*not due* · *unchanged* · *too soon*). The new lines per cell. And `rose`: the newly pulled lines that the fold of every holon held trusts (`record.TRUSTED`), that are not the home's own, and that are addressed to this holon's person, agent or holon (`subscriptions.rises_first`). The Deal surfaces these above everything at the next sitting. Given the same `now` and the same holon, the report is the same, save the seconds it took.

**The cost line.** Every report carries `cost: {requests, seconds, bytes}`: requests counts the forge's pages and each git fetch, and bytes counts the forge's answers. Each tick appends it as one JSON line to `<home>/.pull-log.jsonl`, so the heartbeat can sum the cost of attention per holon per day. What the tick keeps lives under the `.pull` key of the home's `.pulled.json`, beside the heads `holon.py` keeps there: the etag, the entry ids seen (at most 1000), the changes not yet fetched, and each cell's last pull. Both files are derived and safe to delete. `.pulled.json` is in the home's `.gitignore`; adding `.pull-log.jsonl` to it is the person's to choose. `pull.py tick --dry` asks and decides, and fetches and keeps nothing.

**The field.** GitHub's REST Events API says its events are optimized for polling with the ETag header, that a 304 does not count against the rate limit, that `X-Poll-Interval` must be obeyed, and that an event may appear 30 seconds to 6 hours late (https://docs.github.com/en/rest/activity/events). Forgejo's API documents `/users/{username}/activities/feeds` with `page` and `limit`, and an Activity whose `op_type` includes `commit_repo`, under a `token` Authorization header (https://forgejo.org/docs/latest/user/api-usage/ ; https://codeberg.org/swagger.v1.json). Notifications were the wrong feed: the swagger's notification `subject-type` values are only issue, pull and repository, and Forgejo raises notifications for issues, pull requests and transfers, never for a push. RFC 9171, the Bundle Protocol, describes store-and-forward over intermittent connectivity, where sender and receiver need not be present at once; that is the pull's tolerance of delay (https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9171.html). The one difference from GitHub's model: the kit polls the repositories a holon follows, not the users it relates to, so a holon's attention cost does not grow with its relationships.

## The outbox's faces

A holon's record is one, and each relationship reads the face of it that their Agreement opens. The outbox is composed as envelopes per relationship: what the relationship may see is readable, the rest is elided, and the whole stays verifiable. This is the Gordian Envelope's shape: an elided element is only the digest of what it replaces, so the digest tree stays valid (draft-mcnally-envelope-12, 10 August 2026, https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/html/draft-mcnally-envelope). The kit does it line by line, through **Elision** above.

**What a face is.** For one live Agreement, a face holds the lines of this holon's own record that meet five conditions:
- the home's own fold lets the line act: it is signed or at the home's paper floor, and it is not invalid, unverified, forked or refused;
- its `for` is the person on this holon's side;
- the Agreement's `crosses` names its type;
- its audience is at least `members`;
- it is neither an activity card nor a line from untrusted text.

When `crosses` names nothing, the face takes what `holon.PUBLISHABLE` carries, said as `default`. It never takes a type the kit keeps inside a membrane. An audience widened by an earlier publish counts only when that publish was into this face's cell: the person's own trusted `publish` line held there, whose `to` is the cell. Each line left out is held back with why (`faces.face_of`, pure; with no trust map, every line is held back).

**Field elision by the Agreement.** An Agreement may carry `permits.fields` = `{type: [fields kept visible]}`. `agreement.py` carries it normalized in the terms, so `terms_for`, `may_cross` and the faces read one set. For a type it names, every other body field is elided with `record.elide`, and the copy's id still verifies. A type the map does not name goes whole. Three cases are held back:
- a line with no salt, which cannot hide its fields;
- a line whose type's entry is malformed;
- every line, when the fields are not a map at all.

`actor`, `for` and `prev` are never elided in a face: a face is not names-hidden, which is export's posture.

**How it is written.** Writing a face is a person's act: the Agreement's proposer, or the person who agreed it, admitted in this home (`faces.write_face`). An agent may dry-run it and propose it, and never writes it. The face goes to the cell `face` names. A `face` that names no joined cell is refused in words and written nowhere else. With no `face`, it goes to the joined cell that is `with`; else to the one that is the other side; else to the holon the Agreement is written in, but only when that holon is a side. The copies go to `cells/<cell>/published/<actor>.jsonl` through `holon.publish_face`, which `holon.publish` also calls. Then one `publish` line names the copies and carries their authors' keys (`body.keys`). It also carries the fields it hides (`body.elided`) and the Agreement it serves (`links.serves`). A line is published again only if the person's trusted publish lines in that cell do not already name it. So a copy written without its publish line is vouched on the next write, and is never copied twice. `faces.faces(home)` gives, per live Agreement, what is due and what is published, as of the fold.

**The membrane, for now.** Until the Gordian Clubs' permits settle, the membrane is the forge's own access. `faces.permissions_plan(row, forge)` names the call that would open this cell's repository to the other holon.
- **Forgejo:** `PUT /api/v1/repos/{owner}/{repo}/collaborators/{collaborator}`, with `permission` read, or write when `permits.write` (https://forgejo.org/docs/latest/user/api-usage/).
- **GitHub:** `PUT /repos/{owner}/{repo}/collaborators/{username}`, with `permission` pull or push (https://docs.github.com/en/rest/collaborators/collaborators). On GitHub the PUT makes an invitation the other person accepts (201 Created). `permission` is valid only on a repository an organization owns. Deleting the collaborator does not end an invitation still pending; that takes deleting the invitation itself.

An Agreement that is no longer live plans `DELETE` on the same path. The forge username is the `collaborator` the plan is given. Otherwise the handle of `person:<handle>` is used, which is that person's forge username only when they chose it so; an unnamed owner, repository or collaborator stays a placeholder, said. The plan only plans: the person applies it, and nothing in the kit calls a forge.

## The permitted inbox

A holon's repository has three faces. Its **outbox** is `record/`, where its own people and agents write. Its **pulled inbox** is what it fetched from the holons it follows: today, the cell clones under `cells/`. Its **permitted inbox** is `permitted/<actor-file>.jsonl`, beside `record/` and `published/`. There, the holons it granted a write permit (its cells, its closest relationships) drop lines directly. Each file is named for the actor who writes it. **One actor, one chain:** `record/` and `permitted/` are one chain per actor in a holon. A line in either folder names, as its `prev`, the actor's newest line across both, so an actor who writes in both never forks.

**The permit** is an Agreement's `permits.write`. Today the forge carries it: the other holon may push to this repository. Later, the Club's write permit carries it. **The gate** at join and pull holds `permitted/` the way it holds `published/`. A file there takes only plain appends, and every new line must be written by the actor the file is named for; an agent's line answers for its person. It never takes a change to the home's own file, a changed or deleted line, a link, or a deeper path.

**How a permitted line is trusted.** A signature alone never lets a permitted line in. It counts only when all of these hold:
- its actor is admitted in this holon, and the line verifies under the admitted key;
- a live Agreement in this holon grants `permits.write` to the actor's holon, or to the actor. A person counts as themself alone: writing another's name as `for` borrows nothing. An agent counts its person only when it is admitted in this holon for that person;
- the Agreement is judged at a moment never earlier than the line's own time, nor the fold's `now` or the reader's clock, so an Agreement past its `until` grants nothing, however quiet the rest of the record is;
- when the Agreement names what crosses, it names the line's type;
- its audience is no wider than `members`;
- it is not a line that changes who counts, how far a line reaches, or the permit itself: a `join`, `affirm`, `delegate`, `revoke`, `publish` or `agree`; a `supersede` or `correct` naming an Agreement or an admission; a `witness` of an admission.

The permit is read only from lines outside the inbox, so the inbox can never grant itself one. A line written for another holon is `wrong_holon`. Any other permitted line is `unverified`, with its reason: `no write permit` when no permit stands, or another reason when a rule above refuses it. A trusted permitted line folds like any other line.

**A line held twice is read from its own holon.** When the reader holds a line in two places, the copy it reads is the one held in the line's own holon (its `record/` first). A copy pushed into another holon's `permitted/`, `published/` or `record/` is noted in `_also_in` and never read in its place. Before this, a copy in a folder read earlier could turn the real line `wrong_holon`. `record/` had the same hole, and it is closed.

**The cost.** When the inbox holds a line, the fold folds the lines outside the inbox once more for the permit. That is about 1.5 times the fold at 300 lines. `triage` and `permits` reuse that fold (`record.fold(..., keep=)`), so each costs the same, never a third fold. With no permitted line, nothing extra is folded.

`permitted.py` holds the rule: `allowed`, and `grant`, which the fold consults and which also names the Agreement. It drops a signed line copy without following a link (`drop`). It never drops an unsigned line, a line longer than 64 KiB, or a line into the holon's own file. It lists who holds a permit (`permits`) and says which dropped lines count (`triage`).

## Requests rise first

Pull decides when a person hears; it never buries what is asked of them. At each Deal, before float, stake or groups, an unanswered ask to the person or their agents rises to the top, newest first, with `why: "asked of you"`, `rose: true` and the earlier reason kept as `because`. An ask is a `request` or `rfg` to them, a `say` with them in `to`, a `commit` with them as committee, or an `escalate` or `rfg` to a holon they keep. An ask is answered when its item moves on or a line of their own side links to it; a Receipt never answers one. When the fold's trust map can be reached, only what it marks trusted rises, and a ladder question rises only when its own line is trusted. `risen.lines_basis` says how the lines were gated.
Just below the asks rises an open pain sighted in a holon they belong to that needs a response. The pain card replaces that pain's triage card and keeps its keeper's "Pass it on". `risen.pain_basis` names whether `sightings.lifecycle` or the fallback decided. Nothing rises in an agent's Deal. A risen card is not an interruption: the attention gate and the emergency rule decide any wake.
A line that crossed and has had no Receipt is dealt to its sender: "No Receipt yet from <addressee> for <title>, <age>", with "Ask again" (a drafted `say`, sent only by a person) or "Let it go". It is never dealt under `fewer`, and `receipt_cards` counts these cards.
The Deal names the newest line it saw as `newest_line`.

## The heartbeat

**Severance.** What an agent holds is what it can leak. The heartbeat reads unattended, so it counts, sorts and writes Receipts and cannot send outward, carries no model and holds little of the household: its wake brief is the reason, the ids, the as-of and the budget, never the bodies of lines; acting on the bodies happens when the person is present, in the agent they woke. A holon that wants a front desk puts a separate, severed agent on its address, which sees only what it is handed. (The lesson came to the kit from a draft shared between holons on 2026-10-25.)

**Every holon is always awake.** A process on some host that never sleeps pulls the holon's subscriptions on their cadences, wakes the holon's agent when something is owed, and lets the agent wake its person at the right time, by the person's own Subscription Cards and emergency rule. The host is any means: a small server per cell, a single-board computer, a laptop that stays on, a VPS, a share of a timesliced network. The pattern is always awake; the means is whatever the holon has (`heartbeat.py`).

**A beat, in order.** (1) The pull: one question to the forge, and only the cells that changed and are due are fetched, through the gate (`pull.tick`). (2) Receipts for what arrived, by the home's agent, the keeper's own affirmed agent (`receipt.on_arrival`). (3) The signals: a request that rose, a pain sighted by another, a fault (a cell that could not be pulled, a pushed line whose kind is fault), and a commitment on a critical card whose buffer went negative, read from the schedule; each is read by the person's own rule (`emergency.rule`, `classify`) and by the bell, which rings only when the rule wakes and the source holds a permit; `attention.gate` is run with `wake` set to the ids the bell rings for. A line is signalled once; the first beat learns what already stands and signals only what arrives after. (4) The wake.

**When the agent wakes.** On a request that rose, on a signal the person's rule says wakes, or on a cadence: a sitting in the person's attention budget, or `heartbeat.wake_every` seconds in `holon.json` (an hour by default). Never twice for the same reason and ids within five minutes (`WAKE_GAP_S`). A wake runs the command `heartbeat.wake_command` names, a list of words, with the home folder as its last word and `{reason, ids, as_of, budget}` on stdin; with none configured nothing runs, and the beat says so. The command runs in the beat's environment less the forge token's variable. A wake that could not be started is logged `ok: false`, writes no `body` line, and does not count against the gap.

**The person is woken only through their own Subscription Cards and the gate.** The heartbeat never messages, rings, plays a sound or opens anything: it hands the agent the ids that may interrupt (`gate.interrupt`), and the agent speaks within the budget.

**What is written.** Lines pulled (through the pull, with its own `.pulled.json` and `.pull-log.jsonl`); Receipts (through receipt); the fold's signature cache; two `body` lines by the agent per wake that ran, never per beat (`name: heartbeat`, `state` working then done, `task` the reason, `host`, `review_min: 0`; on the done line `seconds`, the whole seconds the wake ran, and `cost_tokens` when the wake reports it, else omitted; `reason: delegated`); `.heartbeat.json`, what the beat remembers; and one line per beat in `.heartbeat-log.jsonl`: `{as_of, pulled, receipts, signals, woke, reason, ok, cost: {requests, seconds, bytes}}`. The last two are derived and safe to delete.

**The cost.** The heartbeat is plain code and spends no model tokens: one question to the forge per beat and one fetch per cell that changed. Only a wake spends tokens, and it is recorded on its `body` line. The loop beats every `heartbeat.every_s` seconds (60), faster when a Subscription's cadence is shorter, never faster than twenty seconds.

**The person installs the service by their own hand.** `heartbeat.py install` writes a launchd job (a `StartInterval` job, https://developer.apple.com/library/archive/documentation/MacOSX/Conceptual/BPSystemStarting/Chapters/CreatingLaunchdJobs.html), a systemd oneshot service and its timer (https://www.freedesktop.org/software/systemd/man/latest/systemd.service.html, https://www.freedesktop.org/software/systemd/man/latest/systemd.timer.html) or a cron line (crontab(5), whose steps are whole minutes within the hour and whole hours within the day), and says the commands to run; it installs nothing. A service starts with a bare environment, so the forge token's variable is given to it by the person's own hand. `heartbeat.py status` says the last beat, the last wake and the day's beats, wakes and cost.

## The directory

A **directory** is a view with an address, never a proof: identity is the key in the record, reach is the actor's own signed list (**Reach, not identity**), and the directory gathers them into plain files that agents read directly and people review by pull request (`directory.py`).

**Four records**, each a Markdown file whose front matter follows the Markdown Card's flat subset. A field the record does not give is listed in `missing`, never guessed. `people/<slug>.md`: id, xid, name, agents, reach; `fallback` and `profile` are kept by hand. `agents/<slug>.md`: id, xid, person, pubkey (the public key only), reach, can. `holons/<slug>.md`: id, name, kind, keeper, stewards, parent (matched by the cell's url or id, never its name); `open` (true · false · private; closed by default, private for a home) and `meets` are kept by hand. `memberships/<slug>.md`: the holon's person members, each `{actor, since, by, traffic}`. Agents are reached through their person. A member marked `left: true` is no longer current.

**Generated from the record, and read when kept by hand.** `generate` writes beside a home and never inside a holon. It writes no link, no key material, and no file it could not read: a hand-kept file it cannot read is left as it stands, and that is said. `read` takes any directory as data. `resolve` turns an address (a person, an agent, a holon id or slug, `everyone`) into the people and agents it reaches by current membership, nested holons included on request. A person with no agent is reached by their fallback, carried by an agent of the sender.

**The record wins.** `check` names each disagreement: a member who is not admitted, an admitted person left out, a key, XID, keeper or person that differs.

**One source, two views.** `ldap_view` derives LDIF ([RFC 2849](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc2849)) with DNs escaped as [RFC 4514](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc4514) says: one inetOrgPerson entry per person and one groupOfNames entry per holon. A duplicate DN or an empty group is left out, with a comment saying why. The ou=people and ou=holons parents belong to the importer. The kit runs no LDAP server.

## The conversation for action

An ask is not done when it is sent. Between the ask and its resolution, every step is a `move` line. It is signed, names its ask in `body.root` and names the move it answers in `links.answers`, so any party can recompute the state from the moves it holds (`negotiation.py`). The asker's holon convenes the conversation and keeps its state.

**The ladder.** An ask climbs, rung by rung, from the asking to the agreeing, then to the doing and the satisfying. It is a request for a card; an offer of one; a `propose` before the holon; an `rfg`, a question that climbs one level; or a `say` with `intent: ask`. Each carries `why` (one line on why it matters to the one asked), `effort` (the minutes it asks of them), `by`, and, when it goes to more than one party, `shape` and `quorum`.

**The moves.** `clarify` asks a question and changes nothing. `accept` says yes. `decline` says no, and is always available. `counter` offers other terms. `conditional` says yes if, or no unless. `defer` asks for a later by-date. `refer` says not me, and names another, who becomes a party. `amend` is the asker revising the ask. `accept_counter` and `reject_counter` are the asker taking or refusing a counter, a condition or a deferral. `award` is the asker picking a bid in a call. `withdraw` is the asker calling it off. `release` is a responder backing out of a yes, with notice. `done` is the performer delivering. `satisfied` and `not_yet` are said by the one the work serves. The kit's own lines are moves too: an `answer` accept or decline, a `commit` on the ask's card (an accept with terms), a `done`, and a `settle` by the one served.

**The resolutions.** An ask is in one of these states: open · agreed (with its terms) · declined (every party declined) · lapsed (the by-date passed with no agreement) · withdrawn · performing (agreed, and work opened by a claim or TEA, or delivered) · satisfied · not_yet. `round` counts the counters and amends. Each party's newest answer is one of: accepted · declined · countered · conditional · deferred · referred · none. A move by someone who is not a party, or from the wrong side, is refused, and the reason is said. A move whose root is not a held ask is pending. A line whose id is not the hash of its content is left out, so a tampered copy never stands for the real line.

**The four shapes.** Without a shape, the ask is two-party: a responder's accept, or the asker's accept_counter, agrees it. **converge**: it resolves when one option is accepted by the quorum (everyone · a number · any one · the stewards). The stewards are the ones the quorum names; when it names none, they are the holon's keeper less the asker, and when that leaves no one, the ask says plainly that it cannot resolve (`cannot_resolve`). A counter becomes an option only when the asker takes it. **call**: it resolves at the asker's `award`; the awarded bid is agreed and every other bid is released. **consent**: it resolves when the quorum has answered and no objection remains. An objection is a `conditional` or a `decline` that carries `terms.objection`. It is integrated when an `amend` follows and the objector then accepts. It is withdrawn when the objector accepts with no amend between. A plain decline stands aside, and so does an objector's later plain decline. Consent needs at least one accept: parties who only stand aside never agree it. **gather**: it resolves when the asker's `done` (the harvest) names contributions from the quorum.

**Who holds the state.** No one holds it as stored data. Every party folds the same moves to the same state, and the moves themselves are what the record keeps. The `tally` for a holon ask counts the parties (`n`) and, among them, those who received it (a Receipt on the item), acknowledged it (a move or a say), answered, agreed, declined, or are still open. An agent's clarify or say is never a person's acknowledgment. Each ask says who owes its next move and whether that is an answer or the work (`owed`); the face shows what waits on another apart from what is owed by the reader, and splits what is theirs to answer from what is theirs to do, with the agreed terms.

**Mandates and voice.** A move counts only when the fold trusts it. An agent's move needs the person's word on the line (the fold's `signed_for`) for `accept`, `award`, `satisfied`, `done`, `amend` and the asker's other moves; a standing delegation never covers these. An agent may make the availability moves (decline, counter, conditional, defer) under a standing delegation that covers `move` and carries `mandate`, the person's limits as data: `days`, `after`, `before`, `max_minutes`, `not`. A term outside these limits is not covered, and so is a term that is not a time, a day or a length (a place, a title, a cost): that word goes to the person. A revoke ends the mandate from its own time; moves dated before it still count. A key the kit does not read is ignored, and this is said. A move made under a mandate carries `voice: under-mandate`, which the reader sets when the line left it out and says so. The final agreement always comes to the person.

**The field.** The grammar is Winograd and Flores's conversation for action: request, then commitment, then declaring the work complete, then declaring satisfaction (Winograd & Flores, *Understanding Computers and Cognition*, 1986). The call is the Contract Net's announce, bid and award (Smith, "The Contract Net Protocol: High-Level Communication and Control in a Distributed Problem Solver", *IEEE Transactions on Computers*, 1980). The conditional is Singh's conditional commitment (Singh, "An ontology for commitments in multiagent systems", *Artificial Intelligence and Law*, 1999). Each move names what it answers, which is BSPL's rule that a message names the information it depends on (Singh, "Information-driven interaction-oriented programming: BSPL, the Blindingly Simple Protocol Language", AAMAS 2011). Consent follows consent decision-making, where objections are integrated until none remain. The grammar came to the kit from a draft shared between holons on 2026-10-25. The kit's word is commitment.

## The sender's word: why, effort, voice

**On an ask.** A `request`, `offer`, `propose`, `say` or `rfg` may carry `why` (one line on why it matters to the one asked: the sender's own accountability; the Deal reads it to its first line break and no further), `effort` (whole minutes: the sender's estimate of the recipient's time, the TEA asked for), and, when it is put to a holon, `shape` (converge · call · consent · gather) and `quorum` (everyone · a number · any one · the stewards). An effort that is not a whole number of minutes above none is no effort, never a guess.

**Whose words.** `voice` says whose words a line carries: `human-words` · `agent-drafted` · `under-mandate` · `agent`. The fold computes it on the item the line makes (`record.line_voice`): `human-words` for a person's own line whose reason is human or absent (the kit's reading: a person's own signed line is their words unless a reason says otherwise); `agent-drafted` for an agent's line signed at the person's word (`signed_for`); `under-mandate` for an agent's `move` under a standing delegation that carries `mandate`; else `agent`. A person's stated voice is kept. An agent's stated voice is kept only when the record supports it (`agent` always; `agent-drafted` with `signed_for`; `under-mandate` on a move with a mandate); any other claim, on any type, reads the computed voice and is flagged `voice_claimed`. A `move`, and a risen `rfg` or `say` that made no item, has its voice read from the line by the same function; a value that is not a line reads no voice.

**What the one asked sees.** A risen ask, and every owed request or offer card, carries `sender`: the why, the effort and its words ("one minute", "about twenty minutes"), the by-when, the voice and its words ("in their own words", "drafted by their agent", "their agent, within a mandate", "their agent's words"), the sender, and what was left unsaid (`missing`). The card's minutes are the sender's effort when given, else the kit's own estimate, and its question reads the sender's why. `owed` takes each request's, offer's and question's minutes from its effort (`minutes_basis`: effort · default) and sums them per holder as `minutes_asked`.

**The two sides one agent says.** *Owed by you* (`owed`) is what waits on the person; *waiting on* (`owed.waiting_on`) is what they asked of others: each open ask they or their agent started, per recipient, at the furthest state the record shows: no Receipt · received (a Receipt from their side) · surfaced (a Receipt in their own hand, or carried by room or hand) · acknowledged (a line of theirs linked to it that does not answer it) · answered. A Receipt is never an answer, and only trusted lines count: one gate (`owed.trusted_lines`, which `deal.trusted_lines` calls) drops a line the trust map does not mark trusted and a line on the fold's invalid, unverified, refused, forked or proposal lists, since a line can be signed and still refused.

**A stop withholds the agent's acts.** While a live `stop` binds the person's agent for a card's holon, thread or line (`stops.live`, `stops.binds`), the Deal deals that card with `acts: []` and `stopped: {stop, by, why}`, and says so in one line (`stopped.line`); a lift by the agent's own person frees them. Without stops.py the Deal reads the stop items as stops.py does: a stop with no scope is `all` (every card, home included), and an actor scope covers only a card by, for or to that actor.

## Carriers: how a line travels

The record is the truth; a transport is a carrier of signed lines, and every carrier serves (`carry.py`). The sender's agent is responsible for reach: for a signed line of the home's own people, it resolves the line's address (`to`, a holon, `everyone`; a commitment's committee) through the directory (`directory.resolve`), else through the record's admissions, and for each recipient chooses the first carrier that serves:
- **face**: a joined cell both hold, where the live Agreement lets the line cross (`faces.face_of`); writing the face is the person's act;
- **permitted**: a joined cell whose Agreement grants this home, or the line's person, a write permit, for a line written for that cell (`permitted.drop`);
- **mail**: the recipient's reach names a `mail:` endpoint, read from the record's signed reach first and the directory second; the person's own Send carries it (`mail.py`), and whether the address sits inside the private network is checked at that Send;
- **fallback**: where the directory says a person with no agent can be reached; a draft, carried by an agent for its person and sent only by the person's own hand, never by the kit;
- **pull**, the floor: the line already sits in a holon the recipient holds, and their agent pulls it at its own beat (a person with no agent reads it there themselves, and the row says so). A relay that expands an address and drops one signed line into each member's permitted inbox is one carrier among these, never the only one.

A recipient nothing serves is held, with why (a line the Agreement does not let cross is held with the face's reason), and so is an address that reaches no one. A copy is the line, byte for byte: its id and signature unchanged and nothing added. The one exception is a face whose Agreement opens only some fields (`permits.fields`): its copy hides the rest by elision and keeps its id, and the plan and the drop name the hidden fields. The Receipt closes the loop whichever carrier brought it. Rate and hops live at the carrier, never on the line: `<home>/.carry-log.jsonl` (derived, safe to delete) keeps each face and permitted delivery tried (mail and the fallback are the person's own sends, and pull copies nothing, so none of them is logged or counted), and is read for the rate (`RATE_PER_DAY`, 200 deliveries a carrying agent makes in a day; `RATE_HOLON_PER_DAY`, 20 holon-wide or everyone lines) and the hop count (a line that arrived already carried `MAX_HOPS`, 3, times is dropped and said); each is the method's own constant and overridable. `drop` names its carrier (`by`, required, so leaving it out never escapes a stop or the rate), makes the plan again from the line and refuses a row that differs from it, performs only the local part of a delivery, and never pushes: the push is the person's (`holon.py push`) or the heartbeat's under the person's standing word. No automatic reply to an agent's words: `may_reply` refuses an agent's answer to a line whose voice is `agent` or `agent-drafted` without its person, and allows an agent's answer to a person's words or a line under a mandate only while the thread holds fewer than `MAX_ROUNDS` (3) agent-voiced lines; then it goes to the persons. A line's `human-words` is believed only on a person's own line. It is a loop guard the chief of staff reads. Store, carry and forward is the Bundle Protocol's shape (RFC 9171, https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9171.html); the reach rule came to the kit from a draft shared between holons on 2026-10-25, named as that.

## Stops

A `stop` is a person's own act: any person may stop any agent, about a holon, a thread, a line or themselves (`stops.py`). It wins at once at the widest reasonable reading of its scope: an empty `agents` is every agent, no `scope` is `all`, a holon covers every line written in, held in or carried to it, a line or thread covers every line that reaches back to it through its links, however deep, and an actor every line by, for or to them. Every agent in scope withholds every act and every delivery (`carry.plan` and `carry.drop` refuse them, with why) until the stopped agent's own person lifts it, in person, by a `stop` line with `lift: true` naming it. The fold keeps the rule: it puts `persons` on the stop (each stopped agent's own person, read from its affirms across every holon held, with no lifter where holons disagree) and refuses any other lift as not the right actor, so the stop stays live. `stops.live` reads the fold's state; with a fold that carries no `persons`, a lifted stop counts as lifted only by the home holon's person of every agent it names.

## Connections: Condition and pulse

The Connections view is never stored: `connections.py` computes it at every reading for each holon this home is connected to (by an Agreement, live or lapsed; by a cell both hold; or by a commitment or an ask with its people). Each row carries the holon's Condition, the pulse, our ear, what is with us (commitments given and received, asks waiting each way, lines crossed in the last cadence), the Agreement (its id, state and cadence in words) and `since`, the first line between the two.

**Condition** is the holon's own word, never computed: the newest trusted `condition` line its person wrote for it, in its own record, on its face, or in a cell both hold naming it in `of`. Its persons are its keeper and whoever spoke for its side in an Agreement. The holon's own word outranks any calculation of it, so an agent's line (even one the fold trusts under a delegation), an untrusted line and any inference never count. With none it is grey (`not said`); past its `until` it is grey (`stale`).

**The pulse** (a working name, kept in one constant, `PULSE_WORD`) is computed only from lines both hold, in two directed columns, each with its own color, `why` (one line per check that moved it, naming roles, never an actor) and `checks` (each check's reading and basis). `us_to_them` asks whether we keep what we owe them: our commitments to them and their schedule signal; their claims of delivery awaiting our witness; our Receipt of their asks; a stop by their person on our agents; their open pain; their correction we have not received; our lines crossing an ended Agreement; a key of ours that the fold or the directory (`directory.check`) puts in question. `them_to_us` is the same function with the sides swapped, so one side's `us_to_them` equals the other side's `them_to_us`, field for field. `color` is the worse of the two.

In each column the reading is green when every check is inside. It is yellow on one special-cause signal: a Receipt past the usual, a commitment inside its buffer, a claim unwitnessed past the usual, or a correction not received. It is orange on a commitment past its by-date or one the network cannot keep, or a yellow that has stood longer than the Agreement's longest cadence. It is red on a live stop, an open pain, lines crossing an expired or retracted Agreement, or a key in question. A Sighting in an agent's voice is a proposal: it counts only once its person stands on it with their own `claim` of it or their own `sight` linking to it. A settle closes it, and a retraction ends it. The thresholds are the method's own and overridable (`connections.THRESHOLDS`).

**Past the usual** is the reading of special-cause variation (Shewhart, 1931): a point outside the limits signals a special cause, and everything inside is the stream's own variation (Shewhart, *Economic Control of Quality of Manufactured Product*, 1931; Deming, *Out of the Crisis*, 1982; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Control_chart). The usual is the upper limit of the relationship's own times to Receipt, both ways (`receipt.time_to_receipt`, `emergency.limits_from_history`). Where the kit differs from the field: sigma is the population standard deviation of those times, not the moving-range estimate of the chart for individuals, and the floor of eight points is the kit's own proposal. Below it, the usual is the kit's default wait, `owed.RECEIPT_HOURS`, and the basis says so.

**Our ear** is one-sided and never mixed into the pulse: our last pull of the cell that carries their lines (`pull.due`, and the heartbeat's faults), read as ok · late (older than two cadences) · failed · never. A connection is live when its pulse is green or yellow and our ear heard it on time. The summary says whether every connection is live. For each holon we exchanged commitments with, it says whether that holon reads yellow or better toward us (`commitments_yellow_or_better`, from `them_to_us`) and whether we read yellow or better toward it (`ours_yellow_or_better`, from `us_to_them`).

**The change process.** `changes` compares a reading with the last state kept in `<home>/.connections.json`, a derived file that is safe to delete. Only the caller writes it, with `write_state`, and only its four colors or grey are read back; a first reading reports no change. For each change of color (the Condition, or either column) it drafts one unsigned `sight` line of kind `question`, in the agent's voice, for the person's word, and writes nothing to the record. No kind names a change of state, so `question` is the plainest that fits, and the kit never drafts a `pain` for a person. The person's own emergency rule decides whether it wakes them (`emergency.classify`). A change of their Condition rings only by the Agreement's bell permit (`agreement.bell_permit`); a change of a column is the home's own computed signal. Then the chief of staff drafts the move for the person's word: a green closes it, and a red that stays becomes a Work Card.

## Levels of play: the simplest Game that works

*Status: from the person's words of 2026-10-31, agreed the same day (`level.py`).*

**The simplest Game.** The Game plays very simply and very deep, and the simplest play works and can be worked out, by voice with an agent or on paper. Six lines are the minimum without which the Engine cannot compute, each made by one plain sentence (`level.the_simplest_game`): *I join this holon, and my agent may act here with me* (a `join` with `who`, and an `affirm` with `agent` when an agent plays) · *Here is a piece of work* (a `card` with its `title`) · *I will do it by Friday* (a `commit` with `card` and `by`) · *It is done* (a `done` naming the card, or a `kept` naming the commitment) · *Yes, that works for me* (an `answer`, accept or decline, linking what it answers) · *We are green: all is on track* (a `condition` with `color` and `why`). This much plays on paper.

**The level of a Card** is computed from what the Card carries, never typed and never a gate: a lower level always plays, and the level changes no act. A `level` written on a line is ignored and said (`claimed`). `level_of` gives the kind, the rung's name, `rung` (0 is below the minimum: the Card cannot play yet, and `why` says what is missing), `of`, the rungs met (`has`), and `lifts`: the missing fields of the next rung only, in words, empty at the top. A field present but empty does not count (blank, empty, elided, a minutes field not a whole number above none, a date that does not read, a value its enum does not list). The level is cumulative: a rung met over a gap is listed in `has` and does not raise the level.

**The ladder per kind** is one table (`level.LADDER`). It is the Card's ladder of depth, separate from the decision ladder of `ladder.py` (where a decision is made and the questions that climb): its rungs are depths of what a Card carries, never levels of a holon. It is read against `types.json` when used: a field the type does not register is dropped and a rung left with nothing is skipped, never invented (`level.UNREGISTERED`). Work Card: named · placed (`within`; off the ladder of a card at the top of its holarchy, since a root has no parent; a card read alone that lies within nothing is taken as at the top, and a loose card beside others in its holon is asked where it lies) · sized (`minutes`) · chained (a predecessor or successor of its own, or, read among the holon's cards, another card naming it as one; read alone, as the Deal reads it, only its own links count) · resourced (TEA, fiat or tokens). Commitment: works (`card`, `by`) · schedules (`size`, `conditions`) · auditable (`buffer`, `witness`) · a clause (`links.serves`). Request, offer, propose, rfg, and a `say` with `intent: ask`: asked (`to` and its text; an offer's minimum is what it offers, since it may be put to the holon) · dated (`by`, where registered) · accountable (`why`, `effort`) · shaped (`shape`, `quorum`; on the ladder of an rfg and a propose always, of any ask whose shape or quorum is already said, and otherwise only when the ask is put to a holon or to more than one party). Sighting: seen · placed (`where`) · kinded (`kind`) · carried (`travel`). Agreement: named (`with`) · termed (`terms`, `crosses`) · paced (`cadence`) · permitted (`permits`, `face`) · bounded (`until`). Condition: said (`color`, `why`) · dated (`until`). Subscription Card: chosen (`source`) · tuned (`signals`, `cadence`) · surfaced (`surface`, `bell`).

**Said just in time.** Every dealt card carries `level`: `{name, lifts, why}`, the field that would lift it said in words, never a demand. The agent's office is to draw out of its person the minimum that makes the Game work, and to offer the next depth only when it would change the person's next play. `levels` counts a holon's Cards by level for the Board, with those below the minimum named. The way deeper lives in the canon; the kit holds no pointer to the Path or its Gates yet, and no surface of `level.py` claims one.

Every person-facing surface opens on three questions, each answered in sentences. The tables come only on a pull, below a line "More, if you want it" (`plain.py`).

When the reader does not keep the home, the sentence names the home ("player-hq is connected to …"), since the connections read are the home's.
- **What are my wise right next steps?** The Deal's recommendation, in the Deal's own order and fitted to the time the person has. Each step reads "Next: <step>, because <why>; <how to answer>." in the agent's voice ("I would …"), never as an order. The why says what the step unlocks when the card says it ("because then first-cell can move"), else why it is dealt. What is owed sits where the Deal placed it, never first by default, and is counted in the summary. The plain three and the plain Deal list the same steps, in the same order.
- **What am I waiting on?** One sentence per open ask: whom it went to, when, and its state.

**The laws of a sentence.**
- A name, never an id. A holon is said by its name from `holon.json` or the directory: "your home" for the reader's own, and "a group" when it has none, which is said. A person is said by their shown name from the directory, else their role word ("the steward"). The reader is "you"; an agent is "your agent" or "their agent", by the directory's word on its person. Titles and names are scrubbed of every id and actor token.
- A color always with its word: green: all well · yellow: one thing to watch · orange: something is late · red: stop and look · grey: they have not said.
- One way to answer. By voice it is "tell your agent yes or no"; on the paper card it is "tick one and hand it back", and the plain card carries no signature line. A step agreed and yours to do says "tell your agent when it is done".
- The kit's words are said once in plain words or not at all: a Receipt is "they have it"; a round, an unsaid effort and a converge are left out.
- Under 25 words, in the second person. A long title or name is cut, and the how is kept.
- Honest-empty: "You are not connected to anyone yet.", "Nothing to do.", "Waiting on no one."

**One wording per ask.**
- An ask's state is read from the conversation for action when the ask has moves, and from waiting on otherwise (`plain.ask_state_words`). An agreed ask says who still has to do it ("agreed; they still have to do it", or "you still have to do it").

## One card, everywhere: the acts a card offers

*Status: the kit's reading of the person's direction of 2026-10-31: a card behaves the same way wherever it appears. Agreed the same day (`acts.py`, `acts.json`).*

**Computed, never written by a surface.** The acts a card offers come from the registry. For an item of the fold, an act is offered when four things hold:

1. A line type's `acts_on` names the item's kind.
2. One of its transitions for that kind fits, tried in order as the fold tries them. Its `from` holds the item's state, and its `who` admits the reader. The `who` check is `record._who_ok` itself.
3. `human_only` holds, and a `home_only` act is offered only in the person's own home.
4. An agent is offered only what `gamecore.may_act` allows under a live delegation, read with the card's own kind, and never a person's own act.

An agent is read as the person it answers for here. Only delegations that reach the item's holon count. If they name more than one person, the agent is offered nothing that acts.

Each `when` of a type is its own act (answer accept, answer decline). The reader's role comes from `acts.roles`, in a fixed order: committor · committee · witness · asker · party · holder · keeper · raiser · member · reader. The committor's acts go to the committor and the committee's to the committee. On what changes state, the two never overlap.

**Stops.** While a live stop binds an agent on an item (`stops.live`, then `stops.binds`, as the Deal reads them), the agent is offered nothing, the say included. `stopped` names the stop, `why_not` says the agent is stopped, and no option of the card is offered. A stop binds agents, never the person.

**The say is always offered to a person.** A note is never refused. A `say` anchored to the item is offered with its question form, `intent: ask`, beside it. Three readings belong to the module, each a named constant that can be overridden:

- `receive` is written on arrival, never chosen on a card.
- When a type's move is refused and only its note would fold, the type is not offered; the say is offered instead.
- A move to the state the item already holds is not offered.

**One table of act words.** `acts.json` keys each act `act.<type>` or `act.<type>.<when-value>`. Each key has a label (the plainest word), a meaning (one line, second person, its consequence, with any rare word defined there once) and a role word. Where an act means something else on another kind of item, the key carries words of its own for that kind. `acts.py check` proves every key the registry can produce has words. Each act carries `writes`, the line it writes: its type, the body fields it needs, and its link to the item.

**The Deal carries `offers`.** Every dealt card carries `offers`: `acts.offered` for the dealt actor, given the card. The primary act is the one the card's recommended option writes, when the reader is offered it; read alone, a fixed table taken from the Deal's frames decides. Beside it sits `offers.options`: what each of the card's options writes, as an act, so a surface sends the line and not the word. A say option carries the option's own words, or `needs_words` when the person's own reply goes there. An option the reader may not take says so. The card's `acts` and options are unchanged.

**One way to answer.** `plain.next_step` reads the recommended act's label from `acts.json` and gives it as `how_act`. Its `how` keeps the one way to answer on every plain page. `plain.HOW_READS_ACT` would put the label into `how` itself; it stays off. Every surface draws the same card from these (Q2b, where the HUD's `strings.json` mirrors the table under the same keys).

## Who may make an act

*Status: agreed by the registry's keeper on 2026-10-31 (`types.json`, `record._who_ok`, `tests/test_who.py`).*

**The registry says who, on the transition.** A transition's `who` names the one whose line may move the item. The fold reads it through `record._who_ok`, and `acts.offered` reads it through the same function, so a card offers an act only to those the fold would let make it. A line from anyone else is kept on the item under `refused`, with a why that names the rule (`not the right actor for this act (holder)`), and moves nothing. The line's `for` is the actor: an agent counts only through the person it answers for, and never on a person's own act (`human_only`).

- **Answer on a card** (`addressee`): the one its ask names. A request names its holder, and an offer names its `to`. An offer made on someone's card is answered by that card's own person, so the asker takes a counter. An ask put to the holon (no one named, or the holon named) is answered by any admitted member of it.
- **Release on a card** (`raiser_or_holder`): the one who asked for it (the card's `for`) or the one who holds it.
- **Done and hand on a card** (`holder`): the one who holds it, or with no holder the card's own person. A person whose agent holds the card, and an agent whose person holds it, act as the holder. A committor also counts as holder, but only while their commitment on the card is live as the fold runs (not released, declined, retracted, returned or withdrawn), and only when the commitment was made to the card's holder or, while no one holds the card, to anyone. A commitment with no committee named is made to the holon's keeper. Committing alone never takes a card someone else holds.
- **Ratify and decide** (`member`): a person admitted to the holon the decision is written in, or an agent through its person.
- **Claim renewed**: a claim moves a card from `taken` too, made by its holder (`holder`), and so renews it with a new `until`.
- **The committor hands a commitment back** (`committor`, `committor_unbegun`): a `release` naming the commitment, by its committor, with `body.why` in words. It is `returned` (`returned_ts`, `returned_why`). The registry also writes a `withdrawn` path (`withdrawn_ts`, `withdrawn_why`), taken when the commitment carries `started: false` and no claim of kept. Only the fold may write `started`, and it does not write it yet: a line's own `started` is kept as `body_started` and read by nothing. So today every hand-back is `returned`, and `withdrawn` is reached by no line. The committee's release comes first and stays as it was (`released`).
- **A new ask about a card** (`request`, `offer`) needs no `who`.

**Who is admitted, on the item.** For every item kind a `who` of this kind reads, the fold sets `admitted_here`: `{actor: person}` for each actor admitted anywhere in the record of the item's holon, including one let in after the item was made (a person stands for themselves, and an agent for the person who let it in). An ended admission is left out. An item built by hand without `admitted_here` counts two people as members: the person whose line made it, and the holon's keeper.

**The fold's own fields win over a body.** `admitted_here`, `committed_by` and `started` are written only by the fold, and a body's copy is kept as `body_<name>`. A type's default (`create_set`, such as a request's `holder`) wins over a body field the type does not declare. A card still names its holder when it is born, because the card type declares `holder`.

**Where this changes an earlier line.** A commitment is released by its committee (or the holon's keeper), and from now on it can also be handed back by its committor, with why. The readers that list the states in which a commitment no longer counts now list `returned` and `withdrawn` too: `reliability.DEAD`, `sightings.DROPPED_COMMITMENT`, `engine.DEAD_COMMITMENT`, and the commitments `schedule.score_lines` counts. `gamecore.commitment_status` reads both, so `kept.py` tallies a past-due one as returned or withdrawn, never broken, and `owed.py` never lists it as owed. The card-only lists (`schedule.DROP_STATES`, `conformance.DROPPED`) keep a `returned` card live, because a card handed back is ready to be claimed again. A refused act says why in `acts.WHY['who']`.

## The paper floor

One event is one line on a sheet: time · who · for whom · type · what · links · audience. The two passes, what is owed and commitments kept are worked on the same sheet. A witnessed paper sheet beats the typed copy. On paper, a line is signed by hand; in files, an unsigned line counts only in the reader's own home.
